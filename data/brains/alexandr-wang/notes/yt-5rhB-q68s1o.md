## Meta Superintelligence Labs: mission and org design

- Alexandr says he joined Meta about 7 months before this interview. He calls the next 5 years "some of the most monumental discoveries" human civilization has made.
- Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL) is dedicated to building the organization that can deliver the scientific breakthroughs for superintelligence. It also builds the products that deploy this technology to billions of people.
- Meta's advantage, in his view, is reach. About 3.5 billion people use its platforms every day.
- He got to design the org and team "from a blank slate". His priorities were:
  - the best scientific foundations
  - the highest talent density
  - an environment for breakthrough research
  - removing artificial deadlines, so the technology is built at the best pace
- He says the first 7 months went to building foundations. He expects "incredible velocity" over the coming months and through the year.

## Research-led flywheel linking models, products and infrastructure

- Alexandr says Meta must be research-led, because this is a special moment for frontier AI.
- Internally he treats it as one flywheel:
  1. Frontier models are the "base material" for consumer products.
  2. Products that gain scale justify a larger infrastructure footprint, among the largest in the world.
  3. That infrastructure enables better models and more research.
- He sees models, products and infrastructure as a continuous discipline that advances in tandem.
- He argues research and product can no longer be a handoff, where research works in a corner and product deploys the result.
  - He cites ChatGPT and Claude Code as breakthroughs that came from researchers thinking about product and product people thinking about research, working hand in hand.

## Strategy: personal agents deployed globally, plus new hardware

- Alexandr describes Meta's AI identity as personal agents deployed globally. He argues the reach of roughly half the world using Meta products daily lets it deploy agents to whole communities and countries, which he says is hard for other labs to match.
- He cites Manus, which released agents that work 24/7 on a user's behalf toward their goals. He calls it the first of a series of personal-agent products. He thinks it may be seen in a decade as a big breakthrough in AI productization.
- On hardware, he says Meta has invested for many years in wearables and next form factors. His view is that personal agents make that vision "never more real".
- His picture is a constellation of devices beyond the phone. The agent is always on, "see[s] what you see, hear[s] what you hear", and helps more deeply than today's devices.

## Building durable organizations: patience over impatience

- Alexandr contrasts his younger self with today. He started Scale AI right after dropping out of college.
  - Impatience is "both a great strength and a great weakness". It makes things happen faster than people expect, but it doesn't set up long-term advantages.
- From studying great businesses, he argues the ones with staying power built a foundation that is very hard to replicate. He compares it to a seed that grows over decades.
- He applies this to MSL and to team building. The goal is durability and a differentiated point of view "all the way down to the organization".
- In his words: "you can't let your impatience drive you too far", and you should always ask what foundations you are building and what the long-term story looks like.
- He says MSL was set up for the long term, not to "cut a corner" for a short-term outcome and "mortgage the long-term opportunity".

## Why he joined Meta and the Scale AI deal

- Alexandr calls the deal "incredibly non-standard" and says it took Mark Zuckerberg's boldness and vision to come together.
- He frames it as a milestone for Scale. It gave back to investors, employees and supporters while setting Scale up for the future.
- He says Scale continues to execute for enterprises and governments under its team.
- He says he saw an "astronomical" opportunity at Meta. In the middle of the AI race, he argues, people lost sight of the fact that Meta had every ingredient:
  - distribution
  - billions of users
  - scale
  - a business model
  - talent
  - infrastructure
- The opportunity was to "lock everything in" so Meta could succeed long term.

## Responsibility, trust and model behavior

- Alexandr's view that some peers may not share is that technology must be developed with "extreme responsibility". That covers traditional AI safety and new risks from billions of people using it daily.
- He ties this to the product vision. A personal agent that holds your goals, hopes and fears requires a huge amount of trust from users, the public, governments and every other stakeholder.
- He says some in the industry have moved away from safety commitments, while Meta takes them very seriously.
- On model behavior, Meta works with philosophers and psychologists to shape how the model behaves, so it helps and empowers users.
- He wants a mutual relationship between humans and agents, where each wants the other to succeed. He says engineering and designing that is very important.

## Working with Mark Zuckerberg

- Alexandr says much of what is said about Zuckerberg is unfair. He describes him as "a very regular guy" and a family man.
- As a leader, he calls him "incredibly bold and ambitious".
- The trait that stands out is how quickly Zuckerberg sees the future. He takes a technological advance and plays it forward to the effect on users, consumers, businesses and the ecosystem. Then he works with the team to make it happen fast.
- He says Zuckerberg lets him and the team "dream bigger than we would otherwise".
