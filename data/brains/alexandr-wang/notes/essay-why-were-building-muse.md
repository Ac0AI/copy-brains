## Why Alexandr is building Muse: unspoken wants

- Alexandr's starting premise: "Most people never say what they want." People want "fiercely and constantly" (more time with family, overcoming social anxiety, eating healthier, opening a bakery, building an app, finding calm) but rarely express those wants, and more rarely see them come true.
- His claim: "When we can't name our dreams, we mostly can't reach them."
- He frames every unrealized want as "a quiet tragedy of human potential."

## Why dreams die: friction, not lack of desire

- Alexandr describes the world as "a maze of forms and gatekeepers and schedules and obstacles."
- Most dreams "die by a thousand paper cuts long before they are said aloud."
- They die in "the not-knowing-where-to-start and in the phone calls never made."
- The bottleneck he identifies is getting started and executing the logistics, not wanting.

## The product vision: a second mind that acts as a general manager

- He imagines every person having "a second mind beyond their own": a general manager whose whole job is to find out what you want and make sure it happens.
- It hears a half-formed sentence such as "I feel like there's something more I could be doing" and helps fill in the blanks.
- It is not a genie. It says, "This is the thing you've been dreaming of your whole life. Let's go get it. Here's how."
- What it does:
  - Builds a plan.
  - Sends the email.
  - Makes the phone call.
  - Finds the funding.
  - Keeps you on schedule.
  - Clears the way and solves the problems it can.
- The goal is that "the wanting and the dreaming" is all that's left for the human.

## The bet: unlocking everyone's agency

- Alexandr argues that "we have no idea what people will want."
- Until now the world has been shaped by "the small number of fanatics" who found a way to make their wants real.
- Humanity has never been seen "with every single person's agency fully switched on."
- The premise: eight billion people who "just need to write their dream down in order to get started making it happen."
- The input he wants Muse to require is minimal: write the dream down.

## Muse's mission

- Stated purpose: "we built Muse to help people build the world they want."
- The product is positioned as an agent that turns stated desires into executed outcomes, serving everyone and not only highly driven people.
