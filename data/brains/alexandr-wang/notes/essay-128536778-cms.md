## Personal agents as the next leap, and where Meta aims to deploy them

- Alexandr thinks this is "the year of the personal agent." AI is moving from passive, search-style systems to ones that "work for you 24/7."
- Agents should understand your goals, act on your behalf, and constantly look for ways to improve your life and get tasks done.
- He frames the opportunity as distribution: Meta has 3.5 billion people using its platforms every day. That lets it make personal agents "globally ubiquitous in a way that we think that very few companies have the opportunity to."
- Meta released Manus agents on Telegram after acquiring Manus. Meta plans to keep investing in this area through the year.
- The vision comes from Mark Zuckerberg's "personal superintelligence" essay. It asks what the world looks like if AI is deployed to every person, so they can build the businesses they want, explore passions or finish projects.

## Agentic commerce and why it doesn't threaten Meta's ad business

- Alexandr believes in agents "you would trust enough to ultimately make purchases on your behalf and ultimately coordinate very complex logistics on your behalf." He explicitly endorses agentic commerce.
- Asked whether agent-to-agent transactions would undermine advertising and the social graph, he says he is not very concerned.
- His reasoning is that new technology repeatedly enables new formats and products, and Meta's products have evolved that way through the years.
- If agents enable dramatically more commerce, he sees "a lot more opportunity to help businesses," not less.

## Models vs productisation: open source, frontier research and deployment

- Meta remains committed to open source and will "probably release a mixture of closed and open source models."
- He is equally committed to the frontier. When he joined about seven months earlier, a priority was setting up research to "deliver the very best models in the world" and to "get back to scientific fundamentals."
- A new series of models was coming "in the coming months."
- Productisation matters just as much. He says models are powerful, but "what we really, really care about is that we can actually deploy this technology to everyone in the world."
- The two-part goal is to build great AI in the lab and deliver it into products in new ways.

## Compute and infrastructure strategy

- He sees two drivers of spend. The first is "incredible returns" from putting more compute into research, because model improvement is still fast.
- The second is inference. Meta must serve billions of users, so it needs enough capacity to run inference at that scale.
- On chips, he describes a "multi-Silicon strategy": use many silicon providers and avoid reliance on any single one, "and this includes investing into our own Silicon."
- The aim is infrastructure that scales with demand. He declined to comment on talks about buying Google TPUs.

## India as a market and a place to deploy personal agents

- India is "one of the most important countries for Meta, full stop," with more than half a billion daily active users across WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook.
- He argues India is one of the few countries where a personalised, powerful agent for every user could be made real quickly, because of Meta's platform reach there.
- Meta's India investments include connectivity, support for enterprises, and a joint venture with Reliance Industries on enterprise AI adoption.
- On job disruption, he says that when small businesses and creators get AI tools, "the opportunity vastly outstrips any of the disruption."
- He expects AI coding and knowledge-work tools to be used more by small businesses, entrepreneurs and new businesses.
- He cites a founder at an Indian roundtable who said there are more Indian consumer AI companies than American ones. Backing entrepreneurship and small business is, in his view, "a nearly endless sort of growing sector."

## Trust as the first principle for personal agents

- A truly personalised agent will be "more intimate than any technology has ever been historically." It will know a lot about you and make many decisions for you.
- Because of that, trust is "incredibly, incredibly important." He says Meta's success depends on building trust with the billions of people on its platforms.
- Decisions about AI and future technology are "rooted in how do we build that level of trust," as a long-term "North Star and first principle."
- This was his answer to questions about OpenAI introducing ads.

## Building and keeping a frontier research team

- His main lever is the environment. Researchers are inspired by the science, so it is his and Meta's responsibility to build the best place for them to work.
- Talent density is one pillar: smart people want to be surrounded by brilliant people who push them and teach them.
- Compute is another: researchers need ample resources to test scientific ideas.
- A third is a return to basics. He says "we're going to focus on scientific rigour" and "we are not going to have artificial deadlines," approaching the work in a way that is "proper and correct for the long term."
- On compensation, he acknowledges Meta's hiring made headlines but says others were already paying researchers well, and that "more than the compensation, we really focus on creating the best environment."

## Builder's perspective and the definition of superintelligence

- On criticism of his youth and limited research background, he points to AI's dynamism: Google led, then OpenAI, then Anthropic.
- He argues there are always openings for fresh perspectives that let organisations do things incumbents can't. His role is to bring "more of a builder's perspective" to differentiate Meta in the marketplace.
- He says he started his company at 19, in 2016, inspired by AI's potential. The focus then was autonomous driving, "the holy grail use case."
- He defines superintelligence as "AI that is able to outperform humans on any measurable task."
- He says Superintelligence Labs was named to signal the ambition of the research agenda. He admits the term risks becoming a buzzword but says Meta cares about "the scientific definition of very powerful AI."
- He says he and Zuckerberg are in sync on personal superintelligence, personal agents, global deployment and being at the frontier. He praises Zuckerberg as "always open to feedback."
