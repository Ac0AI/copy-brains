## Meta's AI position a year after Alexandr joined

- Alexandr says Meta released Llama 4 a little more than a year before this talk. He calls it exciting but "not quite on the trajectory that Meta needed" to keep building its products and experiences.
- Since he joined and started Meta Superintelligence Labs, the team has been building a new scaling ladder for its models, new infrastructure and new research to power a new family of models.
- In April, Meta released the first results of that work: the Muse Spark models and an update to Meta AI.
  - The reception was "even better than frankly we'd expected internally".
  - Meta AI usage rose sharply and the app reached the top of many app stores.
- He is candid that Muse Spark "is not at the tier of the leading frontier models". He calls it "a very exciting data point on the trajectory" and expects the upcoming models to be "quite competitive with the leading models in the world".
- Larger models are in training. He says the early results are "very exciting and promising", and that the next releases will sit higher on the same scaling curves.

## Predictable scaling as the research strategy

- Alexandr says Meta built its whole research effort around predictable scaling. The central belief behind the modern AI boom is that scaling models gives "predictable levels of increased capability".
- Muse Spark was an early point on Meta's scaling ladder. Later models are meant to be greater points on the same curve.
- What it takes to reach the frontier, as he lists it:
  - Keep scaling data and compute.
  - Keep driving underlying research breakthroughs.
  - Build the infrastructure to support all of it.
- He describes this as a year when all the labs are "dramatically scaling up" their models. He thinks Meta is on a faster trajectory because it spent the past year on this groundwork.
- He treats resources as secondary. The work is to build the infrastructure, scale the data and compute, train the large models and show them.

## Open source versus closed release decision

- Meta used to open source everything. Muse Spark is not open source.
- Meta updated its "advanced AI scaling framework", its view of the risks in very powerful models and how to handle them in early testing. It published what it saw while training Muse Spark in a preparedness report.
- Muse Spark triggered some high-risk areas in early training, particularly bio risk, and a number of other risks were elevated. He notes the whole industry has seen this as models have improved.
- His reasoning on release format:
  - In a product, Meta has many ways to mitigate risks and launch safely.
  - With an open source model, people can use it "in all sorts of contexts that we may not have full understanding of", so mitigation is much harder.
- Meta is now developing models it believes are "fit and safe to be open source" while keeping as much performance as possible. So open source continues, but he says Llama is not necessarily the brand. He gave nothing on branding.

## Where the next models will be strong

- Muse Spark already stood out in three areas, even as a much smaller model than Meta intends to train:
  - Multimodality (images, video, audio), which he calls critical for Meta's business.
  - Health, which he sees as critical as models reach billions of people.
  - Early "vibe code" results, such as creating small games or artifacts.
- Meta is doubling down on these and investing in agentic capabilities. Upcoming models should be very capable agents combined with those strengths.
- His stated goal is to build "the best personal agents for everybody around the world".

## Agent vision and how he uses agents

- Meta just announced a business agent, which advertisers can use to interact with customers. A consumer agent is also in development, and he says it is being "actively cooked".
- His view of how people will use agents:
  - One, maybe two, or a small handful of agents.
  - A personal agent for health, personal relationships and being a better parent, friend and family member.
  - The same agent may carry into work, especially for entrepreneurs, small businesses and smaller organizations.
  - Inside larger enterprises, work and personal agents may split, much like email.
  - Agents become "deeply personal", and people rely on them for more of their personal and work lives over time.
- Trust and privacy: he calls this one of the most important societal questions for agents. He says Meta has built technology on agentic safety so agents respect privacy and boundaries, and that it is an industry-wide problem, not only Meta's.
- His own use:
  - As a leader, agents help him understand what is happening across the company and support his team.
  - The most exciting uses are personal: an agent to help him be healthier and one to help him keep in touch with friends. Both were historically hard for him to stay on top of, and he calls the change "pretty transformational".

## US leadership, AI and jobs, regulation

- On China: he says it is "incredibly important" for the US to lead on AI technology and its economic benefits, and that Meta focuses on contributing to that. He thinks the US is ahead now, but says it should always track progress in other countries, especially China, and understand the reasons behind it.
- The main thing he wants to protect the lead is continued pace of scaling. He calls research progress today "nearly miraculous".
- On layoffs at Meta at the end of the previous month: he says saying goodbye to teammates is "incredibly difficult" and that Meta does not take it lightly.
- On AI and jobs:
  - It deserves close attention and tracking.
  - He says AI is enabling more new businesses than ever, which Meta sees in its data. He expects more entrepreneurs and small businesses as the tools improve, and says Meta wants to support small businesses worldwide.
- On the Trump executive order for pre-release model review: he welcomes the administration's involvement in responsible deployment. He says regulation is "notoriously difficult to get right", so it is a balance, but models are becoming dramatically more capable, so deployment should be thoughtful.
