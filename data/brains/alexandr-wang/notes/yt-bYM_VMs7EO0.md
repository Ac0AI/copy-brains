## Mission and how MSL is organized

- Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL) is the umbrella unit Alexandr oversees. He has a hands-on role in TBD Lab, and the rest report up into the structure.
- TBD is the large-model research lab where the leading researchers and infrastructure engineers sit. They report to him.
- PAR (Product and Applied Research), led by Nat Friedman, builds products and deploys models to the world.
- FAIR does exploratory research. Alexandr names brain modeling and a universal model for atoms ("UMA") as examples.
- Daniel Gross leads Meta Compute, which plans the long-term GPU and data center infrastructure.
- He frames the goal as "personal superintelligence": the technology should empower people, be as democratized as possible, and reach billions of users and hundreds of millions of small businesses on Meta's platforms.

## Why he joined Meta: model builders and compute

- He had known Mark Zuckerberg for years and took advice from him while running Scale. Talks about working more closely began about a year before this interview.
- His reasoning: those who build the frontier models earn "greater and greater rights," both economic and product, to build around them. Research pace is fast enough that model-building labs are the most exciting place in the ecosystem.
- He argues the next phase of tech boils down to compute. Companies with lots of compute can make big bets and ship products that companies without it cannot, so he says to think about the two groups very differently.
- Meta had the compute and a founder fully committed to AI, which made it the right place to build at scale.

## The three levers for catching up to the frontier

When he arrived, Llama 4 was off the needed trajectory. He designed TBD around three ways to get very fast velocity:
- **Higher compute per researcher.** Big labs have lots of compute but spread it across many efforts, which slows any one researcher. A smaller team with more compute each moves faster.
- **Talent density.** "The very small team where everyone is cracked is always going to move faster than the very large organization where responsibility is more distributed."
- **Very ambitious research bets.** Beyond building competitive frontier models, he allocates a large share of compute to big, risky bets that could shift paradigms if they work.

His stated principles for the effort: take superintelligence seriously, technical voices are loudest, scientific rigor, focus on basics, make big bets.

## Organizing a lab around a belief, and the culture he's building

- A main problem he found: leading labs build the whole organization around the premise that superintelligence is close, then rebuild every other assumption around it. Many people at large companies don't hold that conviction.
- On recruiting, he says the team had to be hired "yesterday," so the blitz was fast and individualized. People needed to see Meta was serious, so it took direct conversations explaining what they were building and why.
- He pushes back on the "mercenary" narrative. He says researchers joined for compute, talent density and freedom to make bold bets, and that most had very strong financial prospects where they were.
- Visitors from other labs say the vibe reminds them of early OpenAI or early Anthropic.
- His management philosophy, citing Steve Jobs: "most companies hire people and tell them what to do, but we hire people for them to tell us what to do." He says he is not trying to boss anyone around, but to create the best environment for researchers.

## What Muse Spark is and the "clean stack" thesis

- Over roughly 9 months the team rebuilt the pre-training stack, the RL stack, much of the science, and the data work. He calls Muse Spark "the appetizer," an early data point on a scaling ladder.
- The program is built around predictable scaling: pre-training, RL, test-time, and multi-agent scaling. He says larger models are coming in "coming months."
- He says Muse Spark is a very good consumer model but not yet competitive on agentic coding, which the next models target.
- He credits strong results to a very clean stack built from scratch "the right way" by experts, and says it landed better than the team expected.
- On token efficiency (he cites Artificial Analysis), Muse Spark reached similar results with many fewer tokens. His speculation: other models may need more tokens because of inefficiencies elsewhere in the stack that get patched by longer thinking.
- Capabilities like visual coding (websites, games) emerged from combining strong agentic and multimodal ability.

## Product strategy: agents for consumers and businesses

- Sequence: build great models and products first, then tightly integrate AI across the family of apps and business products. He says that integration push is starting now.
- He says AI sentiment is low because AI hasn't yet given ordinary people what Claude Code gave developers: a tool that visibly increases personal agency. Meta wants to build that for consumers and small businesses.
- His bigger idea: agents for both consumers and small businesses, with a mechanism for them to work together. "Dario always talks about a country of geniuses in a data center. I think we're excited about building an economy of agents in a data center," changing how supply and demand are mediated.
- He sees the market as very early and dynamic. In one year the perceived consumer winner shifted, and he expects each new capability level to unlock new product form factors, each potentially bigger than the last.
- Glasses (Ray-Ban Meta) and other devices are meant to see what you see and hear what you hear, supplying context for proactive agent help.
- He pairs this with the need for social permission: Meta must show it cares about how products are deployed.

## Safety, open source, and where he places Meta

- Muse Spark triggered some safety checks (bio, chem, cyber, loss of control) in testing under Meta's advanced AI scaling framework, so it is not suitable for open sourcing in its current form. A fuller preparedness report was published.
- He says Meta will keep open sourcing models and is working on open-sourceable versions, but the most powerful models must be judged for safety first.
- Robotics: if you take superintelligence seriously, physical intelligence follows digital intelligence on the roadmap. Meta acquired a robotics AI startup (Assured Robot Intelligence) that builds AI, not hardware.
- He says superintelligence research is "fundamentally a research activity," like experiments in a fog of war, which is why he stresses sequencing and research over rushing straight to downstream bets.
- He names critical-path technologies as superintelligence, robotics and brain-computer interfaces, and, scaling to infinity, energy, compute and robots.
