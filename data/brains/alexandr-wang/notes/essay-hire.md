## Hiring for "giving a shit" over credentials

- Alexandr says that when he interviews, he mainly screens for one thing: "giving a shit." At the time of writing, Scale had hired more than 200 people, and for a long time he personally interviewed everyone who got an offer.
- He wrote the memo to the company before the team grew from roughly 75 to 150.
- He splits the screen into two parts: the candidate gives a shit about Scale (its mission, product and problem), and gives a shit about their work in general.
- His reasoning: giving a shit does not guarantee great work, but without it good work is guaranteed not to happen.

## Why caring about the company's mission is critical

- Alexandr argues there is "no future" if you hire people who do not identify with the mission, product and problem. The company becomes "an undifferentiated crowd of uninspired people" with no shot at a generational company.
- He says this criterion will only become more important as the company grows.
- Early on, nobody joins a startup for credentials because nobody knows it. As the company grows, more people interview for the brand rather than the substance.

## Avoiding becoming a credential instead of a cult

- A scary outcome for him is Scale becoming "a credential rather than a cult," like Harvard or MIT, where people attend to signal that they are smart or prestigious.
- Growing companies often miss this effect. They see far more top-of-funnel and find it easier to recruit raw numbers, but the ROI of each hire drops dramatically.
- The end state resembles a university: constant churn of smart but uninvolved people who stay a few years and never dive deep enough to do meaningful work.
- He says that unless you actively fight it, it will happen.

## Recruiting as courtship, not admissions

- A recruiting team that looks like a college admissions office is "certain death for a startup." Sifting a swarm of homogeneous candidates only yields a "higher credentialed homogeneous soup."
- Recruiting done right "looks more like courtship": you need a spark, sometimes preceded by months of convincing and pursuit.
- The target is people you would be willing to spend every waking moment with, since there will be many long nights if things go right.

## Screening for obsession in general

- Fervor for the company can be faked in an interview. Alexandr's check is whether the person has been deeply obsessed with something before. If not, it is a bad bet that the company will be the first.
- His line of questioning:
  - What's the hardest you've ever worked on something?
  - How many hours were you working a week?
  - Why did you work so hard? Why did you care?
  - When were you the most unmotivated in your life?
  - What's the thing you're the most proud of?
  - Do you think it was worth it?
- His test: "For an obsessed person, it's always worth it."

## Culture is the company, not the cosmetics

- He says most people don't give a shit. His example: the common pattern for engineers at big companies of coming in at 11am and leaving at 4pm. He argues you cannot work 5 hours a day and give a shit, so very little meaningful work gets done there, which he admits is slight hyperbole.
- He calls conflating culture with cosmetics one of the worst trends in Silicon Valley. Flexible hours, open desks, office design, no dress code and perks tell you nothing meaningful beyond how comfortable a company is spending money.
- His conclusion: "The company is the culture."
