## StockAlarm: product, pricing and business model

- Yahia is head of growth and a co-founder at StockAlarm (stockalarm.io), a B2C SaaS selling reliable stock market alerts by text, phone call and push notification, with a variety of indicators and triggers.
- Pricing runs from $5 a month at the low end to $19.99 a month at the top tier. Average revenue per user is around $10 a month.
- Price scales with what the trader needs. A simple trader who wants basic upper or lower limit alerts on a couple of stocks pays little. A trader who wants international calls pays more because those calls cost more.
- The company launched about a year before this 2021 interview. His two co-founders built the MVP, and he joined roughly 1 to 2 months later.
- At the time of the interview it had about 600 paying customers and had just hit about $5.5K MRR. It is fully bootstrapped.
- The whole team works on it part-time alongside full-time jobs. Weekdays are management work such as newsletters, unless something breaks. Weekends are sprints to build new features and triggers.
- The team is six people: five engineers (iOS, Android, back-end) and one content intern. They are expanding triggers and working toward feature parity between the web and iOS apps.

## Lesson: add pricing tiers from day one

- Asked what he wishes he had known, Yahia says: "I wish we'd implemented pricing tiers from the start."
- StockAlarm "doubled our MRR almost instantly" once tiers went in.
- He says that if they had tiered from the start, "we'd have a lot more cash right now."
- Tiering lets light users pay little and heavy users (international calls, more alerts) pay more.

## Equity for a late joiner: milestone-based vesting tied to growth

- Yahia joined after the MVP existed, so he did not get a flat grant. His co-founders gave him equity that unlocks as he hits user-count milestones.
- The tiers are checked at end of quarter. When he started there were about 10 paying users. The structure was an extra 5% unlocked at 50 users, more at 100, and a top tier at 500 users.
- The maximum he could earn was around 15 to 20%.
- He says that "the work I put in ... is directly correlated to the amount of users I bring in," so his equity is linked to company value.
- Once he hit the top tier, the founders planned a revamped plan with new milestones worth roughly another 5 to 10%. Later hires, such as the two Android engineers, get less equity because they joined later.

## Getting the first 100 customers

- **Automated Twitter bot.** The product already had earnings and dividend data, so they built a bot that tweets it out. He says it "started scaling on its own."
- **Paid Instagram promotions.** They paid finance-related Instagram accounts that target their demographic, and put real money into ads there.
- **Quora.** He answered Quora questions.
- **SEO**, which grew into the main channel (next section).
- He frames the social channels as a way to reuse the data infrastructure they had already built.

## SEO: programmatic ticker pages and backlinks

- StockAlarm ranks first for "stock alerts," "stock alert" and "stock alarm," ahead of larger sites. In the interview it sits second for "stock alerts," behind only StockTwits.
- He credits keyword research on those terms, then continuous optimization. He also notes that "it's basically just about your keywords."
- When they launched the web app they created a page for each of about 12,000 to 13,000 supported tickers. Each page has its own SEO and links back to the marketing site.
- This copies the brokerage pattern: searching "Robinhood Apple" returns Robinhood's Apple page, so StockAlarm builds the same kind of page at scale.
- He builds backlinks to the marketing site and the ticker pages and drives users to the site to raise domain authority. He gets weekly ranking updates.
- The weakness: head terms like "stock alerts" rank well, but queries like "Apple stock alerts" often do not, because competitors such as Robinhood have thousands of backlinks per ticker. Closing that gap was the work in progress.

## Unit economics, churn and retention

- About 70% of customers find StockAlarm organically. He measures this from organic downloads in the app store, where people search for the app and install it.
- Blended customer acquisition cost, including costs like the content person's pay, is roughly $3 for a $10-a-month customer.
- Monthly retention is about 75% (fluctuating 70 to 80%), so monthly churn is 20 to 30%.
- He explains the churn with trading behavior: people trade in sprints, so they cancel for a month and often come back. He calls it a lifestyle business.
- His fix is annual subscriptions, which customers have asked for and which he expects to bring churn down a lot.
