## Morning sunlight is the highest-leverage light habit

Andrew ranks viewing morning sunlight in the top five actions for mental health, physical health and performance, alongside sleep, movement, nutrients and relationships. His common refrain is "View morning sunlight!"

- Get sunlight in the first hours after waking, as soon as you can, even through cloud cover.
- It increases early-day cortisol release, which he calls the ideal time for elevated cortisol.
- That morning cortisol spike also benefits the immune system, metabolism and daytime focus, and prepares the body for sleep that night.
- It sets the circadian clock, the body's mechanism for anticipating when to wake and sleep. The clock also manages hunger and body temperature.
- Dr. Samer Hattar (NIMH) adds that morning sunlight, specifically UVB and blue light, improves mood, raises energy, regulates appetite and increases dopamine release.

## How long and how to view morning light

- **Sunny morning:** 5-10 minutes outside. More is fine. You can exercise, walk, eat a light breakfast or journal in the light.
- **Overcast day:** at least 15-20 minutes.
- **Dark or unable to go outside:** turn on as many bright indoor lights as possible, then get outside once the sun is up.
- Contacts and eyeglasses (even with UV protection) are fine.
- Don't wear sunglasses or blue blockers during morning viewing, since you lose the maximum effect.
- Face toward the sun, but never look directly at it or view any light in a way that causes pain. Close your eyes and blink as needed.
- A windshield or window doesn't work: too many of the relevant wavelengths are filtered out.

## Afternoon sunlight reinforces sleep

- Get outside in the afternoon too. When the sun is low, its yellow and orange wavelengths come through even on overcast days.
- Late-afternoon/evening sunlight tells the circadian clock that it is evening and begins the transition to sleep that night.
- It acts as a second "anchor point" for the brain and body to know time and season, which helps on days you miss morning light.
- Afternoon sunlight reduces some (not all) of the ill effects of late-night artificial brightness.

## Using light for daytime energy and focus

- From morning until mid-afternoon, use bright overhead lights. This supports release of dopamine, norepinephrine and epinephrine (motivation, attention, drive) and optimal cortisol, maximizing alertness and focus.
- Raise the ambient light of the workspace rather than the brightness of the computer screen.
- Ideally place the desk near a window, since natural sunlight signals the brain to stay alert.
- In the late afternoon, follow the sun and dim the environment: turn off overhead lights, use lamps or softer lighting, dim the screen and reduce blue light exposure to ease the transition to sleep.

## Avoid bright light at night

- Citing Hattar, Andrew says artificial UVB/bright light from screens and overhead lights between 10 p.m. and 4 a.m. lowers dopamine and worsens feelings of depression and anxiety.
- Occasional exposure is fine. Regularly looking at your phone or using bright overhead lights in that window harms health.
- Bright light powerfully inhibits melatonin, which naturally rises in the late evening and stays elevated into the early night. Keeping cortisol low at night also matters for sleep and immune health.
- For middle-of-the-night trips (bathroom, checking on children), use no light or very dim light, or amber/red light, which affects melatonin minimally.
- Inexpensive red "party light" bulbs work. Andrew switches to red lights around 9 p.m. and says it greatly improved his sleep. Turn them off at bedtime.
- Keep the bedroom very dark. He cites Mason et al. showing even dim light during sleep impairs cardiometabolic function and increases insulin resistance.

## Bright red-light therapy and shift work

- Red-light therapy has been shown to improve eyesight in people over 40, but must be done in the early daytime. It can also help with acne and wound healing.
- Those effects need a special red light, commercially available at a cost of hundreds to thousands of dollars. This is distinct from the cheap red bulbs for nighttime.
- **Shift work:** avoid bright light in the middle of your individual sleep cycle. Use dark window shades and dim lighting for good sleep.
- Try to hold the same schedule for a minimum of two weeks at a time so the circadian clock can better predict your sleep cycle.

## Afternoon skin sunlight for hormones

- Andrew cites Parikh et al.: about 30 minutes of afternoon sunlight on the skin (shorts and short sleeves) increased testosterone, estrogen, mood and libido in both men and women.
- Protocol: get outside in shorts and a t-shirt for roughly 20-30 minutes in the afternoon, at least 2-3x per week. Don't sunburn.
- He calls morning sunlight viewing (#1) and night-time light avoidance (#4) the most important of his eight light tips.
