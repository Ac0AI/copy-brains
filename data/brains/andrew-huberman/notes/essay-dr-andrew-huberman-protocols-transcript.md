## Peptides: categories, what the evidence supports, and sourcing

- Andrew defines peptides as short amino-acid chains the body already makes (insulin is one). Following Dr. Abud Bakri, he frames the trend as "mining the body for medicines" instead of plants.
- He splits them into weight-loss (GLP), growth hormone secretagogues, tissue repair, and miscellaneous.
- **GLPs:** drugs that raise GLP about a thousandfold produce robust weight loss, including some muscle loss. Retatrutide (Lilly, not yet released) hits GLP, GIP and glucagon.
  - It is already sold on the gray market, since it is easy to synthesize.
  - Some people take roughly a tenth of the prescribed dose and report similar effects with less nausea.
  - He has never used a GLP himself.
  - He says people should resistance train regardless.
- **Gray market risk:** "for research purposes only" products carry contamination risk (e.g., lipopolysaccharide) and may not contain what the label says.
- **Compounding pharmacies:** these are cleaner than the gray market, and he names Tailor Made, Upstate and Revive Pharmacy as better known. A doctor must write the script.
- **Bottom-line view:** he says "you do you" on risk, but be logical about it.
  - He expects few robust effects beyond GLPs and, anecdotally, pinealon.
  - He thinks the big opportunity is clean, American-made, verified-supply peptides.
- Women are a massive peptide market, e.g., low-dose tirzepatide or reta, NAD, and GHK-Cu (with red light or sun, for wrinkles).

## Growth hormone secretagogues vs. real GH

- This category covers sermorelin, ipamorelin, tesamorelin, CJC and MK677, which stimulate nighttime GH release. Typical increase is three to eightfold in GH and IGF-1.
- Some, like MK677, stimulate appetite. CJC variants can cause some insulin resistance.
- He tried sermorelin briefly:
  - It dramatically increased slow wave deep sleep and "nuked" REM.
  - It spiked his PSA, which returned to normal after stopping. No one could give him a mechanism.
  - He saw no notable fat loss or strength gain.
- He doesn't find secretagogues worth it. If someone can get a legitimate script, he prefers pharma-grade somatropin or omnitrope.
  - Trial dosing is typically 1 to 2 IU, five nights per week.
  - Bodybuilders use about 8 IU a day.
  - Effects people report: more deep sleep, better recovery and tissue repair, joints that feel good, general well-being. It is less anabolic than testosterone.
- He is not telling people to run out and do GH, but sees a low-dose script as better than unknown secretagogue peptides.

## BPC-157 and tissue repair peptides

- BPC-157 ("Body Protection Compound") is an amino acid chain found in gastric juice. Original use was gut issues (high-dose enemas in one trial).
- Animal data are impressive (cartilage regrowth, fibroblast migration, angiogenesis, nerve regrowth), but there is "not a single published human trial," and he doesn't expect one: trials are expensive and nobody will fund them.
- His experience: after a strain, recovery feels faster, but he can't run a control because it goes systemic. He keeps some in the fridge and injects it for strains only.
- Capsule forms are claimed to work equally well; he doesn't know. Pentadecapeptide is another form.
- Angiogenesis caveat: it could vascularize a tumor, though it isn't mutagenic.
- He would not take it ongoing or without an injury. For recovery, he says sleep (or low-dose GH) does more.
- TB-500 plus BPC is the "Wolverine Stack." He hasn't tried TB-500.
- Tim Ferriss notes he took the Wolverine Stack after elbow surgery and has no idea whether it helped.

## Pinealon, epitalon and other peptides

- **Pinealon** (despite the name) comes from the neocortex, not the pineal. Epitalon does come from the pineal.
  - He uses pinealon only occasionally, by subcutaneous injection (oral forms exist), via a compounded script.
  - Effect: about three hours of REM for him, with slow wave sleep intact, and it carries over to following nights. Few things increase REM; most drugs deplete it.
  - Some people use it in the daytime for cognition; one songwriter likes the hypnotic state it induces.
- **Epitalon:** proposed to preserve histone and DNA structure; vague evidence. Some animal data on visual repair in retinitis pigmentosa models.
  - He tried a capsule form plus morning sunlight for jet lag and it seemed to shift his clock faster, but he won't repeat it because he isn't sure.
- **Melanotan:** causes tanning, can cause priapism and greatly increases libido. Vyleesi is an FDA-approved drug for female hyposexuality.
- **Safety framing:** there are no reported adverse events tracked to non-FDA peptides, but Tim's point is that absence of reporting isn't evidence of safety. Andrew says to use good injection hygiene: alcohol swabs, never reuse needles, and don't dip back into a vial with a used needle.

## Sleep tools: REM, DORAs and meditation

- **More REM without drugs:** heat the sleeping environment in the final two hours (credit: Matt Walker). Cool at the start, cold mid-night, warm toward morning, e.g., a heated blanket, socks or a wool cap if he wakes to use the bathroom.
- **Late workouts:** to avoid caffeine, take 900 mg alpha-GPC. He says it lets you sleep and likely increases REM.
- Intense weight training can triple or quadruple cortisol. Ice baths raise it trivially by comparison.
- **DORAs:** Tim takes Belsomra, partly because Alzheimer's runs in his family, and cycles on and off due to morning grogginess. Andrew tried Quviviq once and got "zombie sleep" (woke in the same position, unrested), so he avoids prescription sleep tools.
- Supplement stack he likes: magnesium threonate, apigenin, theanine. Try light and caffeine management first, before pinealon.
- A PNAS paper shows short meditations increase glymphatic clearance in the daytime, with more clearance in that night's sleep.
- To raise nighttime HRV, do exhale-emphasized breathing during the day, even a few deliberate exhales.
- **Jet lag:** fasting may shift peripheral clocks (liver, gut). Tim reports fasting on long flights helped him.
- **Sleep induction:** a bed-rocking study shows faster sleep onset and deeper sleep. An MIT-linked sleep mask stimulates behind the ears to drive eye movements and induce sleep in under six minutes; Andrew has no relationship to the company.

## Resistance training: the Dorian Yates approach and Andrew's current split

- After training with Dorian Yates (six-time Mr. Olympia), Andrew says the peer-reviewed data caught up to low-volume, high-intensity training.
- Dorian's structure: a couple of warmups, then two all-out sets to failure, or one set plus assisted reps, a drop set or rest-pause. Do 2 to 3 exercises per major body part, about 4 to 8 work sets per body part, in the 5 to 12 rep range.
  - Each body part is trained directly once a week and indirectly a second time.
  - Take a lighter or off week about every 6 to 8 weeks.
  - Mike Mentzer's single set was probably too little for most people.
- Andrew's reasoning: studies recommending 10 to 20 sets per week (e.g., Brad Schoenfeld) usually don't account for people also doing cardio, which collides with high-volume lifting.
  - Most people don't truly train to failure. Compound movements are required, not just isolation work.
- **Andrew's weekly split (about 50, still getting stronger):**
  - Mon/Tue: legs (leg extensions, lying curls, belt squat, hack squat, calves), 2 to 3 hard sets each. He uses a Pit Shark belt squat machine.
  - Next day is a rest day, or 20 to 30 minutes of zone 3 to 4 cardio. He says to do sauna and cold that day if time allows.
  - Chest/back (torso) plus neck: low incline press, weighted dips, chins, rows, pullover, core and low back. About an hour.
  - Next day: max-heart-rate cardio (AssaultBike or Airdyne 30 seconds on, 30 off, six to eight rounds; sprints; or 400s on a track).
  - Next: shoulders and arms (press, curls, overhead triceps extension, bench dips).
  - Sunday: long slow outdoor movement such as a hike, ruck, jog in sand or bike ride.
- **Neck training:** use a 4-way neck machine (Hammer Strength) or Jeff Cavaliere's plate-and-bench side-lying method. Nasal breathe, keep your tongue on the roof of your mouth, don't stick out your chin, and go slowly. He credits it with never having shoulder or rotator cuff issues.
- Core: hanging L-sits and pikes. Low back: a 45-degree hyperextension, not taken to failure.

## Psychedelics, MDMA and neuroplasticity windows

- Andrew's history: adolescent recreational LSD and psilocybin (a mistake, he says). Later, clinically supported MDMA (standard MAPS protocol, four sessions) helped him feel his feelings.
- His most effective work was MDMA followed a couple of hours later by high-dose psilocybin (4 to 5 g of dried mushrooms), done twice with clinical support.
  - He had no "two days later" MDMA drop.
  - The session surfaced what hadn't happened in his life, something talk therapy never reached. Afterward he had a clear mission for daily choices.
- Warnings: these are not for recreational use, and a trained guide matters (MDMA can make anything feel important).
- Tim's points:
  - Plasticity opens a window of roughly 2 to 3 weeks where the work must be done (Gül Dölen on critical periods).
  - Treat it like knee surgery: pick the doctor and expect rehab.
  - Contraindications include a family history of bipolar disorder, schizophrenia or borderline personality disorder. Cannabis combined with these drugs is risky.
- **TMS:** Tim describes a one-day accelerated TMS protocol (Dr. Jonathan Downar, Ampa Health, in which Tim has a disclosed involvement) pre-loaded with d-cycloserine (about 125 mg). He reports a roughly 90% reduction in his anxiety and OCD for 3 to 4 months.
- Plasticity needs directed effort: raising BDNF alone does nothing, so you must go do the hard learning.

## Cortisol and light: the principle behind the protocols

- Cortisol deploys energy (it mobilizes glucose); it is not just a stress hormone. Aim to bookend the day: high cortisol early, low in the evening and night.
- Morning bright light, ideally sunlight, raises the morning cortisol spike by about 50%. Cold water, caffeine, exercise and a little stress also help. Exercise raises cortisol 3 to 4 times.
- A high morning peak helps ensure low evening cortisol, high melatonin and better sleep. This applies to kids, pregnant women, perimenopausal and menopausal women, and men.
- His takeaway is to focus on principles rather than specific protocols, and to choose the tools you will actually do.
- **Light quality (from his book):** long-wavelength light (low sun, red and infrared) penetrates the body and supports mitochondria.
  - Too much short-wave LED light without long-wavelength light can contribute to myopia.
  - The midday sun gives too much UV for many people.
- His book _Protocols_ is due September 15 and covers sleep, exercise, stress, light, neuroplasticity and personal development. He cites Jim Hollis's "shut up, suit up, show up."
