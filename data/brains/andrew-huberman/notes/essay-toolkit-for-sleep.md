## Sunlight and circadian timing

Andrew's first sleep rule is to view sunlight by going outside within 30-60 minutes of waking, and to do it again in the late afternoon before sunset.

- If he wakes before sunrise and wants to be awake, he turns on artificial lights, then goes outside once the sun is up.
- Duration depends on sky: bright cloudless days 10 minutes, cloudy days 20 minutes, very overcast days 30-60 minutes.
- In places with very little light, he suggests considering an artificial daytime simulator source.
- Skip sunglasses if it is safe to do so. Contact lenses and eyeglasses are fine.
- Looking directly at the sun is not required, and he warns never to look at any light so bright it is painful.
- A brimmed hat plus sunglasses plus shade will not "wake up" the circadian clock.

## Consistent wake time and when to go to bed

- Wake up at the same time each day.
- Go to sleep when you first start to feel sleepy.
- He says pushing through the late-evening sleepy feeling and going to bed too late (for you) is one reason people wake at 3 am and cannot fall back asleep.
- He notes a natural spike in alertness about 1 hour before your natural bedtime, observed by sleep researchers. His advice: "Don't freak out if it happens. It will pass!"

## Caffeine, alcohol and sleep medications

- Avoid caffeine within 8-10 hours of bedtime. He says Dr. Matt Walker might advise 12-14 hours.
- Andrew's own pattern: he does fine with caffeine at 2 pm and goes to sleep at about 10-11 pm.
- Alcohol disrupts sleep, as do most sleep medications (discussed with Dr. Walker on the podcast).

## Night-time light rules

- Avoid bright lights, especially bright overhead lights, between 10 pm and 4 am.
- Simple rule: use only as much artificial lighting as needed to stay and move about safely at night.
- Blue blockers help a bit, but dimming the lights still matters. Bright light of all colors is a problem for the circadian system.
- Candlelight and moonlight are fine.
- Shift workers and jetlagged travelers should use the podcast's jetlag and shift-work protocols.

## Naps and middle-of-the-night waking

- Limit daytime naps to under 90 minutes, or skip them. Andrew naps about 30 minutes most afternoons, maybe 45, never longer.
- Waking once or so in the night is normal.
- If you cannot fall back asleep, do an NSDR protocol. Search "NSDR" on YouTube (the top 3-4 options vary in voice and duration), or use any "Yoga Nidra" protocol.
- For sleep disturbances, insomnia or sleep anxiety, he suggests the research-supported protocols on the Reveri app (iPhone). Do the sleep self-hypnosis 3x a week at any time of day. It runs 10-15 minutes and helps rewire the nervous system to relax faster.

## Supplement stack (30-60 minutes before bed)

- 145 mg magnesium threonate or 200 mg magnesium bisglycinate
- 50 mg apigenin
- 100-400 mg theanine
- On 3-4 nights per week he also takes 2 g glycine and 100 mg GABA.

His caveats:
- Start with one supplement (or none) and add one at a time as needed. Some people need none, and some like theanine but not magnesium.
- Do not take theanine if you have overly intense dreams, sleepwalk or have night terrors.
- About 5% of people get an agitated stomach from magnesium. They should not take it.
- He uses Momentous supplements for all of the above, or another source you trust.

## Bedroom temperature and changing sleep needs

- Keep the sleeping room cool and dark, and layer on blankets you can remove.
- The body needs to drop 1-3 degrees in temperature to fall and stay asleep. Rising body temperature is one reason people wake. Tossing off blankets is easier than needing a cooling device.
- Sleep needs change over time. Someone may be a night owl at 15 and a morning person later, or need 6 hours in summer and 7-8 in winter.
- He says not to obsess over the occasional night out or missed sunlight, but drifting from these behaviors for too long leads to suffering. Sleep is "THE foundation of our mental and physical health and performance."
