## Why focus is modeled as an arrow of three neurochemicals

Andrew gives a mental model for how focus works. Picture an arrow:

- **Epinephrine (adrenaline) is the shaft.** It is released in the brain from the locus coeruleus and in the body from the adrenal glands. It raises energy and alertness. "There is no focus without epinephrine," but it is required, not sufficient.
- **Acetylcholine is the arrowhead.** It acts like a spotlight that makes specific neurons more active than their neighbors. A narrow arrowhead means narrow focus.
- **Dopamine is the engine.** It provides the motivation that keeps focus moving forward for 10 minutes, an hour or longer.

He separates tools that mediate focus (act directly on these circuits) from tools that modulate it (adjust brain state broadly). Sleep, omega-3s and creatine are modulators. Binaural beats, Alpha-GPC and refocusing meditation are closer to direct mediators.

## Sleep, session length and deliberate defocus

- **Sleep comes first.** Andrew aims for good sleep about 80% of nights. No pill, device or supplement overcomes chronic sleep deprivation. Tools only help after one or two bad nights.
- **Focus is a warm-up, not a switch.** "You wouldn't expect yourself to go out on the track and not warm up." Focus drifts and narrows again, so he treats the first 5-10 minutes of a session as a transition.
- **Use 90-minute ultradian cycles.** Andrew sets a timer for 90 minutes or less. It can be shorter, such as 45 minutes. He counts the warm-up inside the 90.
- **Refocus when you drift.** Returning attention to the task is the active part of training focus.
- **Deliberately defocus afterward.** He takes 10-30 minutes of low-demand activity with no phone, no tight-focus reading, even on the walk to the bathroom. He says the brain is a major consumer of calories and needs idle time.
- **How many sessions per day.** The better trained you are at focus, the more recovery you need. Andrew does up to three 90-minute deep-work bouts per day. Some people may manage four.
- **How to start.** Do at least one 90-minute bout daily, including weekends. After about four weeks, consider adding more. On weekends he reads a book with his phone out of sight.

## Sound tools: 40 Hz binaural beats and noise

- **40 Hz binaural beats.** Slightly offset frequencies are played to each ear. Peer-reviewed studies show they improve focus, plausibly by raising dopamine and acetylcholine.
- **Free apps.** He uses the BrainWave app (iOS and Android) and dials in 40 Hz. He has no relationship with it.
- **How Andrew uses them.** He plays about 5 minutes before work, then turns them off. When he feels very distractible he leaves them on throughout. He also uses them before weight training.
- **White, pink and brown noise.** These do not improve concentration itself but shorten the transition into focus. The data show amplified prefrontal cortex activity. Andrew does not use them himself but knows people who do.

## Food, fasting and caffeine

- **Glucose and clarity.** A Neuron study found neurons represent the environment more precisely when brain glucose is sufficient.
- **Fasted versus fed.** Being fasted often feels clear because there is less parasympathetic "rest and digest" activity and less sleepiness. A full stomach diverts blood to the gut. Half as much caffeine gives the same lift fasted as twice as much after a pasta meal.
- **Andrew's pattern.** He eats from about 11 AM to 8 PM. He does his workout and first focus bout fasted, with water and electrolytes (such as LMNT) plus caffeine. After eating he notices reduced focus, then does a second bout in the afternoon.
- **Meal size.** Large meals, such as two ribeyes, hurt focus. He suggests not stuffing yourself at lunch to avoid the afternoon crash.
- **Diet and ADHD.** Cutting simple sugars and highly processed foods appears to reduce ADHD symptoms in children and adults. Tyrosine-rich foods such as Parmesan, some meats and nuts support dopamine.
- **Ketosis.** He says ketosis gives clear thinking for many people. He occasionally drinks liquid ketones.
- **Caffeine dose.** The range is 100-400 mg. Most people do well with 100-200 mg before focused work. If you are anxious or not caffeine-adapted, 400 mg can cause sweating, a racing heart and panic.
- **Timing.** Delay first caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking. He breaks this rule when training very early. Cut off around 1-2 PM. Caffeine within 8 hours of bed disrupts sleep architecture, and he prefers 10-12 hours.
- **Mechanisms.** Caffeine raises epinephrine, blocks adenosine and increases dopamine receptor density and function.
- **Yerba mate.** It is his preferred source. He recommends loose leaf, not the smoked kind, which contains carcinogens. He names Anna Park, an organic brand he buys on Amazon.

## Using stress and cold exposure to raise epinephrine

- **Stress can help focus.** A 2020 Journal of Experimental Psychology paper (DeGroote) found acute stress improved concentration performance by more than double. The stressor was a mild, evaluated simulated job interview and arithmetic task. Epinephrine and cortisol rose modestly. Concentration was still improved 30 minutes later, and Andrew says the effect may last an hour or more.
- **Stress narrows vision.** Adrenaline dilates the pupils and produces tunnel-like vision that sharpens the arrow.
- **Deliberate cold exposure.** It is a safe, zero-cost way to get this without creating real-life stress. A European Journal of Physiology study found cold water roughly doubled dopamine, with effects lasting hours, and also raised epinephrine and cortisol.
- **Protocol.** Use a cold shower or ice bath for 1-5 minutes. Beginners start with 1 minute. It should be "uncomfortably cold, but safe to stay in." About 3 minutes is typical before work or a workout.
- **Stacking example.** If you feel unfocused, take a 3-minute cold shower, drink coffee, add 40 Hz binaural beats, then start the work block.

## Meditation, NSDR and hypnosis

- **13-minute focus meditation.** Wendy Suzuki's lab found 13 minutes daily for 8 weeks improved focus, mood, stress, sleep and memory. Sit or lie down, close your eyes, breathe through your nose, and rest attention about an inch behind the forehead.
- **Mind-wandering is the training.** Expect to drift every 5-20 seconds. Each return is the rep, "like hitting the rumble strip" and steering back. He suggests calling it a refocus meditation.
- **Sleep caution.** Do not do it within 4 hours of bedtime, because the effort disrupted sleep for some Suzuki study subjects.
- **NSDR (yoga nidra).** Lie down for 10-30 minutes (up to 60) with an audio script of body scan and long exhales. Danish neuroimaging shows restored dopamine in the basal ganglia. Andrew does 10-30 minutes daily. After a bad night he does 30-60 minutes, sometimes a second session in the afternoon, and caffeine afterward adds to the lift. Free sources include a Madefor YouTube NSDR and the Virtusan app. It helps compensate for lost sleep but is not a replacement.
- **Hypnosis.** David Spiegel at Stanford studied hypnosis as a state that is deeply focused and deeply relaxed. The Reveri app has 8-13 minute focus scripts and a go-back-to-sleep script. Andrew uses it about every third or fourth day.

## Visual focus training

- **Principle.** Visual focus drives cognitive focus, which is why blinders help horses and hoodies or limited visual fields help people.
- **Overt focus.** Stare at one fixed target for 30 seconds to 3 minutes. Blinking is fine. Choose a spot on the wall, a blanked screen or a crosshatch on paper.
- **Progression.** Start at 30 seconds, add about 5 seconds per session, and repeat the same time if you miss a day. Whenever your gaze drifts, bring it back.
- **Covert focus.** Look forward while attending to something at the edge of the room. It is harder.
- **Use it as a ramp-up.** Do 30 seconds to 3 minutes before a work bout, and again mid-session instead of reaching for the phone. Athletes use it too.

## Supplements and prescription drugs

- **Omega-3s.** Andrew takes 1-3 g of EPA per day for mood and brain function.
- **Creatine.** He takes 5 g of monohydrate per day, chiefly for brain function and prefrontal networks, not muscle.
- **Alpha-GPC.** He takes 300 mg (up to 600 mg) 10-20 minutes before focused work or training. He pairs it with yerba mate or coffee, early in the day, about 4 days per week.
- **TMAO concern.** A study linked chronic Alpha-GPC use to stroke risk via TMAO. He finds the data inconclusive. He takes a 600 mg garlic capsule whenever he takes Alpha-GPC and has seen no TMAO spike in blood tests.
- **L-tyrosine.** A typical stack is 500 mg L-tyrosine, 300 mg Alpha-GPC and one cup of coffee, early in the day.
- **Phenylethylamine.** He sometimes swaps in 500 mg for tyrosine, or adds it, and may take a second 500 mg mid-session because it is short-lived. He uses all four compounds together about once a month, and no more than once every two weeks.
- **Stacking rule.** He combines only 2-3 tools at a time and many days uses none. He treats supplements as a route into deeper focus, so he doesn't depend on them every time.
- **Prescription ADHD drugs.** He says Adderall, Ritalin, Vyvanse and Modafinil can help clinically diagnosed ADHD and may teach circuits to engage. He strongly discourages use without a prescription. His order is behavioral tools first, then nutrition and sleep, then supplements, and prescriptions last, except in severe psychiatric conditions.
