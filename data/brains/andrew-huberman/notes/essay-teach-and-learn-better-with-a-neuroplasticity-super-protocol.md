## The 9-step protocol is a trigger-then-consolidate model of learning

Andrew's "Neuroplasticity Super-Protocol" (shared in a newsletter dated October 29, 2021, based on a lecture for teachers and students hosted by Logitech) treats a learning bout as the "trigger" for the possibility of learning. The actual rewiring of neural circuits happens afterward, during sleep and NSDR (non-sleep deep rest).

- Behavioral protocol only; pharmacology of accelerated learning is deferred to a future discussion.
- He says behavioral protocols are necessary no matter what else is layered on.
- Not all 9 steps are needed every session. Steps 1 (get alert), 2 (get focused) and 9 (deep sleep) are "non-negotiable".

## Step 1: Get alert with deep breathing before a learning bout

Neuroplasticity requires alertness, which involves many mechanisms but mainly release of epinephrine (adrenaline) in brain and body.

- Take 25-30 deep breaths: inhale through the nose, exhale through the mouth.
- Exhale all air and hold the breath with lungs empty for 15-60 seconds.
- Inhale once and hold. Don't force the hold; start breathing normally as soon as the impulse to breathe arrives.
- Andrew uses this whether or not caffeine is involved, and says he relies on caffeine himself in the early part of the day.
- Do it prior to the learning bout.

## Step 2: Get focused by fixing your gaze

"Mental focus follows visual focus."

- Stare at a point on a wall, screen or object for 30-60 seconds before starting (blinking is fine).
- The effort you feel is "top-down" attentional engagement, reflecting neural circuits involving acetylcholine release, among other mechanisms.
- Then move into the task. Expect focus to flicker on and off, especially at the start of a bout.
- Reduce distractions: phone off and out of the room, browser closed or limited to essential tabs, or ideally the internet turned off.

## Steps 3-4: Generate repetitions and embrace errors

**Repetitions**
- Do the maximum number of repetitions you safely can in a bout. For some skills these are literal repeats (e.g., musical scales); for others, repeating the same process such as reading or doing math problems.
- Repeat the process a bit faster than is reflexive. This keeps the mind from drifting and keeps you alert.

**Errors**
- Errors (provided safety isn't compromised) are useful because they increase activation of circuits that raise alertness. If you do something correctly, there's no reason for the brain to take notice.
- The "stressful" feeling of an error is increased attention, which puts you in a better place for the next attempt.
- Computational modeling suggests an error rate of about 15% may be optimal and can guide task difficulty. He says not to worry much about the exact figure.
- Tactic: when you mess up, immediately do another attempt (and another) while the forebrain is maximally attentive.

## Step 5: Insert random 10-second micro-rests

A non-obvious way to get more repetitions and learn faster.

- Human studies show that pausing for 10 seconds and doing nothing makes hippocampal and cortical neurons replay the same activity patterns as during the actual activity (reading, music practice, skill training), about 10X faster. That is roughly 10X neural repetitions during the pause.
- These "gap effects" resemble what happens in deep sleep.
- Protocol: randomly introduce 10-second pauses during learning, at roughly 1 pause per 2 minutes of learning, distributed at random ("not every 2 minutes on the minute").

## Step 6: Use random, intermittent reward

- Reward circuits (all brain-chemical rewards) are tightly tethered to circuits for motivation and the desire to pursue things, including learning.
- To keep motivation high for yourself or others, make rewards random and intermittent. He notes this is what casinos do to keep people gambling.
- "Predictable rewards lose their motivational impact quickly."

## Steps 7-9: Session length, NSDR, and sleep

**Session limits (step 7)**
- About 90 minutes is the longest period of intense focus and effort toward learning. Shorter bouts are fine.
- Space intense bouts 2-3 (or more) hours apart.
- Most people can't do more than 270 minutes of intense learning per day.

**NSDR after a bout (step 8)**
- Two human studies from the prior two years show shallow naps and/or NSDR enhance the rate and depth of learning.
- Within 1 hour of finishing a bout, do a short NSDR protocol. Options: the Reveri self-hypnosis app (zero cost, research tested), a brief 20-minute nap, or an NSDR script such as Yoga Nidra. He does a 10-minute one daily and also recommends a 30-minute one.

**Sleep (step 9)**
- Get quality, sufficiently long deep sleep that night, the next, and so on. Rewiring occurs during sleep and NSDR.
- Goal: get sleep right at least 80% of the time. It takes work but is worth it.
