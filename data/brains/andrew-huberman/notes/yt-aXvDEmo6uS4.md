## Daily protocol: waking, light, caffeine and fasting

Andrew structures his day around the 24-hour rhythm because every cell and organ is modulated on that cycle. He stresses he does not insist others copy his schedule, only that the protocols map onto a day.

- **Log wake time.** He writes down when he wakes (about 6:00-7:00 a.m.). His body-temperature minimum falls about 2 hours before average wake time (wake at 8:00 means minimum around 6:00). He tracks the time of the minimum, not the temperature itself.
- **Morning walk outdoors.** Forward motion creates optic flow, which lowers activity in the amygdala (fear/threat/anxiety). He cites a *Neuron* paper (Macé et al., functional ultrasound imaging in mice) and a *Journal of Neuroscience* paper on lateral eye movements and amygdala deactivation. For him the walk is "not about exercise," it is about being "alert but not anxious." Indoors works, but less well.
- **Morning sunlight.** Get outside, ideally without sunglasses, even under cloud cover. Minimum about 2 minutes; 10 minutes is better; 30 is great. On a very bright, clear day about 60 seconds can suffice. Never stare at the sun. Skip it no more than one day in a row.
- **Hydration.** 16-32 oz of water with roughly half a teaspoon of sea salt, since neurons need sodium, potassium and magnesium.
- **Delay caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking.** Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors; drinking it immediately leads to an early/mid-afternoon crash when it wears off. Delaying removed his afternoon crash.
- **Fasting for focus.** He eats nothing until about 11 a.m.-noon. Fasting raises adrenaline (epinephrine), which helps focus and learning. What "breaks a fast" depends on insulin sensitivity, recent meals and glycogen status.
- **What he consumes while fasted.** Water, yerba mate or guayusa tea, and Athletic Greens with lemon. Yerba mate and guayusa increase GLP-1, which aids fat mobilization. He prefers non-smoky mate and reuses leaves for a day or two.

## Focused work block: 90-minute ultradian cycles

Andrew does one deep 90-minute work block in the morning, and a second one in the afternoon. Together that is about 3 hours of deep work, which he thinks matches most people's actual total.

- **Timing.** Place it about 4-6 hours after the temperature minimum (wake at 6:30 gives a minimum at 4:30, so about 8:30-10:30 a.m.). That is the steepest part of the rising body-temperature curve. He says exact optimum varies by person. People who are naturally sharp early should keep using that.
- **Screen height.** Put the screen at eye level or slightly above. Eyes directed upward raise alertness; downward gaze and drooping lids promote sleepiness. Upright posture is more alerting than reclining.
- **Eliminate distraction.** He uses the Freedom app to block the internet, turns the phone fully off (not just airplane mode, sometimes leaving it in the car), and avoids bathroom breaks in the block. The bladder has a direct neural link to brainstem alertness areas. He sets a 90-minute timer.
- **Low-level white noise.** Played at low volume, not through headphones. He cites studies showing it engages attention/cognition/memory areas and increases dopamine-associated brain activity.
- He calls the state "the tunnel" and says the work feels like "a workout for the mind," likely tied to dopamine and norepinephrine release. He admits he is not perfect about it.

## Exercise structure

- **About 60 minutes (plus or minus 15), 5 days per week**, with 2 full rest days. Going much longer than an hour can be detrimental via cortisol.
- **Three-to-two split in 10-12 week blocks.** For 10-12 weeks do 3 strength/hypertrophy sessions and 2 endurance sessions, then flip to 3 endurance and 2 strength.
- **80/20 intensity rule.** About 80% of resistance work should stop short of failure and about 20% can go to failure. Similarly, about 80% of endurance work should stay below the lactate "burn" and about 20% should push into it. Lactate is its own brain fuel, and the burn is not lactic acid.
- **Why.** Exercise increases brain blood flow, BDNF, and anti-inflammatory signaling (lowering IL-6, raising IL-10). Resistance training releases bone-derived hormones such as osteocalcin that support neuron health. In humans, new-neuron creation from exercise is not well supported, so he emphasizes health of existing neurons.
- **Fasted training.** He trains fasted when possible, citing Satchin Panda's *The Circadian Code* for cellular and liver benefits. If hungry, he has water with electrolytes (half teaspoon sea salt plus a 99 mg potassium tablet, or an electrolyte product like LMNT). He is not a fan of energy drinks pre-workout.
- **Occasional aid.** On rare days, 300 mg alpha-GPC (supports acetylcholine) for physical and cognitive performance.

## Food, hormones and supplements

- **Lunch (around noon, about 80% full).** Large volumes divert blood to the gut and cause lethargy. He eats protein, vegetables, nuts and healthy fats, low carb. Starches only if he trained that day, because starch raises serotonin and causes sleepiness. About 25% of people have genes that let them tolerate lots of carbohydrate without sedation; he does not.
- **Post-lunch walk of 5-30 minutes** improves nutrient utilization and gives more outdoor light information.
- **Omega-3s.** At least 1,000 mg per day of EPA (not 1,000 mg of fish oil) has been shown in over a dozen studies to be about as effective as prescription antidepressants for depression. He takes fish oil; algae-based options exist for non-fish eaters.
- **Selenium for thyroid.** A few Brazil nuts per day. He also notes iodine matters.
- **Cholesterol for hormones.** Sex steroids are made from cholesterol, so he eats butter (keeping portions modest).
- **Testosterone/estrogen options.** 400 mg/day Tongkat Ali (a ginseng) to raise free testosterone by lowering SHBG effects, and Fadogia agrestis to raise luteinizing hormone. He notes the evidence is limited and to check with a doctor.
- Allowing 12+ hours per day without eating is beneficial.

## NSDR and hypnosis in the afternoon

- **Non-sleep deep rest (NSDR)** is his umbrella term for meditation, yoga nidra and hypnosis, all deliberate shifts toward deeper relaxation.
- **Daily 10-minute hypnosis after lunch and the walk,** using the Reveri app (reverie.com), based on research by David Spiegel at Stanford. He cites a *Cerebral Cortex* paper (Jiang et al.) showing hypnosis engages executive networks, the default mode network and the insula (interoception). It is not stage hypnosis.
- Unlike a nap, he exits "focused" with no grogginess. It can also increase plasticity and be directed toward goals like focus, creativity, pain reduction, anxiety or sleep.
- After NSDR, he drinks water and does a second 90-minute work block around 2:30-3:00 p.m. Combined with delayed caffeine, he avoids the afternoon slump.
- **Napping.** Keep naps to 90 minutes or less; 10-20 minutes is often better. Know whether naps disrupt your night sleep.

## Evening light, dinner and sleep

- **Afternoon/evening sunlight (about 4-5 p.m.)** for 5-30 minutes lowers retinal sensitivity to later light and keeps the melatonin rhythm appropriate (a *Scientific Reports* study). Light between 10-11 p.m. and 4 a.m. disrupts dopamine, learning, memory, immune function and mood.
- **Blue blockers.** He does not wear them. If used at all, wear them only late at night, not in the afternoon. Bright light of any wavelength still activates the relevant retinal cells. For headaches from screens, look into the distance (over 20 feet) periodically.
- **Dinner with starchy carbs** (rice, pasta) plus protein and vegetables boosts serotonin, aiding the transition to sleep. Low-carb dieters often struggle to fall asleep. He avoids refined sugar, citing Robert Lustig.
- **Skip serotonin/dopamine precursor supplements** (5-HTP, tryptophan): they made him fall asleep fast, then wake after 3-4 hours.
- **Heat to cool down.** A hot bath, shower or sauna 20 minutes before bed accelerates the later temperature drop of 1-3 degrees. For growth hormone, 20 minutes in the sauna, 10 minutes out, repeat.
- **Bedroom.** Very dark and cool, with warm blankets, so you can dump heat through palms, feet and face during non-REM sleep.
- **Sleep stack (30-60 min before bed).** 300-400 mg elemental magnesium threonate or bisglycinate (promotes GABA), 50 mg apigenin (from chamomile), and 100-200 mg theanine. He avoids melatonin: doses are too high, possible effects on sex hormones, a role in suppressing puberty onset, and it does not keep you asleep. Check with a physician first; some get GI upset from magnesium.

## Night waking and weekends

- **Waking at 2:30-3 a.m. after a very early melatonin pulse** (you are tired at 8:30 but stay up): go to bed earlier, or get somewhat more bright light around 7-8 p.m. to delay melatonin.
- **If you wake up, keep lights dim** and turn them off fast. Use NSDR/yoga nidra, hypnosis or long-exhale breathing: "It's very hard to control the mind with the mind. Look to the body."
- **Consistency.** Even after a late night, wake at your regular time. After a bad night, do not go to bed much earlier the next day; stay up to your normal time.
- **Weekends.** He takes one full unstructured day off per week. Daily non-negotiables: morning sunlight and regular sleep.
