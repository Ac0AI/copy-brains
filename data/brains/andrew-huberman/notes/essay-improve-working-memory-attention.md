## Working memory: what it is and why dopamine drives it

Andrew defines working memory as holding a limited amount of information "in mind" for a specific goal, such as a phone number, name or address, then letting it go so capacity reopens for other information.

- It requires attention and depends critically on the neuromodulator dopamine.
- Working memory capacity underpins task-switching and moving through the day in a productive, focused way.
- He cites D'Esposito and colleagues: people with a high working memory span (those who can remember longer strings of letters or numbers) have more dopamine available in the prefrontal cortex (PFC). Those with shorter spans have less.
- He cites Goldman et al.: introducing small amounts of dopamine directly into the PFC improved working memory.
- He notes that D'Esposito and colleagues showed dopamine-increasing drugs such as bromocriptine improve working memory in humans.
- People with initially lower dopamine apparently benefit more from raising it than people whose dopamine is already high, which he says makes logical sense.

## Two quick tests to estimate baseline working memory

Andrew describes two brief tests similar to laboratory assessments. They are a proxy, not a diagnostic tool, and he says not to be alarmed by a low result because many tools improve it.

- **Test one:** a digit or letter span task, available as a written test or in audio format. How many numbers or letters you can remember places you in a low, moderate or high working memory group.
- **Test two:** listen to 6 sentences, then recall a specific word from each.
  - 0-2 correct = low working memory group.
  - 3-6 correct = high working memory group.
  - 3-4 is average.
- Results (low vs. high) serve as a proxy for low or high baseline dopamine, which he says neuroimaging studies support.

## Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) for dopamine and cognitive performance

NSDR (or yoga nidra) is deliberate muscle relaxation that lets the brain enter a shallow, sleep-like state.

- Both practices involve listening to a script with breathwork and focused attention on the body while lying down. Andrew notes zero-cost NSDR scripts exist.
- He cites a study in which yoga nidra increased dopamine in key brain regions by up to 60%.
- He says other studies using cognitive performance tasks show both NSDR and yoga nidra improve performance.
- Protocol: **2-3x per week, in 10-to-20-minute sessions.**

## Deliberate cold exposure before a working-memory-heavy work bout

Cold water exposure (shower or cold tub, 1-3 minutes) greatly increases dopamine and other catecholamines (norepinephrine, epinephrine) for many hours afterward.

- It is uncomfortable in the moment, but afterward you feel alert yet calm and likely have improved focus and attention.
- **Timing:** do 1-3 minutes of cold exposure **30-60 minutes before** a study or work session requiring working memory.
- **Dose:** stay in water up to your neck (hands under as well) for 60 seconds to 3 minutes, in a shower or cold plunge.
- **Temperature:** highly individual. The right temperature is one you can safely stay in for 60-180 seconds, but that is uncomfortable enough that it takes mental effort to get in and makes you want to get out.
  - Check: it quickens your breathing and takes effort to submerge, yet doesn't "burn."
  - For most people 40-50°F is a good range; some may need it warmer.

## Binaural beats during cognitive tasks

Several studies (he cites Engelbregt et al. and Beauchene et al.) show working memory performance increases when listeners use 13-30 Hz binaural beats while doing mental tasks.

- It is unknown whether the benefit comes from increased dopamine or another route.
- Protocol: listen to binaural beats before or during studying or working. They are available as YouTube videos or apps.
- Andrew stresses the data are mixed: some studies show impressive effects, some lesser, and some no improvement in human subjects.

## Supplements to raise dopamine (after trying behavioral tools first)

Andrew's order of operations: try the behavioral tools above first, then consider supplements. He says never add or remove a supplement without consulting your doctor. Find your minimal effective dose for any supplement.

- **L-tyrosine:** the amino acid precursor to dopamine, shown to increase working memory. Starting dose: **500 mg per 100 kg of body weight** (100 kg is about 220 lb).
- **Mucuna pruriens ("velvet bean"):** contains 99% L-dopa, the molecular precursor to dopamine.
  - He calls it a "VERY potent" way to increase dopamine and says to discuss with your doctor before using it.
  - Typical dose is 250 or 500 mg, though doses up to 1-5 g are tolerable for some people.
- **Crash risk:** some people experience a bit of a "crash" when the effects of pro-dopamine supplements wear off. That is why he prefers behavioral tools.

## Prescription options for working memory and attention

Andrew lists prescription drugs that improve working memory and attention: bromocriptine (from the study above) and more commonly known medications such as Adderall, Ritalin, Vyvanse and modafinil.

- If someone decides to explore them, he says it must be done with a board-certified MD.
