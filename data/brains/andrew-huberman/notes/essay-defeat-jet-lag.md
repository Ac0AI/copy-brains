## Why eastward travel is harder, and how to prep

- Andrew says most people find it easier to stay up later than to fall asleep earlier, so eastward travel is harder. Westward gives more time to get the right light signals.
- Jet lag worsens with age. The degree depends on the person and the number of time zones crossed.
- Prep for eastward travel starting two days before departure with three levers (light, caffeine, exercise):
  - Wake 30 to 45 minutes earlier than usual and view bright sunlight or bright artificial light immediately.
  - Drink caffeine early in the day to promote early alertness.
  - Move within 60 minutes of the new earlier wake-up time, even light jogging if a full workout isn't possible.
- Example given: California to New York.

## Prepping before westward travel

- Westward travel is easier, but Andrew still recommends pre-adjusting with light, caffeine and exercise, shifted 30 to 45 minutes later instead of earlier.
- Wake a bit later, take caffeine a bit later (still avoiding caffeine in the 8 to 10 hours before bedtime), and exercise a bit later, even by just 45 minutes.
- Get bright artificial light to the eyes in the one to two hours after sunset, to prepare to stay up later.

## Temperature minimum: the power tool for shifting the clock

- The temperature minimum is the lowest body temperature of each 24-hour cycle. It occurs about two hours before usual wake-up time, so no measurement is needed. For a 7 a.m. riser it is roughly 4:30 to 5:30 a.m.
- Use typical wake time, whether or not an alarm is used.
- Bright light to the eyes in the one to two hours after the temperature minimum shifts the clock earlier (sleep earlier, wake earlier). This suits eastward travel.
- Bright light in the one to two hours before the temperature minimum shifts the clock later (sleep later, wake later). This suits westward travel.
- Andrew stresses that the temperature minimum must be calculated on your home time before you leave. "Get it right and you'll shift fast across time zones. Get it wrong and it can be a mess."

## Common mistake: morning sun on arrival going east

- Example: San Francisco to London (+8 hours). Landing to London morning sunlight, for the first two to three days, equals a giant blast of light at midnight San Francisco time to the brain's clock.
- That pushes the body toward later bedtimes and wake times, the opposite of what is wanted. Many people sleep well the first night, see sunlight, and then have a bad second night.
- Fix: wear dark sunglasses for the two to three hours before your (home-based) temperature minimum for the first two days. Get sunlight in the eyes in the London afternoon, which falls one to two hours after the temperature minimum.
- London is only an example; work out the timing from your own trip.

## Eating, fasting and exercise at the destination

- Eating is a strong entraining signal. Eat breakfast, lunch and dinner at local times, even if not hungry, and start as early as possible.
- Many people fast 14 to 16 hours before local breakfast time to adopt the new schedule.
- Andrew personally skips this because he dislikes eating when not hungry and it lands in his subjective middle of the night. He acknowledges it is known to work.
- Keep a consistent early-in-the-day exercise routine on local time. It can be light aerobic activity like walking or more engaged, depending on fitness and degree of jet lag.

## Light tools: portable lights, cloud cover and red lenses

- If it's cloudy or you land at night, travel with a portable bright light device of 5,000 to 10,000 lux.
- Even through cloud cover, sunlight is almost always far brighter than artificial lighting, so go outside at the correct times. Once shifted, return to standard "view sunlight in the (local) morning and (local) afternoon."
- In the evening, reduce bright light, especially short-wavelength blue and green light. All LEDs are rich in these.
- Andrew wears red-lens glasses after sundown to adjust to the local sleep schedule. He says this protects against the melatonin-reducing and cortisol-increasing effects of bright light. (He helped ROKA design the Wind Down lens, and ROKA sponsors the podcast.)

## Short trips

- For trips under three days, staying on the home schedule may minimize circadian disruption.
