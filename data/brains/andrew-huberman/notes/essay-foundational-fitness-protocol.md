## The foundational weekly structure

Andrew's "foundational fitness protocol" (Huberman Lab Neural Network newsletter, November 2022, tied to Episode #94) covers one key aspect of physical fitness each day of the week. It is built to hit every major goal: strength, hypertrophy, endurance and cardiovascular training.

- Each muscle group is trained twice per week: one day directly and one day indirectly.
- He starts Day 1 on Sunday and ends Day 7 on Saturday, but says to shift the start day to fit your schedule.
- The specific days matter less than spacing workouts apart so recovery is adequate.
- If you miss a day, you can double up on some workouts later in the week. He sometimes swaps days 4 and 5.
- Keep the range of physical goals across the week while adjusting exercises to your fitness level and goals.
- He chooses exercises you can do safely, with full range of motion and proper form: "The best exercise you can do for a body part is one that you can perform safely."
- Adjust to your own strengths and weaknesses. He never trains chest directly because he gets enough from dips.

## Resistance training: periodization and rep schemes

Andrew recommends alternating two training schedules monthly (periodization) to optimize strength and muscle hypertrophy:

- **Schedule A:** about 4-8 reps (heavier weight), 3-4 sets per exercise, 2-4 minutes rest between sets.
- **Schedule B:** about 8-15 reps (moderate to lighter weight), 2-3 sets per exercise, about 90 seconds rest between sets.
- Alternate between A and B each month.

## Exercise selection: shortened and lengthened positions

Choose two exercises per muscle group:

1. **Exercise #1:** brings the muscle into a shortened or maximally contracted position at the end of the range of motion. Examples: leg curl, seated calf raise, bicep preacher curl, leg extension, chest cable crossover.
2. **Exercise #2:** places resistance on the muscle in a lengthened position. Examples: standing calf raise, incline dumbbell curl, deep squat, glute-hamstring raise or straight-legged deadlift, incline press.

## Session length and efficiency

- Weight workouts should be 50-60 minutes of hard work after a warmup, 75 minutes maximum, which aids effectiveness and recovery.
- The 75 minutes includes rest between sets.
- That requires not waiting too long for equipment and not texting between sets. His summary: warm up, then get it done.

## Breathing, recovery and flexibility

- **Between sets:** use physiological sighs to lower heart rate. Take two full inhales through the nose, then one full exhale through the mouth, releasing all the air.
- **After training:** do 3-5 minutes of deliberate slow breathing to "downshift" the nervous system, relax mind and body and aid muscle recovery.
- **Flexibility:** static stretching is the best way to improve overall flexibility. Hold each major muscle group for 30-60 seconds, repeated between 3x/week and daily.
- He stretches at night before sleep, while waiting for dinner to cook, or on work calls. Long exhales during the stretch further relax muscles and deepen it.

## Cold exposure timing and training timing

- Many studies find cold submersion (ice baths) blunts some strength and hypertrophy gains from that day's training. Avoid ice baths immediately after resistance training.
- If you take ice baths on weight days, wait 6-8 hours or do them before training. Cold showers likely don't have the same effect.
- **Fasted vs. fed** is individual. Andrew likes to train fasted and before noon, after caffeine, water and electrolytes, because he doesn't feel sluggish.
- If time is limited and you just ate, it's more important to train than to skip.
- He doesn't weight train at night because it keeps him awake. Cardio doesn't seem to do that to him.

## Training obstacles: illness, poor sleep, stress

- **Mild cold:** a shorter, lighter session (duration and/or intensity cut by 50% or more) can help you feel better.
- **Truly sick:** skip training and recover. Then ramp back up over 3-7 days.
- **Poor sleep or chronic stress:** consider skipping the workout unless you think it will help you reset. Prioritize a good night's sleep or relaxation, and double up on workouts later in the week.
- Try 10-30 minutes of Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) to feel more refreshed. Afterward you may feel up for the scheduled workout.

## Supplements for performance and recovery

Andrew says supplements aren't necessary but that many people, including him, benefit:

- **Alpha-GPC:** 300 mg, 30-60 minutes before key workouts. Promotes acetylcholine release (involved in memory, attention and skeletal muscle contraction) and has been shown to increase power output in athletes.
- **L-Tyrosine:** 500 mg, 30-60 minutes before key workouts. An amino acid that can increase dopamine and related neurotransmitters, supporting energy, motivation and mood.
- **Creatine:** 5 g daily. Stores high-energy phosphate groups that regenerate ATP, and has been shown in thousands of studies to improve exercise performance.
- **Whey protein:** 40 g, 30-60 minutes after the workout. A well-absorbed protein that research links to muscle gain and fat loss, particularly post-workout.
