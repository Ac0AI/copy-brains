## Dopamine baseline: what sets it and how to support it

Andrew defines dopamine as the molecule most tied to motivation, depth of focus, and a lower threshold for taking action toward goals. He quotes Dr. Anna Lembke: "Dopamine is about wanting, not about having." Serotonin, by contrast, is linked to well-being about what you already have. When dopamine is low, people feel unmotivated, get less pleasure from pursuits, and feel physically tired.

Baseline dopamine is shaped by genetics, behaviors, sleep, nutrition, and the dopamine experienced on previous days. Andrew says the baseline should be neither too low nor too high, and that it needs to be sufficient to sustain day-to-day motivation.

## Daily behaviors that raise or protect baseline dopamine

- **Morning sunlight:** view early morning sunlight for 10-30 minutes daily. No sunglasses and don't stare at the sun; eyeglasses and contacts are fine. This releases dopamine, and done consistently it increases gene expression for certain dopamine receptors.
- **Cold shower:** optionally take a 1-3 minute shower, as cold as you can safely tolerate. He says this dramatically increases baseline dopamine for hours.
- **Tyrosine-rich foods:** red meat, nuts, or hard fermented cheese. Tyrosine is an amino acid and a building block of dopamine. Plant-based sources exist too; consider the calories and other contents of these foods.
- **Avoid melatonin supplements:** they can decrease dopamine and disrupt normal sleep patterns. He recommends melatonin only for jet lag, and says there are better options.
- **Avoid bright light between 10 p.m. and 4 a.m.:** he calls this "essential." Bright light then activates the habenula and drastically reduces circulating dopamine. If you must be up, keep light very dim. Once in a while is fine, but don't make it a habit.
- **Caffeine:** about 100-400 mg from coffee, tea, or whatever form you prefer. It mildly raises dopamine and increases dopamine receptor availability, so the body is more sensitive to circulating dopamine. Andrew avoids caffeine after 2 p.m., with rare exceptions.

## Random intermittent reward timing (RIRT)

Andrew says "success breeds success" holds, but if you don't manage the dopamine around pursuit and wins, your baseline and the satisfaction from milestones diminish over time, and you feel far less satisfaction from everything.

- RIRT is the most powerful schedule for dopamine release and staying motivated. Casinos use it.
- Celebrate your wins, but not every win. When you hit a milestone, sometimes enjoy it, and other times, at random, just keep going.
- Even better, associate "winning" with the effort process itself. He calls that "the holy grail of dopamine management for success."
- He says it won't make you dull or unhappy. It makes things easier and more pleasurable, without the peaks and valleys of people driven by external rewards, and you still obtain the external rewards.

## Dopamine is subjective: use cognitive control

- The brain doesn't know external rewards; it only knows the associations of events with internal dopamine release.
- The prefrontal cortex is part of the dopamine pathway and provides top-down control, a "belief effect" on motivation. Andrew says these are not placebo or small effects.
- Telling yourself you are moving toward your goals is a huge stimulator of dopamine release, and it is under your control.
- Don't lie to yourself and claim a win when you lost, but as you progress toward milestones, register it in your mind.

## Spotlighting for focus

- Citing Dr. Emily Balcetis (NYU), Andrew says dopamine interacts with the visual system.
- Physically focusing your visual attention on one specific point (a "spotlight") helps maintain focus during bouts of goal work.
- Focusing on a point recruits a mix of neurochemicals (dopamine, epinephrine and others), putting you in a state of readiness and clear focus.

## Don't stack too many dopamine sources

- Layering sources (pre-workout energy drinks, music, friends, nootropics, etc.) can raise dopamine and energy and get you working hard.
- Stacking them all causes a crash afterward, undermining longer-term motivation and drive.
- Instead, do some workouts without music or with just caffeine, and change it up.

## Supplements for microspiking dopamine

- **L-Tyrosine:** 500-1000 mg taken 30 minutes before a mental or physical work bout increases focus and motivation.
- **Andrew's stack:** some days, 500 mg L-Tyrosine plus 300 mg Alpha-GPC (which increases acetylcholine, and thereby focus) before a hard workout or focused cognitive work.
- **His favorite pre-work stack:** 300 mg Alpha-GPC, 500 mg phenylethylamine, and sometimes 500 mg L-Tyrosine. If he really wants to drop into focus early in the day, he chases it with espresso.
- He doesn't do this every day, and sometimes uses none.
- Don't take these after 2-3 p.m. if you intend to sleep that night.
- Be very cautious if you have bipolar depression or another dopamine-sensitive condition.
- People with ADHD may need MD-prescribed Ritalin, Adderall, or similar. Those without ADHD should avoid these drugs for "recreational" focus, since they are powerful and can lead to dependency.
- He says the list is a buffet of options, and you don't need to do all of it.
