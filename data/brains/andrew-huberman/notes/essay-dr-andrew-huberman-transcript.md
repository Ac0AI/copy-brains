## Daily foundations: the five things to re-up every 24 hours

Andrew argues that your state of mind and body at any moment is strongly set by what you did in the preceding hours and days. Sleep architecture works this way: the REM share of a 90-minute sleep bout depends on the slow wave to REM ratio of the previous bout. Focus works this way too, since it is "not a square wave function" you can drop into on demand. He says there is no real "optimization" until five foundations are handled, and each needs to be re-upped every 24 hours:

- **Sleep.** Quality sleep brings gain of function, not just avoided loss.
- **Nutrients.** Macronutrients, micronutrients and hydration. Roughly 80% or more of food should be unprocessed or minimally processed. Supplements like Ashwagandha, Shilajit, tongkat ali and Fadogia agrestis are things you "won't get from food."
- **Movement.** Both cardiovascular exercise and resistance training.
- **Light.** Sunlight early and through the day, and less bright artificial light in the evening and at night.
- **Relationships.** This includes your relationship to yourself.

His take: "nothing else really happens for very long unless those five things are tended to." Before asking which focus supplement to take, he steps back through all five. He adds that this is not about being soft; it makes you resilient and effective.

## Andrew's weekly training split: one adaptation per day

Andy Galpin's research convinced Andrew that concurrent training works. You can build endurance and strength at the same time, because the body has only a small set of core adaptations and many methods for triggering them. He assigns each day one target:

- **Sunday, endurance.** A 75-90 minute slow jog with hills, wearing an 8-10 lb weight vest and nasal breathing. With others, he does a 3-5 hour loaded hike instead. The first 10-30 minutes feel bad, then central pattern generators take over repetitive movement without conscious attention, and effort feels easier.
- **Monday, legs.** About 10 minutes of warmup, then about 55 minutes of work. Each exercise gets 2-4 hard sets to failure with tight form, and he rests 2-5 minutes between sets.
- **Tuesday, heat and cold.** The leg recovery day. No cold immersion in the six hours after hypertrophy training, because it can blunt the adaptation.
- **Wednesday, short run.** Five minutes of warmup, then 25-30 minutes of steady running.
- **Thursday, "torso."** Overhead press, ring dips or dips, and neck work.
- **Friday, VO2 max.** Three minutes of warmup and about 12 minutes of work.
- **Saturday, small muscles.** Biceps, triceps, rear delts, and a lighter tib and calf session.

Other rules he follows:
- He keeps resistance sessions under 60 minutes, because beyond that cortisol rises and recovery suffers.
- He trains no later than about 11 a.m.
- He tracks sessions in a handwritten journal and adds a rep, a set or weight each time.
- He rates sessions subjectively from level 1 to 10.
- He plays low-stakes sports (e.g. badminton) about an hour a week.

He is 47 and says he is still getting stronger.

## Leg day, tib raises and why legs matter

Andrew's Monday leg session in order:

1. Tibialis raises as a warmup: a couple of warmup sets, then 3 sets of 6-10 reps. Alternatively, lean back against a wall at an angle with heels on the floor and lift the toes for 25-30 reps.
2. Standing or sled calf raises, 2-4 sets of 5-10 reps.
3. Lying leg curls on a machine, 2 warmups then 2-4 working sets.
4. Glute-ham raises, 3-4 slow sets (a deadlift rotated 90 degrees, ending in a leg curl). Nordic curls are a substitute.
5. Leg extensions, 2-3 sets.
6. Hack squats, heavy, 2-3 sets after a warmup. Back squats give him hip pain. Weighted sissy squats are an alternative.

On tib raises (from Ben Patrick, "Knees Over Toes Guy"): Andrew credits them with getting rid of his right-side sciatica, improving posture, and making him able to "run like a beast" with no knee, back or shoulder pain. They also made his calf work more effective, and fixed his shin splints. He had believed his problem was flat feet, but it was weak tibialis muscles. He also says the idea that knees can't pass the toes is "silly" (see skateboarders and Olympic lifters).

Leg training has outsized value:
- Cognitive gains from resistance training come from big lower-body work, not "bicep curls."
- Legs create a "systemic anabolic effect."
- Protein synthesis peaks around 48 hours after training and stays elevated for a couple more days, so Friday's sprints and jumps indirectly keep the legs online.

If he could pick only three exercises, they would be glute-ham raise or Nordic curl, ring dips, and sprinting.

## Heat, cold, neck training and the soleus push-up

**Tuesday heat and cold protocol.**
- 20 minutes in a very hot sauna (he works up to about 260°F).
- 3-5 minutes in a cold plunge up to the neck.
- Repeat for three sauna rounds and three plunges.
- He uses the time to listen to books.

**Sperm and heat.** Because he hopes to have children, he wears shorts in the sauna and puts a cold pack at his groin.

**Neck training.** Andrew says to do it for spinal strength and injury protection, and because it changes posture and presence. He stands more upright and finds it easier to look people in the eye.
- Lie on your side on a bench holding a towel-wrapped plate against your head, 10-15 reps, with one light warmup and three work sets.
- Then lie on your stomach with the plate behind your head and do head raises.
- Tim adds that manual resistance from a partner, done very slowly, also works.
- For women who don't want a larger neck, use lighter weight or hand resistance. He says women who want to avoid rounded shoulders should also go easy on upper-trap work.

**Soleus push-up.** A University of Houston study had people do seated calf raises (toe down, raising the heel every few seconds) for a couple of hours. They showed a meaningful improvement in insulin sensitivity. The soleus is about 1% of body musculature but can account for at least 15% of daily energy expenditure. It is not a replacement for exercise. Andrew's line: "if you're unwilling to try that, then your bar for entry is just way too high."

## Sleep stack, morning vs. evening, and hydration timing

**Sleep stack.**
- Magnesium threonate, theanine, apigenin (a concentrated chamomile extract), and sometimes 900 mg of myo-inositol.
- If limited to two: magnesium threonate and apigenin.
- Theanine can make dreams more vivid, so people with intense dreams or nightmares should be wary of it.
- The myo-inositol helps him fall back asleep after waking to urinate. It is a mild sedative that is also studied for insulin sensitivity and depression.
- He tracks sleep on an Eight Sleep. He says wristbands and rings (WHOOP, Oura) can misread limb movement.

**Hydration timing.**
- Gulp fluids in the morning. The kidney is a different organ in the morning than at night.
- Sip, don't gulp, your last drink of the day. Rapid fluid intake sends a fast signal to the bladder, so slower intake means fewer 3 a.m. bathroom trips.

**Early day vs. late day.**
- Get sunlight (or bright artificial light), caffeine, hydration and movement early. Sunlight raises cortisol about 50% above baseline. You want cortisol and catecholamines (dopamine, epinephrine, norepinephrine) high early and low late.
- Late-shifted cortisol is associated with depression and immune problems.
- High-intensity interval training early, or cycling, appears to improve sleep stages, including REM, which is when emotions from the previous day get unpacked.

## Omega-3s, gut-brain sensing and fermented foods

**Omega-3 dosing.**
- Minimum 1 g of EPA per day, sometimes more. Up to about 3 g per day has antidepressant effects in clinical trials.
- His routine: three capsules with his big midday meal, plus a tablespoon of Carlson's lemon-flavored liquid fish oil that he stirs into oatmeal with salt.
- He recommends refrigerating liquid fish oil after opening.
- Tim's problem: nausea after 5-7 days at about 2 g in capsules. Andrew suggested trying liquid, because the capsules themselves might be the issue.

**Two mechanisms for mood.**
- EPA is a structural lipid in neuronal membranes, including those of neurons that release serotonin and dopamine.
- Gut "neuropod cells" sense sugar, essential fatty acids and essential amino acids, and signal via the vagus nerve to the brain's dopamine centers (work from Diego Bohorquez at Duke and Charles Zuker at Columbia).
- Nutrient-poor but highly palatable food gives a dopamine hit without that matching nutrient signal.
- Getting enough omega-3s or small amounts of L-glutamine reduces sugar cravings. Quality BCAA or essential amino acid powder can too.

**Fermented foods.** One serving a day is not enough. He says to aim for three or four servings of low-sugar fermented foods, such as nattō, sauerkraut, kimchi and sour Bulgarian yogurt. Since few people manage that, he notes AG1 as a convenient option, and he does not have high-dose probiotics as a first choice because they are expensive and need refrigeration.

## Rhodiola, Ashwagandha, tongkat ali and Fadogia agrestis

**Rhodiola rosea.**
- 200 mg, 30-60 minutes before training. Effects last about four hours.
- It reduces perceived effort and allows more power and endurance output, and it reduces the post-workout energy dip.
- It does not work by suppressing cortisol. His guess is it blunts adrenaline's energy-depleting trough.
- He takes it before hard, high-intensity or hike sessions, not before every leg day.
- He avoids taking it all day every day, because studies suggest its effects taper.
- If it doesn't work the first time, he'd try a slightly higher dose.
- He calls it "probably the best addition to my kind of physical performance stack" in a long time.

**Ashwagandha.**
- It suppresses cortisol.
- Take it late in the day, not early, and not in high doses (400-600 mg) before exercise, because the cortisol spike drives adaptation.
- Low doses (25-50 mg) can be taken continuously.
- For higher doses, take at least two weeks off after about two weeks.

**Tongkat ali.**
- Use the Indonesian variety.
- Typically 400 mg a day, single or divided, taken before noon or 2 p.m. because it can increase energy.
- It lowers sex hormone-binding globulin, freeing testosterone. He cringes at doses of a gram a day, since high-dose herbals risk immune reactions.

**Fadogia agrestis.**
- 300-600 mg a day, with or without food, any time of day.
- It raises luteinizing hormone, and so testosterone in men and estrogen in women. It can increase testicular size and density.
- Rat data suggest testicular toxicity only at very high doses.
- He does not cycle tongkat ali or Fadogia, and he does blood work including liver enzymes twice a year.

**Estrogen in men.** He notes estrogen matters in men too: crashing it hurts libido and cognition, and estrogen is cardioprotective.

**Testosterone range.** He prefers the high end of normal (about 600-900 ng/dL) over exogenous testosterone, which suppresses endogenous production.

If limited to two across these and omegas, he would pick omegas and tongkat ali.

## Fertility, sperm and egg health: dos and don'ts

Andrew spent a year researching this, and he has monitored and frozen his own sperm. He treats fertility as a proxy for vitality. He defines vitality as the ability to wake with energy, move between driven and relaxed, and feel good doing it.

**Do:**
- Get quality sleep, sunlight, low stress and good relationships.
- Get enough omega-3s.
- Take L-carnitine. It improves egg and sperm health, including forward motility. Injectable (about 1 mL a day) needs a prescription. Oral capsules need 4-5 g a day, which can raise TMAO. He says to offset that with about 600 mg of garlic (allicin).
- Get 20 minutes of sun on bare skin (shirt off, or tank top and shorts) three times a week. An Israeli study found this raised free testosterone, estrogen and libido, with afternoon sun.
- Consider banking eggs or sperm. In California, eggs can generally be frozen up to about 42 (after that only embryos), and he suggests sperm banking before about 45.

**Don't:**
- Smoke or vape anything, including cannabis.
- Carry a phone in your front pocket. A meta-analysis found it lowers sperm quality, motility and testosterone, and even short durations matter. Use a back pocket, a bag, or a shoulder pocket.
- Drink more than two or three drinks a week (women), which reduces egg quality. He says negative health effects begin past about two drinks a week for men and women.
- Handle printed receipts, which he calls a major BPA source, and avoid airborne pesticide exposure (rural areas, per Shanna Swan's work).

Tim reports his own sperm quality is better now than 10 years ago after L-carnitine, maca and other changes.

## Cannabis and psychedelics: Andrew's stance

**Cannabis.**
- Smoking or vaping is worse than edibles or tinctures.
- Very high-THC cannabis in teens and young adults carries about a 4x higher chance of later psychotic episodes that don't reverse.
- Low-dose edibles in adults past the critical period of brain plasticity (older than about 25) are very unlikely to be a problem. 2.5 mg is a sub-perceptible dose.
- Pure CBD cannabis (Charlotte's Web) is a powerful anti-epileptic with essentially no psychoactive effect.
- Tim notes THC at about 2.5 mg helps his onset insomnia caused by rumination.

**Psychedelics and MDMA.**
- His view has changed completely in the last 24 months.
- He took LSD recreationally in high school and regrets it.
- He has done three physician-guided MAPS-style MDMA sessions for trauma, and says they were immensely beneficial.
- Session one was somatic and left him comfortable in his body and emotions. Session two brought deep acceptance and forgiveness of old resentments. Session three, at a higher dose with the booster, gave him boundaries between his emotions and others'.
- He says the benefits have held for years.
- Psilocybin trials for intractable depression show 60-70% of patients getting substantial, ongoing relief.
- Eye masks and music appear key to the introspective effect.

**Safety.** Street drugs are often laced with fentanyl. Tim recommends DanceSafe test kits. Sourcing MDMA through MAPS is the clean route. He also continues psychotherapy "like going to the gym."
