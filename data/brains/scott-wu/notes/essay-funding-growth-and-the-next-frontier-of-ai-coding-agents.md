## Cognition's $400M raise and growth numbers (Sept 2025)

Cognition raised over $400M at a $10.2B post-money valuation, led by Founders Fund. Existing investors Lux Capital, 8VC (who jointly led the previous round), Neo, Elad Gil, Definition Capital and Swish VC doubled down. New investors were Bain Capital Ventures, Hanabi Capital and D1 Capital.

- Scott frames the mission as "the future of software engineering": a world of software abundance where engineers become architects and task "an army of autonomous agents" with everything else.
- He says that when Devin launched in March 2024, it wasn't clear this future would become real. Devin "could knock some tasks out of the park" but was mostly "a very junior engineer."
- His view: the baseline has changed drastically, and agents already do real work alongside individual developers and inside large enterprise teams. What "first seemed a fringe theory quickly became an obvious reality."

## Devin's growth and capital efficiency

- Devin ARR (before the Windsurf acquisition) grew from $1M in September 2024 to $73M in June 2025, as usage increased exponentially.
- Growth stayed efficient: total net burn was under $20M across the company's entire history.
- Scott presents this as the proof point behind the raise: fast ARR growth with low cumulative burn.

## Why buying Windsurf worked: IDE plus agent

- The Windsurf acquisition more than doubled Cognition's ARR. Scott says the bigger value was getting "the complete product suite for AI coding."
- His framing of the market: the two main forms of AI coding tools are IDEs and agents.
  - The IDE is for when the engineer wants to make each decision personally, with an AI speedup.
  - Agents are for delegating complete tasks asynchronously.
- His conclusion: "Engineers naturally want both." Cognition will keep investing significantly in both Devin and Windsurf, and customers are already seeing the combination's power.

## Post-acquisition growth and enterprise distribution

- Combined enterprise ARR was up over 30% in the seven weeks after buying Windsurf.
- Pre-acquisition enterprise customer overlap was under 5%, so the two customer bases were largely additive.
- Scott calls the combination of Devin's rapid adoption with Windsurf's IDE product and "scaled GTM machine" a "massive unlock." Buying an existing go-to-market engine was part of the value, not just the product.
- Named customers across Devin and Windsurf: Goldman Sachs, Citi, Dell, Cisco, Ramp, Palantir, Nubank and Mercado Libre. He calls them "category-defining customers."

## Team and hiring

- Scott says the thing he's most proud of is the team.
- Windsurf's team joined. Two early Cognition investors, Christian Lawless (Conversion Capital) and Emily Cohen (Neo), made a high-conviction decision to join full-time.
- swyx joined the team; Scott is a longtime fan of his work, from Latent Space to AI Engineer.
- Founding story: a small group of engineers with a lifelong love of coding and over a decade of friendship, who "hunkered down in a New York apartment and built the product we always wanted for ourselves."
- The core mission stayed the same as the company scaled: build the future of software engineering.
