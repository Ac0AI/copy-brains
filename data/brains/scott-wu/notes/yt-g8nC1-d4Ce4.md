## Cognition's acquisition of Windsurf: how Scott found and closed the deal

Scott Wu is the CEO of Cognition, which builds Devin, the first AI software engineer agent. In July 2025, Google struck a licensing and acqui-hire deal with Windsurf, and Cognition bought what remained of the company within days. Scott first heard the news on Friday afternoon, and the deal was done by the start of the following week.

- Scott saw "such a clean fit": Windsurf is one of the leading IDEs, Cognition builds the agent, so together they offer IDE plus agent.
- Both companies have a clear enterprise focus, in how they do business, in their customers and in their teams.
- Scott describes the Windsurf team as a group he had been "huge fans of" from the beginning.
- He calls the announcement a "real acquisition", as opposed to an acqui-hire.

## How Cognition got to conviction quickly

- Cognition had tried Windsurf's product itself and used it a lot, so it did not need a long diligence process to form a view.
- Scott frames Windsurf as complementary rather than competing: a "very different" offering in the AI code space.
- After one conversation with interim CEO Jeff Wang, both sides saw it as a perfect fit.
- Scott's reasoning for speed: "the sooner that we could get to an outcome, the better for the team and for the customers."
- Terms disclosed: every Windsurf employee received "immediate consideration" as part of the deal, and Windsurf employees became part of Cognition as one team. Scott did not give a cash/stock split or a price.

## Why the Google deal left a usable company behind

- Scott's account of the structure: the Google deal was meant to be a "clean cut", leaving the product, the IP, the go-to-market team, the marketing team and the enterprise engineering team intact.
- Those left-behind components were exactly what Cognition was looking for.
- Scott says it is "a bit disappointing" that acqui-hires are becoming more common, because "a lot of great teams … get left behind."
- He treats the Windsurf deal as an alternative: instead of leaving the remainder of a company stranded, buy it and keep the team, product and customers together.

## Product thesis: the foreground and background agent combination

- Windsurf is an IDE that lets a developer at the keyboard "go faster and produce code faster."
- Devin is an AI software engineering agent that developers delegate tasks to.
- Scott's workflow examples:
  - Kick off the agent to do a first pass on a project, then use Windsurf to add the finishing touches yourself.
  - Plan in the IDE, then hand the sorted-out task to the agent.
- Jeff Wang (Windsurf's interim CEO) put the combination as "the foreground agent and the background agent" under one roof.
- Scott says both companies were always focused on "making software engineering work in the real world", rather than demos.

## Enterprise distribution as the shared advantage

Scott points to enterprise traction as part of why the pairing works:
- In the days around the deal, Cognition announced that Goldman Sachs and Citi (referred to as "City Bank" in the transcript) were rolling out Devin to their teams.
- Windsurf was already working with JP Morgan.
- Scott says that going down the customer list and the sectors, "we found a lot of natural partnership."

## Why the deal prioritized the team and the customers

- Scott's stated priority was an outcome that was good for Windsurf's team and its customers, which pushed toward moving fast.
- He and Jeff Wang say the structure meant all Windsurf employees came with immediate consideration, and Wang notes that investor and founder shares were wiped from the cap table, making the remaining company employee-owned. Wang's view is that this left many people happy.
- Scott closes by endorsing the idea that the approach could be repeated: after an acqui-hire, a lot of valuable pieces remain, and a buyer can keep them whole.
