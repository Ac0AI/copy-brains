## How Scott frames the Windsurf deal: speed, complementary strengths, and one boat

- The deal came together in about 72 hours: a first call after 5 p.m. on a Friday, almost no sleep, a signed definitive agreement by Monday morning, and an announcement that afternoon.
- Cognition prepared to go to Windsurf's offices on Saturday with a printed LOI, "ready to sign on the spot."
- Scott describes the moment four people (Jeff and Graham from Windsurf, Scott and Russell from Cognition) got in a room as "time seemed to slow down. Everything clicked."
- His fit logic was complementary gaps:
  - Cognition had built one of the best engineering orgs in Silicon Valley and wanted a GTM org.
  - Windsurf had built one of the best GTM orgs and wanted engineering.
  - The products did not overlap: Cognition had a cloud agent (Devin), Windsurf had an IDE.
- Windsurf brought millions of users, a brand developers loved, and a strong team.
- The merger message to the Windsurf team: "there's only one boat and we're all in it together."
- Context he describes from the Windsurf side: many team members had just left for Google, recruiters were contacting those who remained, and customers were calling to ask what was happening.

## Post-merger cadence: "Just Keep Shipping"

- Three days after the deal closed, the team shipped Wave 11, named "Just Keep Shipping." Scott says it "set the tone for the year."
- The shipping record over 12 months, month by month:
  - July 2025: Voice Mode, Named Checkpoints, @-mention past conversations, Planning Mode on by default, Devin's MCP Marketplace.
  - August 2025: DeepWiki in the IDE, Dev Containers support, 100+ bug fixes in a single wave.
  - October 2025: SWE-grep, IL6-ready.
  - December 2025: parallel agents, Git worktree support, Cascade Hooks, system-level Rules and Workflows for enterprise.
  - January 2026: Plan Mode, Megaplan, Agent Trace, Arena Mode.
  - March 2026: new token-based plans including Max, Devin can manage Devins, scheduled Devins.
  - April 2026: self-serve Devin plans, daily limits removed, per-message token counts.
  - May 2026: Android emulator support, Devin gets a Windows PC, Auto-Triage.
  - June-July 2026: Security in Devin Review, FrontierCode, Devin Fusion, Devin Security Swarm, Devin Security Vulnerability Remediation Program.
- The shipping pace included small quality items (bug-fix waves, limits removed) alongside headline features.

## Growth numbers since the merger

- Revenue run rate grew from $73M to $500M+ since merging the brands.
- The team grew from 44 to 350 people.
- The team wrote 20M+ lines of code locally.
- Offices now span San Francisco, New York, London, Singapore, and Tokyo, with more international offices planned and teams on the ground in additional places.
- The company entered new industries and partnered with new teams such as automakers and systems integrators.

## Product strategy: own models, stay neutral, unify the brand

- Cognition built its own models: SWE-1.5 (served at about 1000 tokens per second), then SWE-1.6, then SWE-1.7, launched the week before the post and described as "the most capable and efficient model we've trained to date."
- Positioning: Scott calls the company "the Switzerland of AI." Evidence for it: model evaluation is put in the open through Arena Mode and a public leaderboard.
- Surface strategy: Windsurf 2.0 brought every agent into a single command center. The company then unified into one brand with Devin Desktop, "your home for software engineering." Devin is now available "on every surface with any agent and any model."
- Other launches in the year: Devin Review, Devin CLI, Devin 2.2, and Cognition for Government.
- Commercial confidence move: the AI Productivity Guarantee, which Scott frames as putting "our money where our mouth is."

## How Scott describes the agent's progression

- A year earlier, most AI code tools handled simple tasks like autocomplete or explaining a function. Devin was effective at isolated tasks such as script writing and bug fixes, with a gap to the long-term vision.
- Today he says Devin operates at a mid-to-senior level.
  - It launches, schedules, and manages other Devins.
  - Each new Devin learns from the history of the agents before it.
  - It tests its own work with computer use, self-verification, and autofixes.
- Where he thinks it goes next: a shift to "self-driving software development." Engineers become "super engineers," and people who never coded can leverage themselves in ways they never imagined.
