## Why Cognition raised $1B at a $26B valuation

- Scott raised over $1B, led by Lux Capital, General Catalyst and 8VC, at a $26B valuation.
- He gives two reasons for the round:
  - Scale aggressively: "scale our compute" and grow the team.
  - Stay independent: remaining an independent business is "really really important for us."
- His framing for demand: "every company in 2026 is a software company." AI is "doing real work at real companies everywhere."
- Enterprise proof points he cites:
  - Cognition works with all of the top five US health insurers, which are building far more tooling for their care providers, cutting costs and covering more people.
  - Banks use it to deliver software to their customers.
  - Other customers include the Treasury and NASA.

## Revenue growth trajectory and the 2026 target

- Run-rate progression Scott has shared over time:
  - Early 2025: a few million dollars.
  - June 2025: about $37M.
  - June 2026: "close to 500 million," in about two years of business.
- His 2026 goal for the team is one metric: "we firmly intend to cross a billion in revenue run rate." He wants to keep going beyond that and grow "at as many of the companies in the world that we can."
- He reads the growth as evidence of demand rather than of a crowded-market win: there is far more demand for software than supply of engineers.

## Market sizing logic: 10x the engineers, more than 10x the software

- Scott's sizing argument:
  - There are about 30 to 35 million software engineers in the world today.
  - Cognition wants to make all of them 10 times more efficient.
  - He thinks there is "a lot more than 10 times more software to build."
- His supporting stat: there were under 1 million software engineers 20 to 25 years ago (the start of the century), and that rise came with a massive increase in the software produced.
- His conclusion: making engineers more efficient produces more software, not less.

## Independence and neutrality as a positioning strategy against the labs

- Asked about the threat from big labs, Scott says it works "the other way around": being fully independent and neutral is "the best way for us to be aligned with our customers."
- How the position works:
  - Cognition works closely with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI and others, including on research.
  - Devin is a "compound system" that works with all of these models.
  - Customers get "the best model for every different use case."
  - He calls Cognition "the Switzerland in the equation."
- The incentive point: Cognition is incentivized to serve whatever model is best for each case, rather than a single series of models.
- On Cursor possibly being acquired by SpaceX, he argues the ecosystem is "broad enough and vibrant enough" for different players in different positions. First-party lab products will keep existing, but model neutrality is "an important position in the space."
- On further M&A (he references last year's Windsurf deal), he says there will "sure" be more, but his own focus is growing the business and serving customers.

## Devin adoption and the effect on engineering teams

- Scott describes Devin as the first AI software engineer, and says teams adopting coding agents at scale "are able to do much more and execute much more aggressively on roadmaps."
- Work mix: the large majority is on existing code bases, continuing to build and add features, with new code as well.
- Dogfooding: more than 90% of the code written at Cognition is written by Devin, and the team uses it all day to build Devin itself.

## Hiring and the future of the software engineering job

- Even with that productivity, Cognition keeps scaling headcount.
- Scott expects the job to evolve and some skill sets to change, but says "we will have far more people doing this and building software and building products, not less."
- His reasoning is the same demand logic: more efficiency means more software gets built.
