## Losing Apple and the drive to keep building

- Steve had hoped his life with Apple would be "an interesting tapestry" in which he weaved in and out. He would be there for a period, go off and contribute elsewhere while staying connected, then perhaps return for a lengthy stretch.
- He says it did not work out that way, but still calls it "ten of the best years of my life" and says he doesn't "regret much of anything."
- He could not have imagined the ending "in my wildest imagination."

## Why he wants to build, not teach or comment

- At thirty, Steve says "I want to build things" and is "not ready to be an industry pundit."
- He turned down three offers to be a professor that summer, telling the universities he "would be an awful professor."
- His self-assessment: "What I'm best at doing is finding a group of talented people and making things with them."
- He respects the direction Apple is going, but he wants to make things himself.
- If there is no place for him to make things at Apple, he will do what he did twice before: "I'll make my own place." He built in the garage when Apple started, and in the "metaphorical garage" when the Mac started.

## His yardstick for success

- Steve says the outside world measures success numerically, but his yardstick is different.
- His measure is that every computer designed from here on out will have to be at least as good as a Macintosh.
- Success for him is raising the standard for the whole industry, not revenue or market share.

## What Apple is, and what losing it would mean

- Steve says Apple "exists in the spirit of the people that work there" and in the philosophies and purpose by which they go about their business.
- He would feel he had lost Apple only if it became a place where computers are a commodity item, where "the romance is gone," and where people forget that computers are "the most incredible invention that man has ever invented."
- If he were "a million miles away" and those people still felt that way and were working on the next great personal computer, he would feel "my genes are still in there."

## Being sidelined, and how he coped

- In June and July he went to work with only a call or two and a little mail. Most corporate management reports stopped crossing his desk.
- He would get "really depressed," go home after three or four hours, and eventually judged that routine "mentally unhealthy," so he stopped going in. "There was nobody really there to miss me."
- One of his five most difficult days was when John Sculley told analysts there was no future role for him, and repeated it at another analyst meeting a week later. Sculley said it to the press, not to Steve directly.
- Steve compares it to being punched in the stomach: you can't breathe until you relax. That was how he felt all summer, and the task was to try to relax.
- His response was long walks in the woods and not talking to many people.

## Owning his rough edges without apologizing for who he is

- Asked whether he contributed to his own downfall, Steve says he is not "a sixty-two-year-old statesman that's traveled around the world all his life."
- He is sure there were situations at twenty-five he could have handled much better knowing what he knows now. He expects to say the same at thirty-five about 1985.
- "I can be very intense in my convictions." He adds: "all in all, I kind of like myself, and I'm not that anxious to change."
- He says the experience is changing him: "I think I am growing from this, and I think I'm learning a lot from it," though he isn't sure yet how or what.
- He repeats, "I'm not bitter."

## Buddhism, monasteries, and staying in America

- On press interest in his Buddhism and vegetarianism, he jokes about descending "into the isms." He says he doesn't eat meat and doesn't go to church every Sunday.
- He had considered going to Japan to sit in a monastery and says he is glad he didn't.
- He admits it will sound "really, really corny," but he feels American and was born here.
- He believes "the fate of the world is in America's hands right now," and says he will live his life here and "do what I can to help."
