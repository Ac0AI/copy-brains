## Opening the first Apple Store: show the whole product line and put help in the store

Steve gave a private tour of the first Apple Store at Tysons Corner Mall in Virginia, recorded for software developers, six days before opening in May 2001. He divides the store into four parts.

- The first quarter has the home section (home and education products) and the Pro section (Pro products).
- Every product Apple makes sits in this first 25 percent, so customers "can see the whole product line."
- The rest of the store is organized around what a person does with the products, not around boxes on shelves.

## Selling the ecosystem: other makers' digital devices that connect to the Mac

Steve decided that carrying Apple's own products "wasn't enough."

- The store stocks six digital camcorders, six digital cameras, six MP3 players and six handheld organizers.
- Customers can buy these devices, and they can also hook them up to the Macs in the store "and take them for a spin."
- The idea is to let people try the whole digital lifestyle, not only the computer.

## The Genius Bar: service and answers inside the store

Steve pitches it as a customer question: "Wouldn't it be great if when you went to buy a computer—or after you bought a computer—if you had any questions, you could ask a Genius?"

- The Genius Bar is staffed by someone who can do service right in the store.
- That person answers questions about the Mac and about the peripherals and software that work with it.
- If the Genius doesn't know the answer, they have a hotline to Apple headquarters in Cupertino, where someone does.
- Steve jokes that he isn't a Genius himself but will stand behind the bar.
- The bar's name was debated. The source notes he once suggested calling it the Geek Bar.

## The in-store theater and the software wall

- The back of the store has a theater with "an exceptional rear projection screen."
- It plays Apple's latest commercials, "some incredible videos," and iTunes visualizations.
- The store also carries over 300 software titles, "from games to the most sophisticated pro applications," with "something for everybody."
- Steve calls this "the most exciting part of the back of the store" and points to the software as a draw for the store.

## Courting developers: shelf space for their software

Steve ends the tour with a direct appeal to the developer audience.

- "We can't wait to feature your software right here, in every single one of our stores."
- "Go write some more for us, and we'll build more shelves for as much as you can write."
- He treats third-party software as part of the product experience and promises to give it real retail space.

## Leadership after 9/11: Steve's email to Apple employees (September 17, 2001)

Steve wrote to the whole company at 7:15 a.m. a week after the attacks. The email is short, plain and human.

- **Giving:** Apple donated one million dollars to the families of the firefighters, police and other emergency personnel who died, through the American Red Cross Disaster Relief Fund. Apple also gave one iBook to each of those families with children that holiday season.
- **Putting people before the event:** Apple cancelled Apple Expo 2001 in Paris (September 26-30). He wrote that customers' and developers' "safety must be our primary concern" and that "having a highly publicized event at this time would be irresponsible," because Apple is "a very visible American company."
- **Safety:** Apple security stayed on heightened alert at all facilities worldwide, and employees were told to report suspicious events or personnel immediately.
- **Honest outlook:** "It's going to take time for the world to return to 'normal,' and some things will never return to the way they were. The next few months may be rocky."
- **Care for the team:** He told employees to take the time they need for their families and to "lean on one another." He closed with: "Together, we will all get through this." He also wrote: "As always, I am very proud of this team."
