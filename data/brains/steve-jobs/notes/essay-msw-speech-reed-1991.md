## Hunger as a school for character

- Steve ties character to adversity: "Character is built not in good times, but in bad times; not in a time of plenty, but in a time of adversity."
- He credits Reed with nurturing that spirit of adversity and says it "does build some character."
- He closes by thanking the school "for teaching me how to be hungry and how to keep that with me my whole life."
- He gave the speech on August 27, 1991, when NeXT computers were not selling well and Pixar had just had a round of layoffs. He was speaking from a hard stretch himself.

## Why the humanities mattered in hindsight

- Steve was forced to attend humanities lectures "it seemed like every day" and studied Shakespeare with Professor Svitavsky.
- At the time he thought they were "meaningless and even somewhat cruel endeavors to be put through."
- With time he came to thank God for them: "It has helped me in everything I've ever done, although I wouldn't have ever guessed it at the time."
- He argues that the value of such study shows up only years later, once "the patina of time takes its toll," and that it can't be judged in the moment.

## Dropping out and dropping in

- He ran out of money after one semester at Reed and dropped out.
- He then dropped in for another year and a half, so he stayed "by choice."
- He never graduated, and says that makes him an unusual but not unique alumnus.
- During that time he stayed on campus without paying and took classes without being a formal student or paying tuition.
- Experiences from Reed "have stayed with me my whole life."

## Living on almost nothing

- He was hungry "all the time." The cafeteria taught him quickly to be a vegetarian.
- He gathered Coke bottles and returned them to the store for money to eat.
- The cheapest food he found was Roman Meal, a cereal. He lived on it for many months.
- Several friends hitchhiked across town on Sundays to the Hare Krishna temple, which fed all comers. After days without eating, they timed their arrival for after the religious practices and just before the food.
- Steve treats this scarcity as formative rather than something to regret.

## Situational ethics from the Hare Krishnas

- After meals he and friends sometimes stayed over, too full to move. The temple woke them at 4 a.m. to gather flowers to honor Krishna.
- The group stole flowers from neighbors' gardens. Neighbors learned to guard their beds, so the group had to range in a wider circle around the temple.
- They also sold incense to local department stores and stole it back, so the stores would buy more.
- Their ethics held that "anything in the service of Krishna was fine."
- Steve says that in interacting with them, "I think I learned more about situational ethics than I ever did on campus."

## Generosity from Jack Dudman

- Jack Dudman, then dean of Reed, was "one of the heroes of my life" while Steve was there.
- Dudman looked the other way while Steve stayed on campus without paying and took classes without paying tuition.
- When Steve was "at the end of my rope," Dudman would walk with him, and Steve would later find a twenty-dollar bill in his tattered coat pocket, with no mention from Jack before, during, or after.
- Steve says: "I learned more about generosity from Jack Dudman and the people here at this school than I learned anywhere else in my life."

## A perspective on age and return

- Steve says people's "spark of self-consciousness" turns on at about fifteen or sixteen.
- Normalizing age to that point, he says the freshmen are two to four years old and he is about twenty.
- He frames his return to Reed as a look back on formative experiences, offered as things that "maybe could be of some value."
