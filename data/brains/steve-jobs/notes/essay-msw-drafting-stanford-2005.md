## Calling in the dark: the people-smarter-than-you and teacher principles

Steve's early drafts of the Stanford address open with advice he considered most important for people leaving college. He says: "try to always surround yourself with people smarter than you. This is how you get smarter and deeper."

- The people can come from any field. He adds that a completely different walk of life "is even better."
- Example: soon after founding Apple, he hired a genius engineer in his 40s who was the best analog engineer on the planet and also a revolutionary socialist. That engineer introduced him to *Das Kapital* and *America's 60 Families*, things he had never been exposed to.
- On experience: at 20 he said "Don't trust anyone over 30." At 50 he sees more value in experience, whether it shows up as IQ points or life experience.
- On mentors: "a teacher is someone who stands with you in the dark and holds their flashlight just long enough for you to find your own flashlight."

## Following curiosity: the calligraphy story and "Stay curious"

Steve's drafts treat curiosity as a core habit, under the note "STAY CURIOUS."

- At Reed he took a calligraphy class that had no practical value. He learned about typography and fonts.
- Ten years later, while working on the Mac, that knowledge shaped the product. Without the class, there would be no multiple fonts and no proportionally spaced fonts on Macs or PCs.
- In an early January 2005 outline he also planned to quote *Autobiography of a Yogi*: "what is miraculous is all around us."
- He noted that he took classes at Stanford at about the age Apple was getting started, with no money.

## Starting Apple: investing in yourself when you have nothing to lose

Steve drafted this story as a reason to take risks early.

- Wozniak had a great job designing handheld calculators at Hewlett Packard. Steve had a fun job designing games at Atari. Friends and family told them they were nuts to leave to build a primitive computer on a PC board that a handful of hobbyist friends might buy for less than it cost to make.
- His reasoning to Woz: "We may fail, but we have no responsibility now, no wives, no kids, no house payments, nothing. If we don't do this now, we never will. We have nothing to lose - the worst we'll get out of this is that we'll have the memories of having gone for it."
- The goal was to experience what Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard did by starting a company.
- Choice of where to put money: rather than invest in better cars, better apartments or bank accounts, they decided "to invest in ourselves."

## You can't plan to meet the people who will change your life

Steve drafted a story about meeting his wife at a Stanford business school talk.

- He almost skipped the evening because he was unwell and had a dinner with important customers at a winery on Page Mill Road. After the talk he noticed a woman in the front row, caught her in the parking lot, and asked her to dinner on Saturday.
- Walking to his car he asked himself: "If this was the last day of my life, would I rather have dinner with the important customers or her?" He raced back and asked "How about dinner tonight?" She said sure, and they married 18 months later.
- He jokes that the customers might have given Apple a few more orders had he shown up, and that Saturday might have worked too.
- Lesson: "You can't plan to meet the people who will change your life. It just happens. Maybe its random, maybe its fate." What matters is to "recognize it when it happens, and have the courage and clarity of mind to grab onto it."

## Remembering you'll die soon as a tool for big decisions

Steve's draft for the death story ("Story #3") explains why mortality clarifies choices.

- "The most important thing I've ever encountered to help me make the big choices is to remember that I'll be dead soon." Expectations, standards and the restrictions of others and society "mean nothing in the end," so there is nothing to lose by following your heart and intuition, even if you embarrass yourself or fail in others' eyes.
- At 17 he read a quote like "If you live each day as if it was your last, someday you'll most certainly be right." Since then he has asked the mirror each morning: "If today was the last day of my life, would I want to do what I am about to do today?" When the answer is "NO" too many days in a row, he knows to change something.
- He plainly says "we are all going to die" as the third piece of advice in an early draft.

## Treat your life as a story in the making

A second framing in the same draft helps with major decisions.

- Imagine yourself as an old person looking back. Your life is "your story, with its highs and lows, its heros and villains, its forks in the road that mean everything."
- Hard to see at 22, but useful: when choosing between a prestigious, well-paid job and an offbeat job with no future that "makes your heart sing," imagine looking back in 50 years and "you'll know what path is yours."
- He says you will "intuitively know if something is part of your story or not."
- At 50 he describes his own story as "entering its third act," and says that following his gut, heart and intuition has paid off.

## Habits, health and the closing line: "Stay hungry. Stay foolish."

Steve's January 2005 outline and final drafts include practical and closing ideas.

- Habits are powerful. Over 20 years (now to age 42) he suggests meditating 20 minutes a day, and he works out that walking to work 30 minutes each way, five days a week, adds up to over a year of time across 20 years.
- "You are what you eat": he lists cows, chickens, milk, soda and coffee, and considers fasting one day a week.
- Quote from Gandhi in the outline: become the change you want to see in the world. He decided to focus on the graduates' inner world and how they approach life.
- Ending: the *Whole Earth Catalog*, created in Menlo Park near Stanford, put out a final issue in 1974 when Steve was the graduates' age. Its back cover showed an early-morning country road, the kind you might hitchhike on, with the words "Stay hungry. Stay foolish." He closes: "I have always wished that for myself. And I wish that for you."
