## Apple's purpose: making high technology easy for "mere mortals"

- Steve's private note (Oct 29, 2000) defines Apple as "the world's premier company at building high technology products that are easy to learn and use by mere mortals."
- The motive is love for what personal computers can do to "enhance and enrich the lives of regular people", not just spreadsheets and databases but creative uses.
- Apple "marries state of the art technology with Apple's legendary ease-of-use."
- He keeps rewording the idea. The final version: Apple is the premier company at making the exploding world of high technology easy to learn and use, so mere mortals can enrich their lives with it.
- He calls Apple "the world's premier bridge builder between mere mortals and the exploding world of high technology."
- Apple has set the standard for easy-to-use systems and software for over 20 years.

## Apple as a creatively driven company

- Steve calls Apple "a creatively driven company in everything we do": breakthrough features, operating systems, culturally leading product design, and advertising.
- His proof points: "we INVENTED the personal computer, spawned desktop publishing and are now bringing desktop movies to millions."
- He notes engineers were working on more breakthroughs due in 2001.
- Creativity is the identity, and technology is the means.

## Demystified technology and the purpose of the stores

- He writes that demystified technology "will have a much greater impact than any other thing we can do."
- Stores should be treated "as a mecca for understanding technology and making all of the digits a part of your life."
- Target audiences he lists:
  - digital music
  - digital photography
  - people who have migrated to broadband
  - families who want to build a home network
- The stores exist to teach and make technology approachable, not just to sell boxes.

## A building should capture the company's soul

- Opening Pixar's Emeryville headquarters (Nov 2000), Steve says a rented building "defines who the landlord is", but designing your own forces the question "Who are we?"
- The goal was to "capture some of the soul of the company in the building and reflect who we are."
- He says a building's purpose is "not just to be a cool building or to impress anyone on the outside. It is to house and inspire the most talented group of filmmakers in the world."
- He notes he had seen every square millimeter of the building except people in it, and that "it makes all the difference."

## Designing a building the way Pixar makes a film

- Process: "First, a treatment; throw it away. Then another; throw it away. And finally, one that is worth developing", followed by "zillions and zillions of iterations, model after model after model, detail after detail."
- "We drove our architects crazy." Development took three years, like a film.
- Steve treats iteration and rejecting early ideas as normal for any creative work.

## Handmade craft and the people who build it

- The building was deliberately handmade:
  - Steel was hand-joined on site and bolted, not welded. The connection drawings alone ran 2,500 pages.
  - Every brick was custom hand-made with an old process and hand-laid.
  - Every floorboard was hand-laid and sanded on site.
- The workers found "this crazy owner was letting them build a building like they always had dreamed of," and brought their families on weekends to see their craftwork.
- He compares this to how Pixar's people feel about their films.
- Steve credits the architects, facilities crew and IS crew for the result.

## Space that forces collaboration

- Pixar moved from four buildings to one, after eleven years in Point Richmond. Steve expected people to spend the rest of their professional lives there.
- The design is urban, with a "Town Square" as "a place for you to meet people you have never met before, a place for us to interact and collaborate, which is really the basis of our success."
- People were seeing coworkers for the first time on move-in day.
- A park is included so people can get away when they need to.

## Investing in talent, paid for in cash

- The land was an abandoned Del Monte Fruit Cocktail factory, bought four years earlier. Steve says Pixar "saw the land underneath it that no one else saw." It is 16 acres, a rare large Bay Area parcel.
- Land, building and grounds cost "just shy of $100 million", about the cost of making one film, and roughly one year of salary and benefits for the whole studio.
- It was paid in cash, with no debt, from profits of A Bug's Life, and over $200 million remained in the bank.
- His rationale: "the best place in the world to support the best talent in the world making the best animated features in the world."
- He hands the building to the staff as "the creative owners": "go make some history here!"
