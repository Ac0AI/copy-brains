## Back to basics: great products, great marketing, great distribution

- Steve says that when he returned (about eight to ten weeks earlier), his aim was not "highfalutin": "We're trying to get back to the basics of great products, great marketing, and great distribution."
- His view: Apple "has pockets of greatness but in some ways has drifted away from doing the basics really well."
- The new "Think Different" ad was finished at three o'clock the night before he showed it to employees.

## Cutting the product line by 70 percent

- Steve and the team reviewed the product road map for the next few years and concluded: "A lot of this doesn't make sense, and it's way too much stuff, and there's not enough focus."
- They cancelled 70 percent of the road map.
- He could not figure out the product line himself after a few weeks ("What is this model? How does this fit?"), and customers could not figure it out either.
- Result he promises: a product line that is "much simpler" and "much better", with more focus on the remaining 30 percent, the "gems", plus a few new things that take Apple in new directions.
- Engineers whose projects were cancelled left the meeting "three feet off the ground with excitement" because they finally understood where the company was going and liked the strategy.

## Taking inventory out of the pipeline

- Apple held two to three months of inventory in its manufacturing supplier pipeline and about the same again in its distribution channel, forcing guesses four, five or six months ahead about what customers want.
- Steve's verdict: "We're not smart enough to do that. I don't think Einstein's smart enough to do that."
- Fix: get simple and take inventory out of both pipelines, so the customer tells Apple what they want and Apple can "respond to it super fast."
- Ambition: catch up with the best in distribution, then break new ground in distribution and manufacturing within the coming months.

## Marketing is about values, not specs

- "To me, marketing is about values." The world is complicated and noisy, and no company gets people to remember much about it, so a company must be very clear about the one thing it wants them to know.
- Apple is one of "half-a-dozen best brands in the whole world", alongside Nike, Disney, Coke and Sony, but even a great brand "needs investment and caring if it's going to retain its relevance and vitality." Apple's had suffered neglect for a few years.
- Do not talk about "speeds and feeds", MIPS and megahertz, or why Apple is better than Windows.
- Apple spent a fortune on advertising: "You'd never know it."

## Lessons from Got Milk? and Nike

- The dairy industry spent twenty years telling people milk was good for them, and sales fell. Steve says that claim is "a lie" but they tried anyway.
- "Got Milk?" reversed the trend, and it "doesn't even talk about the product"; the focus is on the absence of the product.
- Nike is "one of the greatest jobs of marketing that the universe has ever seen". It sells a commodity (shoes), yet people feel something beyond a shoe company.
- Its ads never discuss air soles or why they beat Reebok's. Instead Nike honors great athletes and great athletics: "That's who they are. That's what they are about."

## How the Think Different campaign came about

- On arrival, Apple had just fired its agency and was running a competition among twenty-three agencies that would have picked a winner "four years from now". Steve blew it up and hired Chiat/Day, whom he had worked with before.
- That earlier work included "1984", voted the best ad ever made by advertising professionals.
- Work started about eight weeks before the speech. The question: customers want to know who Apple is, what it stands for and where it fits in the world.
- The campaign is Apple's first brand-marketing campaign in several years.

## Apple's core value: people with passion can change the world

- Apple is not about "making boxes for people to get their jobs done", though it does that better than almost anybody.
- Core value: "We believe that people with passion can change the world for the better." Those crazy enough to think they can change the world "are the ones that actually do."
- Apple has worked with developers, customers and employees who have done it, in big and small ways.
- Market, products, distribution and manufacturing have all changed, "but values and core values: those things shouldn't change."
- "Think Different" honors people who changed the world, living or not; the ones who are gone "would have used a Mac" if they had used a computer. Steve: "It touches the soul of this company."
- The ad text he screens: "Here's to the crazy ones. The misfits. The rebels. The troublemakers. The round pegs in the square holes... About the only thing you can't do is ignore them. Because they change things. They push the human race forward... the people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world are the ones who do."
