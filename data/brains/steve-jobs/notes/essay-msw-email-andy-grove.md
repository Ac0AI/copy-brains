## Reciprocity over transactional pricing: Steve's 180-degree reversal with Andy Grove (1995)

- Pixar built its 3D graphics "secrets" through "significant investment over ten years or more." Steve believed they could let Intel's general-purpose processors render 3D graphics well enough to beat dedicated gaming machines (Sega, Nintendo, Playstation) and dramatically enlarge the PC's consumer market share.
- His initial position was commercial: disclosing the "correct" way to do high-quality, high-performance graphics would cost Pixar its competitive advantage, "with no work on their part" from competitors, "hence, the need for compensation." He asked Intel: "What does Intel propose to give Pixar for disclosing and licensing its secrets to Intel?"
- He argued that anyone about to "make hundreds of millions of something" should be willing to pay "for the best advice money could buy."
- After an Intel engineer replied that Intel had never paid for ideas, Steve fired back that this approach "has not served you well in the past, as evidenced by your poor graphics architectures and performance." He privately called the engineer's approach "extremely arrogant."

## How Andy Grove changed his mind

- Grove sided firmly with his engineer. The engineer had taken Steve's offer to help seriously and "rounded up the best technical people" before Steve "introduced a brand new element into the discussion: money."
- Grove's argument was from precedent and friendship: he had offered Steve free advice, from porting NextStep to the 486 to presenting on repositioning NextStep to Steve's staff, and "it never entered my mind to charge for it."
- His principle: "that's what friendly companies (and friends) do for each other. In the long run, these things balance out."
- He warned that refusing would leave Intel "worse off... and so will the industry."

## Steve's reversal

- Steve answered within days: "I have many faults, but one of them is not ingratitude. And, I do agree with you that 'In the long run, these things balance out.'"
- He declared "I have changed my position 180 degrees" and committed to freely help the engineer make Intel's processors "much better for 3D graphics."
- He arranged for the Pixar technical team to meet as soon as they could be "freed up from the film," so the film still came first.
- He thanked Grove for "the clearer perspective."

## Takeaways on leadership and mindset

- Steve could hold a hard, even combative line on the value of Pixar's work, then reverse completely when a mentor reframed the relationship.
- He weighed gratitude and past help (Grove had helped NeXT) against short-term deal logic, and the mentor's reminder was enough to change course.
- He accepted correction from someone he respected, quickly and without face-saving, and acknowledged the new perspective in writing.
- Context: Steve and Grove were close enough that Steve could write candidly ("this isn't a sales pitch; I just wanted you to know what I thought, as always") and Grove could answer bluntly in return.
