## Why Steve returned to Apple, and how he reset it

Steve sold NeXT to Apple only because a NeXT colleague pushed past his objections. He says "when you hire the right people, they don't always listen to you in key moments in time." The colleague raised the deal twice after Steve dismissed it, and Steve finally agreed to talk. Afterward he stayed on as a consultant to help the NeXT team avoid being "totally crushed" by Apple's weaker management.

- Steve says the NeXT management team was "actually quite a bit better" than the one Apple had.
- He was nearly out of the picture until an Apple board member he had never met asked for his honest view of the CEO. He decided to tell the truth because Apple was a company he had started and loved, even though it might cost him.
- Months later the director called to say the board would dismiss the CEO and wanted Steve to run the company.

## How Steve decided to come back (and the one-line test that settled it)

- Steve first refused. He was CEO of Pixar, a public company, and felt he could not desert its employees and shareholders.
- The board then asked him to be interim leader while they found a new CEO. He was still unsure.
- He called a friend at another company to talk it through. After about four minutes the friend interrupted: "Steve, I don't give a shit about Apple. Why are you telling me all this?"
- Steve hung up and realized: "I do give a shit about Apple." That crystallized the decision.
- He returned as interim CEO, terrified that Pixar's people would feel deserted, and planned to stay about ninety days.

## Acting like the permanent CEO of a company in intensive care

- The CEO candidates Apple was attracting were "not so good." Steve almost hired one and backed out at the last minute: "I can't do this to these guys."
- Ninety more days to find someone turned into a year.
- He decided up front to act as if he were the permanent CEO, because "they didn't need a caretaker."
- His description of Apple then: "in intensive care," about ninety days from bankruptcy.
- His view on CEOs: "you need a license to drive a car, but you don't need a license to be the CEO of a company. And maybe you should need one."

## August 1997: the first decisive steps toward renewal

In an August 12, 1997 email, "A More Entrepreneurial Apple," Steve (not yet CEO) framed renewal as a journey. The first four weeks had brought:

- A new Board of Directors.
- A new product strategy and product roadmap.
- A decision to focus on two market segments: education and creative content.
- A new advertising agency.
- A détente and working partnership with Microsoft, announced at MacWorld the previous week.

The next steps aimed to take Apple "back to its roots as a more egalitarian, entrepreneurial company."

## Stock options as egalitarian ownership

- Stock options became the primary form of "beyond-salary" compensation. Steve calls them egalitarian: when anyone's stock goes up $1 per share, everyone's does.
- The aim was for employees to be rewarded as public shareholders are, through stock appreciation, and to have a true stake in the company's future.
- The Executive Team gave up its current and future cash bonus plans in exchange for more options, to "lead the way."
- All existing options were repriced to $13.25 on July 11. On August 5 the board approved new grants of six million shares at $19.75.
- Apple had granted options on over 10 million shares since the start of the calendar year. Employees held options on over 20 million shares, more than 16% of outstanding shares. Steve calls that very high for a company of Apple's size and comparable to many Valley start-ups.
- His framing: public shareholders and employee option holders all benefit "in harmony" as Apple's fortunes are restored.

## One severance plan, one travel policy, no sabbaticals

- **Severance:** a single plan for all employees replaced the separate executive plan. It gives a 60-day notice period with full pay and benefits, plus one additional week of severance pay per full year of service. For example, three to four years of service means three weeks of severance.
- **Sabbaticals:** discontinued at the end of the fiscal year because "Apple needs all hands on deck." Employees who earned one by September 26, 1997 could take it during fiscal 1998.
- **Travel:** limited to essential trips. The "egalitarian" policy is coach for everyone on trips under 10 hours and business class for everyone on trips of 10 hours or more. Employees may upgrade with personal funds or mileage, and San Francisco to Tokyo flights qualify for business class.

## Consolidating facilities for "beehive" effects

- Steve moved as many employees as possible onto the R&D Campus.
- The goal was the "beehive" effect: faster communication paths and more unplanned interactions between groups.
- The R&D Campus was renamed the Apple Campus.
- He signed the email "Steve and the Executive Team," presenting the changes as a shared effort.
