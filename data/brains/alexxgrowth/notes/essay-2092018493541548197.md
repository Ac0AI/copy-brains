## Starting from a proven format before scaling a campaign

Alex says founders make a common mistake: they launch a clipping campaign before they have a working format. The app in this case study already had a great product and a tested winning format, and scaled by amplifying it with clippers.

- "You can't skip this."
- Clippers need an idea of which formats will do well and which won't.
- Scaling something that already works is what let the campaign "go crazy".
- Alex's rule: "find your format before you scale it."

## The case study results: 651M views for $0.27 effective CPM

An app on Content Rewards scaled to $200k MRR in 3 months. Alex gives these numbers:

- 651 million views generated.
- $173K paid out to creators ($173,365 spent in total).
- Over $1,000,000 generated in the last 90 days.
- Effective CPM of $0.27 across all seven campaigns.
- For comparison, Meta charges $8-14 per thousand impressions.

He argues "no other marketing technique will ever generate a result as good as this". He says many app founders still run Meta at very high CPMs or pay influencers flat fees and get poor results.

## Running several campaigns at once to learn faster

The app ran seven campaigns instead of opening one and hoping it pops off.

- Each campaign had different rates, caps and formats. Some paid a $1 CPM and some a $0.50 CPM.
- Each campaign tested a different variable.
- Running them together showed which formats worked in weeks instead of months.
- The biggest campaign did $77,225 in payouts and 187.5 million views.
- The smallest did $1,546 in payouts.
- Both were useful: the winner showed what to scale, the flop showed what to stop doing.

Alex's rule: run multiple campaigns at the same time and change certain settings in each, so you learn what does best while spending.

## How the brief changed approval rate and CPM

The first campaign brief was "$1 per 1,000 views to make videos about the app". It had no strict rules. It generated 212 million views with an 89.9% approval rate.

A later brief was "submit any video where you talk about a TikTok feature, then mention the app at the end".

- It had the highest approval rate of all campaigns, 98.7%.
- It pulled 169.2M views with a very strong engagement rate.
- It ran at a $0.50 CPM, half the earlier campaign's rate.

Alex's explanation: a video that starts with something interesting pulls the viewer in and keeps them watching to the end. A video that opens with the product is more boring and tends to generate fewer views and conversions. His takeaway is to ask creators to mention your app at the end.

## Small groups of serious creators beat large groups of casual ones

Alex says big campaigns don't necessarily have thousands of posters.

- The campaign with 169 million views had only 33 creators.
- The campaign with 67.3M views had only 42 creators.
- One creator posted 366 times and earned $12,084. Another posted 387 times.

He concludes that "a small group of serious creators is SO much better than a big group of less serious ones".

## Building dedicated creators instead of renting them

Alex says creators who work nonstop and make consistently high-quality content tend to be loyal to the platform they already work for, so you have to make your own. His three-part approach:

1. **Make a very thorough course.** Guide creators from A to Z and teach everything you know about clipping and what works for your product.
2. **Host regular live calls.** Teach, do page reviews and bring in a guest speaker if you can. The more valuable the calls, the more creators learn.
3. **Run private 1-1 mentoring.** Set up a Discord server and hire mentors who know what they're doing. Select 100-200 clippers and have coaches guide them until they're experts.

## Payout caps turn viral reach into free distribution

Alex treats the payout cap as a main lever on return.

- On a $1 CPM with a $500 cap, a creator is capped at 500K views. Everything past that costs nothing.
- He says this gets "insanely effective once your campaign goes big".
- The carousel campaign paid out $15,151 for 67.3M views, an effective CPM of $0.22.
- The story format campaign paid out $33,751 for 169.2M views, an effective CPM of about $0.199.
- Across all seven campaigns the final CPM was $0.27.

His rule: set a good cap, because every view past it is free reach and a decent cap gives a very good ROI.

## Tightening campaign settings with each round

The campaigns improved as the team learned, and they spent "wayy better" than before.

- Minimum views per post (MVR) went from 300 in the first campaign to 500 in the next.
- The first few campaigns had no submission cap. Towards the end they capped it at 5 per user.
- Approval rates rose from 89.9% to 95.3% to 96.5% to 98.7% from first to last campaign.
- Each campaign taught them how to set MVR, the best cap and which formats work.

Alex's rule: keep adjusting the MVR, the cap and other settings as you go.
