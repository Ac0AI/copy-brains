## Choosing campaigns by niche and CPM, and why Alex starts with high CPM

Alex's pick for zero money, zero followers, zero reputation is content clipping. He spent 2 years on dropshipping, SMMA and freelancing first, and says none of it worked from zero the way clipping does.

- Campaign selection is "the highest-leverage decision you make as a clipper", and most beginners overlook it.
- Niches he lists, in the order he'd try them:
  - Streamer clipping (Kai Cenat, Drewski, Clavicular): $0.8-$1 per 1k views.
  - Event clipping (gatherings, boxing matches, weddings): $2-$5 per 1k views.
  - Crypto (fintech, memecoins, games): $5 per 1k views.
- Day 1 plan: pick 2-3 campaigns in the highest-CPM niche available, probably crypto or fintech, maybe betting if a strong campaign is running.
- He knows people "absolutely PRINTING" on the lower-CPM campaigns, because they like the niche (streaming, comedy, entertainment) and are simply better at it.
- He would spend a few hours trying every niche until he finds what he's genuinely good at and enjoys clipping.
- Day 1 setup takes about 30 minutes: sign up on Content Rewards (about 10 minutes, connect socials, get verified) and download CapCut (free), the only tool he needs.

## Why clipping works: the shift away from paid ads

- Brands used to put $10M, $20M, $100M+ a year into Meta, Google and YouTube ads. Alex says that model is breaking: ad costs are up 40%+ year over year, and Gen Z skips anything that looks sponsored in under a second.
- Brands instead pay regular people to post organic-looking content from their own accounts at about $0.30 CPM, which he calls 10-30x cheaper than ads. The content gets watched because it looks like a real person sharing something real.
- How it works: the brand uploads raw footage and a campaign blueprint to a platform. The clipper edits a short clip with a strong hook and posts it from their own account. The brand gets organic reach, and the clipper is paid by views.
- The clipper doesn't film, doesn't need an audience and needs no experience. The brand supplies the footage, direction and angles that work.
- A 0-follower account can get hundreds of thousands of views because short-form algorithms "care whether people watch past the first 3 seconds", not who you are.
- Pattern he has seen in hundreds of clippers (students, laid-off people, people in countries with no access to traditional job markets): a slow start, a sharp inflection around day 30-45, then compounding.

## Dedicated accounts per niche

- Alex creates a fresh account for each niche rather than using a personal account.
- Reasoning: posting memes, personal content and clips from 3 niches on one account confuses the audience graph, so the algorithm doesn't know who to show content to and performance is inconsistent.
- Dedicated accounts give "clean signal", which means better distribution from day 1. He calls this a detail that separates serious clippers from hobbyists.

## Week 1: the ugly phase, and the 30-day curve

- Week 1 goal is not money, it's two things: build the editing muscle and wake up the algorithm.
- Earnings might be about $10 for the whole week (he later cites about $60). Clips take too long and hooks feel awkward.
- Speed compounds with reps: clip 1 may take 20 minutes, clip 15 about 10, clip 30 under 6.
- New accounts need consistent activity before they get real distribution, so he posts 3-5 clips a day even if they're mediocre.
- Week 1 routine:
  - Pull raw footage from his 2 campaigns.
  - Study the campaign blueprints (exact direction on what to make, which hooks and angles work).
  - Watch top-performing clips in the niche on TikTok and Reels, studying hooks: "what makes you stop scrolling?"
  - Make 3-5 clips a day and post them anyway.
- He says people who fail almost always fail here, judging everything on 7 days of data, while the person who pushes through is about 3 weeks from a first real payday.
- Days 15-30: after 100+ clips he reads his Content Rewards dashboard (which hooks performed, which posts paid, which campaigns have the best CPMs) instead of guessing. Hook instinct built on real reps is "worth more than any course or guide".
- Account velocity: platforms reward accounts that show up daily. Posts 1-5 on a new account average about 800 views; posts 50+ average 23,000+, for the same quality of content.
- Earnings trajectory he projects: about $60 in week 1, $200-$600 across weeks 2-3, $1,000-$3,000 by day 30, depending on niche and volume.

## The batch editing workflow (days 8-14 onward)

Alex calls batch editing the unlock that separates $500/mo creators from $10k+/mo creators. Total is about 2 hours a day for 10+ unique clips, and each clip works for the next 48-72 hours.

- 10 minutes: pull raw assets from active campaigns.
- 15 minutes: write 5 hook variations per campaign in a notes app before opening the editor. He calls this the most important 15 minutes of the day because "the hook determines 90% of a clip's performance".
- 60-75 minutes: edit the first clip for campaign 1, then duplicate the project, swap the hook and export. Each variation after the first takes 3-4 minutes because the body stays the same.
- 10 minutes: schedule everything.
- Posting windows: early morning (6-7am EST) and late night (9-11pm EST). He cites data across 120,000+ clips on his platform: these windows average 84k and 91k views versus 22k in the midday dead zone.
- By end of week 2 he expects 60-80 clips posted and a few clips reaching 5k-15k views.

## Compounding: trust score, premium campaigns and CPM stacking (days 30-60)

- Trust score on Content Rewards rises with each completed campaign. Higher trust unlocks premium campaigns paying 2-3x base CPM, so the same clips earn more per view.
- CPM stacking: running 2-3 campaigns in the same niche from one account. He says it's not recommended and not allowed in most campaigns, but worth experimenting with. Logic: an account that has posted crypto for 30 days has a tuned audience graph, so a second crypto campaign benefits from that graph-building.
- By then the batch routine is locked in at 2 hours each morning, producing 12-15 clips a day that perform better than month-1 clips.
- Projected earnings at day 60: $5,000-$10,000/mo.

## Scaling with editors, and what clipping builds (days 60-90)

- Up to day 60 earnings are tied to his own output. Around day 60-75 he'd hire 1-2 editors (local or found online), teach them his exact batch workflow, give them campaign access, review their clips and post them.
- Pay: 10-30% of the revenue their clips generate. His output doubles or triples without more of his time.
- Projected earnings at day 90 with 1-2 editors: $10,000-$20,000/mo.
- Skills built along the way: hook writing, video editing, content distribution, algorithmic understanding, direct response marketing, campaign analytics. He says these are what brands and startups are hiring for, learned while getting paid.
- Also built: connections with brands and creators, a reputation (trust score and a portfolio of executed campaigns), and real cash profit rather than revenue that goes to overhead or loans.
- Clipping can be a destination or a launchpad: start a business with the marketing skills, launch your own brand and promote it for free with clipping, build an agency, or invest. He himself started a business making this easier for brands and creators.
- His closing advice: skip the "lock in leap of faith" and just sign up, pick a campaign and make the first clip (about 20 minutes, probably won't go viral). Then make the second, the tenth and the 69th.
