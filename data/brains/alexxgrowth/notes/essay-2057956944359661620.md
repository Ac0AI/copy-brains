## How Alex scaled an app to $300k MRR with 596M views on Content Rewards

- **Result (Jan to May 2026):** An app that started at low-to-mid five figures MRR reached $300k+ MRR and about $700k in revenue.
- **Spend and reach:** $162,805 in creator payouts bought 596M views.
- **Volume:** The campaigns drew 38,882 submissions, of which 24,773 clips were approved.
- **Network:** Alex says Content Rewards has a network of 696k creators and clippers.
- **Comparison:** He says the same spend-to-revenue ratio "would be impossible with Meta or any other type of marketing."
- **Ongoing spend:** The brand's marketing spend was about $80-90k per month.
- **Process, not content:** "you don't need to copy their exact content. you need to copy their process."
- **Platform handling:** Content Rewards handles creator discovery, joining, submissions, view verification, content review and payments through Whop. The founders never touched an invoice or a spreadsheet.

## Find viral formats before scaling spend

- The app was not starting from zero. It already had a product users loved.
- The founders found their own viral formats by testing with clippers, creators and influencers.
- That testing took them to 10-20k MRR organically.
- A viable format has three traits: proven viral, easy to replicate, easy to scale.
- Alex says they spent about a month on format research: "the research is what makes the scale work."
- His advice to brands is to test the formats yourself, or hire a creator or clipper on a retainer to find proven viral formats.
- After formats were proven, the campaigns were distributed to tens of thousands of creators across Instagram, TikTok and YouTube.
- Alex recommends running all three platforms from the start. Each has a different audience and algorithm, so one budget gives compounding reach and shows quickly where the format performs best.

## The content formula: the content is the hook, the app is the payoff

The content needs no production budget, studio or spokesperson. The recipe:
- Take trending social content (slideshows and videos).
- Use mostly stock images and videos from Pinterest and stock video sites.
- Alter them with AI so they look more original.
- Add a text overlay, then show the app.
- Always show the app through a video or picture overlay.
- Always use trending sounds. Alex says people underrate this and "it helps a lot."

Why it works, in his words:
- People watch the content, not an ad.
- The algorithm doesn't suppress it because it doesn't look like an ad.
- The product is seen by every viewer without a "sponsored" label.
- Rule of thumb: "if your app is the entire video, nobody watches. if it shows up as the punchline to something people were already engaged with, it converts without ever feeling like an ad."

## The cap mechanic: why blended CPM keeps falling

Alex's central economics argument is that a per-post payout cap turns viral outliers into free reach.

- **Base rate:** The campaign CPM was $0.32 per 1k views (an average of $0.31-0.32 overall).
- **Benchmarks:** He says Meta averages $8-14 per 1k views, and organic influencer deals rarely go below $2 CPM once fees and flopped posts are counted.
- **Cap:** Each post had a payout range of $500 to $600.
- **Break-even:** At $0.32 CPM a creator hits the $600 max at about 1.875M views. The brand pays nothing for views beyond that.

Top clips:

| Clip | Views | Payout | Effective CPM |
|---|---|---|---|
| #1 | 53,317,845 | $500 | $0.009 per 1k |
| #2 | 18,281,051 | $600 | $0.033 per 1k |
| #3 | 13,311,281 | $600 | n/a |

- Across all 596M views and $162,805, the blended CPM landed at $0.31 and was still dropping.
- One campaign produced 450M views on only $100k of spend.
- His summary: "the bigger the campaign gets, the cheaper every view becomes."
- Takeaway: set the max payout high enough to motivate creators, and treat every clip that blows past the cap as free reach, which is "where the real ROI compounds."

## The creator operating system behind 24,000 consistent clips

Alex says finding a format is easy compared with getting thousands of creators to execute it consistently. This brand's brief included the following, which he says separated it from about 90% of campaigns on Content Rewards:

- **Creator agreement and confidentiality upfront.** Every creator signed before seeing materials. It filters out low-effort creators and protects the format from competitors. "most brands skip this entirely. it's a costly mistake."
- **Module-by-module onboarding.** Not a PDF: a full video playbook covering account creation, warm-up, production workflow, account management rules, dos and don'ts, and payment info. It keeps quality consistent at 24,000 clips.
- **Weekly-updated content bank.** A library of angles, hooks, prompts and templates, so creators see the newest viral formats. "the brief tells creators what to make. the content bank makes it frictionless to actually make it."
- **Influencer version of the app.** A website where creators could build personalized overlays and videos of the app and download them for their content. Alex calls this probably the biggest differentiator from other app campaigns.
- **AI content training.** Videos teaching creators to use AI for app content, plus a prompt bank.
- **Account warm-up targeting.** Creators consumed content from the brand's exact demographic before posting, so the algorithm learned who the account was for and showed clips to the right audience.
- **Leaderboard bonus on top of CPM.** Top creators were rewarded by actual app downloads via link-in-bio. Alex's logic: "views are great. installs are better." It created a second incentive layer driving reach and conversion at once.

His rule: "treat creator onboarding like a product." The brief is the bare minimum; the system produces consistent clips.

## Scaling timeline

- **January-February:** Small test campaigns with $1k-$5k budgets, validating which formats translated into the Content Rewards ecosystem.
- **March:** First large campaigns went live and momentum built.
- **April:** The spike month. Peak days hit 28M views in 24 hours, and "the system is fully locked in."
- **May:** Sustained 15-22M views per day and still running.
- **Why margins held:** As an app there is no inventory or fulfillment, so the margin on the $700k looks nothing like a traditional business.
- **His closing claim:** "running campaigns on Content Rewards in 2026 is like running Facebook ads in 2018."
