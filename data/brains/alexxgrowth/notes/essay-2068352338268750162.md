## Build the app around a clippable "aha moment"

Alex says every app that blew up through Content Rewards was built around virality. It had a moment that is easy to understand in a slideshow or screen recording: you do something, and the app produces a result.

- Examples he gives: Cal AI (photo of food, shows calories), a fishing app (photo of a catch, says it is "top 1% rarest fish"), a room app (photo of your room, designs a better layout).
- Test for success: ask "how likely are people to pay to know this result/get this outcome". That answer predicts how well the app will do.
- Lookmaxing apps went viral because rating people is controversial, and everyone wanted to know their own rating, which drove high conversion.
- Context he cites: one app that joined Content Rewards in February passed 830 million views by mid-June. It did close to 400 million views in a month, on about $250,000 total spend across all its campaigns. He has seen apps in different niches reach six-figure MRR in 2-3 months.

## Find viral formats, then keep the content simple

Alex's process for finding formats (from a May 30 post he links): brainstorm about 50 niche keywords with ChatGPT, then log findings in a Notion table with tabs for link, sound, hook, format, visuals and editing notes.

Once a format is producing a couple of million views a month, he looks for scalability through clipping:
- The best-performing videos and slideshows look like they took 30 seconds to 5 minutes to make.
- Simple content looks less ad-like and blends into the algorithm, so it reads as a natural recommendation and converts better.
- Easy content means more clippers can post it, more clippers can be attracted, and there are more chances to go viral. "2 slide slideshows often have higher conversion rates than your 1-2 min long UGC video."
- He recommends hiring a few creators and clippers to test angles and find 10 to 20 easily replicable viral formats, then turning them into a content bank.

## Build the brief like a course

Alex calls the brief the most important part of a campaign. To get thousands of videos with a consistent message, it must be "practically a course", not a requirements list ("nobody follows that at mass scale"). Components:
- **Content bank:** for each format, give a name, example posts, the music to use (viral songs tend to boost videos), context on what happens in the video or slides, caption ideas, and AI prompts if used. Give clippers as much as possible.
- **Content creation course:** Notion modules covering A to Z: campaign details, account setup (profile picture, bio), warming up the account, creating the content (with videos and all links), managing content to push the app, and submitting posts to the campaign. He says his template cuts setup time by 50%.
- **Do's and don'ts page:** examples of correct posts plus deliberately wrong ones (captions placed too high, wrong template or logo, wrong editing style).

## Add a creator contract and a no-install web version

**Contract.** Most founders skip it. Draft it with ChatGPT, have creators sign it (e.g. on Tally) before posting, and put it at the top of the brief. Clauses he suggests:
- Right to reuse the content in ads later.
- A minimum time the content must stay up, with payment at risk if deleted earlier.
- CPM and max pay terms stated clearly.
- Account ownership if the account takes off.
- No working for competing apps.

It prevents disputes and legal headaches once content blows up.

**Influencer web version.** He calls this underrated: creators should not need to download the app or sign up. Build a web version where they enter their own inputs and get a customized output (screenshot, video, the aha moment) to overlay on their videos and slideshows. The goal is to make hundreds or thousands of versions of the aha moment easy to produce.

## Run the creator program: Discord, bonuses, and refreshing the machine

**Two fulfillment routes on Content Rewards** (he is its CMO, so this is a promotion of his company; the platform claims over 2 million clippers and UGC creators):
- Agency route: verified agencies do outreach, find creators, manage the campaign, and can even write the brief.
- Self-fulfill: run everything yourself and recruit extra creators beyond those the platform brings.

**Train creators on Discord:**
- Bi-weekly calls to answer questions.
- Personalized tickets to coach individuals.
- A resources category.
- A top-performing category showing best videos and creators posting payouts.
- A leaderboard channel with the weekly top earner or view accumulator. Seeing others win motivates creators.

**Milestone bonuses on top of CPM.** Example: $100 for every 100 downloads from a creator's custom affiliate link. He says creators sometimes grind harder for a milestone than for passive CPM, because it feels like a target they can win. Design it so top creators always earn more and stay for months.

**Keep refreshing.** To sustain 300-400 million views a month, keep adding new angles, hooks and content bank entries, and keep helping creators find new formats. Repurposing proven formats in German, French and Spanish opens new markets, and they act as practically new formats.

## Economics: organic CPM campaigns vs ads

Alex's pitch for the "new model" of organic campaigns:
- CPMs on Content Rewards can be as low as $0.16 to $0.30, with the highest usually $1 or less. He contrasts this with traditional CPMs of $20 to $30.
- A max payout cap limits cost on any single viral video to about $300 to $600, or whatever cap is set.
- You pay only for videos that actually generate views, not for influencer posts that do not convert.
- He says apps 10xed revenue in a month at a fraction of what ads would have cost.
