## From 5-Euro Freelancing to a 25,000-Member Community

Romàn Czerny started out as a freelancer on 5-euro platforms while studying engineering. He sold services there, then PDFs in an automated way, before telling himself "I'm selling my time."

- He spent months observing what buyers wanted and what could be recurring without human intervention.
- He ended up among the platform's top sellers with well-crafted PDFs.
- He then offered this method to clients, and later launched Ecom French Touch with Maxime.
- For him, betting on a recognized skill starts with freelancing or "done for you." Selling your time at the beginning is normal: "you have to start somewhere."

## Why Sell Low-Priced Products With High Recurrence

Romàn thinks that five years ago, he could have sold his content for 500 to 1000 euros. Today, the market has changed.

- Buyers of training courses got "slaughtered" by expensive, bad products, back when "you could sell any PDF for 500 bucks." They lost trust.
- Ecom French Touch therefore offers very low prices (17 euros per month for the training of the month, with no subscription) with a high repeat-purchase rate. This restores customers' trust, and they apply what they learn and get results.
- A cheap product means low commitment: the customer expects little, so you can give them an experience that exceeds their expectations.
- A buyer from a year ago is very often still a buyer.
- He considers the opposite view short-termist: a seller who collects 500,000 euros with 1000-euro courses would, in his view, have made far more by selling cheaper with recurrence over the following years. He acknowledges that some expensive products are excellent, but that is not his model.
- He cannot justify a high price for videos, because someone who does nothing with them gets nothing. He only sells coaching at a high price, where he can transform a business ("x5, x10 or x20") and "give a kick in the pants" when needed.
- His value ladder: low-priced training to get started and get results, then coaching, which is more specific and more human.

## Selling on Proof Rather Than Curiosity

Curiosity sells cheap products, but Romàn does not rely on it.

- The positioning rests on their own results: "everything we sell, we can prove 100%."
- Proof cited: a Facebook group grown from 0 to 25,000 members, a Telegram group, a YouTube channel with over 100,000 subscribers.
- The second pillar is customer recurrence. He says nobody on the market offers better for this price, and that is their credo.

## The Facebook Hack That Launched the Community

At the start, Facebook let you add thousands of people from competing groups with one click.

- You joined groups in the topic area with your profile and added the members as friends, then into your group. The next day, about half accepted.
- When the groups were exhausted, they moved on to pages via Facebook graph search, for example a page with 120,000 fans, to invite everyone.
- To scale up, they bought the equivalent of 200 Facebook accounts, used three computers, and coded scripts that crawled through the groups and added everyone automatically.
- The groups' audiences overlapped, so they ended up covering most of the niche's audience. This gave a base to launch the YouTube channel: a thousand subscribers from the first video.
- Facebook closed this loophole after about two months of use. Insurance groups were already offering them one euro per member added, and they estimated they could earn several thousand euros a day. He says that with a two-year head start they would be "very, very rich."
- In his view, this is no longer possible today and he has no desire to do it again.

## Keeping the Community: Value and Engagement

The hack is not enough, he repeated. With 50,000 people in another niche, "I'd crash."

- What worked: giving enormous value and having a very active community manager. On Facebook, they reply to everyone in the group. They added lives and plenty of resources.
- Result: only three or four members out of 25,000 complained about being added, and people stayed loyal.
- The group became the biggest marketing/dropshipping/e-commerce group on Facebook, which recommends it first for those searches.

## Multi-Platform: One Platform at a Time, Then Audience Transfer

Romàn describes each channel as serving a different type of audience.

- YouTube: the most formal, almost no interaction; you deliver content and that's it.
- Facebook: community, with answers to every question.
- Telegram: close to texting, more intimate, with behind-the-scenes of the business.
- Advice: choose one platform and saturate it, then transfer the audience. Starting from zero on a platform is very hard. With a base, for example 200 fans on Instagram, the algorithm starts pushing you. In his view, you could open Snapchat or another network and get people by announcing it to the existing community.
- Choose the platform closest to your "zone of genius": YouTube if you're good at video, Facebook if you're good at community management, Telegram if you prefer quick exchanges. He can make YouTube and Facebook explode, but says he is unable to do anything on Twitter.

## The Udemy Hack: Free, Reviews, Then Paid, With Lead Capture

Romàn put all his programs on Udemy, reluctantly (he dislikes their pricing policy), but he feels that an extra 200 to 300 euros in monthly revenue is not something to turn down.

- Put a free course on the platform.
- Post it everywhere, in every group, announcing that it becomes paid in two days. Ask people to leave a good review.
- Once it's well ranked with reviews, switch it to the price you want. The platform promotes it and it generates revenue.
- Free enrollees are also leads to collect. He says he can easily gather 1000 emails this way, and has used this method several times.

## The Bold Move With a Footballer (Djibril Cissé)

One evening, while looking at Patrice Evra's account (2.8 million followers), who was selling a nutrition ebook, Romàn concluded that footballers selling programs are obvious targets.

- He sent voice messages to footballers: France national team players, Ligue 1 stars, Benzema. Two minutes later, Djibril Cissé replied with his number.
- On the phone, he said Evra had released a product and that he was offering to do better. Cissé invited him to his home the next day.
- He spent the night on a PowerPoint, showed up, presented, and Cissé accepted. He could present with confidence because the subject (sales funnels, marketing) is his field.
- Contract signed, sales funnel built, launch of Cissé's fitness program: a success, even though they had expected more media coverage.
- Lesson: he proved he can put together a project overnight with a big name.

## Failure and Diversification: The PayPal Freeze

Romàn cites his hardest ordeal: PayPal froze about 30,000 euros after a spike in volume in a single day, for 90 to 180 days (he doesn't remember exactly).

- The company was just starting out, they had to pay charges and taxes on money that wasn't available, and 50% of customers paid via PayPal.
- Support was ineffective: he even contacted a manager via LinkedIn, without result, because the decision came from California.
- Lessons applied since: diversify networks, payment processors and payment systems, have two accounts, two email sending addresses, a lawyer for the terms and conditions and legal matters, and always have a backup plan before launching.
- He advises not depending on PayPal.

## Work and Mindset

- "You get nothing for nothing": results come from enormous work, even if online business makes access easier.
- He gets bored quickly, needs new challenges, and works in sessions of a few days on one subject.
- His motto: perseverance, "God helps those who help themselves." He refuses certain practices that don't fit his code of conduct.
- You can make money on the internet without advertising, and it has become the easiest path.
