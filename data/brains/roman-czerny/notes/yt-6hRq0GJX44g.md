## Why Romàn uses cold email to start a B2B business

- Romàn calls cold email his favorite way to start a B2B business. It lets him test the market without launching on social media, which he says is a lot of work and hard to pivot on every two weeks.
- His approach is to email and LinkedIn-outreach a bunch of people and book as many demos as possible before building the product.
- At Goji Berry (high-intent lead finding and outreach software), the first customers were sold lead files before the product existed. He did the work manually, which took the company from zero to 10K.
- He stopped the manual work once he knew he was onto something, knew who to sell to and how to sell it, and then built the software.
- He says the same playbook helped grow his previous SaaS, Coco AI.
- Goji Berry is at roughly €2.5M a year. He estimates cold email plus LinkedIn outreach drove about 30% of the first year's revenue.
- His view: cold email is "way easier than getting viral on X, way easier than getting viral on LinkedIn, way easier than making a YouTube video."
- He compares the cold-email-versus-no-cold-email debate to bootstrapped-versus-VC-backed: people love taking sides, but both can work.

## The three ingredients of cold email

- Romàn says you need three things: a targeted lead list with real emails in your ICP, the infrastructure, and the right messaging.
- He argues people over-focus on infrastructure, such as spam and domains. In his view the hardest part is getting demos, meaning the messaging and offer.
- Cold emailing is legal if you:
  - let people unsubscribe,
  - stop when asked,
  - send something related to their business that helps them grow.
- First step for a beginner: set up infrastructure, get leads (high-intent, scraped, or from a database like Apollo), enrich the emails, write the messaging, then launch.
- Budget: be ready to spend about $100 to $500 to try it.
- Small volume: 1,000 to 2,000 emails a month is "more than enough" to start, and needs only two or three mailboxes.

## Infrastructure setup and volume rules

- Romàn used Instantly in the early days. He added new mailboxes, used done-for-you email setup, or bought pre-warmed accounts. He spent roughly $500 to $800 a month on Instantly. He was moving off it to Goji Berry's own email infrastructure, which now has email steps in multi-channel campaigns.
- Goji Berry lets you send email, but you bring your own domains. Instantly lets you buy domains.
- Deliverability rules he follows:
  - No links in the original message.
  - Avoid spam words, such as 18+ content, money, gambling and drugs, and "promotion" or "promo code".
  - Send a maximum of 30 emails per mailbox per day.
  - Use three mailboxes per domain.
  - Spin the email so a word changes per recipient (Instantly does this almost automatically), or use Goji Berry personalization per lead.
- He says spam placement mostly comes from irrelevant offers that nobody replies to. When recipients reply and engage, Gmail and Outlook see healthy conversations.
- Mailboxes eventually land in spam, so he expects to rotate them regularly.

## The blueprint offer: give before you ask

- Romàn calls asking for a demo in the first email the biggest cold email mistake. You are asking a stranger for their time before giving anything.
- Instead, he offers a "blueprint" (a free guide). The recipient replies "yes", which is low effort compared with agreeing to a meeting.
- His example email goes to people who commented on his LinkedIn post. It says they commented to get the "cloud power prospecting system", described as the exact setup used to book 100+ meetings in a few weeks. It then asks if they received it and says to reply "yes" to have it resent.
- After the reply, he sends a document containing:
  - a video,
  - another video,
  - a full playbook on getting leads,
  - at the bottom, a link to start a trial or book a demo.
- This turns outbound into inbound. People who book have read the document and chosen to book, so they show up wanting to buy.
- He says that since discovering this, he has "never asked for a demo in cold email ever again."
- The same pattern works with other assets:
  - a YouTube video ("I broke down in a video how we did X for Y, can I send it?"),
  - a free tool,
  - a Slack group.
- He claims you could get around 1,000 people a month watching a video this way.
- Cold email at volume also drives traffic to your site and works as retargeting, because recipients then see you on X, LinkedIn and ads.

## Lead sourcing

- Romàn has two ways of getting leads:
  - Scraping a high volume of leads, enriching them, and uploading them to Instantly.
  - High-intent leads generated by Goji Berry, sent with more context and personalization. Volume is lower but response rates are far higher.
- Goji Berry's high-intent signals:
  - engaged with a competitor,
  - liked LinkedIn posts with specific keywords,
  - similar to the ideal lead and active.
- Goji's extension also pulls leads from Sales Navigator, search results, or the likers and commenters of any LinkedIn post. He then enriches the emails and sends via Goji or Instantly.

## Reply rates, volume and iteration

- Romàn's numbers from an Instantly campaign:
  - Last 7 days: about 25K emails sent, reply rate roughly 1.19% including auto-replies.
  - One month: around 549 people replied, with an 84K-email campaign producing over 10 replies per day.
  - He says he probably sent more than 1 million emails in the last 12 months across campaigns.
- His view: 1% looks low, but at volume it is hard to do much better. He cites Adam Robinson of RB2B sending millions of emails a month at a 0.7 to 0.8% reply rate, and calls that "insane" given the cost.
- He suggests studying Robinson's approach: it pushes the free trial rather than demos.
- How he tests:
  - He doesn't run a split test. He finds a winning blueprint, goes all in until he has emailed everyone he can, then changes the blueprint and emails everyone again.
  - He sends few follow-ups, and sometimes just a second email with a different blueprint.
  - His rule: it is better to cold email someone new than to follow up on someone who hasn't responded. The exception is account-based marketing with big deals, where follow-ups are worth it.
- He says you never really run out of leads because you can always try a new angle. When he does, it's usually because he was busy with other channels.
- He copies and tests winning templates that others share publicly.

## Demos vs free trials, and when cold email fits

- Romàn is trying to avoid demos. The demo link sits at the bottom of the document, and most people just take the free trial.
- Routing for people who do book:
  - Individuals and small teams go to a webinar (he runs three per week).
  - Teams of two to three go to someone else.
  - Teams above three get a meeting with him.
- When to use cold email:
  - It fits a B2B SaaS selling for roughly $100, $200 or $500 a month.
  - For $20 to $30 a month products, it is useful for getting on calls and learning what customers want, but not for making money.
- If you already have an audience, he would first test blueprints on it. Once one sticks, he would start cold email.
- If you have no audience, cold email lets you talk to customers and learn what they want without posting on social media.
