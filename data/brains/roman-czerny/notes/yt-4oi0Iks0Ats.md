## Distribution philosophy: one channel per revenue stage

- Romàn argues that each stage of a SaaS needs a different revenue channel. GojiBerry's rule: move to a new channel once you have mastered one or cannot get more out of it.
- GojiBerry's path by MRR:
  - $0 to $6k: only outbound.
  - $10k to $25k: almost only Reddit.
  - $25k to $75k: content plus blueprints (free lead magnets).
  - $75k to ~$150k: Y Combinator exposure, X (Twitter), B2B influencers, sponsored newsletters and a lifetime affiliate program.
  - Later: UGC, Meta ads and viral X launches (see the paid-playbook section).
- Channels are added on top of each other. Romàn only cut one, Reddit, after about $250k MRR.
- He cut Reddit for two reasons:
  - Hate towards tool promotion ("I think we went a bit too hard").
  - Opportunity cost. A few $K of MRR from Reddit takes a lot of time, and the same time in B2B influencers or UGC returns more. Other people now talk about GojiBerry on Reddit without GojiBerry posting.
- He reached $300k MRR with GojiBerry. The company turned 1 year old days before the recording.

## Test a channel yourself, then hand it to an agency

- Romàn does not hire much on the growth side. He handles growth himself and uses the best agency in each domain:
  - Agencies for B2B influencers, SEO, UGC, Meta ads and content.
  - Cold outreach is run in-house by an intern.
- The workflow:
  1. He tests each new channel himself.
  2. He writes his own blueprint of what works.
  3. He delegates to an agency that must follow that blueprint ("I know exactly what I want").
- Agencies cost money, but the cost is acceptable because he knows exactly what he wants.
- Next step: an in-house growth hire who takes his spot and manages agencies while he tests new channels.
- On X, he did everything himself at first. Now an agency suggests posts. He rejects most, but uses the hooks or angles as raw material and rewrites them.
- Founder-led content always needs rework because an agency cannot know what is in a founder's head.

## X and founder accounts

- GojiBerry opened X accounts for all three founders. Romàn manages content for all of them.
- Before, they only posted on their own account. Paying B2B influencers to post lead magnets expanded the reach.
- The YC logo or "in YC" in a post brought a lot of exposure from the startup world, plus credibility.

## The paid and viral playbook (UGC, B2B creators, quote retweets)

- Romàn follows a blueprint he attributes to companies such as XField, Arcads and Lovable.
- Steps:
  - **Organic to paid:** create content on TikTok and Instagram. When a piece goes viral, boost it with Meta/Instagram ads.
  - **LinkedIn:** pay B2B influencers to give away lead magnets about your brand.
  - **X viral launches:** look at who quote-retweeted the biggest SaaS launches. DM them and pay them to quote-retweet your tweet.
- The quote-retweet tactic: he had just started it. Per Romàn, $1,000 to $3,000 can buy a viral video on X that brings a lot of revenue.
- His forward plan is to focus on these three channels (UGC, B2B creators, quote retweets), raise the volume, and aim for $500k to $1M per month.
- Trade-off: this content attracts mostly individual users, small sales teams and small agencies, so retention is lower and going upmarket is harder.
- Romàn's answer to the churn: go all in on the channels. Heavy growth generates enough volume that the teams worth keeping consolidate from it. He also plans to bring in bigger teams.

## Target the AI niche, not your literal category

- Biggest growth lever, per Romàn: free blueprints (lead-magnet content) aimed at the AI niche.
- His logic, using SEO as the example:
  - "How to improve SEO" targets a niche everyone already targets.
  - "How to improve your SEO with AI" reaches a bigger, newer audience. Claude puts out large budgets and creators get more reach talking about AI than SEO.
- GojiBerry's version: instead of "how to get more leads", it publishes content on how Claude, LLMs and ChatGPT get you better leads.
- This brings in people from AI and from outreach, not just the narrow category. He says distribution can go "10x".
- Trade-off: the niche is broader, so churn is higher.
- He says he would not change this move. GojiBerry's landing page also pitches an AI agent.
- He could not say how much the headline change lifted conversion. Traffic sources changed at the same time and tracking was not in place ("that's my fault"). He only knows the landing page converts well.
- He cautions that this will not fit every SaaS. He says to take what you can reproduce, not copy everything. He cites other founders' blueprints that may suit some products better.

## What Y Combinator gave GojiBerry

- Process: three months in San Francisco. You need a US company to receive YC's money.
- Perks on day one included:
  - Almost $1M in OpenAI tokens.
  - $300k from Gemini and $300k from Claude.
  - Credits from AWS and Azure and other tools.
- Cost saving: for an AI tool these are costs you do not have to pay.
- Rhythm:
  - One conference per week.
  - One meeting per week with your assigned partner.
  - A group talk every two weeks with other founders.
  - Mostly working in a room with co-founders. Many city events.
- Network: roughly 5,000 YC founders, and he says you can DM any of them (for example the Airbnb founder, with a good reason) for help.
- Mindset: meeting founders who sold companies for $300M to $600M "unlocks your brain" about what is possible.
- Demo Day: GojiBerry raised money at a high valuation and diluted very little. Romàn says they now own about 85% of the company, do not plan to raise more, and have lots of cash to go all in once they find how to scale their channels.
- His view: raising at a high valuation while giving up little is a faster path to $10M or $20M than staying bootstrapped.
- Partner advice (his partner was Andrew Miklas, whose company IPO'd): talk to customers a lot, do not build features before talking to them, do not spend money if it is not profitable, do not hire too fast, fire fast if it is not working, and be all in. The partner also had a similar business in the same niche, so knew acquisition and how to talk to those users.
- Specific priorities come as hints, not orders, such as "maybe you should prioritize this because that company did that, but talk to your users."

## Mindset after $300k MRR

- Romàn says he does not feel successful. The jump from $50k to $300k MRR changed little, and they are far from their objective.
- The team celebrated neither $1M nor $3M ARR despite promising to celebrate.
- He no longer plans to sell the company. He enjoys building and testing channels, and works in the office from 8 a.m. to 1 a.m.
- If they achieved a third of what they are doing now, he says he would still be very happy. He would not change anything about the path so far.
