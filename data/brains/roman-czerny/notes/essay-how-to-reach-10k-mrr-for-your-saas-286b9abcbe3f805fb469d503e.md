## From idea to first demand: validate before building

- Romàn says launching a SaaS without validating the idea is a "costly gamble". Validation confirms real demand, saves time and money, and shapes the concept before full development.
- His validation toolkit: customer interviews and surveys, landing pages or pre-sales to gauge willingness to pay, Reddit and community feedback, competitor analysis, an MVP or beta, free trials, and Product Hunt launches.
- He tried two ideas for his second SaaS. The first was an AI note taker. It had a landing page and email signups, so he built it, but it didn't sell. It was "too complex too early", so he pivoted before going further. The second was a GTM platform to get leads and do outreach, which became Gojiberry AI.
- He found the need to pivot by talking to prospects. He calls building without talking to people the classic founder mistake.
- The strongest signal is people ready to give a credit card before a single line of code is written. A good early test is posting on Reddit to see whether people will pay.
- No developer is needed at this stage.
- His action plan:
  1. List ideas.
  2. Build a very simple landing page.
  3. Find potential customers to talk to.
  4. If they're ready to pay by the end of the call, he treats that as the sign he's on the right track.
- He notes a SaaS takes several years, so "choose your battles wisely".

## Building the product: partners, one feature, no contracts too early

- A solo marketer can hire a developer or team up with a CTO. Romàn has always preferred a CTO partner. Hiring developers is expensive, a co-founder is far more invested, and the right partner "is worth ten developers". The trade-off is owning only 50% when selling the company.
- A solo developer is "a jackpot", but teaming with a marketer helps growth and lets the developer stay in their zone of genius.
- He avoids dev agencies. He imagines a bug on a Saturday afternoon and an agency that only replies on Monday. His ideal is an in-house developer reachable anytime.
- He found the Gojiberry AI developer through a referral from his network. He also recommends posting in startup and incubator groups.
- He advises not signing anything before the first euros come in. Build an MVP, launch a landing page, and see if you can work together. "Focus on traction first, the paperwork can wait."
- He wants one feature at the start. Never build a feature unless you are 100% sure it will increase retention, improve customer results, or grow revenue. Build one feature, sell it, see results, then iterate.

## Pricing: match competitors first, then test

- Romàn says there is no magic formula apart from testing. Early on, he aligns pricing with competitors, because they are already selling at that price and the audience is comfortable paying it. He has done this with every SaaS he has launched.
- This gives initial cash flow, and he adjusts later based on results.
- Gojiberry AI pricing history:
  - At first he took only demos and showed no pricing on the landing page.
  - He then tested 499, 297, 199 and 99.
  - At 497 he could sell, but customers churned quickly.
  - At 297 he sold more, but it required calls and manual sales.
  - He now sells only at $99/month with a 7-day free trial, mostly self-serve.
- He describes pricing as a constant process of testing and adjusting.

## Getting the first 10 customers

- Romàn says the first ten customers give the confidence to keep going. As with Y Combinator and successful SaaS founders, there is no magic formula: "you have to grind for them".
- Sources include friends' introductions, phone calls, manual LinkedIn DMs, and cold emails. The goal is 20 to 30 meetings and closing ten of them. "Try everything you can."
- For Gojiberry AI, he did LinkedIn outreach from his personal account, "sending messages like crazy". He offered a demo to everyone who replied. A small slide deck converted them to paying customers. He built the deck in 15 minutes and calls it "VERY basic".
- Any method is valid if it gets people on calls. Don't accept "I'm interested, but maybe later". By the end of the call you want "Okay, let's do it". You can then say either "We're currently building it" or "you can start today".

## Support and customer success

- Add a small chat bubble to the site. Gojiberry AI uses Crisp, and he says free alternatives exist.
- With early customers, schedule quick calls at day 7 and day 15. Ask:
  - Which parts of the product do you use?
  - What do you like or dislike?
  - Would you leave a review?
  - Would you recommend us?
- Gojiberry AI offers review calls to every client. Romàn says most people don't do this.
- Keeping a customer is much easier than acquiring one. Keep support fast and communication easy, and check in proactively with your best users.
- "When someone decides to leave your product, it's already too late." Spot signs of churn earlier.

## LinkedIn high-intent outreach (about 25% of sales)

- Romàn gets about 25% of Gojiberry AI sales from LinkedIn outreach because he targets warm leads.
- Cold outreach, per his figures: 1-2% response rate, about 100 messages per meeting, and defensive prospects.
- High-intent outreach: 25-40% response rate, about 10 messages per meeting, and prospects who are active in the market.
- A comment like "interested" is a raised hand, which he calls "a 100x stronger signal than any data point you can buy".
- A high-intent lead is someone showing signals right now, not just an ICP on paper. Signals include:
  - Liking or commenting on competitor posts
  - A new role or key hires
  - Fresh funding
  - Following relevant influencers or hashtags
  - Publicly sharing pain points
- Doing this manually takes 5+ hours a day, so he uses AI agents. Gojiberry agents cover competitor profiles, influencer profiles, recent job changers, the top 5% of ICP, recent fundraises, LinkedIn engagement, interactions with your company, and profile visits. Company page visits, page followers and hiring signals are listed as coming soon.
- He says one agent search produced 300 high-intent leads in a week.
- Message sequence (short, no pitch):
  - Initial DM (23 words): "Hey [Name], Quick question - what's your biggest challenge with [topic] right now?" He says 80% answer.
  - Meeting DM (after a reply): "Interesting - we just solved that exact problem for [similar company]. Mind if I send you a 3-minute video showing how?" He says 60% say yes. He then records a Loom covering their challenge, a similar client case study, his approach, and a soft CTA.
  - Backup DM if no response: "Hey [Name], did you get a chance to check my message above? Noticed you're [specific observation] - we should chat."
- His AI prompts: a research prompt (biggest challenge, current project, a genuine compliment, under 50 words), a writing prompt (30-word DM that sounds like texting a friend, one simple question, no sales language, no exclamation points), and a follow-up prompt (25 words acknowledging their situation with social proof).
- Mindset: treat LinkedIn as "a warm lead generation machine". "Every post is a net. Every comment is a lead."

## Reddit as an inbound channel

- Reasons he gives for Reddit in 2025:
  - 97.2M daily active users in 2024, up 47% year on year.
  - Google paid $60M for exclusive indexing rights, so Reddit mentions help SEO.
  - About 40% of AI-generated answers reference Reddit, so mentions help LLM visibility.
- Finding subreddits:
  - Start with SaaS-friendly subs.
  - Follow where active authors post elsewhere.
  - Go niche by industry, using a ChatGPT prompt for 10 relevant subreddits.
  - Search competitor mentions to see where they're discussed.
- Commenting rules to avoid bans:
  - Give more than you take, and mention the product only when it's the natural solution.
  - Be careful with links. Mention the product by name, share a link only if asked, and never reuse the same linked comment across threads.
  - Balance promo with genuine contributions. Max 15 comments/day per account, under 50% promotional. Spread volume across accounts and avoid VPNs.
  - Warm up first. Get at least 10 karma before mentioning the SaaS. At 10 karma do 3-4 mentions/day, and at 20 karma scale to 6-7/day.
- Measuring: use Yandex.Metrica (free, with Webvisor session replay). Run an experiment of about 20 mentions/day for 3 days, pause 3 days (traffic peaks in 1-2 days then fades), analyze visits, signups and conversion, then compare subreddit groups week by week.
- His own routine for Gojiberry AI: 7 new posts a week, each reposted in 10+ subreddits (about 70 posts/week), plus 35 SEO comments a week.
- Four formats that work:
  - Storytelling (90% value, 10% subtle mention)
  - Case studies (e.g. "403 demos in 60 days")
  - AMA threads
  - Comparisons/alternatives (e.g. "Instantly alternatives"), which are evergreen and rank on Google and LLMs
- Keyword method: seed 20-30 buyer-intent keywords, search `site:reddit.com` plus the keyword, qualify threads by traffic, recency and sub rules, then track Keyword, URL, Angle, Date, Views in a sheet. Threads that already rank for years give free compounding exposure.
- He writes posts by dictating to ChatGPT and asking it to format for Reddit. He also turns strong Reddit comments into blog posts and links back.
- Conversion funnel: posts and SEO comments, then replies/DMs with a diagnostic question ("What's your main lead source right now?"), then a resource, then a demo CTA, then a live-signals demo to close. He reports a 30-40% close rate.
- Scaling safely: always disclose your brand ("I build Gojiberry AI"), keep one account for posts and one for SEO comments, and do posting and replying manually. Vote manipulation, mass DMs and fake reviews are forbidden.

## Cold email on real-time intent leads

- Romàn's central claim: the number one reason cold email fails is the lead source, not copy or deliverability. Static databases like Apollo and CSVs go stale, so he emails real-time high-intent leads from LinkedIn activity. At equal volume he says this gives 3-5x more replies.
- His results: about 5,000/day (~150,000/month), roughly a 2.5% reply rate, 70% of SaaS growth from outbound, and 400+ demos in 2 months without ads.
- Flow: LinkedIn signal, then Gojiberry, then enrichment, then Instantly, then Slack/CRM, then reply handling. He uses Instantly because it works from 100 to 50,000/day, has inbox placement tests and rotates domains and inboxes. He says it has been the most reliable past 5,000/day.
- Deliverability setup:
  - Buy several domains close to the brand (e.g. trygojiberry.com, getgojiberry.ai), with 3 inboxes per domain.
  - Start at 5-10/day/inbox and raise by 5-10% a day. After about 3 weeks, 20-30/day/inbox.
  - Set SPF, DKIM and DMARC, keep bounces under 2%, and cap at 30/day/inbox.
  - Run inbox placement tests (target above 80%) and pause if results go red.
- Scaling roadmap:
  - 1 domain × 3 inboxes: about 500/month
  - 5 domains × 15 inboxes: about 15k/month
  - 10 domains × 50 inboxes: about 45k/month
- Ten intent signals: engaging with a competitor's post, a new role, funding, a job post, event registration, downloading competitor content, repeat visits to your LinkedIn profile, commenting on your CEO's post, migrating away from a tool, and posting publicly about a pain point. He says signal-based timing is the difference between 0.5% and 3% reply rates.
- Copy rules:
  - Subject: 4-7 words, natural, personalized.
  - Opener: reference the exact trigger, not "Hope you are well".
  - Body: about 140 words, simple sentences, one idea per paragraph, with proof such as numbers and similar clients.
  - Personalization: use real context, with two or more layers.
  - CTA: one clear, easy step, such as a five-minute chat.
- Sequence: first email gives value and context, day 3 a soft reminder, day 7 proof or case study, day 10 break-up. Three to four touches max.
- Tested subject lines: "About your new role at {{company}}", "Quick idea for {{company}}", "Noticed this about {{competitor}}".
- Targeting: start tight, e.g. CEO / Head of Growth / Demand Gen, SaaS, 5-50 employees, US/EU, seed to Series B. Expand only after consistent results, and track ICP segments separately.
- Track weekly: emails sent, open %, reply %, positive reply %, demos booked, and results by signal type.

## Secondary channels: LinkedIn content, free tools, blog, directories, YouTube

- LinkedIn inbound content: he posts daily, sending people to a resource or the website. He copies structures that already perform, adapting them to his style. He says attribution is hard to measure, but it reliably opens new DM opportunities.
- Free tools: build simple tools in a few hours that attract the ideal audience, who can then try the product. Gojiberry AI built several, such as a free outreach message analyser, which drive a lot of traffic. He isn't sure how much they have converted, but they boosted overall website traffic.
- Blog: publish one article a week and check which get the most impressions and clicks, then improve those. He talks to ChatGPT about what he wants to say, asks it to format the text, and publishes. He says it brings some traffic but "is not what will make your business take off". It is a good habit to start before 10K MRR.
- AI directories: listing the SaaS in 100+ AI directories takes two to three hours. It is often free and can bring a few dozen users per day, sometimes high-quality paying ones.
- YouTube: he calls it excellent, with great demos booked. The strategy is long-tail, SEO-ranked videos that drive people to the website or Calendly. He records with ScreenStudio, doesn't edit, and makes thumbnails with ChatGPT. Some videos passed 1,000 views. Videos can be reused in lead magnets and on Reddit, and they build trust through seeing his face.

## Benchmarks, rollout plan and first 48 hours

- Y Combinator's "magic formula", as Romàn cites it: €10,000 MRR from 100 customers paying €100/month. For him, €10,000 MRR is where you know the product is scalable and demand is real.
- His track record: Coco AI (WhatsApp automation for Shopify) scaled to €500,000 ARR before he sold it because the team wasn't the right fit to take it further. Gojiberry AI is his second run at the same milestone.
- His four-week rollout, with his expected results:
  - Week 1: set up agents and sync leads to CRM, then start warm conversations. Expect 50-100 leads and 10-15 conversations.
  - Week 2: review which signals work, adjust filters, run LinkedIn and email campaigns. Expect 100-150 leads, 25-30 conversations, 5-8 calls.
  - Week 3: activate more agents and push multi-channel outreach. Expect 300-400 leads, 40-50 conversations, 10-15 calls.
  - Week 4 on: signals keep compounding into pipeline.
- First 48 hours:
  - Activate the first agents.
  - Sync leads into the outreach tool the next morning.
  - Start connecting with everyone who just showed a buying signal.
  - Follow up within 24 hours.
  - On day 2, re-engage non-responders and push for call booking.
- He claims you will book a first meeting within 7 days.
- His tool stack: Gojiberry (leads), Instantly (email), Fathom (call recording), Crisp (chat), ScreenStudio (video), ChatGPT (writing, prompts, thumbnails), Yandex.Metrica (Reddit tracking).
