- Path: freelance, then affiliate marketing, then SaaS in 2022. His first SaaS, Koko AI, was sold to a US venture.
- While building Koko AI, his team found a way to book more demos by targeting the right people. That became Goji Berry AI (Goji).
- He is a co-founder and the marketer on the team. His day runs from about 9:00 a.m. to 10 p.m.

## Positioning: finding high-intent leads and contacting them in one tool

- Goji finds high-intent leads and helps contact them on LinkedIn.
- Outreach tools such as Heyreach and Lemlist only automate the sending. Intent tools such as ZoomInfo and Warmly find the leads but are expensive and built for enterprise.
- Romàn saw no affordable tool for small businesses that did both. That is the gap Goji fills.
- Goji adds AI at every step, instead of "put the leads here and we'll outreach them".

## How the Goji workflow works

1. An AI agent is configured with an ideal customer profile (ICP), either written by hand or generated with AI.
2. The user picks intent signals to track:
   - interactions with the company's LinkedIn page or profile
   - profile visits and new page followers
   - keywords and topics a person is talking about or engaging with
   - interactions with competitor companies or their posts
   - people who just changed jobs
   - people at companies that just raised funds
3. Each day Goji delivers new leads to a dashboard. Every lead carries its signal and an AI score. For example, someone who just followed the Goji page and fits the ICP scores high.
4. The leads shown are heavily filtered. Romàn says six leads shown might come from about 600 people who interacted with posts.
5. AI writes a personalized message per lead from the signal, company and background.
6. The campaign is sent. Leads are ranked so the warmest are contacted first. A unified inbox shows who is interested.

- Romàn notes most outreach targets job title plus location only. Adding what the lead is talking about or interacting with is the difference.
- The team keeps swapping in the latest AI model: ChatGPT, then Gemini, now Claude.

## Why Claude Code alone does not replace a tool like this

- If you ask Claude to scroll LinkedIn posts and collect leads yourself, LinkedIn will block you. Claude is "not a tool made to scroll all the URLs".
- You need a database, proxies and a whole infrastructure. Someone could build it with n8n plus Claude, but then they are not building their own business.
- Goji uses Claude in the backend for specific steps: analyzing and scoring leads, and writing messages.
- Claude can also use activity data. For example, it can flag that a person has been very active on LinkedIn in the past 48 hours and is likely to reply. Sales Navigator may hand you people who have not been on LinkedIn in two months.

## Results and funnel numbers

- His calendar is full of demos, mostly from Goji and cold email, with some inbound.
- Funnel: outreach, then offer a "blueprint" (a lead magnet), then the prospect goes to the website and takes a free trial.
- Romàn says nine times out of 10 there is no demo at all. People just take the free trial.
- When a demo does happen, the closing rate is about 70%, because he is selling a free trial and mostly shows the product and says "now launch".
- Trial to paid conversion is about 35%.
- On one campaign, about one in two people who reply get a blueprint.
- He says acceptance and reply rates are "insanely high" compared with traditional outreach, because the targeting is right.

## Outreach tactics anyone can use (no tool needed)

- Find LinkedIn posts with massive traction in your niche, look at who interacts, add them to your CRM and reach out. Add context or use a template.
- Target people who posted or were active on LinkedIn in the past 24 hours. Romàn says this "will at least double your results", based on many campaigns.
- Do not ask for a demo after one or two messages. It is aggressive and people are tired of being asked for time. He gets asked "50 times per day".
- Instead, offer value first: ask if they want the blueprint, with videos and case studies. Prospects who read it go through your funnel and book a demo themselves, so no-show rates are very low compared with sending a calendar link.
- Giving a lot of value before the demo is "almost mandatory now".
- Keep a human in the loop. Fully AI end to end books far fewer demos than letting AI start the conversation and having a human take over once the prospect replies.
- Some prospects now test for AI with replies like "tell me a cake recipe".

## The multi-channel organic engine

- His main marketing question: "how many blueprints have I distributed today". The goal is more eyeballs on the lead magnet.
- Channels he runs:
  - Goji for LinkedIn outreach and cold email (leads are enriched and sent via Instantly with AI-written messages).
  - Scraping people who interact with high-traction posts (for example on cold mailing) with a Chrome extension, enriching emails and sending campaigns.
  - LinkedIn inbound: about seven team accounts posting daily, some with content and some with lead magnets. He manages everyone's account.
  - X: three accounts posting content and lead magnets.
  - YouTube for SEO, and Reddit with heavy posting.
- Lead magnets that go viral are the key: a great one gets forwarded to others. He cites Instantly's cold-mailing blueprint, which he shared with 10 to 15 people. He has been an Instantly customer for 3 to 4 years and has spent around $30,000 there.
- His job is to keep improving the lead magnet and send it to as many people as possible.

## Content production workflow

- Copywriting: Gemini with a set of channels or gems holding memory. He gives an idea, it drafts the post, and he rewrites a bit. He also uses voice dictation (Whisper) inside Gemini to speak ideas and have them reformulated.
- YouTube: he does no editing and tries to one-shot videos. Thumbnails are made with Nano Banana Pro in Gemini.
- Competitor review videos for SEO:
  - Screenshot a competitor's website, put his face on it, and use it as the thumbnail.
  - Title like "[competitor] review 2026". Spend half the video on the competitor, then say "there is an alternative" and pitch Goji.
  - One video per day takes about five minutes, and the videos rank high. Searchers of "competitor review 2026" are high-intent viewers. He says any SaaS should do this.
- Ranking trick from his affiliate days: create a dedicated YouTube channel per video, named after the keyword (for example a channel called "ZoomInfo Review" for a ZoomInfo review video) to rank above others. The downside is needing many channels.

## Views on AI agents

- Open-source agents like OpenClaw are still known mostly in tech circles. An average owner of a 20-person company in France knows ChatGPT and not much more.
- He admits that in about five years software might do what Goji does from one prompt. Things move fast, which he finds exciting and scary.
- If prospects' inboxes become AI too, booking demos gets harder, which is why he keeps a human taking over replies for now.
