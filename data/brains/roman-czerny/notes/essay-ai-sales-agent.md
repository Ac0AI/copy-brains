## AI sales agents come in two families, and the type matters more than the ranking

- Romàn says "AI sales agent" covers two different products, and the type decides more than any vendor ranking does.
- **Autonomous agents** run the whole loop with nobody watching. They find leads, write messages, handle replies and book meetings. You set the ICP, positioning and approval rules once, then the agent works through the week.
  - The trade-off is control: "an agent nobody supervises produces volume rather than pipeline."
- **Assistive agents** (copilots) research the account, draft the reply and flag what deserves attention, but "a person still presses send." They sharpen a rep who already has a list and a full calendar. They fill neither.
- Romàn's rule of thumb:
  - A team of 2 to 10 with no list, no traffic and nobody spare to supervise a tool needs an autonomous agent.
  - Reps who already sell well and need better preparation suit an assistive agent (he names Clay and Gong).
  - A chat agent that qualifies inbound visitors only works the traffic you already earned and does not raise visitor numbers.
- He separates an agent from a chatbot: a chatbot waits for a question, while an agent picks the account, writes the message, sends it, reads the reply and moves the record.
- He separates "AI SDR" from "AI agent": AI SDR describes a job, priced against a salary. AI agent describes an architecture that decides and acts on its own. "Every AI SDR counts as an AI agent, and most AI agents do something far narrower."

## Three capabilities that separate a real agent from a sequencer with a chatbot bolted on

Romàn suggests checking each one against any demo.
- **Prospecting and enrichment:** the agent builds the list from databases, company sites and live signals, not from an export that is stale by Thursday.
  - He cites US median job tenure of 3.9 years, with 22 percent of employees in post under a year (BLS).
  - He cites B2B databases decaying about 2.1 percent a month (HubSpot), close to a quarter of a list per year.
- **Multi-channel outreach:** email, socials, SMS, WhatsApp and calls, with conversation history following the prospect.
  - "The channel count matters less than the reason for writing, and it isn't close."
  - Volume without targeting leads to blocked domains. Google holds bulk senders to a spam complaint rate below 0.3 percent.
- **Qualification:** the agent answers prospect questions from your documentation, scores the thread against your playbook and routes what clears the bar.
  - An assistive agent shows you the score. An autonomous one acts on it.

## Price grid for ten AI sales agents (August 2026)

Romàn checked each vendor's pricing page in August 2026. The spread runs from 99 dollars a month to more than 45,000 dollars a year for products that describe themselves in nearly identical words.
- **Gojiberry:** 99 dollars a month for the whole team (2 AI agents, 1,800 prospects contacted). It is monthly with no commitment, and a free plan exists.
- **Artisan:** no published price. Third-party write-ups put annual contracts around 50,000 dollars.
- **11x:** Growth starts at 3,750 dollars a month billed annually (up to 5 end users, 2,000 new prospects). The FAQ quotes 36,000 a year, so ask which applies. Billing is per lead.
- **AiSDR:** publishes every tier. Solo is 250 dollars a month for 200 researched contacts, Explore 900 for 800, Scale 2,500 for 2,500. Only Solo is contract-free. The others want a quarter up front. Unlimited users from the middle tier up.
- **Alta:** no public grid. The Salesforce AppExchange showed a Starter tier at 1,250 dollars per company per month.
- **Topo:** no grid. Third-party listings report 69 euros per user per month, unconfirmed on the vendor page.
- **Clay:** Launch is 185 dollars a month (167 annual) with 2,500 Data Credits and 15,000 Actions. Growth is 495. Seats are unlimited.
- **Apollo:** Basic is 49 dollars per user per month annual (59 monthly). Professional is 79, Organization 119 with a three-seat minimum. Credits move the rest.
- **Gong and Cognism:** no published price. Both are annual platform fee plus per-seat licenses.

## How to read the price: the three variables behind the invoice

Romàn says the headline number hides three things that set the real bill.
- **Per seat or per team:** a 49 dollar seat costs 147 a month for three reps and 490 for ten. A flat team price doesn't move.
- **Data bundled or billed apart:** Apollo and Artisan include contacts. Gong and Clay assume you bought data elsewhere.
- **Monthly, quarterly or annual billing:** he cites AnyBiz at 497 dollars a month on a quarterly rhythm, which asks for 1,491 before a first meeting is booked.
- Worked example for a team of three:
  - Apollo Basic costs 147 dollars a month on annual billing, or 1,764 dollars a year, before any credit top-ups.
  - Gojiberry costs 99 a month, or 1,188 a year, because the price covers the team.
  - 11x Growth starts at 45,000 dollars a year.
- Budget advice: a team with a few hundred dollars a month and three people should pick on arithmetic. A flat team price beats every per-seat tool, which triples before you start.
- On free tiers: "No meaningful free tier exists in this category." Treat a free plan as a way to see the interface and data quality on your own market. Once you send at volume, you are on a paid plan.
- Fit for small teams: most of the category prices for twenty people. "Two people can run an autonomous agent well, and they can't run a configuration project."

## Choosing by situation

Romàn's decision guide:
- **Need pipeline and nobody has time to build lists:** autonomous. The list is the bottleneck, and "a copilot sharpens a list you haven't got yet."
- **Reps sell well and need better preparation:** assistive (Clay for the research layer, Gong to read calls back). Neither adds names to the top of the funnel.
- **Want the machine to handle inbound enquiries:** an inbound qualification agent. It sits beside an outbound tool and does not compete for the same budget line.
- **Want to approve every message before it goes:** assistive by default, because full autonomy defeats that point. An approval step can also be added to an autonomous tool.
- **Have a RevOps person and a real budget:** Alta or Artisan, which reward teams that can put hours into configuration.
- **Budget is a few hundred a month and team of three:** a flat team price.
- Vendor-specific takes:
  - Clay: "Building is where it costs you. Fill rate is where it pays you back." Nothing runs until somebody designs it.
  - Apollo: one invoice instead of three is the argument for it, and the credit meter is the argument against.
  - Gong: sharp for a team that already talks to enough people, but it does nothing for the half that finds them.
  - Cognism: the annual contract ends it for most teams of two to ten. Phone-verified mobiles and EMEA coverage are why teams pay for it.

## Do AI sales agents work? Evidence and limits

- Romàn's verdict: "AI saves real time and it doesn't replace your sales team." They replace the repetitive half of an SDR job (list building, research, first drafts) and leave judgment to you. You set the ICP, approve positioning and take the call.
- No independent body audits reply rates in this market, and nobody has run one identical list through ten agents. He did not run a controlled test and says an invented percentage would tell you less than admitting that. Treat vendor figures as customer stories, not benchmarks, and ask for the sample size.
- Gojiberry customer results he cites:
  - Wispra rebuilt outbound around intent signals and now sources 60 percent of its weekly demos that way.
  - Mindflow wrote only to accounts that had moved and lifted its reply rate by 31 percent.
  - GTE Localize shifted from volume email to intent-driven outreach and passed 100 meetings booked.
  - Baur Software doubled its network without losing the personal tone.
- Limit of signal-based outreach: a signal engine reads what a market leaves behind (job changes, funding, hires, posts, competitor interactions). "When your buyers leave no trace online, no signal engine invents activity that isn't there." Rural contractors who never post, public bodies, or a market of about forty known companies gain little from an intent layer.
- Phone calls: some agents make them. 11x sells Julian for the phone, Alta's Alex handles AI voice conversations, Artisan offers a dialer add-on that briefs a human before the call. Check whether the voice agent talks to the prospect or only prepares your rep.

## Why Romàn built Gojiberry around signals, and what it does

- Before Gojiberry he ran Coco.ai and spent his days prospecting on socials. He noticed the people who replied were never the ones on any list. They had just changed jobs, posted about a problem, or started following a competitor.
- He chased those signals one browser tab at a time. It worked but "stopped scaling around thirty accounts." Past that, the reason for writing collapses first, and a month later you are running mass outreach with a first-name variable. Hence Gojiberry "starts from the signal rather than from a database."
- What a team of two to ten needs: "neither an AI employee priced against a salary nor a canvas to build one on." It needs the list to arrive already sorted, at a price that doesn't move when the third rep joins.
- How Gojiberry works:
  - Two AI agents. The first watches more than 30 buying signals (job changes, funding rounds, hiring, competitor engagement). The second writes to whatever clears your ICP filter, with the signal attached as the reason for writing.
  - ICP filtering comes first, so nothing unwanted reaches the inbox.
  - Email waterfall across 15+ providers, with contact data included in the plan.
  - Pro is 99 dollars a month for the whole team, 1,800 prospects contacted, no annual contract and no RevOps person required.
- Where he says it fits less well: markets that leave no traces online, and teams whose reps already have a full list and only need preparation help.
