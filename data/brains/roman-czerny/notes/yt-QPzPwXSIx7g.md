## Validate before building

- Romàn insists on validating the idea before writing code. He makes a 3-6 slide PowerPoint and takes calls with his ICP.
- The first signal is a prospect asking "how much is that?" at the end of the pitch. Willingness to pay is the real test.
- GojiBerry's version: he sold lead lists, manually finding high-intent leads for paying customers. When manual delivery got too hard, he built the product and added outreach.
- Channel order he recommends: start with outbound if you have no clients (fast feedback). Add inbound next, then paid once money comes in, and do SEO/GEO a bit every week to compound.
- GojiBerry reached $1M ARR in under 9 months with a multi-channel engine: inbound (LinkedIn, X, Reddit), outbound (LinkedIn, cold email), paid (Facebook/Meta ads, Trustmrr ads, influencers) and SEO/GEO (one freelancer plus one software).

## LinkedIn outreach with high-intent leads

- First check that your customers are actually on LinkedIn.
- Limit: about 150-180 invitations per week per account, so targeting must be precise.
- ICP-only targeting (job title + country, e.g. "CFOs in the US") no longer works. Many profiles are inactive for 3+ months or outdated.
- High-intent targeting gets "three five times more responses". Intent signals: engaging with your profile or company, engaging with competitors, posting meaningful content, hiring, recent job changes, engaging with relevant keywords or specific profiles.
- Most outreach tools only send; he avoids static databases because "data moves very fast" and prefers dynamic, signal-based lead feeds (his own tool, GojiBerry).
- Accounts: any LinkedIn account works, ideally branded with a headline and active. Stay under limits, don't spam, use a residential proxy. Nobody on his team or among customers has been banned.
- Setup in GojiBerry: paste the website, connect a LinkedIn account, let the agent define the ICP, pick signals, set the objective (book qualified demos).
- Results shown: one campaign at 71% acceptance and 52% reply rate. He runs 8+ accounts, e.g. one sending 29 invitations and 30 messages per day.
- Expected funnel per account: 200 invitations/week gives ~50% acceptance (100 people), ~50% reply (50 people), then roughly 8-12 booked demos. Varies by offer and market.

## Cold email at 6,000 emails per day

- Lead sources: (1) high-intent leads from GojiBerry, ~100-200 per day, enriched for emails; (2) scraping with the GojiBerry Chrome extension (Sales Navigator, post engagers, event attendees, search) then enrichment; (3) static databases like Apollo, which are easiest but get the fewest replies.
- Infrastructure: Instantly (used since 2022). About 124 inboxes across ~40 domains, 3 inboxes per domain, 50 emails per inbox per day (150 per domain). Buys pre-warmed or done-for-you accounts, never connects existing ones. Done-for-you domains look like the brand (e.g. a "team" version of the company domain) but need ~2 weeks warm-up.
- He doesn't optimize for cheap inboxes: "I'm trying to get business", so he pays for reliable infrastructure. Cost is about $500/month; one customer has lifetime value of roughly $400, so he needs one customer per month to break even.
- Results: 400,000+ emails sent, 1.43% reply rate excluding auto-replies.
- Content strategy: find what's hyped in the market, especially lead magnets that get thousands of LinkedIn comments, rebuild a similar blueprint, and email the same audience offering it (e.g. "I've built a blueprint about [topic]. Want to see it?"). Sequence is just 3 emails with varied wording and a simple signature.
- Settings: text-only emails, tracking of opens disabled (triggers spam filters), stop on auto-reply, prioritize new leads, send every day including Sunday (little competition, good replies).
- When someone replies, they get the blueprint plus demo video, screenshots, social proof and a booking link.

## LinkedIn inbound: building in public plus lead magnets

- Output: 500k-1M views per week. Two building-in-public accounts (his and his co-founder's), plus ~8 accounts total including employees and family "satellite" accounts posting lead-magnet content daily.
- Method: find the posts getting the most engagement on LinkedIn, adapt to your niche, post. Images made with ChatGPT. Tie viral topics (e.g. Claude Cowork) to the product.
- Lead magnet flow: people comment, he replies with the guide link; the same guide is reused in cold email. Example: a lead-magnet post with ~12,000 views.
- Satellite accounts like, comment and share each post to give the initial boost. He feeds each account's network daily by connecting with new people through GojiBerry.
- Funnel: post, lead magnet, profile visit, free trial, customer. Profile tips: "visit my website" link, clean photo, banner.

## Growing on X

- Two accounts (his and his co-founder's). He was inactive for 6 months, then posted 1-3 times a day. Reported 6M+ impressions in a year, 51k profile visits, 50k+ website visitors, all free.
- Follower hack: list the tools you use, find their founders on X, and publish genuine posts about how those tools helped your company. "Founders love free marketing." A post tagging a large SaaS account (300k+ followers) got retweeted and brought 100-500 followers at once.
- After that: follow and interact with good creators, then build in public with actionable proof, not just numbers (e.g. "I did this, I got that"). Tag the biggest players so they retweet.
- Content mix: promotional-with-value, lead magnet (a "course" comment post drew 300+ comments), and journey posts. Pinned post got 29,000 views and 3,000+ site clicks.
- Articles get strong reach. He pays for the top Premium plan (~$50-60/month), saying you won't go viral on the free plan. Posts are drafted by voice-noting ChatGPT. He recommends one X account per co-founder.

## Reddit and YouTube SEO

- Reddit: 30M+ impressions, 13M+ views cited elsewhere, hundreds of customers; helps SEO, backlinks and LLM visibility. Top posts 100k-200k views.
- Warm-up: join niche subreddits (SaaS, MicroSaaS, Startup Ideas). Days 1-7 only upvote (~50/day); days 8-14 only comment (~50/day) with no links; then post one post without a link, then one with a small link 5 days later, then everywhere with proof.
- Posts: long storytelling with proof (e.g. "I paid five influencers on LinkedIn to promote my SaaS", "Rejected from Y Combinator", ranking #1 on Product Hunt). Every marketing win becomes a post. "If you try to promote, it's not going to work. You always need to entertain."
- Process: voice-chat the story to ChatGPT (often in French), translate and format. Post 3x per week on 12 subreddits with the same post and a tweaked title. Friends upvote, targeting 10 upvotes fast.
- Moderation: ban haters rather than argue. He's been banned 3-4 times; recreate accounts on a different browser. No VPN or proxies.
- YouTube SEO: make ~5-minute review videos on competitors (e.g. "PhantomBuster 2026 review"): thumbnail with his face over their site screenshot, walk through features and pricing, then "there is an alternative" and pitch GojiBerry. Some videos drive hundreds of views from people searching the competitor's name. Advice: map all competitors and rank on each.

## B2B influencer marketing

- Find influencers by searching LinkedIn for your niche keywords and seeing who gets traction.
- Offer: he writes the post, builds the lead magnet and visual; the influencer posts and replies to every comment with the guide link and software. He only does one format, viral lead-magnet posts (no videos, no point-of-view posts).
- Price: $300-$800 per post, usually about $500, which can bring $1,000-$3,000 in ARR. Negotiation tactic: if they ask for more, trade by adding posts (e.g. $1,000 for two posts). Ask the influencer to introduce friends. He keeps a pool of 5-10 influencers, working with them every 2 weeks.
- Rules: resource must be sent in comments, not DMs (visible proof, and non-commenters can still click). Don't pay upfront on the first deal; pay once it's live. Ask for proof and impressions of recent posts.
- Vetting: check the comments. The same people liking, or AI-generated comments, means bad. Fake-engagement influencers brought no visitors; a good one (Eric) delivered about 1,000 site visitors per post.
- Agencies save time but cost about twice as much per influencer.

## Demos, routing and Meta ads

- Demos: 5-8 per day, could get ~20. All content, outreach and ads push a blueprint, and the blueprint pushes a booking link. He took all demos himself, which crowds out marketing: three hours of content can drive 50 customers versus 5-8 calls converting 4-6.
- Calendly routing: not an ICP match gets the blueprint or group demo; small teams (0-5 salespeople) get a webinar or blueprint ("for 99.9% of teams, you don't actually need a demo"); only big teams book a call. He's hiring two salespeople plus a freelancer.
- Demo style: don't sell. Ask what they want to achieve, show the product step by step, show results. Close with the 7-day free trial ("you will not spend $1 before knowing that it works"). Demo-to-trial rate is 70%.
- Meta ads: spy on SaaS competitors' ads in the Meta ad library, copy and adapt them. Static ads and memes via ChatGPT, UGC via Arcads, some agency video. Runs 67 ads, retargeting plus lookalike acquisition campaigns, pixel on the site and a Stripe webhook to track trials.
- Numbers: ~€7,000 (~$9,000) spent last month, under 10% of revenue. Retargeting cost about €91 per trial, ~35% trial-to-customer, so ~€270 per customer versus ~€400 LTV; one day showed €75 per trial and another €6 (possible winner). Goal: scale to €3,000-10,000/day at the same cost per result.
