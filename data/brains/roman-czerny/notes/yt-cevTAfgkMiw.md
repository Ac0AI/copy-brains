## Roman's LinkedIn intent-signal outreach model (Goji Berry)

- Roman is co-founder of Goji Berry (Goji AI), started with three people in August. Roman reports about 1,800 customers, mainly in the US, plus France and Europe.
- Before Goji, he sold a SaaS called Koko AI (WhatsApp for e-commerce, aimed at Shopify merchants). No database or Sales Navigator list was good enough, so he hand-picked leads on LinkedIn.
- His manual method: type keywords like "Klaviyo" or "fulfillment e-commerce", see who interacts with them, and build a lead list one by one. He later realized this was already high-intent outreach.
- His definition of a signal: the right job title and location, active on LinkedIn in the past 48 hours, plus some intent.
- He ranks intent from low to high:
  - Active on LinkedIn in the past 48 hours (already roughly doubles response).
  - Interacted with a competitor or a specific keyword.
  - Just hired, just fundraised, or interacting with an industry leader.
  - Viewed your profile or followed your company page (warmest).
- Goji works the warmest signals first.
- His thesis: reply and connection rates on traditional outreach keep falling. There are two ways to book more demos:
  - Volume: cold email, for example 10,000 emails a day (he does this too), at the risk of burning accounts.
  - Targeting: on LinkedIn you can only reach about 200 connections a week, so maximize the return on those 200 by contacting only high-intent leads.

## Setting up signal agents

- Goji's flow has four steps: set up an agent, get contacts, send the campaign, handle replies in the inbox.
- A signal agent starts with ICP filters (for example developers in San Francisco in SaaS), then adds tracked signals.
- Signals he lists:
  - Engagement with your own page or profile.
  - Profile views and new company-page followers.
  - Keywords or topics (people who like, comment on or post about them).
  - Specific LinkedIn profiles, such as an industry expert whose audience cares about LinkedIn outreach but may not know Goji exists.
  - Competitor pages (he tracks HubSpot-style pages).
  - Companies that raised funds and people who changed jobs.
  - A "top active profiles in your ICP" option.
- Keyword signals track what people engage with, not what they say themselves. Likes mean they liked posts with the keyword, comments mean they commented, and posts mean they posted.
- GDPR example: a data officer engaging with GDPR posts is a reason to reach out about what they may be doing wrong.
- The "most active profiles" signal samples about 2,000 ICP members and returns only the top fraction (shown as 5%, in practice closer to 0.1%). It suits niches with no keyword signals, such as HR people in Norway. It is lower intent, but those people will see the connection request.
- A signal agent can track at most 15 signals.
- Volume target: at least 25 leads per day, enough to feed 25 daily outreach actions.
- Optimization loop:
  - Once demos arrive, check which keyword or signal produced them.
  - Delete keywords that produce zero leads and replace them.
  - Review the launch history (leads scanned vs. leads that made the cut) and the per-signal insights.
- Sample numbers from his own account: 239 leads scanned and 5 kept; 305 scanned and 8 kept; some keywords returned zero out of 264 or 61 and were candidates for removal. A zero from a person or page can mean it is inactive (for example, a banned company page).
- If an agent returns no leads, he suggests switching from "high precision" to "discovery". Discovery gives broader matches, including adjacent roles.
- He hasn't touched his own agents in about six months because results were fine; low-volume keywords need changing more often.

## Lookalike agents and segmenting by awareness

- A lookalike agent starts from the LinkedIn URL of your best customer. It matches on industry, country, job title and behavior on LinkedIn, and can include similar roles or related industries.
- It runs daily and finds more leads with a match percentage. It gives less control than signal agents, but it's useful for finding ICP matches without tracking explicit signals.
- He separates agents by awareness so each gets its own campaign and results can be compared:
  - "Solution aware": people interacting with competitors.
  - "Super hot": people who interacted with his profile or page, viewed his profile or followed the company page, plus people interacting with his own team.
- His campaign results:
  - Solution-aware: 62% acceptance, 49% reply.
  - Super hot: 61% acceptance, 30% reply.
- For comparison, he says a great campaign on other tools is about 20% acceptance and 20% reply.
- Beyond signals, Goji also has an AI "ask" feature that searches the web (including Google News) for leads with a custom signal. His example: CEOs who run webinars, or companies that closed a Series B or above in San Francisco in the past month. The signal then becomes the personalization hook ("I saw you run weekly webinars").
- Leads can also be imported by CSV, via the Chrome extension from Sales Navigator, or from first-degree connections.
- He used first-degree connections early on, to get fast responses when starting a new business. He is cautious with them, and Goji limits messages to existing connections to 30 per day.

## Messaging: the blueprint / lead magnet play

- His best-performing campaign asks permission to send a lead magnet instead of pitching. Example message: "Hello, I've just created a blueprint about how we get 12 demos per week using AI. Just reply yes and I'll send it to you." A variant: "we booked 12 demos in 5 days using warm leads plus on LinkedIn. I wrote a short doc breaking down exactly how we did it... Want me to send it over?"
- When someone replies yes, he sends a blueprint with a video of his face explaining what they do and how the lead can get value. At the bottom are a free-trial link and a demo-booking link.
- His view: "to get demos, the blueprint strategy is by far the best strategy in B2B SaaS."
- Why it works:
  - The reader should have to think as little as possible. A long message forces a 2-5 minute reply, which busy people won't give.
  - Replying "yes" costs almost nothing, while missing out on "how to get 12 demos a week" feels costly.
  - Even a little value in the resource keeps the lead engaged, so they watch videos and click links, and some convert.
- Goji's AI ice breaker names the intent signal that triggered, adds context, then asks for a conversation. Roman says it beats what about 90% of people write, so unless you're in the top 10% of outreach writers, it will beat your own first message. He prefers to follow up manually.
- One account of his sends only AI ice-breaker messages and reaches an acceptance rate that's high, with a 50% reply rate on AI messages.
- Other formats that he says perform well:
  - A CFO-focused breakfast invitation in a big European city (in person, closing many deals).
  - Personalized case studies.
  - Personalized Loom videos ("I made a video about your business, may I send it to you?").
- Campaign options in Goji include invitation notes, AI personalized messages, follow-ups, voice messages, profile visits and email (a multichannel addition). It doesn't support InMail yet. Campaigns can be generated by AI from a selected lead list, a goal (start conversations or book demos) and a tone.

## Account safety and profile quality

- None of his roughly 1,800 customers has had a hard ban, he says. The risk comes from running multiple automation tools on one account or using a fake LinkedIn account that hasn't been warmed up (which may get locked and asked for an ID).
- He has used automation tools for about 10 years. The worst result was a LinkedIn message saying automation isn't allowed, about three years ago.
- His safety rules:
  - Use a residential proxy in your own country (Goji runs in the cloud from that country) so you aren't disconnected.
  - Respect recommended weekly limits, which vary by account type: new, active or Sales Navigator.
  - Messages are spread over about five hours instead of being sent in bursts.
- His reasoning: LinkedIn has no interest in banning people doing real business. High reply and connection rates are a good signal for the platform; only spam or nonsense gets restricted.
- Profile quality drives acceptance rate. Accounts with no picture, banner or job history see poor acceptance. A LinkedIn profile gives proof of identity that cold email lacks, and acts like a sales page for anyone who looks at it.

## Who the approach works for, and closing advice

- Roman's customer mix: about 50% B2B SaaS (easy to find customers on LinkedIn), a large share of services and agencies, and about 25% brick-and-mortar businesses and professionals such as doctors and dentists.
- For brick-and-mortar, he says Goji works no worse than any other tool, but high-intent leads are scarce because plumbers and dentists aren't active on LinkedIn. He advises scraping Google Maps or using another tool for them.
- Closing advice: whether or not you use Goji, high-intent outreach beats volume. Take time to find leads, find a real reason to contact each, and personalize genuinely, because inboxes are flooded with AI nonsense. He says, "people are still looking to buy from humans."
