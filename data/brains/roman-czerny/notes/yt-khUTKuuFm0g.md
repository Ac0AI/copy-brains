## Positioning: Gojiberry as the cheap link between intent signals and outreach

- Romàn is CMO of Gojiberry, a B2B lead-gen SaaS. It started as "signals only": enter your website and it lists leads in your niche showing buying intent.
- User interviews showed customers had lead lists but didn't know what to do with them, so outreach (LinkedIn, email) was added. That kept users returning and let them test whether the leads were any good.
- The earlier SaaS, Cocoi (e-commerce/Klaviyo/SMS marketing), was 100% outreach. Romàn found e-merchants by searching LinkedIn and closed them in demos, which led to selling intent-based lead lists.
- He treats "best data" as a non-claim: Apollo and similar tools all say it. "Intent data" was a hot topic in the US, and the promise of "contact high-intent leads" translates to "we bring you business".
- His thesis on red oceans: "if it's a red ocean, it means that there is business, and if there is business, if you are seen, well you simply take the business."
- Competitor gap: tools did signals or outreach, rarely both. One good competitor, Autom-style, charged about $600/month for its cheapest plan; Gojiberry's $99 made it accessible.
- Product direction: weekly new signals and AI features (chat that builds campaigns, near-automatic replies). He wants a "collective brain" where data across all customers shows which lead types respond to which offers (for example, growth managers respond 3x more than sales managers).

## Pricing, trial and funnel structure

- Self-serve plan: $99/month, includes 2 LinkedIn seats, with credits for extra emails/phone numbers. Mostly solo users and small teams (PLG).
- Teams segment (5+ seats): 2-week to 1-month pilot, a shared Slack channel, then quarterly or annual contracts. Harder to crack, but bigger tickets and longer retention.
- Price never changed from $99; the offer was tested heavily instead.
- Trial: 7 days, card required. Reasons: costs from API/enrichment abuse, and it filters out non-serious users. Personally he would skip the trial and charge $99 directly.
- A/B test of a $1 trial vs free trial (card required): the free trial earned more despite more users and removed fake/temporary cards.
- About 20% of site traffic is from India, with very few paying customers there.
- Trial-to-paid conversion is about 40%. He attributes it to the card on file plus near-immediate leads, since users stay engaged waiting for replies.
- Funnel: every lead magnet has a "book a call" button. Small teams go to a near-daily webinar; large teams go to a sales call (Romàn or the sales rep). Big names (Qonto, Revolut, HubSpot, Nokia) often appear as a solo signup who then brings colleagues, after which a head of department asks to talk.

## Growth by phase: $0 to 10k MRR via manual outreach and selling before building

- Earlier Gojiberry versions had failed twice. For v3, Romàn told his developer to stop coding until the need was validated: "Sell the solution, and then as soon as you sell it, we start coding it."
- He sold manually with a crude 6-slide PowerPoint: $99/month for 100 leads with purchase intent. He found the leads himself on LinkedIn for an afternoon per customer, which taught him how the product would work.
- Tools: Walaxy (scrape people who liked relevant LinkedIn posts, run sequences), Instantly (email) with pre-warmed domains. Never use your own or brand-new domains.
- Message was a simple "I can help you get better leads, let's talk". Several demos a day.
- Pricing tests: sometimes 500 leads for $500/month, 100 for $100/month.
- Demo-to-paid conversion was about 70%, helped by a money-back guarantee if the leads didn't fit.
- Delegating lead-finding to Fiverr freelancers was a disaster. He closed the agency and moved to the tool.
- His advice: if starting from scratch, do outreach first, because you can launch in 2 days and test the offer.

## Reddit: $10k to $25k MRR

- He ran about 10 accounts with proxies, posting in about 50 SaaS subreddits a day. Workflow: voice-talk stories to ChatGPT (how he sold his old SaaS, how to find leads), turn them into long posts, change titles per subreddit, drop the product link inside valuable posts.
- Friends boosted posts. He hit the top of SaaS categories, with days of about 5,000 visitors. Of 5,000 visitors, roughly 10 to 20 became customers.
- Reddit leads booked more calls than LinkedIn leads, as they needed more trust-building.
- Totals: about 300,000 to 400,000 visitors and over 14 million views in 6 months, at no ad cost.
- Downsides: very aggressive toward self-promotion, banned accounts, brand nickname "Spamberry", harassment (fake calendar bookings, parasitic SEO threads like "Gojiberry got my LinkedIn banned" that rank on Google). Reddit users are not the best buyers; heads of sales don't browse there.
- He says it is now much harder than when he did it. A gentler route he suggests: distribute lead magnets without your brand name. His verdict: worth it to test how an idea is received and how the site converts.

## LinkedIn lead magnets with Claude: $25k to about $250k MRR

- He noticed "comment 'Claude'" posts getting 3,000 to 5,000 comments. Since Gojiberry uses Claude in its backend, he made Claude-themed lead magnets, for example "how to book more appointments with Claude", where the resource pushes Gojiberry.
- Results: 2,000 comments per post, 1,000 to 3,000 MRR per post; posts sometimes reach 200,000 views. ARR went from about 1M to almost 3M largely from this.
- Lead magnet page structure: founder intro, a demo video of the tool, a playbook explaining why intent data beats generic lists, how to find leads with Gojiberry, templates, a book-a-call button, case studies. Effectively a sales page.
- Metric: "The more lead magnets you distribute, the more MRR you bring in."
- Operations: 3 lead magnets per day across 3 accounts, a new magnet weekly, a French account reusing the same structure. He manages all team accounts on X and LinkedIn.
- All three founder profiles and the company page got banned from LinkedIn 2 days before the recording, a typical outcome for outreach tools. He notes few channels are truly owned.

## Buying LinkedIn influencer posts

- About 100+ influencers: about 50 managed by Romàn, 50 via the agency Obvious (about 20 posts/month, fixed monthly price).
- Sourcing method 1: approach accounts with strong traction who don't run lead magnets, saying "I'm looking to buy posts". Offer lower figures, but Gojiberry writes everything and pays upfront.
- Method 2: search "Claude" in the LinkedIn search bar, find lead-magnet posts, ask for a post "exactly like this one" and negotiate.
- Execution: give the influencer the post word for word plus the image, and have them reply to every comment with the magnet link.
- Pricing: small freelancers about $200 to $250 per post, often with outsized engagement; $500 for established or ambassador accounts. Sweet spot was $250 to $500. Executive-level accounts cost more but reach big companies.
- Pay-per-click alternative: nano.xyz at about €2 per highly qualified click (limited supply since competitors joined).
- Tracking: a simple Excel sheet of who posts each week, what content was prepared, and whether it performs. Keep what performs, stop and revisit the rest.
- He says very few companies pay LinkedIn influencers, so deals are easy, and the value is huge: a $1,000 to $10,000 post can equal 2.5 to 3 signed clients in LTV.

## Meta ads, X and AI usage

- Ads are about 10% of traffic, roughly €2,000/day, run by an outsourced friend. Creative is UGC, mostly a creator holding up the product. UGC agency results were inconclusive on TikTok, but the UGC works in ads.
- Unit economics: trial cost about €96; with 40% trial-to-paid, customer acquisition cost about €230 to €240. LTV about €500 after one year, so around 2x and profitable within about a month and a half. He notes ads cash is advanced and platform-dependent, and ad tracking is unmeasured (no attribution tool). Impressions also help demos and other channels.
- X: posts only in English, follows only US/UK accounts, long voice-to-GPT posts, lead magnets, videos and launches. One video reached 800,000 views and gave about 1,000 subscribers. Not the biggest revenue channel but a stable audience. Accounts he follows: Tibo Maker, Marc Lou, an Origami competitor, and others.
- Current numbers: ARR $3.2M, MRR about €235,000 to €236,000.
- AI use: ChatGPT voice for posts, GPT image generation for visuals, a note-taker for sales calls that drafts follow-up emails, Cursor to build his own Chrome extensions for LinkedIn automation (previously a five-figure developer cost). In the product, AI qualifies and scores leads, writes personalized messages and orders outreach hottest first.

## Y Combinator and fundraising

- He finished YC a week before recording. He is CMO, his partner Pierre Elliot is CEO and pitched at demo day.
- The cost is 7%, and he calls it worth it "a billion times": network, mindset shift (from "sell at 100M" to a much bigger ambition), and mentorship from people who sold companies for $300M to $600M or IPO'd.
- Mechanics: 3 months, biweekly goals reported to a small group, partner one-on-ones, talks from Sam Altman and Airbnb's co-founder. They worked 18 hours a day in a San Francisco house. He is convinced they would never have gone from 1M to 3.2M in 3 months otherwise.
- The YC logo makes selling in the US much easier. YC also gave over $2M in AI tokens.
- Raise: gave up 10% for $4M on a SAFE capped at $40M, total dilution 17% (83% retained). Investors came to them via demo day, with money arriving quickly. They converted to a US entity; the French company is inactive.
- Plan: spend the $4M on product, go-to-market help and ads, or choose between a Series A and a cash-flowing business. Advice: spend at least 10 days in San Francisco (use Luma to find events, especially Y Combinator-linked ones).
