## Off-platform contact is banned until a contract starts

- Ross says chatting with prospects on WhatsApp, by phone, or via emails or phone numbers posted in job posts breaks the Upwork Terms of Service.
- His reasoning: off-platform it is easier for a scammer to ask for personal or banking details, and harder to report them. Calls should be started through Upwork, so Upwork can help if someone tries to scam or take advantage of him.
- Communication outside Upwork (such as a shared Slack channel) is fine only once a contract has started. He notes many clients still keep chatting on Upwork during a project because files and communication stay in one place.
- His script when a prospect asks to move off-platform: say he can only chat on Upwork until a contract starts.
  - A genuinely innocent client (often new to Upwork) agrees without argument. Some have thanked him for flagging the rules.
  - "If they keep pushing, I won't work with them."

## Always invoice through Upwork, and why the protections matter

- Invoicing a client off-platform, or getting added to their payroll, after meeting on Upwork breaks the terms and can get an account shut down.
- Ross also treats it as a risk. Upwork's setup protects freelancers:
  - **Fixed price:** the client funds the first milestone up front. Upwork holds it in escrow and releases it when the job is done, which avoids clients who run off without paying.
  - **Hourly:** the client pays after approving logged time. If they don't, Upwork still pays for work that qualifies for hourly payment protection.
- Ross finds the setup "really brilliant for everyone", both as a freelancer and as an Upwork client himself. He can always reach Upwork support, and never has to hand out bank card or account details.
- The one exception is the conversion process, where the client pays a conversion fee to take the relationship off-platform. Only then can he invoice directly or join their non-Upwork payroll. Upwork support checks with the freelancer too, and he is under no obligation to agree.

## Never manipulate reviews or ratings

- It is fine to ask a real client for a review (Upwork also reminds clients).
- Ross lists what gets accounts banned:
  - Creating a second account, posing as a client, and "hiring" your own profile.
  - Having friends or family hire you, real or fake, just to leave five stars.
  - Applying to scam posts that promise five-star reviews in exchange for Amazon reviews, YouTube clicks, social follows or PayPal transactions.
  - Threatening to withhold finished work or leave negative feedback unless the client rates you well.
  - Paying or bribing clients for good reviews.
- His position: "The best way to get good feedback on your Upwork profile is by doing high-quality work for real clients." That is also the only way to build Job Success Score and reach Top Rated status or higher.

## Don't pass off work that isn't yours (subcontractors are fine)

- Ross distinguishes this from subcontracting. Teams and agencies are allowed on Upwork.
- His example: he runs an agency, DigitalME, and works with several business managers through Upwork. They are part of his agency and he pays them through Upwork to help on client projects.
- What is not allowed is claiming rights or originality you don't have:
  - Buying a personal-use Canva template on Etsy and telling the client you created it.
  - Removing watermarks from stock footage you didn't buy and delivering it.
- On AI: some projects may require disclosing AI use, and generative tools like Stable Diffusion can produce borderline copyright-infringing images. Ross errs on the side of caution and uses these tools "for inspiration, not final products".

## Use your real identity and never share your account

- **Real name and photo:** a fake name or generic profile image can get an account suspended. Ross's analogy is that you wouldn't do business with people wearing ski masks or paper bags over their heads. Clients want to know they are working with a real person they can trust.
- **Privacy is still possible.** Profile visibility has three levels:
  - Public: visible to everyone and indexable by search engines (helpful for visibility).
  - Upwork users only: visible to logged-in clients, not indexed by search engines.
  - Private: visible only to someone you send a direct link to.
- Upwork also hides your full last name until you interact with a client, for example by sending a proposal or exchanging messages.
- **Never let anyone else use your talent profile.** Agency and client profiles can have multiple users, but a talent profile must be only you. Someone else could deliver low-quality work, break the terms or run a scam. Sharing login details also exposes your personal information, clients and finances. Even a stranger or someone you know with a "story" should be sent to Upwork support instead.

## Log hours honestly

- Padding hours risks losing account privileges.
- Clients can allow manual time logging without Upwork's desktop tracker. If you use that option, track hours separately in a tool such as Everhour or Toggl and log only hours actually worked.
- Ross logs manually. He is "fully transparent beforehand with clients" about how and why he logs that way.
- His recommended default is Upwork's desktop app: no need to transfer hours between trackers, and it is simple to use.

## Avoid illegal or shady jobs and services

- Ross's benchmark: if a job or service is generally illegal or widely regarded as shady, don't do it through Upwork.
- His list (not exhaustive):
  - Helping people cheat on tests or writing students' essays.
  - Sexually explicit content, child exploitation, or asking children to do any work (even a legitimate child-actor search).
  - Mining or scraping data that isn't yours to collect.
  - Fake profiles or "bot" followers.
  - Fake reviews (on or off Upwork).
  - Fake news reports.
  - Hacking any person or system.
  - Exposing private individuals' details or researching them for others.
  - Phishing tools or phishing attempts.
  - Violent content or threats.
- If someone offers or requests this kind of work, don't engage. Flag the content or message to Upwork and let them review it.
