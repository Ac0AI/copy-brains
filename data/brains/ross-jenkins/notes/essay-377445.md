## Evaluating traffic by revenue, not volume

Ross argues that website traffic only matters if it converts into revenue. "The name of the game is revenue. If your website traffic isn't converting into revenue, it's essentially worthless." He criticizes digital-marketing agencies that report raw traffic volume as success: "visits alone mean nothing."

- Break traffic down into sub-metrics to scale online growth.
- Once you know which traffic sources are most valuable, increase those efforts. Pull back on the lowest-performing sources.
- Ross lists five metrics to run an online campaign: unique visitors, bounce rate, referral traffic sources, CPV/RPV, and page views with average visit duration.

## Unique visitors as a campaign health check

Ross treats total unique visitors per campaign as a starting point for deciding whether a campaign is worth continuing.

- A unique visit is counted once. If someone clicks a Facebook ad, lands on the site, and returns an hour later, the second visit is not counted.
- Raw visits can mislead. Example: a blog post drives 500 visits, but 300 are the same person, leaving 200 uniques.
- Track uniques over time per source. A steady decline signals the source is drying up and is less effective at introducing new people to the business.

## Using bounce rate to find disconnects

Bounce rate measures visitors who leave without clicking to another page or taking an action such as submitting a form or completing a conversion goal.

- A high bounce rate suggests visitors aren't finding what they expected.
- Likely causes he names: an offer that overpromises and a site that underdelivers, poor user experience, or confusing navigation.
- Fix: install a heat-map tool to see where visitors click and how far they scroll, which helps locate the disconnect.

## Reverse-engineering referral sources by cost per conversion

Ross says identifying every traffic source lets you reverse engineer which is most valuable in terms of revenue generated and cost per visit.

- Example: if one source delivers sales at a $15 conversion cost and another delivers the same traffic value at $8 per conversion, scale the cheaper source.
- Track organic Google traffic, referral visits from embedded links on other sites, and social media.
- When running multiple campaigns, use Google's UTM parameters to analyze all the data in Google Analytics.

## CPV and RPV for campaign profitability

Cost-per-visitor (CPV) and revenue-per-visitor (RPV) show how profitable each campaign is, so you can budget and plan for growth. Ross says to know these numbers for every traffic source.

- RPV = revenue from a channel divided by visitors it drove. Example: a Facebook ads campaign with 100 tracked sales worth $10,000 and 1,000 visitors gives an RPV of $10 ($10,000 / 1,000).
- CPV = total dollars spent in a channel divided by the visitors it drove.
- Comparing CPV to RPV per channel tells you where to put budget.

## Page views and visit duration to guide optimization

Ross recommends tracking pages viewed per visit, time on site per visit, and what visitors ultimately do (leave without acting, submit a form, make a purchase) in Google Analytics.

- Reverse engineer the actions that generate revenue to make better optimization decisions.
- If most conversions follow interaction with a specific content piece, send paid traffic directly to that content asset.
- If most conversions happen after a visitor stays at least three minutes, improve content and create more long-form articles to raise average visit duration.
