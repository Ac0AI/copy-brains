## LinkedIn profile setup as the base of website traffic

- Ross treats the profile as the first step in driving traffic from LinkedIn. It does not need to be complex, but it must cover the basics.
- A winning profile has three parts:
  - a clean, presentable headshot
  - a brief introduction of what you do
  - a concise, straight-to-the-point work history
- His reasoning is credibility: "the more organized and professional a profile is, the more credible it appears to a target audience."
- Context he cites: LinkedIn has 740 million members worldwide and almost 65 million in the U.S. A 2015 Recruiter Nation survey found 87% of recruiters use it to look for talent.
- He argues that few industry pros use LinkedIn as a route to website traffic, even though it can be one.

## Growing first-degree connections to widen reach

- Ross says to build first-degree connections, meaning people you choose to accept as a connection.
- More connections expand the network and raise the chance of sending people to your website.
- The mechanism he gives: a higher connection count makes it more likely other users see you in their "most viewed connections" list. It also broadens reach, so more people see and click the website link on your profile.
- Target: start with at least 500 first-degree connections, then keep building.
- Three ways to keep building:
  - personalize connection requests
  - explore LinkedIn groups
  - engage more with your current connections

## Joining relevant LinkedIn groups

- Ross recommends joining groups relevant to your industry for three reasons:
  - You can build and gain more connections there.
  - You can post your own content in the groups to entice target audiences to visit your site and see more of it.
  - You find and connect with like-minded people in your industry, which can further boost traffic.
- He notes that some groups have more than 500,000 members, which makes them useful for reach.

## Creating your own LinkedIn group

- Ross suggests starting a group in addition to joining others. Benefits:
  - It establishes you as an expert in your industry.
  - The more people who join, the broader your reach and the more traffic you drive.
  - You can message members with helpful resources or articles from your website, which strengthens your expert reputation.
  - If members share those links with others in the industry, the traffic effect is amplified.
- He flags the cost: running a group means constantly engaging the audience. Ways to do that:
  - post content regularly
  - ask questions
  - comment on posts

## Posting frequency and content rhythm

- Ross compares LinkedIn to Facebook and Twitter. The audience differs slightly, but the same principle applies: to drive traffic, give people a steady stream of engaging content.
- Share new content at least once a day. It can include images, videos and other striking elements. This keeps attention and entices people to visit your site for more.
- Avoid oversharing (posting too much or too often) and spamming, which he says "can have the opposite effect."
- Keep updates engaging and relevant, and post at a reasonable rate.

## Writing curiosity-driven link headlines

- Ross calls engaging titles on links a proven traffic driver, because they pique curiosity and interest.
- Weak example: a link simply called "My Blog".
- Stronger examples:
  - "Boost Your Page Rankings with These X Tips"
  - "Top X Ways to Increase Your Website's Traffic"
- His rules for titles:
  - simple and concise
  - appeal to what the target audience is looking for
  - effectively summarize what they get by clicking

## Showing latest blog posts on your profile with an RSS feed

- Ross recommends adding a Really Simple Syndication (RSS) feed to the profile. It displays your last couple of blog posts from your website.
- Steps as he describes them (this was LinkedIn's Applications feature in 2021):
  1. Go to the "Applications" section of your profile.
  2. Select the application you want under "Add an Application".
  3. Copy-paste your website's blog URL into the relevant field.
- Two benefits he names: it improves the look of the profile, and it gives visitors extra motivation to check out your site.
