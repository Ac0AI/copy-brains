## Doubling Upwork income: proposal volume and responsiveness

- Ross says sending more proposals is one of the most effective ways to earn more on Upwork. His funnel logic: more proposals lead to more interviews, more interviews lead to more jobs.
- His worked example: 10 proposals produce two interviews, and one interview becomes a job. Bidding on only five jobs might have missed the client he actually signed.
- Volume also builds skill. More practice writing proposals and interviewing means he lands a higher share of the jobs he pursues.
- He still reads job descriptions closely and only spends Connects on jobs that fit his work and income goals.
- When proposal volume rises, replies rise too, so he checks his Messages inbox throughout the day.
  - He works with clients in many time zones and uses the Upwork desktop and mobile apps to stay responsive.
  - He doesn't reply in the middle of the night, but shifts his hours a bit earlier or later when clients are likely in the office.
  - He checks notifications for interview invitations and periodically reviews "My Stats" (under "Find Work") to confirm he answers every invite within 24 hours.
- Before any of this, he keeps the profile current: specialized profiles, certifications, location, portfolio items. He also works toward Talent Badges (he holds Expert-Vetted).

## Building multiple income streams on Upwork

- Ross says doubling income within 12 months means actively cultivating new opportunities, not just bidding more. He uses all three channels Upwork offers:
  1. Proposals through Talent Marketplace.
  2. Paid consultations.
  3. Productized services listed in Project Catalog.
- Paid consultations earn in two ways:
  - One-time fees from clients who want to pick his brain.
  - A route to being hired as a long-term partner on bigger projects, because clients pay to learn about him and his services.
- Clients find consultation offers through search filters, but only if consultations are switched on in the profile. He calls it a quick change that adds money every month.
- He admits most of his income still comes from sending proposals and accepting interview invites, but Project Catalog income is growing.
- Catalog projects are not passive income, since he still does the work. The upside is that clients can buy at any time, and he sometimes wakes up to new project requests without sending a proposal.
- Listing services isn't enough on its own. He keeps sending proposals and collecting testimonials and talent badges, and over time catalog projects become a larger share of income.

## Weekly retainer contracts for steady income

- Ross proposes weekly-payment (retainer-style) contracts to smooth out fluctuating income, which worries many new independents.
- Example: a client pays $500 per week for five hours of availability. He is paid the $500 every week even if the client needs fewer than five hours.
- If a week runs over, he bills the extra hours hourly on top. Eight hours worked means three hours billed through the work diary plus the usual $500.
- Upwork proposals normally ask for an hourly rate or fixed project price, so he raises the weekly arrangement with the client during the interview.

## Using search filters to find long-term, steady-revenue jobs

- Ross says top Upwork users use search filters deliberately. First decide how long a client relationship you want and how much work you can take on.
- Filter choices he describes:
  - Project length "less than one month" for a few hours a week and a new client alongside existing work.
  - Project length "more than 6 months" for dependable long-term income.
  - Hours "more than 30 hrs/week" if you want to work with one client at a time.
- He combines these with other filters, such as job type, to get curated lists matching his length and pay criteria.
- His income structure: one or more steady long-term clients form a solid revenue base, then he builds on top with one-off projects, shorter contracts and booked paid consultations.

## Saving profitable searches and using Job Digests

- After a filter combination lands good clients, Ross saves the search ("save search", upper right) so he can return to it.
- Example search: "YouTube channel video editing" jobs, intermediate skill level, paying at least $50 per hour.
- Saved searches feed Upwork Job Digests, which deliver relevant jobs to his inbox. The more profitable searches he saves, the more relevant the digests.
- Job Digests are only available to Rising Talent, Top Rated, Top Rated Plus or Expert Vetted freelancers, which he cites as another reason to pursue those badges.

## Enterprise jobs and Talent Clouds

- Ross recommends applying to Enterprise client jobs. If hired, you may be added to that company's Talent Cloud, a subset of the marketplace for one Enterprise client's jobs and the talent they know and trust.
- Talent Cloud jobs can be any duration, not necessarily long-term. Many Enterprise clients post directly into their Talent Clouds, so membership gives first access to work that may never reach the standard Talent Marketplace.
- If no Enterprise jobs appear in the marketplace, he suggests watching the inbox for Enterprise invites. As your profile and expertise grow, they may find you.

## Raising your rates regularly

- Ross treats regular rate increases as part of growing revenue and says freelancers often overlook it, since cost of living rises each year.
- Factors that justify a raise:
  - Recent investment of time or money in developing skills and expertise.
  - New clients and projects over the past year, which grow the portfolio.
- His example: moving from $80 to $100 per hour is a 25% revenue increase with no extra hours worked.
- He points to Upwork's rate calculator for working out what to charge, especially if rates haven't moved in a while.
- His closing advice: make one change from the list, evaluate its impact, and keep decisions aligned with goals (income, professional path, client load, work-life balance).
