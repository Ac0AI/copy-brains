## Pandemic response: automate the business instead of cutting costs

Ross frames the Covid-19 market as having two options for a struggling business. One is cutting costs and raising funding to buy time until the storm passes. He calls this "rather unrealistic" unless the business sells something like hand sanitizer. The other is to "push your boundaries and absolutely transform your business by automating it." He backs the second option, drawing on about 10 years of running his marketing agency DigitalME and doing this kind of work for clients.

## Automation targets: social media, email marketing and PPC

Ross names the three areas where businesses most often get stuck: social media strategy, email marketing and PPC (pay-per-click advertising). He argues that automating these channels is what lets a company keep trading during and after a disruption like a pandemic. He also says the goal is to be ready to "trade and serve your customers whether there's a second wave of Covid19 or not."

## Worked example: a gym moving its services online

Ross uses a gym as the model case and notes the same logic applies to dance, cooking or language classes. When gyms closed, many members cancelled, were afraid to return, or adjusted to training at home. The gyms that adapted followed this sequence:

1. Moved services online, with group and one-to-one workout sessions.
2. Created an irresistible free lead magnet (his example: a "dream abs at home" freebie) and pushed it across social media.
3. Used that freebie to build an email list quickly.
4. Used the list to pre-sell meal plans and 30-day fat-burn packages.
5. Put the content into a membership site, so revenue keeps coming in "in their sleep."

The pattern is: free lead magnet, social distribution, email list, pre-sold digital products, then a membership site that runs automatically.

## Knowing how is not the same as making it work

Ross says the automation steps themselves are not hard to understand. The difficulty is making the system reliably produce quality leads 24/7. He states that "there's a massive difference between knowing how to do something and making it work."

## Why not to DIY the tooling

Ross reports that clients repeatedly tell him they wish they had come to him earlier instead of trying to work out CRM platforms, ActiveCampaign and Infusionsoft on their own. His advice is to get expert help with the CRM and email automation stack rather than teaching yourself the tools while the business is under pressure.

## DigitalME's agency model

Ross started DigitalME to reshape the B2C and B2B markets. The agency has a team of experts involved through the whole process of taking a company online and getting it ready to trade and serve customers. The offer is end-to-end implementation (strategy, CRM, email automation and ads) rather than courses or advice alone. He contrasts this with the small business owners who bought a "how to create a webpage in 5 simple steps" course, got no leads, and asked for refunds.
