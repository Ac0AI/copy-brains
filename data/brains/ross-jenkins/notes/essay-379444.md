## UFC's business model and how it markets

Ross describes UFC as "one big brand" that follows the business model of pro wrestling. It promotes fighters by building their personas and stories rather than promoting the events themselves, which he calls an effective way to draw more people in.

- UFC is a single entity controlling promotions, regulations, contracts, broadcasts and the sanctioning body.
- It sets a fixed price for every fighter, and even fighter bonuses are fixed.
- UFC decides who fights whom, so fighters cannot cherry-pick opponents.
- Fighters mostly just follow through with the marketing strategy, and because the focus is on their stories, they are more engaged with fans.
- Because everything is produced in-house, UFC as a whole earns more, though the same is not true of its fighters.
- The downside for fighters: they have to pitch themselves to UFC to get hired, so their pay can be smaller than a boxer's.

## Boxing's fragmented model and promoter dynamics

Boxing is made up of many entities, with different promoters and sanctioning bodies. Ross contrasts it with UFC on who approaches whom and who controls the product.

- Promoters usually scout boxers and convince them to sign, rather than boxers pitching themselves.
- Once signed, the promoter sets up the boxer's fights. The price of promotion is a deal between the boxer's team and the promoting team.
- The promoter and manager have a say in whether a fight happens.
- Because the boxers are the product, their teams protect the boxer's legacy. Ross concludes that is why big fights rarely happen: "Each side is working hard to promote its title."
- Boxing has a clearer set of rules and, having been around so long, has produced legends.

## Boxing's traditional distribution channels

Ross says boxing leans on traditional media and live attendance, which suits its more traditional audience.

- Promotion is mostly in print (newspapers and sports magazines, in digital or physical form), plus radio and talk shows.
- Fights rely on live viewings at the MGM Grand, often with full seats.
- Fights air on Showtime PPV and ESPN+ by pay-per-view, and companies can advertise in between the fights.
- He calls boxing more accessible and more old school than UFC.

## UFC's digital-first distribution and pricing

Ross notes UFC focuses on digital media and draws more viewership from younger adults.

- Following its recent deal, UFC fights are broadcast on ESPN+.
- Each pay-per-view costs $70, on top of the monthly and yearly ESPN+ subscriptions.
- UFC uses social media to promote fighters and events.

## How Ross says UFC could improve: pay fairly and allow sponsorships

Ross argues UFC's strategy is solid but needs tweaks to be as stable as boxing.

- Pay fighters more. His reasoning: people are very conscious of what they buy, and if a product wasn't sourced ethically, they drop it even if they like it. The same applies to UFC.
- He says the impending lawsuit hurts the brand UFC is trying to create, and that "fighters should be paid fairly for their hard work."
- Allow fighters more sponsorships and deals. He argues restricting them can cost UFC opportunities from ad revenues.

## How Ross says UFC could improve: use digital marketing more fully

Ross's second recommendation is to exploit digital marketing more, given UFC's younger audience.

- Both sports are combat sports but have different audiences. Boxing's traditional audience is reached through traditional marketing, while UFC's younger audience sits online.
- UFC already uses social media, but he says it could optimize its use of different digital-marketing tools.
- His conclusion: "Utilizing every aspect of digital marketing will help UFC leverage the sport."

## General marketing lessons from the comparison

- Marketing plays a key role in why people show up to see athletes in the first place, even when they come for the fighters and their sportsmanship.
- Stories sell: every champion has a great story to tell, and promoting the persona and story (UFC's approach) pulls in more people than promoting the event.
- Match the channel to the audience: traditional print, radio and live attendance for an older audience, digital and social for a younger one.
- Owning the whole value chain (UFC) maximizes the organization's earnings and control, but can create friction and reputational risk with the people who create the product.
