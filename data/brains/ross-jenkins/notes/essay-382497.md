## Why Ross favors buying a turnkey business

Ross defines a turnkey business as one that already exists and includes everything needed to operate. It is bought outright, and the buyer's only responsibility is managing it. He notes the preference for turnkey businesses has been rising and calls them "the best" route compared with starting from scratch.

- Starting from scratch means building everything; buying means stepping into a running operation.
- He frames the choice as getting value for money from a reliable, previously proven model.

## Skipping market research and using a proven model

- A new business needs extensive market research on the target audience and what they like. A turnkey business removes that work because it has been in the market (often at the same location) for a while, so the buyer inherits insight into customers.
- Ross says to start due diligence by reviewing the business's full inventory records. They show regular purchasing cost and how quickly stock moves.
- A turnkey business is a proven model that removes the guesswork new entrepreneurs face when choosing a strategy to maximize profit.
- It has a "predetermined play" the new owner can follow.
- These models are replicable and simple enough for a new owner to manage.
- They come with existing customer loyalty and built-in awareness.

## Speed and ease of setup

- Starting a business involves many complex, lengthy factors. Turnkey businesses, especially mobile concepts, are quick to start because they don't need an extensive hiring process or a pricey build-out.
- Most hire only one or two people, with the entrepreneur operating the business. The owner can add staff as the business grows.
- As a franchise, a turnkey business skips steps like selecting the optimal site and constructing a building, so it begins making money sooner.
- It has an active sales unit with equipment and systems already in place.

## Seller support and an established customer base

- When selling, most owners offer "unlimited support" to the new owner. Ross lists technology, marketing, training and operations optimization as areas of help, and calls this what sets turnkey apart from other businesses.
- Partnering with a supportive franchisor improves the odds of steering the business in the right direction.
- Ross argues the hardest part of any business is getting people to switch from a competitor's product, which typically means proving your brand is best and offering hefty discounts.
- A turnkey business has an already established, loyal customer base, so the product or service has a ready market. This improves the odds of success.

## Cost, fees and room to grow

- Most turnkey businesses have no specific rules, guidelines or limitations, so the owner can grow without constraint.
- Caveat: some turnkey franchise models have regulations that protect the brand name, which the buyer must follow under the contract.
- The initial investment is substantial, but Ross says you get value for every penny. If you can afford it, expansion is easy, which matters most for first-time owners.
- It helps with multi-unit franchising: avoid paying unending fees and manually setting up branches from scratch.
- He says turnkey businesses rarely have hidden fees or continuous fees, and are highly unlikely to charge marketing fees or land royalties.
- A well-established business with a solid customer base saves the marketing costs and promotional offers needed to attract new customers when starting from zero.

## Due diligence before buying

- Ross cautions not to settle for the first turnkey business you come across.
- Conduct thorough research.
- Schedule a consultation with the current business owners before signing a contract.
- Consult experts to help with the search so you choose the right turnkey business.
