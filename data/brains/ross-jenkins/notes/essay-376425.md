## Deliverability is the first metric to watch

- Ross argues deliverability rate is "one of the most overlooked, yet more important things" in email marketing. Open rate, click-through rate and revenue all matter, but "if nobody is receiving your emails, everything else is irrelevant."
- If a low percentage of the list reaches the inbox, or deliverability is dipping, he says to make changes quickly.
- Shared sending IPs are one risk. Some email platforms send from IP addresses that get flagged because some users spam. Other users then suffer from a "bad neighbor" sending from the same IPs.
- Spam filters are far more advanced than they used to be. They filter out what they consider junk based on spam-trigger words in the subject line and the email body.

## Write and design every email for mobile first

- Ross says to assume every recipient will receive, open and read the message on a mobile device.
- Implications:
  - The subject line must grab attention right away.
  - The message must be direct and to the point.
  - The design must be eye-catching and flow seamlessly on a small screen.
- Mobile share is hard to pin down. Some sources claim more than 50% of emails are opened on mobile, others north of 70%. He expects the number to keep rising, so designing every campaign with mobile in mind is safe.
- A custom responsive email template is a low-cost way to make branded emails stand out.

## One call to action per email

- Ross criticizes emails that leave the reader unsure what is being promoted. These often mix links to blog posts with random sales or promotions, giving "zero direction, zero clarity."
- He says such emails don't convert and push many people to unsubscribe.
- His rule: include a single call to action, and tell the recipient exactly what the action is and what you want them to do.
- Example: an apparel brand sending a pre-sale notification for a limited-edition drop. The email should make that purpose clear with:
  - A short text blurb.
  - An image of the product.
  - One large button reading "Pre-Order Now!"
- He says that is "all it takes to drive results."

## Segment the list instead of sending one message to everyone

- Ross says sending the same message to the entire list leaves "a lot of money on the table" every time.
- He advises dividing the list into as many segments as possible, using whatever data fits the business, such as purchase history and order frequency.
- His underlying point: not all recipients want the same thing. The sooner a business accepts that, the sooner it can send emails that match what each group wants.
- Segmentation is not an overnight process. A simple start is two groups, customers and non-customers, then segmenting further from there.

## Subject lines: earn the open with humor and shock value

- Ross says the recipient has to open the email before anything else matters. An excellent offer inside produces no return if the email is skipped or deleted.
- The subject line must command attention and trigger a click, and the email has to stand out from the dozens of others in the inbox.
- His recommendation is to experiment with humor and shock value in subject lines.
- Related warning: he links the "screamed advertisement" feel and weak subject lines to people hitting delete.

## Why email deserves real effort

- Ross calls email marketing one of the most affordable and effective online-marketing strategies. It drives revenue with a click and keeps a strong relationship with customers and users.
- He says it is highly measurable, so campaigns can be optimized and fine-tuned to deliver ROI that outperforms all other marketing channels.
- His observation: many businesses put very little effort into email, "and it shows."
- His five improvements for the next campaign:
  1. Monitor deliverability.
  2. Optimize for mobile.
  3. Use a single CTA.
  4. Segment the list.
  5. Use humor and shock value in subject lines.
