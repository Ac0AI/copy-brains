## Sourcing referrals before marketplaces

- Ross's first move for a remote hire is to email and text fellow entrepreneurs and friends. Many businesses went fully remote, so his circle likely has talent sources and recommendations.
- Screening effort should match the role. A remote content writer producing a few blog posts a month needs a much lighter qualification process than a back-end developer for a SaaS company. The two are "two completely different approaches."
- He calls a few emails and texts to his entrepreneurial circle "a great starting point."

## Posting on multiple freelance marketplaces

- Ross says never limit the search to one marketplace, because many talented candidates don't register on all of them.
- He names Freelancer, UpWork and Toptal as the major players and recommends creating a profile and posting the requirements on each.
- The goal is the largest possible talent pool. He calls it the "lazy route" to assume the site you think is best will have what you need.
- The extra effort is worth it, and detailed listings help cut low-quality applicants.

## Defining job requirements and qualifications up front

- The clearer the description of the role, expectations, qualifications and duties, the better qualified the applicants.
- Clear listings also reduce unqualified applicants clogging the inbox.
- A generic listing draws interest from a very broad range of talent. Ross says to spell out exactly what you want and don't want.
- Detailed listings that state both wants and don't-wants limit low-quality applicants and bring talent aligned with the need.
- Context: demand for freelancers and remote workers spiked as companies went remote after the pandemic, bringing top talent but also an influx of unqualified applicants.

## Screening freelancers with a qualifier, video call, references and tests

Ross says remote hiring still needs a thorough interview process. His steps:

- **Qualifier in the application.** Ask candidates to upload a 60-second YouTube video explaining why they are the best fit, and include the link in their response. "If they cannot follow a simple instruction, delete them, as they are not attentive to details."
- **Always do a video call.** Nothing gives a better feel for who someone is than seeing and interacting with them face-to-face.
- **Confirm references.** Contact past employers by simple email or phone call. This can surface issues or drawbacks that don't show up right away.
- **Give test assignments.** "Anyone can look good on paper." Tests show how someone performs in the real world and with your team.

## Hire slow, fire fast

- Ross calls this "the most important tip."
- Take your time hiring and don't feel rushed. There is a lot of talent available and it may take a while to sift through.
- Mistakes happen. Sometimes a hire turns out to be the opposite of who you thought.
- When that happens, fire fast. The longer a bad fit stays, the more damage they do. He describes such a person as a "cancerous team member" to remove from the equation as quickly as possible.

## Why remote teams are now mainstream

- Ross notes remote teams aren't new, and many startups have long run this way, but the pandemic made the model more widespread, adaptable and accepted.
- Companies kept operating virtually even as areas reopened, for three reasons he cites: they learned a remote workforce is workable, employees like remote work and are more productive, and there are cost savings.
