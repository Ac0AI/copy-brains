## Jake Paul as a marketing playbook: appreciate your audience and put them first

Ross Jenkins uses Jake Paul (then 24, a Vine-born social-media personality who moved to the Disney show _Bizaardvark_, music, YouTube and an undefeated boxing run) as a case study in how businesses should market themselves. He treats Paul's methods, "however polarizing they may sometimes be", as having made him "a household name across the country and even worldwide".

- Ross says clients are the reason a business prospers. Show them they are the reason you have made it this far.
- He points to Paul's appreciation of his viewers and fans as a standout trait. Paul credits his boxing career to the support and encouragement of fans on YouTube and other platforms.
- Tactic: when Paul plans an event, he makes sure his audience hears the news first. Ross applies this to business: give your existing customers announcements before anyone else.
- Paul uses the internet and his YouTube following (millions of followers) to advertise upcoming events. Ross notes this has grown his sports popularity and the view counts on his videos.

## Build your personal and company brand without fear

Ross argues that a business must get noticed in order to grow, and that founders should not be afraid to build up their own name because it helps growth and sales.

- Paul "became famous because he always came up with new ways to market his brand".
- He built his brand by doing business differently and embracing the controversies that made him more famous.
- Ross's framing: "bad publicity is still publicity". Controversy around Paul has not stopped him finding success in his various endeavors, which is why Ross calls him a marketing playbook for businesses.
- Ross notes Paul's sustained fame (still gaining popularity "day in and day out") despite the controversies, and treats it as evidence the approach works, whether people love or hate him.
- Takeaway: do business differently, stay visible, and keep inventing fresh ways to promote the brand.

## Set high targets and have plans to succeed

Ross credits Paul with always aiming high.

- Paul's stance, as Ross relays it: do not let yourself fail. Set targets that guarantee more returns in the business.
- Ross frames this as having concrete plans and goals tied to returns, rather than drifting.
- Paul's trajectory (Vine video, a Disney TV role, being fired from the show, then exploring music and boxing) is used to show that setbacks can free up creativity to try new ventures. Ross says being fired "allowed him to explore his creativity more".

## Believe in your offer and be ready to support it

Ross pairs two mindset and preparation points.

- **Believe in yourself:** when you are set to do something, go for it and do not let anything discourage you. For a business to prosper, you have to believe that what you offer is the best in the market at that time.
- **Support your business:** be ready to back your business when you introduce yourself to the market, whatever the product or service.
- Be prepared with helpful information about your product or brands whenever you pitch or introduce them.

## Use sponsorships: financial and in-kind

Ross calls sponsorship a strategy Paul uses and "the majority of influencers use nowadays", and recommends finding the right sponsors for your content to improve your chances of online success.

- **Financial sponsorship:** influencers are paid so they can keep making content. Common on YouTube, for example when a creator suddenly diverts a video to talk about a particular product.
- **In-kind sponsorship:** usually product-placement advertising, common on Instagram and Facebook, where creators promote products in stories and posts. Creators must now indicate that such posts contain paid advertising, so they are easy to spot.
- **Why companies sponsor figures like Paul:** it is a win-win. By using a large influencer following they increase brand awareness and credibility.
- Paul's popularity and influence in the business world attracted many parties willing to strike deals and offer him sponsorship.
