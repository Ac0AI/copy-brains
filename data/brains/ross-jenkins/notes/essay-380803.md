## Marketing as a competitive edge, not a product afterthought

Ross Jenkins argues that high-quality products and services are no longer enough. A strong marketing game sets a business apart from competitors and pushes consumers to choose it. He notes that "there are a lot of brands out there with mediocre products and services, but they still find success as a result of their excellent marketing strategies."

- Marketing reaches a wider audience that may want the product and lifts later sales.
- It improves brand credibility, which makes consumer loyalty easier to win.
- Options range from traditional channels (TV, print, radio) to modern ones (social media).
- He says the hardest part is knowing where to start.
- He closes on consistency: people can't buy from a business they don't know exists, so the business has to keep making itself known.

## Start with a simple plan and an elevator statement

Ross says any marketing effort starts with a plan, and the plan doesn't need to be big. What matters is that it is beneficial for ROI.

- Begin with brainstorming sessions, themes, lists of items and targets on a calendar.
- Write an elevator statement: what you want people to know about your brand, products or services in 30 seconds. It should be interesting.
- Get feedback from people in advance as another way to refine the plan.
- The goal of whichever method is to create a good impression.

## Website, SEO and analytics basics

Ross says many customers search for a business on Google, so a website lets them see what you offer.

- Build it yourself or hire a professional web-development agency. Hire a professional if you want more features.
- Focus on SEO and make the site work on different devices.
- Use Google Analytics to monitor traffic.
- Be wary of anyone who guarantees a top search ranking, "unless this person works as a developer for Google."

## Social media presence and claiming your business listings

Ross advises being visible on social media to tap into different audiences.

- He says most of the traffic is on Facebook, and a business without a Facebook page may be missing opportunities.
- Social media use is growing, including among older people, who are there because their children, grandchildren, friends and former colleagues are.
- He also recommends claiming your business online. Information about a business can be found online regardless, and you want to control what people read about your products or services.
- Search your business in different browsers to see what is shared, then claim it by creating a business listing.

## Paid search with Google AdWords

Ross recommends Google AdWords to target the specific products or services you sell.

- Choose a few quality keywords rather than a lot of them.
- AdWords lets you pick a target location and work within a budget.
- More broadly, if other methods aren't enough, advertise: show how you differ from competitors and how people can contact you.
- He says advertising is not one-size-fits-all, so figure out what works best for your business.

## Community involvement, networking and promotional items

Ross says to build awareness locally by joining business associations, community organizations and groups. Anything that gets the business involved helps.

- Networking expands contacts, brings new referrals and raises awareness of the business.
- It also gets the attention of other business leaders.
- Support events and programs for a good cause. This makes the business known and helps the community.
- Give away promotional items carrying the business name, logo and contact information at these events.
- He singles out T-shirts as "free walking marketing."

## Coupons, free trials and protecting your content

Ross suggests giving away coupons or free trials of products or services to start building followers.

- A satisfied customer returns and shares the experience with family and friends.
- Reach out to people you know to help promote the business.
- He also advises protecting ideas and content with copyright in a form the law recognizes. For printed material, he suggests obtaining federal copyright protection.
- If you invest heavily in a website, protect ownership of its contents, graphics and general design.
- He mentions agencies like Upwork as a way to get help with this.
