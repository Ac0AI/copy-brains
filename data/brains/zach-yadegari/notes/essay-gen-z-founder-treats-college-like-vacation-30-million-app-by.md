## Starting out: apps as a teenager

- Zach started coding at 7, gave coding lessons at $30 an hour by age 10, and put his first app, "Speed Soccer", on the App Store at 12.
- In high school he built "Totally Science", a site where classmates could play unblocked games at school. It grew to 5 million users over two years and made $60,000 a month from banner ads he placed on the site, six figures in total.
- He sold it at 16 for $100,000. He says "that's when I started building apps". Everything he built up to that point was free.
- He calls the early apps "small projects". Cal AI is the one that took off.

## Finding the idea: solve your own annoyance

- Cal AI came from his own fitness journey. He was very skinny his whole life and wanted to bulk up. He found most of the progress came from diet, so he tracked calories and ate in a surplus.
- The most popular calorie app at the time was "an awful experience". He couldn't eat in the cafeteria with friends because he was eating pre-portioned meals weighed on scales, and he skipped restaurants because he couldn't know the calories.
- He argues that wasn't sustainable for a kid in school. It was bad enough that he gave up on much of his fitness journey for a while.
- He had basic coding skills and saw the rise of AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude, so he decided to build the app himself: take a picture of your food and get the calories.

## Team and launch

- He brought the idea to people he knew he could trust: a friend from coding camp and two people he met on X. The co-founders (Henry Langmack, Blake Anderson, Jake Castillo) were all older than him.
- They launched Cal AI in May 2024.
- Early growth came from TikToks and influencer partnerships: "We started making TikToks, we started partnering with influencers, and then we had pretty explosive growth."
- The positive feedback encouraged him. In July 2024 he moved to San Francisco with his co-founders to focus and hired about 15 employees.

## Growth, pricing and funding

- Roughly a year and four months after launch, Cal AI brings in about $30 million annually. Fortune reviewed financial records showing several million dollars of revenue per month. The team is over 30 people.
- The app is free to download on the App Store and Google Play, with subscriptions at $2.49 per month or $29.99 per year.
- He says the app has a 90% accuracy rate.
- Investors constantly tried to "throw money at us", and the team turned it down.

## Views on college and the standard path

- He rejects the formula that everyone should follow: "get good grades, go to a good college, get a degree, get a job, and then start making money". He says he hated that notion and used to fight with his parents about it.
- He was widely rejected by Ivy League schools despite a 4.0 GPA, a 34 ACT and entrepreneurial success. He went to the University of Miami anyway.
- He frames it as lifestyle, not career: "I look at it as I'm paying $100,000 a year for a vacation."
- He is undeclared, dropped out of the business school, and now takes philosophy classes. He keeps one entrepreneurship class but says he's "not gaining much from the class material".

## Building a community: App Mafia

- In Miami he lives in a house with other app-building friends aged 18 to 26, all successful entrepreneurs.
- They are building "App Mafia", a community and brand that educates people online with content on how to build apps.
- His stated aim: "We're basically trying to lead the forefront of Gen Z builders."
