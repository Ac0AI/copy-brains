## How Zach found his influencer growth loop

- Cal AI launched in 2024 as an AI-fueled calorie tracking app. Zach built it into a company that generated over $30 million in revenue in two years, and MyFitnessPal acquired it in March (2026) for an undisclosed amount.
- His main growth strategy was deploying "a small army of influencers" to promote the brand.
- The first signal came when a single influencer's post about Cal AI drove roughly a thousand new users over two days.
- He treated that as proof the channel could work: "we figured out, okay, this can actually work for us, and so we started recruiting influencers as fast as we could — paying as many people as possible to start posting for us."

## Why paid influencer posting scaled predictably

- Zach describes the result as "a clear formula": "It was pretty linear, but it was extremely fast."
- "Linear" means each post reaches a certain number of viewers, and a consistent share of those viewers converts to users.
- Because output per post was predictable, adding more influencers produced a proportional increase in installs, so growth became a matter of recruiting and paying more creators.

## How Zach structured influencer deals

- He negotiated rates with influencers, promising a specific payout tied to a specific scale of reach.
- Many influencers are "broke college kids" who "just want cash," so straightforward cash deals work.
- The pitch to the creator is: "we'll pay you this, but we need to get us at least this amount of use[rs] and keep posting until you get there."
- The influencer's obligation is to keep posting until the agreed audience or reach target is met, not just to publish one post.

## Choosing influencers whose reach can be measured

- The "biggest thing" for Zach was finding influencers with "very tight communities."
- The key criterion is that an influencer's reach can be quantified, which makes the pay-for-reach agreements and the linear conversion math workable.
