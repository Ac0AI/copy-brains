## Cal AI's first year: $35M run rate, 20-30K daily downloads, 250 influencers on retainer

Zach describes Cal AI in May 2025, about a year after launch. The app tracks calories from a photo of your food. He says it is only possible now because of AI breakthroughs, but "mainly because of APIs like ChatGPT. We did not train our own model to do this."

- First line of code was written around March 2024, and the app launched in May 2024.
- It reached its first $1M in sales on September 4, 2024, about five months in.
- In May 2025 Zach said the annual run rate was "over 35,000,000", assuming no growth.
- He said revenue collected in 2025 was "a little over 10" million (he first said about 9 million, then corrected himself).
- The app gets "20 to 30,000 new downloads" every day.
- The company is bootstrapped, with no outside funding (stated in the page's summary, not in his words).
- He expected 25 to 50% of annual subscribers to renew in year two. Cal AI had no data yet because it had just passed its first anniversary.

## Influencer marketing on monthly retainers is the biggest channel

Influencers on TikTok and Instagram are Cal AI's biggest channel. The reasons for that, per Zach:

- About 250 influencers are on monthly retainer, each integrating Cal AI into their content.
- Zach says the largest earns "tens of thousands per month" for four posts.
- Total influencer spend the previous month was "mid six figures".
- Pricing starts from the creator's account metrics. The team looks at views and engagement, because "not all views are valued the same". It then sets a minimum and maximum it is willing to pay.
- Creators get no script. "They are the professionals. They know what goes viral for their audience." Cal AI's job is making sure they integrate the product well.
- He prefers commission-only pay ("that would be the dream"). The reality is that creators want guaranteed payment, and "if we're not gonna do it, another brand will."
- Doubling down on influencers was the main focus of the last twelve months, and new creators are signed every month.
- Of the 17 staff, about half work on marketing and influencer management.

## The company was meant to be an app studio, but Cal AI absorbed all the attention

- The founders saw their influencer strategy as the thing other app builders lacked. The plan was to apply it to several apps under a holding company.
- By the time the other app ideas finished development, Cal AI was growing so fast that "it deserved all of our attention."

## Onboarding: add questions, add reassurance screens, animate them, test each change

The onboarding flow was built through A/B testing.

- **Start:** no animations or GIFs. It was bland and asked only the questions needed to compute daily calorie intake.
- **Added questions:** he added questions that don't affect the result, such as which diet the user follows. Conversion rose. His hypothesis, which he says other people see too, is that the user invests more time.
- **Reassurance screens:** a "thank you for trusting us" screen and a "you're gonna lose weight faster with Cal AI" screen both lifted conversion.
- **Animation:** animating those screens lifted conversion again. He sums it up as "a game of creating hypotheses and then testing them out."
- **Sign-in at the end:** moving sign-in to the end of the flow was "the biggest by far" improvement, because of the drop-off at that step. Zach confirmed asking for sign-up at the end was intentional.

## Paywall and trial: Superwall, high conversion, and heavy testing

- Paywalls are built in Superwall. Zach says conversion is "much higher" with it and it "has allowed us to run so many tests."
- He says the team lacks feature testing but runs "so many tests" on paywalls.
- There is a three-day free trial. He says 20 to 25% of new users convert, either to paying right away or into the trial.
- Testing for the rest of the product is custom-coded and uses Mixpanel. Zach says it only started around January, "three months ago", and "we are behind on that front and have mainly operated through intuition." Now every new feature is tested.

## App Store optimization: stuff keywords into the name and subtitle, use intuition first

- The team fits in "as many keywords as we can" in the app name and subtitle.
- It used no keyword tools such as AppFigures. Early keywords came from what the founders, as power users, would search for.
- Later it talked to agencies for more input, but "most of that is just what our intuition told us."

## Speed, specialized hiring, and recruiting from X

- Zach says speed is Cal AI's number one core value: "push things out fast, develop it fast, push it out fast, see the test results quickly." He thinks most companies move too slowly.
- Hiring is the top priority, but for specialized talent. Examples he gives: one person whose only job is paywall experiments, one who only makes the food scanner more accurate, and one who only owns the homepage.
- Most of the talent came from X. A user redesigned a Cal AI screen and the team is now interviewing him. Zach also posted looking for a Facebook ad buyer.
- Headcount was 17 for several months and was heading to about 22 within a month or two. Everyone is remote.
- No agentic automation yet. The team is all humans, and some developers use Cursor.

## A viral founder brand turned into TV appearances

- Zach's X post about his college admissions got 28 million views. His personal statement post got 40 million views on its own. He expected two or three million.
- He says he is "wired to know when posts will go viral" from growing up on social media.
- TV stations reached out, one or two at first and then more as he appeared. He calls it a snowball effect: other stations get FOMO and see that he "can present well on camera."
