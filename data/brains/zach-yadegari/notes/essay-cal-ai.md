## Paywall experimentation as a compounding growth system

Zach treats the paywall like any other product surface: "We treat paywalls the same way we treat the product — ship something, measure it, and improve it tomorrow." He argues that spending weeks debating what a paywall should look like means you've already lost: "Just test it."

- Cal AI joined Superwall in March 2024, shortly before public launch. It started with one onboarding paywall and a few price points.
- Hard-coded paywall changes, App Store review waits and one-variant-at-a-time learning would have throttled the team. They needed experimentation cheap enough to run constantly across pricing, design, messaging and placement.
- Over about two years the team ran 123 A/B experiments on iOS, 160 unique paywall designs, 424 total variants and 46 trigger points. That is roughly five real experiments per month.
- 61 of those experiments were on the onboarding paywall, testing layouts, offer framing, pricing presentation, urgency treatments and creative direction against live traffic.
- Zach's takeaway: speed beats perfect theory. Ship, measure and iterate fast enough to learn from real users.

## Results of the paywall program

- Monthly revenue grew more than 3x in 10 months.
- Trial-to-paid conversion improved 31% (relative) over 12 months.
- 87% of new users see a paywall.
- 57% of paywall viewers start a transaction.
- 63% of started transactions complete.
- During the January New Year's resolution surge, installs nearly doubled month-over-month. The heavily tested funnel was ready, which he frames as compound interest on over a hundred small optimizations paying off at the right moment.
- Cal AI's growth curve: $28K in month 1, $115K in month 2, $1M monthly recurring revenue within 6 months, and 15M+ downloads and roughly $40M annual revenue within 18 months.
- The case study credits distribution through short-form video and creator-led acquisition for demand, but says installs alone don't create a subscription business. The conversion system did.

## Monetize every high-intent moment, not just onboarding

Cal AI built 46 trigger points instead of relying on a single onboarding paywall. The lesson is a much larger testing surface: more chances to learn and more places to improve revenue.

- **Onboarding:** the first high-intent purchase moment for new users.
- **Transaction abandon:** exit-intent recovery flows when users back out of checkout.
- **Freemium feature gates:** camera scan, barcode scan, label scan, macros, analytics, progress photos and health score.
- **Win-back flows:** separate messaging for expired trials, lapsed subscriptions and reactivation campaigns.
- **Lifecycle messaging:** paywalls tied to push notifications and email journeys.
- **Upsells:** family plans, lifetime deals, body scan reports and nutrition chat add-ons.

## Treat the paywall as a creative surface

Cal AI tested formats that felt like product experiences rather than static purchase screens.

- **Spin-wheel paywall:** users "unlock" a discount by spinning a wheel. This turns a standard purchase decision into a more dynamic moment. The team kept iterating on discount framing, urgency, animation and how aggressively the offer was positioned.
- **Video paywalls** were also tested.
- Messaging, motion and visual framing can change how a product pitch feels.

## Test price architecture, billing cadence and checkout together

Performance is rarely driven by one variable, so Cal AI tested combinations rather than debating them in the abstract.

- The matrix of active price points expanded broadly: weekly, monthly, quarterly, annual and lifetime combinations were all part of the test surface.
- Creative, price architecture, billing cadence, trial structure and checkout destination all interact.
- They added web checkout via Stripe. Giving users more than one way to pay opened another revenue channel and reduced unnecessary checkout friction compared with only the default App Store flow.

## Never settle on a winner

- Per the case study, the team found a winning variant and immediately started testing against it. That relentlessness is why numbers kept compounding month after month.
- Zach's operating principle is "small weekly improvements compound into outsized revenue growth when the team keeps shipping and measuring."
- Even losing experiments are treated as learning about what users respond to.
- His advice for subscription apps: start with your first experiment, and the compounding takes care of the rest.
