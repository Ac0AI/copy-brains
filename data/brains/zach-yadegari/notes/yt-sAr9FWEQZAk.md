## Building Cali AI with a four-person founder team

- Zach's co-founders each owned a domain and trusted the others to run theirs. He says that's why they "didn't really have any fights": everyone was "so focused on the one thing that we became experts in."
- Roles at Cali AI: Zach was CEO, Blake Anderson was co-founder/investor/advisor, Jake Castillo was CMO/COO (marketing and operations), and Henry was CTO.
- Zach met Henry at a coding camp at age 10, and the friendship turned into daily gaming and shared projects.
- Zach DM'd Blake to meet him. As Cali ramped up, the team needed a COO, and Zach recruited Jake, whose work at StudyBody he knew.
- When the founders met, Zach says they decided to "burn all the boats" and go all in on Cali, because they now had "the dream team."
- The team was still working for a salary on another app when Cali began. It was hard to believe it would be the app that let them go self-employed.

## Cali AI revenue trajectory

- Zach was 19 at the time of the interview. He started coding at 7, put his first app on the App Store at 12, and started a company in his freshman year of high school.
- That earlier company reached 5 million users over 2 years and made about $60K a year. He sold it at 16.
- Cali AI made $30K in its first month and over $100K in its second month.
- Within 8 months it reached $1M a month.
- At the time of the interview, Zach put it on track for roughly $50-60M for the year.
- Cali AI was later sold for multiple millions of dollars. The exact price was not disclosed.

## Choosing not to sell early, and how trust settled it

- At the end of 2024, the team received low eight-figure acquisition offers.
- The team was conflicted. The money was life-changing, but they cared deeply about the product and saw a lot of growth left.
- They resolved it through constant open communication. They called each other all the time during that period.
- Through that trust they reached a collective decision not to sell then.
- Zach's view: without trust between co-founders, "you're not going to take your company to its true ceiling and its true potential."

## Finding ideas and marketing by solving your own problem

- Zach says building something that solves your own problem makes everything fall into place.
- You know what you'd want from the product, so the design becomes intuitive.
- You know who you're marketing to because you're marketing to yourself, so you know the pain points to hit.
- If you don't have an idea, he suggests looking for something that would actually help you.
