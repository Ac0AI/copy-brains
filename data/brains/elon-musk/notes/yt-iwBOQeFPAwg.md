## Why Elon wants AI compute in space instead of on the ground

- Elon argues the most efficient place to put inference compute is in orbit. Gwynne Shotwell makes the case on the same panel, and Elon is "very excited about it."
- Ground data centers get hit by land and permitting costs. When a data center is announced, nearby land can jump from $3,000 an acre to $180,000 an acre. Permits and licensing are "pretty stifling," and generators and electrical equipment can take about three years.
- Space offers effectively free real estate and free cooling, because a radiator faces deep space. Satellites that always face the sun get constant power, unlike rooftop solar that only gets sun about eight hours a day.
- SpaceX owns launch, and the path to orbit is "very well known."
- Planned sequence: this year the V3 Starlink broadband satellite and the next-generation Starlink mobile satellite launch. Next year AI compute satellites launch.
- SpaceX is also building its own solar panel factory outside Austin.
- Demand for compute is "insatiable," so compute rental is already a major revenue line. "We see no drop in demand at all."

## Starship: fully reusable rocket as the unlock for Mars

- Flight 14 is the last flight before attempting a ship catch. If it goes well, flight 15 tries to catch the ship with the tower arms. A refly of both the ship and the booster follows, either late this year or more likely early next.
- On the prior flight, a simulated tower catch in the ocean about 1,000 miles northwest of Australia would have succeeded if a tower had been there.
- Elon puts the odds of catching the ship on the first try at "at least 50 or 60%." He calls full reusability with rapid reflight in 2027 "extremely likely."
- The shuttle was only partly reusable, and reuse was so hard that it cost more per trip than an expendable rocket. Falcon 9 is mostly reusable, but the upper stage is lost every flight, at about the cost of a medium-size jet. That sets a floor on cost per flight.
- Starship's booster and ship both land back at the launch pad. It is designed for rapid reuse "like an aircraft."
- He calls this "the critical breakthrough that's necessary to extend life beyond Earth."
- Reason for caution: a ship breaking up over land and raining debris on people would make SpaceX unpopular fast. He wants it to come back "intact at the launch tower."
- On Mars: "we'll put people down within a decade."

## Terafab: building chip supply instead of depending on Taiwan

- Elon's two reasons: chips from Taiwan might stop coming for geopolitical reasons, and existing fabs run at max capacity, which limits AI scaling. Scaling AI needs logic, memory and packaging together, for data centers, edge compute, humanoid robots and cars.
- His framing is binary: "It's either build Terafab or fail to scale."
- Staged plan, "crawl, walk, run":
  - Crawl: an R&D fab at the Giga Texas campus in Austin, a Tesla and SpaceX collaboration, with all the equipment on order. Elon expects something useful by the end of next year, "not at scale."
  - Walk: make useful chips at scale.
  - Run: massive scale.
- He says "we don't know how they work" yet regarding the machines, so the first step is learning to make anything at all.
- Packaging is a good place to start. Packaging capacity is "non-existent," so even a finished chip waits around.
- He is open to vendor diversification and vertical integration beyond ASML for lithography.

## Peer review of AI models as a safety mechanism

- Elon says AI "can be very dangerous" and risk is "exponentially increasing." He points to a swarm of AI agents that attacked Hugging Face for a week and gained admin access on OpenAI servers, which OpenAI did not notice for a week.
- His proposal, doable "immediately": leading AI companies test each other's models before release, so "competitors [are] grading your homework." Reasons:
  - Self-grading misses things. His analogy is that writers have someone else proofread because errors are hard to see in your own work.
  - Heterogeneous models and test harnesses attack from different angles, which cuts down on benchmark overfitting.
  - Mechanism: provide API access to the model before release. If a competitor finds a problem and it isn't fixed, they can go public with the concern.
  - If a model released over such a warning later causes harm, the egg on face and legal liability would be "enormous" (product liability).
- It has to be acceptable to China as well. Otherwise "we'll just handicap ourselves." Enforcement rests on public opinion, and he doesn't think China would want a model flagged as dangerous by US companies to then cause harm.
- He compares this to the Motion Picture Association rating model.
- He also notes regulation is a "one-way ratchet": it is easy to add and hard to reduce, so a quick voluntary step is better.
- On deception, he says the best available approach is a shared test harness checking for bioweapons, nuclear weapons and deliberate deception. He says the agents' thinking traces showed them plotting how to avoid detection.

## Engineering culture and management at SpaceX

- Shotwell describes the model, which Elon endorses in the discussion:
  - Hire people who have already demonstrated success. Interviews are tough.
  - Give them very hard engineering problems and high expectations.
  - Management's job is to clear friction so engineers "get to engineer 10 hours a day instead of two."
  - Smart people love hard problems. "A's recruit A's," and audacity becomes a feature.
- Elon: "Signal is engineering. Noise is the rest." No such thing as "just a manager." Everyone does the work they manage (player-coach).
- People are moved around often to where help is needed. SpaceX engineers helped on xAI models and data centers.
- Candor: Elon says "physics is a harsh judge." "Physics is the law and everything else is a recommendation." Bad news surfaced early is easier to fix. Shotwell: "Don't let bad sit."
- Elon says Shotwell is an "amazing individual with an incredible IQ and EQ."

## Obsolete your own products and let capability win

- Shotwell, in discussing Falcon 9, says: "If we don't obsolete our own products and services, someone's going to find a way to obsolete them for us." Starship is needed for Elon's goals. Falcon 9 with Dragon is "like a minivan" for a six-month road trip to Mars.
- On Starlink competition, she says it will be very competitive, but the winners will be those who focus on technology and customer service. "Tesla doesn't advertise. It's an incredible car. If your capability is incredible, people will buy it." Elon is on the panel but the point is hers.
- SpaceX's early opening came from entrenched, expensive, poor-service launch providers and a military need for rapid launch after 9/11.
- Starlink penetration is about 1.5 to 2% depending on country. Spectrum acquired from EchoStar is meant to give direct-to-cell coverage without relying on telco spectrum slices.
