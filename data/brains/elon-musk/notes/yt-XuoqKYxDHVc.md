## AI timeline and the economics of abundance (10-year outlook)

- Elon expects AI to exceed the sum of human intelligence in "roughly 5 years", and to be far greater by 2036.
- He frames the economy as digital intelligence plus physical intelligence. Digital AI is advancing fast but is "still somewhat confined to the digital realm" without end effectors.
- Humanoid robots are the end effectors: "You need lots of bots." Robots plus vast digital intelligence turn intelligence into "shaping atoms", which he calls a "quasi infinite economy".
- Economy defined: "the production of goods and provision of services".
- Most likely outcome by 2036: "an age of amazing abundance where anyone can have anything they can think of".
- He predicts "money won't matter in 2036". If robots and AI supply more goods and services than any human can consume, there is little reason to need money.
- He says it would be "vanity" to think he will control a super-genius AI. His goal is to give AI good values: maximally truth-seeking and curious.
- He thinks work becomes optional. His analogy is gardening: you can grow tomatoes yourself, but store-bought ones are more perfect.

## Constraints on AI: electricity, chips, and orbital data centers

- Elon says AI capability is a function of the limiting factor, chips or electricity.
- He says power and cooling are currently the binding constraint outside China, because chips are made faster than new electricity comes online.
- In China he says the constraint is chips, because of US export bans. He expects the constraint to be power in the US and elsewhere until space-based power and cooling relieve it, after which it becomes chips again.
- He claims China already generates more electricity than the US, Europe and India combined. He guesses it heads to roughly four times US production, which he says is roughly proportionate to population.
- He claims China is closer than most people realize to solving lithography, which would let it make AI chips in volume.
- He says AI water usage is "negligible almost nothing". The issue is electrical demand.
- He expects Chinese AI firms to lead at some point if they get more compute, given what they do with relatively little. He also rates Chinese robotics firms as very strong.
- He argues that US laws banning Chinese models would not stop the rest of the world from using them and "won't stop China from being the leader in AI".

## Governance of frontier AI: peer testing before release

- Elon's proposal is that leading AI labs hold a call every week or two. Before a new frontier model is released, competitors get one to two weeks of private access, for example through an API, to test it.
- His reasoning is that competitors have the technical depth and the incentive to keep each other honest, while government officials often lack both.
- If a lab refuses to fix a danger flagged by peers, the labs should alert the government (US or Chinese) to limit release.
- His analogy is the Motion Picture Association, an industry group that sets ratings without government writing each one.
- He says this should include Chinese frontier labs.
- He cites Mythos as the precedent. He says Amazon flagged the cybersecurity risk and called the White House, not the government itself.
- He says he would raise the idea with Dario Amodei after discussing it with Demis Hassabis.
- He says Anthropic should choose its words more carefully. Calling Mythos terrifying and then releasing it was predictably alarming.
- He says his actions (creating OpenAI as a counterweight to Google, then Anthropic spinning out of it) ended up accelerating AI. His conclusion: "join the club".

## Why he took SpaceX public and keeps voting control

- He took SpaceX public so the public could own a piece. As a private company, he says, it was limited to a few thousand shareholders. As a public company it can have millions.
- He wants voting control so he can invest on 5 to 10 year horizons. Quarterly pressure punishes spending such as a Moon or Mars base, which he says is listed in the S-1 prospectus as something SpaceX will do.
- He explains the pressure through incentives. Portfolio managers are judged on short windows (sometimes a year or less), so they push companies for immediate returns. "Once you understand the incentive structure, the behavior naturally follows."
- He praises retail investors as insightful with long-term views.
- He names Alphabet (Larry and Sergey) and Meta (Zuckerberg) as similar founder-controlled companies.
- On key-man risk, he says SpaceX and Tesla have clear roadmaps and would do well for several years without him. He compares this to Apple after Steve Jobs, which has strong products but no new breakthrough product.
- On his own taxes, he says he pays about 45% on stock-option income (40% federal plus roughly California's state rate). He expects about half again to be taxed at death. He says the main thing his wealth gives him is control of company direction for a period.

## Jobs, software, and policy for the transition

- He says AI already beats at least 90% of professional software engineers at writing software. He expects it to pass 99%, reaching "Stockfish level", where humans cannot compete.
- He expects this to hit anything done by a person at a computer or phone "very soon". Physical work follows as robots, managed by a large AI model, gain capability.
- He calls the transition "a bumpy road" and says the pace of change is the new factor. He compares it to the job called "computer", which disappeared when machines took over calculation.
- His policy answer is "universal high income", with the Treasury issuing checks.
- He argues inflation is the ratio of money to goods and services. If output grows faster than the money supply, "deflation will be the issue not inflation". He says taxation also becomes less relevant in a world where money is.
- He recommends Iain Banks' Culture novels as the best vision of an AI future, though he says the humans there have little agency.

## Space ambitions, Starlink, and his stated outlook

- His goal is to "propagate consciousness into the future to the maximum degree possible", with a space-faring civilization on the Moon, Mars and beyond. He cites Star Wars as the first film he saw in a theater, at about 6.
- He says Starship is the largest, most powerful flying object ever made and that SpaceX intends to launch it "more than once per hour". He says this will happen.
- He now stresses using the Moon as a base for orbital AI satellites alongside Mars.
- On Starlink in Ukraine, he says SpaceX was never selling to Russia. Terminals ordered through Ukraine were smuggled into occupied areas, so SpaceX worked with the Ukrainian government on a whitelist of approved terminals. He acknowledges that innocent users also lost access.
- On DOGE, he says he "got a little too involved in politics". He describes the goal as tackling the deficit and says payments could be approved simply by confirming the recipient's contact details or evidence of intended use.
- On his predictions, he says AI and robotics are "pretty much happening as I thought it would". He places the AI and robot singularity about 10 years away.
