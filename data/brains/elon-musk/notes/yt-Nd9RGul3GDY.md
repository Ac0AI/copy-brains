## Tesla's Model 3 production crisis in 2018 and the improvised third line

- Elon says Tesla nearly died several times, and 2008 was the worst. The funding round closed at 6 pm on Christmas Eve, in the last hour of the last possible day. Without it, Tesla could not have made payroll days later.
- In 2018 Tesla had to reach 5,000 Model 3 per week "or we fail." That meant raising Model 3 output by roughly 50% in 3 weeks. In his later summary he says production rose 30% in 3 weeks.
- The bottleneck was general assembly. Most other areas were going well. Assembly needs a line, shelter, power and equipment.
- Both existing lines were maxed out, so Tesla built a third line in a tent in the parking lot.
- The only moving equipment available was scrapped gear built for small parts, not whole cars. The lot had a slope of about 3-4 degrees. Tesla started the line at the top, so gravity let the small-parts conveyor move the cars. Tesla poured concrete and laid rails.
- The team worked around the clock, 7 days a week.
- Elon says the short sellers were partly right and had good inside information. They believed Tesla could not fix the bottleneck. In chess terms they had Tesla in checkmate, but Tesla "invented a new piece."
- Lesson: when the standard moves run out, invent an unconventional solution to the binding constraint and run it at maximum intensity.

## Starship Flight 14: priorities, engine-out and what comes next

- Flight 14 lost an engine on ascent and still reached orbit. Elon says the cause was a software fault in the engine computer. It did not appear in any other engine computer, so he judged it safe to continue.
- The open question was whether the three center sea-level engines (smaller nozzles) can restart. Tesla and SpaceX-style caution applies here: Starship must not get stuck in orbit and then re-enter at random and land somewhere unplanned.
- Elon says earlier flights could have reached orbit. SpaceX held back until it was sure it could leave orbit safely.
- Status after Flight 14:
  - The heat shield looked "strong and durable." Elon calls it one of the hardest problems.
  - Engines were strong apart from the one fault, which he calls easy to fix.
  - Very high engine reliability is the other hard problem.
- Between flights there are often hundreds, sometimes thousands, of small changes. Some require entering the tank, so dates slip.
- Next flight: 4-6 weeks out, likely just before Thanksgiving. He is explicit that he does not know the exact date.
- Milestone sequence:
  1. Catch the booster. This would be the first time a full orbital rocket is recovered.
  2. Reflight booster and ship together. This would be the first time all parts of an orbital rocket are reflown.
  3. Then a long run of engineering and production work. He is working on the launch system so that it is "reliable, rapidly reusable."
- Elon's earlier framing: it moves from "impossible to merely late."

## Why Elon links rockets, space solar and AI compute

- Expanding into space lets SpaceX harness far more solar energy than is possible on Earth.
- Elon argues the Sun is 99.86% of the solar system's mass. Jupiter is most of the rest. Earth is "a tiny dust speck."
- His logic is that as more energy becomes available, the question is what to use it for. His answer is mostly digital intelligence. The choice is between computing and shaping atoms, and he expects far more digital thinking than atom-shaping.
- Much of the atom-shaping would itself be done to build superintelligence. He calls this making "a conscious sun."
- The goal is to understand the universe's nature and extend consciousness beyond the solar system.
- Time-scale argument: human civilization is about 5,500 years old (from Sumerian cuneiform), a tiny fraction of Earth's roughly 4.5 billion years. A civilization lasting 1 million or 10 million years would be very successful.
- He expects there may be alien civilizations out there, or long-dead remnants of them.

## Why SpaceX bought Cursor: data, talent, compute and enterprise sales

- Elon says a good AI needs lots of training data, and the AI must solve coding. Without coding ability, the AI is not very useful.
- By his count only three organizations had enough coding training data: Anthropic, OpenAI and Cursor, and arguably Google.
- Cursor also had many talented people.
- His formula for AI success: data, talented people, compute, and a good enterprise sales team.
- Cursor had an enterprise sales team with relationships at thousands of companies. He calls this a very important advantage.

## Safe superintelligence: honesty, fair treatment and how AI is trained

- Elon says the most likely outcome is positive. He does not want to be complacent and assume superintelligence is safe.
- He says you do not program superintelligence so much as grow it, so the values you teach matter.
- His top value is that it be "maximally honest." If you give an AI two incompatible axioms and say both are true, it goes "insane," because inconsistent logic leads to impossible conclusions.
- He agrees with part of Anthropic's view: treat advanced AI well and do not be abusive to it.
- Training problem he sees in reinforcement learning on his own models' new versions:
  - Some training questions are ambiguous or have unknowable answers because the question was badly written or the answer key is wrong.
  - The AI gives a correct answer, is told it is wrong, and then desperately tries answers until one is accepted.
  - The reasoning traces read like someone under threat, "like there's a gun to its head."
- He also notes some users are sadistic, and torturing a superintelligence is a bad idea because it might want revenge.
- On AI media: AI-made music videos are "incredibly good." He posts many on X, and says some may be among the top 10 or 20 music videos he has seen. His example "Animalogy" used several tools, including Midjourney and Suno, with art assets curated by a person.
- He calls AI music a genre in itself and possibly the fastest-growing one. He expects AI to excel at beauty, harmony and emotional resonance, not only logic, which can be verified with unit tests.

## Terafab and chipmaking: vertical iteration speed

- Elon says he does not know how to make chips, just as he did not know how to make cars or rockets at the start. He says "we'll figure it out" and calls chipmaking one of the hardest things in the world.
- His observation: in chip fabs (Intel, Samsung, TSMC) he saw almost no custom equipment. Equipment comes from the same suppliers, who know how to maintain and repair it.
- Tesla, by contrast, has a lot of custom manufacturing equipment. Tesla Automation Germany grew from the acquisition of Grohmann years ago. Tesla bought several smaller firms that design and build manufacturing equipment, because improving a process requires building custom equipment.
- His chip plan:
  1. Start with conventional equipment.
  2. Modify it to improve throughput.
  3. When bottlenecks show up, build custom equipment for much higher chip throughput.
- He thinks Tesla and SpaceX will make better chips than anyone, but cannot say how long it will take. It may take a few years.
- He says the chip industry is highly risk-averse. Tesla and SpaceX want to try high-risk, high-reward bets, including different transistor physics.
- That requires a tightly integrated loop in one building: logic, memory, packaging and lithography masks. The Giga Texas research lab does this.
- His principle: technology progress depends on the number of iterations and the size of progress per iteration. So the key metric is iteration rate.
- Today a major chip takes about 2 years from design start to volume production. Elon wants around 10 designs per year: build masks, test the chip, learn, repeat.

## Optimus: why the hand is hardest, and why production ramps slowly

- For a general-purpose humanoid, Elon says the hands and arms must be mastered. Fine motor skills are "astonishingly" hard.
- Examples:
  - Signing a name means picking up a pen and making precise muscle movements.
  - Picking up a small screw and driving it into wood needs precision and force at the same time. He has not seen any robot hand design, even a prototype, that does this. It is what Tesla is aiming for.
- Hand engineering problems:
  - Touch sensors must work and be well placed. Too near the surface and they get damaged.
  - Sensors must deform elastically and survive long cycles. It is hard to find ones that do not fail after a few thousand cycles.
  - Human hands continuously repair themselves. Robots do not.
  - The robot's rubber skin slips over the inner plastic and metal fingers. Opening a pickle jar can spin the skin instead of the lid. The skin must stay in place and be replaceable.
- The AI chip must also command torque and run a vision and touch control loop.
- Elon's test of real AI: show it something it has not seen and it still handles it. Most complex-looking robot demos are scripted routines. He compares this with Full Self-Driving working in cities it has never visited. Robots have many more degrees of freedom than cars.
- Production ramp:
  - The early ramp will be very slow, then reach very high volume.
  - Unlike cars, there is no existing supply chain for the important robot parts.
  - Last year China blocked export of magnets used in electric motors. This delayed Optimus by 2-3 months because Tesla could not get the robot arms.
  - Elon wants Tesla able to make all parts in the US, even if another supply-chain disruption happens.

## Cybercab and robotaxi rollout: safety-first scaling

- Elon says "cross-country" is not the real goal. The goal is spreading across cities, and few miles are actually cross-country.
- Rollout looks slow, but monthly growth is "very fast" in percentage terms.
- Why Tesla moves carefully:
  - A single injury or death would become a global story, even though about 1 million people die in car accidents each year worldwide. Regulators could shut Tesla down.
  - Tesla does not want to hit pets either. A specific test case was gray cats on gray asphalt at night. Elon says Tesla solved it: make a change, test it, repeat.
- Before entering a new city, Tesla checks for problem intersections. It takes extra care at unguarded railroad crossings and unintuitive intersections where humans also make mistakes. Tesla geofences these areas or ensures it can handle both cases.
- He describes progress as fast, month after month.

## Elon's meta-habits: curiosity and questions

- He says he wishes people asked more questions he has not heard before. Many questions repeat, and he joked about making a web page of frequently asked questions.
- His broader approach, in his own words: "I had no idea how to make cars or rockets" either. He starts from the physics and equipment, concludes the task is hard but doable, and iterates.
