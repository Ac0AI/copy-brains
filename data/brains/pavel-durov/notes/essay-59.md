## Why Pavel left the US out of Telegram's plans: the FBI approach in May 2016

- In May 2016, during a one-week trip to America, the FBI tried to influence Pavel and bribe a Telegram engineer to make Telegram less secure.
- Neither of them is a US citizen, so they "could afford to refuse their offers" and Pavel told the public about the attempts.
- He points to a legal tool, the "gag order": US authorities can demand a company plant a backdoor in its app and also forbid it from telling the public, on pain of jail.
- If the team was pressured this much in one week, he asks what US-based tech companies face every day, and how a privacy-oriented company can operate permanently from America.

## Why jurisdiction matters for a privacy product

- Pavel's view is that a legal system being "open" does not help when gag orders are secret: "we would never even know if things went wrong."
- He treats Edward Snowden's revelations as confirmation of "some of the worst fears."
- The practical lesson he draws: where the company and its founders are based determines whether they can refuse pressure and speak publicly about it.
- Non-US citizens on a short trip could say no and disclose; US citizens could have been silenced.

## Distrust of government-funded "privacy advocates"

- He cites the article's facts as confirming a long-held fear: some famous, vocal US-based influencers in the cryptography world are sponsored by the US government to push its agencies' agenda.
- Known precedent he names: the NSA infiltrating RSA.
- He believes collaboration between US agencies and influential "privacy advocates" runs "much deeper" than the known cases.
- Implication he draws: don't take security advice or reputation signals at face value; check who funds the source.

## Platform dependence as a structural weakness

- Telegram depends on Google and Apple for mobile operating systems, and both are US-based, which makes protecting privacy "really hard."
- Pavel says he sees "no easy recipe or solution."
- His wish: huge companies like Apple and Google become independent of any government that can distort their founders' mission, and he jokes they might "start their own countries."

## Pavel's stance on resisting pressure

- He will keep building Telegram and protecting users, "even if that will require speaking out under gag orders."
- He acknowledges it could get him into trouble, as it did when he lived in Russia.
- He says it is the only way he can imagine going forward, so he has no regrets: "It's all worth it because of you guys – the millions of users who entrusted their private data to Telegram."
- He frames user trust (millions of users) as the reason the personal risk is worth taking.
