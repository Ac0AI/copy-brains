## Pavel's account of the Paris arrest and why he argues it was misguided

Pavel says he was interviewed by French police for 4 days after arriving in Paris. He was told he might be personally responsible for other people's illegal use of Telegram, because French authorities said they got no responses from Telegram. He disputes the premise on three grounds:

- **Reachable representative:** Telegram has an official EU representative that accepts and replies to EU requests. Its email address is publicly findable by googling "Telegram EU address for law enforcement".
- **Direct access to him:** As a French citizen, he was a frequent guest at the French consulate in Dubai. He personally helped French authorities set up a hotline with Telegram to deal with terrorism threats.
- **Wrong legal target:** The established practice, he says, is to take legal action against the service itself. Charging a CEO with crimes committed by third parties, using "laws from the pre-smartphone era", is in his words "a misguided approach".

## Founder liability and its effect on innovation

Pavel argues that personal liability for users' abuse of a tool kills building. His reasoning:

- "Building technology is hard enough as it is."
- "No innovator will ever build new tools if they know they can be personally held responsible for potential abuse of those tools."
- Regulators should pursue the platform as a legal entity, not the executive.

## Balancing privacy and security as a platform

Pavel describes this balance as hard and lists what a platform has to reconcile:

- Privacy laws versus law enforcement requirements.
- Local laws versus EU laws.
- Technological limitations.
- A platform wants consistent processes globally, but those processes must not be abused in countries with weak rule of law.

He says Telegram has been committed to engaging with regulators to find the right balance. Its principles are shaped by a mission to protect users in authoritarian regimes, but it has "always been open to dialogue".

## Willingness to exit markets rather than compromise principles

When Telegram and a regulator cannot agree on the balance, Pavel says Telegram is ready to leave that country, and has done so many times:

- **Russia:** Russia demanded "encryption keys" to enable surveillance. Telegram refused and was banned in Russia.
- **Iran:** Iran demanded Telegram block channels of peaceful protesters. Telegram refused and was banned in Iran.

His stated rationale: "we are not doing this for money." Telegram is driven by the intention to bring good and defend basic rights, particularly where those rights are violated. He frames being prepared to lose markets as the cost of staying compatible with the product's principles.

## Moderation facts he cites against the "anarchic paradise" claim

Pavel rejects media claims that Telegram is an anarchic paradise. His evidence:

- Telegram takes down millions of harmful posts and channels every day.
- It publishes daily transparency reports.
- It has direct hotlines with NGOs to process urgent moderation requests faster.

## Admitting gaps and committing to improve

Pavel concedes Telegram is not perfect and names specific problems to fix:

- Authorities being confused about where to send requests is something Telegram "should improve".
- The abrupt jump in user count to 950M caused growing pains that made it easier for criminals to abuse the platform.
- He made it his personal goal to significantly improve moderation. The process had already started internally, with details on progress promised "very soon".

He hopes the events will make Telegram, and the social networking industry as a whole, safer and stronger.
