## Health: seven things Pavel never does

- Pavel avoids seven things because he found they reduce productivity and clarity of thought:
  - alcohol
  - meat (fish is fine)
  - pills and meds (except at a dentist)
  - nicotine and other addictive drugs
  - coffee, black and green tea, and energy drinks (he drinks herbal teas)
  - fast food, sugar and carbonated soft drinks
  - TV and its alternatives
- He has lived without these for many years and says he has "been feeling great and healthy".
- He does not tell people with permanent health issues to stop their medication.
- He frames the list as a tradeoff. Young, healthy people may prefer fun to productivity and can ignore it. People who "enjoy building stuff more than consuming it" may benefit from abstaining.
- In 2025 he restated it: "My success and health are the result of 20+ years of complete abstinence from alcohol, tobacco, coffee, pills, and illegal drugs. Short-term pleasure isn't worth your future."

## Alcohol: the one piece of advice for people in their 20s

- Asked for one piece of advice for people in their 20s who want to build something great, Pavel says: "Never drink alcohol."
- He has not drunk alcohol in nearly 20 years. He has seen many successful people ruin themselves with it.
- Alcohol clouds mental clarity and intuition for days after drinking.
- He compares it to a loan: it lifts mood now, but "you'll pay it back with interest".
- He acknowledges social pressure, since humans evolved to copy those around them. He notes that most people drink alcohol, eat fast food and live passively, and says that is fine for them.
- His claim is that anyone aiming at something extraordinary improves their odds by keeping mind and body healthy.

## Information diet: feed the brain real-life data

- Pavel says the mind is our most powerful tool and generates new ideas even when we rest. Solutions to hard problems often arrive after a full night's sleep.
- Mental state depends on the quality of information consumed, just as physical state depends on food.
- Feed the brain real-life data that lets it solve fundamental problems, and it will process that data in the background and produce unexpected solutions.
- Most people instead consume random Netflix series or TikTok videos. He says the brain cannot tell fiction from reality at a deep level, so the subconscious ends up solving problems that do not exist.
- To be creative and productive, clear the "sticky mud of irrelevant content" that recommendation algorithms push daily.
- Personal practice:
  - He never watches horror movies and prefers uplifting success stories ("Wonka" was his favorite of 2023).
  - He believes songs replayed many times work like spells that plant suggestions, so he picks lyrics without undesirable scenarios.
  - He starts the day with joyful mid-20th-century songs such as "Oh, What a Beautiful Morning" and "What a Wonderful World".
- He ties this to Telegram's design. Users get only news from channels they joined and can unsubscribe at any time, which keeps Telegram independent and lets people choose their own feed.

## Undervalued and overvalued things (age 37 list)

- Undervalued:
  - **Sleep:** boosts immunity, creativity and psychological well-being.
  - **Nature:** the environment humans are biologically designed to feel good in.
  - **Solitude:** gives freedom for spiritual and intellectual breakthroughs.
- Overvalued:
  - **Big cities:** pollution, crime and noise. Use their resources but live outside their borders.
  - **Restaurants:** the slowest and least efficient way to eat. Home cooking gives healthier diets and ingredient control.
  - **Hot weather:** brings a lax attitude, cancer risk and faster aging. Cold "clarifies spirit, body and mind".
  - **Fashion:** conforming to trends is expensive and unnecessary. Comfortable clothes simplify life and free space for what matters.
  - **Real estate:** limits choices and is a questionable investment. Renting gives freedom to move.
  - **Social media:** the junk flow lowers happiness and creativity. Disconnecting is the best thing to do on any given day.
  - **Celebrity advice:** famous people advise outside their fields. For important things, rely on hard science and expert opinion.

## Independent thinking over imitation

- Pavel says humans are hardwired by evolution to copy others, and for millions of years that was the most efficient survival strategy.
- In an age of rapid change, he says, copying becomes "a recipe for mediocrity and decay".
- His New Year 2025 message: rely on your own opinions, not "imposed stereotypes and outdated thought patterns".
- He argues that trusting your instincts and following your own path is how people fulfil their potential and contribute to the world.
- This links to his warning that the habits of the majority (drinking, fast food, passive lifestyles) are self-destructive.

## Lessons from his father (age 80)

- Pavel's father is a leading expert in Ancient Roman literature. When he turned 80, Pavel asked what advice to pass to the next generation. He gave three points:
  1. **Lead by example.** People, especially kids, follow what you do, not what you say. Watching his father work tirelessly on books and scientific papers showed Pavel and his brother dedication and inspired them to work hard.
  2. **Focus on the positive.** Growing up in post-war Leningrad, his father learned to control emotions to be a positive force for family, colleagues and society. He taught Pavel to frame thoughts in ways that bring the most good, even in hard times.
  3. **Prioritize conscience.** After studying and translating thinkers from Julius Caesar to Seneca, his father concluded that morals outlast talent. Pavel calls moral compass "the ultimate human quality that will not lose value even in the age of AI".

## Why he defends a free Internet (age 41)

- On his 41st birthday in 2025 he said he did not feel like celebrating because "our generation is running out of time to save the free Internet".
- He argues the promise of free information exchange is being turned into a tool of control, citing:
  - digital IDs (UK)
  - online age checks (Australia)
  - mass scanning of private messages (EU)
  - prosecution of online criticism in Germany, the UK and France
- He warns that his generation risks being "the last one that had freedoms — and allowed them to be taken away".
- He says people were told the main fight is to destroy tradition, privacy, sovereignty, the free market and free speech, and that this path leads to moral, intellectual, economic and biological self-destruction.
