## Telegram's contest model: hiring and building through competitions

Pavel treats crowdsourced contests as the main way Telegram finds engineers and gets work done. In 2019 he announced the Instant View 2.0 Competition, a crowdsourcing contest with a $300,000 prize fund ending on 4 April.

- The task was to write rule sets ("templates") that generate Instant View previews for links shared on Telegram. Anyone who understands HTML/CSS could take part.
- Pay was $100 for each correct template, plus $10,000 and $5,000 for the top 2 contributors.
- He said "Eventually there'll be a competition for everything we do (Android, C++, voice calls etc)." Winners get large prizes and a chance to join the dev team.

By 2025-2026 contests were the only route into the engineering team.

- In January 2025 Telegram and TON Core launched a developer contest with a $200,000 prize fund for the first round alone. Exceptional participants could join Telegram or TON Core, and their contest code might be used to improve the TON blockchain. His line: "Competition drives progress!"
- In June 2025 he advertised an Android developer role paying $1M a year after tax. The only way in was to win a coding contest ending on 11 July. The task was to implement an animation from a mockup in Telegram's open-source Android app.
- In May 2026 $100,400 went to 15 winners of the TON Consensus Challenge. Engineers, some with AI assistants, helped maximize the security of the new consensus mechanism. He stated that "Telegram contests offer generous prize money — and the only path to joining our engineering team."

## Scale of contest spending and backing the competitive-programming world

In 2026 Pavel put contests at the center of how Telegram spends on talent.

- In one month (reported 6 September 2026) Telegram awarded over $2,222,000 to winners.
- In the latest week alone $222,000 was split between the winners of the Design Contest and the Digital Freedom Contest.
- Over the same month Telegram awarded prizes worth more than $2 million to winners of the 2026 International Olympiads in Informatics and AI.
- On 11 September 2026 Telegram became the sponsor of Codeforces, the largest competitive programming platform in the world. Codeforces runs over 100 coding contests a year and has 4 million contestants signed up.
- Pavel has supported Codeforces since 2010 through the companies he started. He said this year would be special because its founder Mike wants to take Codeforces to the next level.

## Betting on new technology: the Swift rewrite of Telegram for iOS

Pavel describes a deliberate habit of adopting new technology early.

- For a couple of years Telegram quietly rebuilt its iOS app from scratch in Swift, Apple's faster alternative to Objective C.
- The Swift version looked identical to the old one but was faster, slicker and more battery-friendly. The old app was already considered the fastest among messaging apps.
- It shipped publicly from January as a separate app called "Telegram X". Thousands of users tested it and sent feedback, which polished it before the switch.
- In September 2018 he planned to replace the default iOS app with the Swift version within a week or two. He accepted that a new codebase could bring minor bugs and promised quick fixes ("swiftly").
- This would make Telegram the most popular messaging app written fully in Swift. His view: "somebody has to take such risks and be the first to implement new technologies, such as e2e encryption – or Swift."

## Shipping fast and building features users ask for

Pavel shows how quickly the team turns an idea into a shipped feature.

- Users wanted to move chat history from WhatsApp to Telegram. He realized two weeks before 29 January 2021 that it was theoretically possible, and the feature shipped on iOS and Android the day before his post.
- It also supports KakaoTalk and Line. He noted nothing like it had been done by a major app and the team "had to implement some serious magic" to make it work.
- Imported messages are appended to the end of the target chat but keep their original timestamps.
- Next, the team started work on merging imports into one unified timeline for target chats with fewer than 100 messages.
- He promised free APIs so third-party developers can build import tools from any app.
- Rationale: moving messages saves disk space and removes worry about third-party backups or changing devices.
- He frames this as principle: the paper plane in the Telegram logo means "freedom", and data portability and freedom of choice are paramount. "People should be in complete control over their own data – and their own lives."

## Copy only what works, then innovate: how Telegram and MemHash won

Pavel's rule for entering a crowded market is to borrow selectively and differentiate elsewhere.

- Using the mini app MemHash as the example, he said it was built single-handedly over a weekend. It inspired dozens of copycats and created a new genre of mining mini apps on Telegram.
- His lesson: "there's always room for a new app on Telegram", provided you deeply understand which features made predecessors popular. The key is to borrow only what works while innovating and differentiating elsewhere.
- He applies the same logic to Telegram. It did not invent messaging; in 2013 it took only what was necessary from existing apps.
- It then kept listening to users and innovating, adding hundreds of useful features over 12 years. That brought popularity and helped establish a new genre of messaging.

## Solo builders and the mini app platform

Pavel celebrates small-team and solo success on the Telegram mini app platform.

- In November 2024 he described a developer who launched a mini app that earned $500,000 in revenue within ten days of launch. It was built in two days in time off from his main job.
- With no team or marketing budget, he handled design, concept, client-side code and server-side code himself. He was inspired by Pavel's own solo journey creating VK in 2006.
- Credited drivers were a brilliant concept and minimalist design, which made it go viral. It ranked in the Top 4 grossing mini apps on Telegram for those ten days.
- Pavel's takeaway: "This is the power of hard work, determination, and the Telegram mini app platform!"

## Pavel's stance on app store gatekeepers

Pavel argues that platform review processes slow shipping and hurt developers.

- In August 2022 he said the team is often unable to distribute new versions because of the "obscure 'review process'" imposed on mobile apps by tech monopolies.
- Telegram's update that was about to "revolutionize how people express themselves in messaging" had been stuck in Apple's review for two weeks, with no explanation or feedback.
- He noted that if a top-10 global app gets this treatment, smaller developers fare worse. The delays are demoralizing and cause direct financial losses to hundreds of thousands of apps.
- He added that this harm comes on top of the 30% tax Apple and Google take, which they say pays for app review. He said regulators in the EU and elsewhere were slowly looking into these practices, but damage already done would not be undone.
- He still voiced enthusiasm for the work itself: "What could be more exciting than improving the way hundreds of millions of people communicate?"
