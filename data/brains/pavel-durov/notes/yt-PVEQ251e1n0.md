## Telegram's positioning: freedom, no ad-data selling, and sharing revenue

Pavel frames Telegram around freedom: a logo (paper airplane) that "symbolizes freedom to move in three dimensions," freedom to communicate privately and to express yourself publicly. He says Telegram allows more freedom to third-party developers than any other major communication app, and that it integrated blockchain more deeply than any big social platform.

Scale he cites:
- 900 million monthly active users.
- 360 million of them interact with mini apps each month (stated as "300 UH 60 million" in the transcript, read here as 360 million).
- Telegram is the sixth most used and sixth most downloaded mobile app.
- Later this year Telegram plans to start suggesting mini apps and chatbots to users, which he expects to lift mini-app/chatbot users to over 700 million monthly.

How he says Telegram differs from the other top-five apps:
- It does not use personal data to target ads and does not sell it to advertisers. All ads are context-based.
- It does not put algorithmic feeds between creators and their audience, and does not shadow-ban creators for posting their own promotions.
- It shares revenue. He credits Telegram's success to app developers, community leaders, content creators, sticker artists and users who invite friends, and says users are "equal to us."

## Ad revenue sharing: 50% to channel owners, paid on blockchain

- Last month (March 2024) Telegram launched what Pavel calls one of the most generous revenue-sharing models in social media history: 50% of proceeds from ads shown in broadcast channels goes to the channel owners.
- First withdrawals were happening on the day of the keynote, and many people had already earned money.
- Ad payments and withdrawals run exclusively on the TON blockchain. He notes this puts a market of "several hundred million dollar" today, and potentially tens of billions in ad spend, "on the rails of blockchain."
- Next step: optional revenue sharing for large public group chats (many-to-many communities), also with half of proceeds going to owners.
- Within a few weeks: revenue sharing for tips to creators, which he calls a multi-billion-dollar market. Purchases are constrained by Apple and Google in-app purchase mechanisms, but withdrawals will be on blockchain (TON).
- His stated rationale is moral as much as commercial: the earlier model where a platform "operated almost like a digital slavery should be over," even if it "may not make much sense in terms of commercial interest of Telegram."

## Merchants and digital goods

- For several years Telegram has let developers and merchants sell physical goods and services on the platform free of charge.
- It has integrated 40 payment providers, such as Stripe, so users can easily buy from mini apps and bots.
- Next stage: a no-code store setup so simple merchants can open a shop and accept payments without coding.
- In a few weeks: users will be able to buy digital goods and services from app developers. Again limited by Apple/Google in-app purchase rules, but withdrawals will happen on blockchain, with another revenue-sharing model he calls one of the most generous in history.

## Tokenizing usernames and anonymous numbers on TON

- Telegram was the first social platform to tokenize its entire username space, via the Fragment platform. He says that market reached $350 million in sales.
- Users can own a username directly, sell it, auction it, and attach multiple usernames to an account.
- Fairness example: Pavel tried to win a nice username for a famous friend in a Fragment auction and lost. "The smart contract... doesn't care whether it's the CEO and owner of Telegram."
- Telegram was also the first major platform to let users sign up and log in with a blockchain wallet, using "anonymous numbers," NFTs on TON that work like usernames.
- Anonymous numbers had utility, unlike "monkey NFTs," and their price rose 33 times. He says many people made money buying and reselling them, which he did not expect.

## Next step: stickers and emoji as NFTs

- Pavel says Telegram believes in "socially relevant NFTs" deeply integrated into culture and communication, ones seen "tens of billions of times" that can spread virally.
- The next step is tokenizing Telegram stickers. His example is a smoking-duck sticker made by one of the platform's most talented sticker artists. It got "hundreds of millions of views at least" and became a meme outside Telegram. He suggests owning it as an NFT from the start might have rivalled the anonymous-number price rise.
- Stickers are shared more than 700 billion times a month on Telegram.
- He says emoji could become NFTs too, and sticker and emoji sets might even become their own meme coins.

## Why TON, and Telegram's lean team

- Pavel says Telegram relies on TON because of scale: it needs a chain able to process tens or hundreds of millions of transactions, and TON set a record for transactions per minute last year. He also cites fast transactions and low fees.
- He says the logo and its resemblance to a paper airplane are not the reason.
- Pavel states that Telegram runs with a small headcount of around 60 people (this is attributed to him by Paolo Ardoino later in the talk, so treat the figure as secondhand).
