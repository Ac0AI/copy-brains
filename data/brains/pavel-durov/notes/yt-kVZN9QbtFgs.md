## Why Telegram exists: other messengers "suck"

Pavel built Telegram because he thinks existing messengers are poor products. His case against WhatsApp (as of 2015):
- If the phone dies or the battery runs out, you lose access to your messages.
- No real cross-device sync.
- Limits on sending documents and large media.
- Many limitations in group chats.
- It is not private.

He says "it doesn't matter how many other messaging apps are out there if all of them suck." He had been a fan of WhatsApp a few years earlier, but changed his mind.

## Privacy as a product and retention advantage

- He ties privacy to real harm. A friend in Russia was shown a printout of her WhatsApp messages at a police department, which he says was used to try to blackmail her.
- Business users are the most exposed, because their information is more valuable and they can be blackmailed or compromised.
- He says users stay with Telegram because they trust that messages can't be intercepted. It is one of several differentiators.
- In its first two years Telegram "haven't disclosed a single byte of data to third parties including governments," and he says that "was not easy."
- On terrorists using the platform, he argues that the right to privacy matters more than the fear of bad things like terrorism. Groups such as ISIS would switch to another channel if one were insecure, and open-source encrypted apps already exist. He doesn't think Telegram should feel guilty and says it is doing the right thing by protecting users' privacy.

## Bot platform as an ecosystem and app store

- The bot platform launched in June 2015. Third-party developers use a simple API to create a bot, a "user" you talk to on Telegram, with a machine handling messages on the other side.
- At about three months old, categories included dating, education and productivity.
- He had learned the day before that one of the most successful bots received an eight-digit acquisition attempt.
- He compares bots to apps for Telegram, and thinks developers may end up building real businesses on it.
- Planned next step: a payment API so bot developers can accept money from users inside Telegram.
- Advertising: some third-party bots already send ads, and there is a third-party agency for it. He is "not a big fan of ads", but users opt in to receive messages from those bots, so it is allowed.

## Content moderation as a business decision

- Telegram blocks porn bots in markets where porn is prohibited, such as Iran, Iraq and Saudi Arabia. Blocked porn websites were reappearing as bots on Telegram, bringing in users the team didn't want.
- He says it is a business decision, not a secret government deal, and doesn't want Telegram perceived as a source of porn.
- He points out Apple, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube and Google do the same.
- Hard line: Telegram would never block a bot that criticises governments or supports opposition activity.

## Growth and engagement metrics

- Over 60 million users. Messages delivered daily grew from 1 billion in February 2015 to 10 billion announced the previous month, and over 12 billion by the day of the interview.
- He reads the jump as users switching: people who installed Telegram as a backup app now use it as their primary messenger.
- He says people tend to start their day in their messaging app, and messaging is "probably the biggest thing going on in social media in this decade."
- He dislikes announcing user numbers. Media compare Telegram's 60-100 million with WhatsApp's 800-900 million, ignoring that Telegram was two years old and WhatsApp over six.

## Super-app strategy: cautious about the Asian model

- On WeChat-style services (ordering cars, goods, pizza), he says "yes and no."
- That model needs very high market penetration and social relevance everywhere. Telegram is number one in a few markets but lacks the dominance WeChat has in China, Line in Japan or KakaoTalk in Korea.
- Telegram will experiment with payments, bots and third-party apps built on top of it, but doesn't see itself going the full super-app route in the near future.

## Staying independent, lean and nomadic

- No outside funding for Telegram. He prefers independence and says "there's more than market shares and revenue streams."
- He has enough money to last a long time and says Telegram will keep doing "what we think is right."
- The team is about 15 people. They rent houses short term around the world (Finland in summer, Paris in spring) and build together there.
- Design and customer support are outsourced to Eastern and Southern Europe.
- He sold his VKontakte stake just before the Ukraine events and the currency fall, and says the company's value is now probably three or four times lower. He calls himself very lucky on timing.
- He plays down Russia as a market. It is roughly Telegram's number seven by size, and he already owned the Russian market with VKontakte, so it is "not a new challenge."
