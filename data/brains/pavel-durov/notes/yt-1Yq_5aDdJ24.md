## The ship of personal freedoms is already sinking

- Pavel opens with the Titanic: passengers stayed aboard for almost 2 hours after the iceberg strike, lifeboats left half empty, and panic came only in the last half hour, when it was too late.
- He says the ship of personal freedoms has already hit the iceberg, and that freedoms have eroded almost everywhere in the world.
- His authority comes from running large social media platforms for exactly 20 years. He says he has seen the PR tricks, legal tricks, political tricks, manipulations and official excuses governments use to take away basic rights.
- His main worry is that tactics used by authoritarian regimes in Russia, China and Iran are now being used in some Western countries.
- He says "there is no second West" and "no backup civilization". If Western freedom sinks, he expects the rest of the world to follow. Dissidents used to flee to the West, and in a decade or two they may not be able to tell whether they have left a repressive homeland or entered another open-air prison.

## Why age verification and teen social media bans fail

- Pavel cites the European Commission's push to require ID before anyone can access social media. The stated reason is child protection, via a ban for under-16s that needs age verification.
- He says the experiment has already been run in Russia. Russia banned Telegram, then the largest social platform there, because Telegram refused demands for mass surveillance and censorship.
- Even so, 95% of Russian teenagers still use Telegram every month, through VPNs.
- Russia has invested billions in fighting VPNs. It bans hundreds of VPNs every month using IP addresses and traffic patterns, and installed surveillance hardware in every data center of every internet provider. He says it failed anyway.
- Iran has blocked Telegram since 2018, and the majority of the population still uses it.
- His conclusion is that banning social media for part of the population just pushes people onto VPNs. Forcing teenagers onto VPNs unlocks nasty, illegal, fringe content and previously banned websites, so children end up in more danger than before.
- He points to the UK Online Safety Act. Publicly it was sold as protecting children, but in a submission to the High Court of England published the year before, the British government said the main part of the Act "was aimed at capturing large platforms with significant influence over public discourse". Pavel reads this as child protection used as a pretext for control over political speech.

## "Protect the children" as a pretext that bypasses debate

- He argues that "child protection" triggers ancient, deep parts of the brain. Everyone fears for their offspring, so it "bypasses logic. It bypasses debate. It bypasses rationality."
- He says authoritarian regimes over the last century have smuggled all kinds of repressive legislation in under this guise.
- The same justification is used for "chat control", the European Commission's 5-year effort to force every messaging app to put a backdoor in its encryption. Every private message and photo sent on apps like Telegram or WhatsApp would be automatically monitored, with alerts to authorities if the system detects something illegal.
- The French government has tried a national version. The French Senate approved a mass surveillance law, which got stuck in the National Assembly. France's justification was fighting crime, terrorism and drug trafficking.
- He calls the trade of essential liberty for temporary safety "always a scam" and quotes Benjamin Franklin: "Those who are willing to give up their essential liberty for some temporary safety deserve neither liberty nor safety."

## Why surveillance does not stop crime but does harm ordinary people

- Pavel asks whether people in Russia or Iran feel safer than 10-15 years ago. They lost many freedoms and he doubts they gained safety.
- Once police can read messages, criminals switch first to more secure tools: VPNs, private secret apps, even their own encrypted messenger, which he says AI now lets them code themselves. "The criminals will be fine." Law-abiding citizens bear the cost.
- Personal experience at VK: about 15 years earlier, while he ran VK, the largest Russian social network, police data requests began to look odd. VK then disclosed private message contents in some cases with an official justification, unlike Telegram today, which he says never does.
- The people named in these requests had nothing to do with the crimes described. VK investigated the investigators and found the police were corrupt: they sought innocent people's private messages to blackmail them, sell their data to criminals and harass them.
- He says this can happen in the West too. Last year a French tax official was caught selling financial data on holders of large crypto assets and other wealth to criminals.
- He links such leaks to a catastrophic rise in kidnappings in France: over 40 victims in the first 3 months of 2026, with people tortured, harmed and humiliated. His point is that the government collected too much data and failed to protect it, yet now wants more (private messages, and IDs of everyone on social media).

## Selective enforcement of laws as a tool of control

- Pavel describes a method used by authoritarians for decades and now exported from East to West, in steps:
  - Introduce a large number of laws.
  - Overburden an industry with excessive, mutually exclusive, contradictory regulation, so compliance is impossible and every entrepreneur or CEO can be treated as a criminal.
  - Choose carefully whom to persecute. A loyal owner who complies with unlawful political demands is ignored by law enforcement and courts. An owner who stands up for citizens' constitutional rights or refuses political demands gets full enforcement.
  - When criticized, the response is always "we're just enforcing the law".
- He says he experienced this in Russia, with a criminal investigation against him in 2013.
- Elon Musk's claim: he said the European Commission offered him a secret deal to censor certain political narratives on his platform in exchange for not enforcing certain fines and laws. Musk said he refused, while most other platforms accepted. His company was then hit with a 120 million euro fine for seemingly unrelated issues, to do with transparency.
- Pavel admits Musk's words can be doubted, but says the US Congress investigation indirectly confirms them. Congress published emails from the pandemic in which European Commission officials pressured Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and others to ban, censor, down-rank, shadow ban and demote inconvenient accounts and narratives.
- The stated purpose was fighting misinformation and conspiracy theories. He says many of those theories later proved at least partially true or worthy of debate (the artificial origins of COVID, the limited nature of anti-COVID measures, the harm of school closures). In his words they were silenced "because they were inconvenient", not because they were untrue.

## Pavel's own case in France and the AI surveillance warning

- After he was arrested in Paris in 2024 for reasons he says are still unclear, he says the head of French intelligence offered him a secret deal asking him to silence certain political voices. He refused and is still under investigation, facing many years in prison.
- During roughly 3 days of interrogation, a translator appointed by the judge, who had left the Soviet Union in the 1980s to escape limited freedoms, told him she felt like she was "back in the Soviet Union".
- He compares today to the late Soviet era. His parents sent letters and telegrams hoping the KGB would not open them, and surveillance had a human limit: only so many officers and hours in a day.
- AI removes that limit. If privacy is given up, he warns, every message will be monitored and analyzed, every draft, thought and idea scored, and every relationship mapped. The future could be "much darker and more dystopian than the Soviet Union of the '80s".

## Pavel's call to action

- He says there are no lifeboats and no backup plan, unlike the Titanic's passengers who at least had some. The only option is to fix the ship.
- He urges recognizing the gravity of the situation and acting now, before the loss of freedoms spreads everywhere.
- His platform-level stance, as stated: Telegram refused demands for mass surveillance and censorship even when it was banned in Russia as a result, and it does not disclose private message contents.
