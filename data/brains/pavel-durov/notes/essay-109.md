## Why Pavel says WhatsApp can never be secure

- **Core claim:** Pavel argues that WhatsApp's security failures are structural, not accidental. His stated position is that "backdoors in WhatsApp would keep getting discovered, and one serious security issue would follow another."
- **Scope of the damage:** He says WhatsApp's flaws compromise more than WhatsApp messages. A hacker only had to send a user a video to put all data on the phone at the attacker's mercy. He calls the app "a Trojan horse" used to spy on non-WhatsApp photos and messages.

## Why Pavel doubts Facebook's intentions

- Pavel notes that Facebook was part of surveillance programs (he cites PRISM and the NSA's data taps on major net firms) before it acquired WhatsApp.
- He calls it "naive to think the company would change its policies after the acquisition."
- He points to WhatsApp co-founder Brian Acton's admission about the sale to Facebook: "I sold my users' privacy."
- His conclusion: WhatsApp is "a honeypot for people that still trust Facebook in 2019."

## How Pavel reads Facebook's "no evidence of exploitation" defense

- Facebook said it had no evidence the video backdoor had been exploited by hackers.
- Pavel calls this a deliberate confusion of the public. His reasoning is that Facebook can't have evidence because it can't analyze the videos users share. WhatsApp doesn't permanently store video files on its servers.
- He adds that WhatsApp sends unencrypted messages and media of "the vast majority of their users" straight to Google's and Apple's servers (the cloud backup issue). So there is "nothing to analyze," hence "no evidence. Convenient."
- He argues a flaw of this magnitude is "bound to have been exploited."

## The track record Pavel cites

- The previous WhatsApp backdoor was used against human rights activists and journalists. Pavel cites reports of about 100 journalists and dissidents targeted, and of government officials around the globe targeted through WhatsApp.
- He cites a September 2019 report that data obtained through such backdoors will be shared with other countries by US agencies, and notes a UK deal that gives police access to suspects' Facebook and WhatsApp messages.
- Three WhatsApp backdoors are referenced in sequence: the latest (the video flaw), the previous one, and the one before that.

## Telegram comparison and the "accident vs. design" argument

- Pavel concedes it might be coincidence that WhatsApp "accidentally implements critical security vulnerabilities across all their apps every few months," but says "I doubt that."
- His benchmark: Telegram, "a similar app in its complexity," has had no issues of WhatsApp-level severity in the six years since its 2013 launch.
- His inference: "It's very unlikely that anyone can accidentally commit major security errors, conveniently suitable for surveillance, on a regular basis."

## Pavel's advice to users

- Regardless of the underlying intentions of WhatsApp's parent company, the advice is the same: "unless you are cool with all your photos and messages becoming public one day, you should delete WhatsApp from your phone."
