## Why Iran and Russia charges don't affect Telegram or Pavel

- Pavel says the charges "won't" affect Telegram or him personally.
- Travel bans cost him little. He has no ties to Iran. Russia is harder, since he is ethnic Russian (with a St. Kitts passport) and his parents live there, but he calls missing visits "a small price to pay considering the matters at stake."
- Telegram has no legal presence in either country, so there is nothing for those governments to pressure.

## How Telegram cut its last link to Russia

- The last link was "Telegraf", a Saint-Petersburg company that Telegram outsourced spam fighting to (the spam came from Russia and Iran).
- Telegraf mattered more in the early days, when Pavel lived in Russia. Its role shrank after he left in 2014 with the core team.
- Telegram terminated the contract in July 2017, after Russian authorities threatened to block Telegram in June. Telegraf let go all its employees and changed owner.
- When Russian authorities sent warnings to the London office in September, there was nobody in their jurisdiction to target, "not even Russian spam moderators."
- The tactic: keep operations, people and contracts outside any jurisdiction that can be used as leverage.

## Restrictive laws hurt the country that passes them

- Pavel argues: "it's 2017, and the world is open and connected." Archaic laws that limit freedoms mostly kill the passing country's own economy.
- Example: in recent years Google, Oracle and Microsoft (Skype) shut down development offices in Russia, and many smaller companies followed.

## Iran as a market

- Iran is not blocking Telegram. For the last few years about 40 million Iranians have used it to communicate securely and get news from independent channels.
- Instagram and WhatsApp are also accessible there, while almost all major internet services were blocked several years ago (some still are).
- Pavel sees Iran as becoming "more open and market-driven," though he wants a more IT-friendly prosecutor of Tehran.
- Contrasts: Chinese and North Koreans have far fewer freedoms, and Saudi Arabia throttled Telegram's traffic until recently.

## Stance on legal pressure

- Telegram serves users in Russia and Iran from "places that respect freedom," and hopes to open offices there someday, as other IT companies could.
- "We don't care if specific countries press charges against us for defending the privacy of our users."
- Policy: Telegram is always ready to cut all personal and business links to a place so it has no leverage over the company.
- Blocking a service in a territory doesn't always work, as he showed in an earlier post about China.
- His long-term view: "freedom and privacy will prevail," and those who want to return to the 1930s "will find themselves on the wrong side of history."
