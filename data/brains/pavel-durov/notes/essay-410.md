## Why Pavel opposes encryption backdoors

- Pavel argues a backdoor can't be limited to police: "it's technically impossible to guarantee that only the police can access a backdoor."
- Once introduced, a backdoor can be exploited by other parties, "from foreign agents to hackers," so the private messages of all law-abiding citizens can be compromised.
- He frames it as a rights issue: France would have been the first country in the world to strip citizens of their right to privacy. Even countries many Europeans see as lacking in freedoms have never banned encryption.

## Backdoors don't stop crime

- Pavel notes the French law was aimed at preventing drug trafficking, and says it wouldn't have helped fight crime anyway.
- Even if mainstream encrypted apps were weakened, criminals could still communicate securely through dozens of smaller apps.
- They would also become harder to trace because of VPNs.
- His takeaway: the policy burdens ordinary users while failing against its intended targets.

## Telegram's policy: exit a market rather than weaken encryption

- Pavel states that "Telegram would rather exit a market than undermine encryption with backdoors and violate basic human rights."
- He positions this against competitors: "Unlike some of our competitors, we don't trade privacy for market share."
- Privacy is treated as a non-negotiable principle that outranks growth in any single country.

## What Telegram will and won't disclose to authorities

- In its 12-year history (as of April 2025), Telegram "has never disclosed a single byte of private messages."
- Under the EU Digital Services Act, with a valid court order, Telegram would disclose only the IP addresses and phone numbers of criminal suspects, not messages.
- This sets a clear line: cooperate narrowly and lawfully on identifiers, never on message content.

## The 2025 French and EU timeline

- Last month (March 2025), the French Senate passed a law requiring messaging apps to implement a backdoor for police access to private messages.
- The National Assembly shot it down.
- Three days before the post (about April 18, 2025), the Paris Police Prefect advocated for it again.
- In April 2025, the European Commission proposed a similar initiative to add backdoors to messaging apps.

## How Pavel says to respond: keep explaining and defending

- Pavel's view is that lawmakers must keep hearing the argument that "encryption isn't built to protect criminals — it protects the privacy and safety of ordinary people."
- He treats these fights as recurring, not one-off: "The battle is far from over."
- He warns that "no country is immune to the slow erosion of freedoms" and that freedoms must be defended every day.
