## How Pavel prioritizes freedom over wealth and control

- Pavel says: "for me everything in my life was about becoming free", and that his mission is to let others be free through the platforms he builds.
- He owns no real estate, jets or yachts. His reasons: assets tie you to a location, and managing them eats time better spent on Telegram. He would rather "make decisions that would influence how a billion people communicate" than choose seat colors.
- He holds a few hundred million dollars in bank and Bitcoin (held for about 10 years) and does little with it.
- At VK he refused government demands to ban opposition communities (2011) and to hand over private data of Ukrainian protest organizers (2013). He sold his stake, resigned as CEO and left Russia rather than comply.

## Telegram's growth: zero marketing spend

- Pavel states Telegram has spent $0 on acquiring users or promoting itself on other platforms. Growth is "purely organic".
- Scale cited: almost 900 million users, about 2.5 million sign-ups per day, and he expects to cross 1 billion monthly active users within a year (said April 2024).
- His reasoning: "people are smart, people like to use good things." Once users discover the speed, security and features, they don't go back and recommend it to friends.
- He argues competitors are "five six years behind", copying features Telegram shipped years earlier, and only as pale copies. Quality, independence, privacy and freedom are the reasons people switch.
- Competitors, in his view, spend tens of billions on marketing and use PR firms, which is why smear claims (e.g. that Telegram is tied to the Russian government) spread easily.

## Staying small and private: ownership, team and hiring

- Telegram is 100% owned by Pavel. He avoided venture capital early because investor goals might conflict with the mission, and because independence lets the company move faster.
- Outside money so far: bonds issued about 3 years before the interview (debt, no equity), plus funds from an earlier cryptocurrency project. No one received equity or voting control.
- He is the sole owner, director and product manager. He still comes up with most features and works directly with every engineer and designer.
- The team is about 30 engineers, "like a Navy Seal team". There is no HR department (zero).
- Hiring runs through contests: Telegram hosts competitions for engineers on contest.com, repeated every month or two. The best performers across a series of contests may join the team.
- Division of labor at founding: his brother (two PhDs in maths, a cryptography expert) designed the encryption standard, still used with minor changes. Pavel handled the user interface, how the app works, and features.

## Why a private company is more efficient than a public one

- Pavel says most tech founders would agree that running a public company is less efficient: more accountability, redundancy and bureaucracy.
- He recounts that a former Twitter CEO told him a 20-person team could run the company, but mass layoffs would scare Wall Street, so headcount stayed.
- Public listing has upsides (cheap capital, using stock to acquire companies), but he is not pursuing it: "why would we lose this momentum right now."

## Choosing jurisdiction: why Dubai

- Before Dubai he tried Berlin, London, Singapore and San Francisco. Bureaucracy blocked hiring: in Germany he was told to run local newspaper ads for six months before hiring non-EU engineers.
- In San Francisco he was mugged by three men (he fought back and escaped), and he says US agencies gave Telegram too much attention: FBI agents met him at the airport, one appeared at his rented house at 9 a.m., and he says an engineer was approached to introduce open-source tools that could serve as back doors.
- He moved to the UAE about 7 years before the interview, planning a half-year trial.
- His reasons for staying:
  - Ease of doing business: hire anyone worldwide, and residence permits are granted automatically for well-paid hires.
  - Tax efficiency.
  - Great infrastructure for the taxes paid.
  - Neutrality: a small country not aligned with any superpower, which he sees as the right base for a neutral platform.
- He reports zero government pressure from the UAE in 7 years.

## Principles on neutrality, censorship and pressure

- Telegram applies rules equally to all sides, opposition or ruling party. He believes competition between ideas produces progress.
- He distinguishes legitimate requests (e.g. groups promoting violence or terrorism, which Telegram helps with) from ones that cross the line against free speech and private correspondence, which it ignores.
- His example: after January 6, a Democratic congressman requested data on the "Uprising". Weeks later a Republican letter said complying would violate the Constitution. Telegram ignored both. His rule of thumb: "if you ignore your problems most of them do go away."
- During Covid, Telegram helped dozens of governments spread their messages but did not remove skeptical accounts. He says it was probably the only major platform that didn't, and that "looking back I think it was the right strategy."
- He says the biggest pressure on Telegram comes from Apple and Google, who can remove the app from their stores. Rules are generic (no violence, discrimination, child abuse); the conflict is in interpretation. Telegram appeals, sometimes wins, and otherwise removes content only in the store-distributed version.

## Personal security and competition

- He assumes the devices he uses are compromised, and says he has little faith in US-developed platforms for privacy after his US experience.
- He avoids traveling to big geopolitical powers (China, Russia, the US) and "weird places".
- He welcomes Elon Musk's purchase of Twitter because X is innovating rather than just copying proven features, and he sees more such companies as good for the industry.
- He recalls a meeting with Mark Zuckerberg over 10 years earlier: Zuckerberg asked whether Pavel would expand internationally and Pavel said no; they both ended up entering each other's territory within weeks. He says Zuckerberg's team copied what he told them about VK's app platform (launched 2009).
