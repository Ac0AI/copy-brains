## Telegram survived Russia's block by shipping anti-censorship tech before the ban landed

- Roskomnadzor blocked Telegram in Russia in April 2018.
- Pavel's team knew the block was coming and prepared in advance.
- By the time the block went live, the apps already supported rotating proxy servers, ways to hide traffic and other anti-censorship tools.
- The first week of the ban was still rough, and many Russian users had connection issues.
- Roskomnadzor blacklisted millions of IP addresses to stop access.
- After May 2018, Telegram was largely accessible in Russia anyway.

## Decentralised community support turned a ban into a growth non-event

- Thousands of Russian engineers set up their own proxy servers for Telegram users.
- The result was a decentralised movement called "Digital Resistance".
- Pavel credits this movement with restoring access after the first week.
- Telegram did not have to run all of the counter-measures itself. Users and volunteers carried a large part of the load.

## The ban did not shrink the user base

- Telegram's Russian user base has doubled since 2018.
- In May 2020, Telegram had 400 million monthly active users. At least 30 million of them were in Russia.
- Growth in Russia kept pace with growth in other countries.
- Pavel's verdict: "the ban didn't work."
- Roskomnadzor, under a director who had been in the role for two months, announced Telegram was no longer blocked. The announcement referenced Pavel's 4 June message explaining why the ban made little sense.

## Staying ahead of the censors requires constant upgrades

- Over two years, Telegram had to regularly upgrade its "unblocking" technology to stay ahead of the censors.
- Pavel calls the result unique among social media apps and says he is proud of it.
- If the block returns, he hopes few users will notice any difference.

## Redeploying anti-censorship capability instead of letting it decay

- Pavel does not want the technology to "get rusty and obsolete".
- Telegram is redirecting its anti-censorship resources to places where governments still ban it, such as Iran and China.
- He asks the admins of the former Russian proxy servers to focus their efforts on those countries.
- He expects more governments to try to block privacy-focused apps like Telegram as the political situation becomes more unpredictable, and wants the volunteers ready for that.
- Framing: "The Digital Resistance movement doesn't end with last week's ceasefire in Russia. It is just getting started – and going global."
