## Ads on Telegram: no ads in chats

- Pavel says Telegram will never show ads in private chats or group chats. They "are and will always be ad-free."
- Ads are considered only for large one-to-many channels, which don't exist in other messaging apps.
- Users who rely on Telegram as a messaging app, not a social network, will never see ads.
- Pavel's pitch to people switching from older apps is that switching won't increase the number of ads in their lives.
- This is how he answers an article warning users away from Telegram because "Telegram is going to introduce ads."

## Contextual ads, no user data targeting

- Pavel calls collecting private data to target ads "the way WhatsApp-Facebook do" immoral.
- He points to privacy-conscious services like DuckDuckGo as the model: monetize the service without collecting information about users.
- Any ads in one-to-many channels would be contextual, based on the topic of the channel and not on any user data.
- Positioning point: refusing data-driven targeting is both a principle and a differentiator against WhatsApp and Facebook.

## Fixing the existing channel ad market

- In most markets, content creators on Telegram already monetize by selling promotional posts in their channels.
- Pavel describes this as a chaotic market. Multiple third-party ad networks push intrusive ads that create a negative user experience.
- Telegram's plan is to fix this by offering channel owners a privacy-conscious alternative to those networks.
- His framing: the ads are already there, so an official, cleaner option improves the experience instead of adding to it.

## Opt-out and creator monetization options

- Users will be able to opt out of ads.
- Pavel considers privacy-conscious ads a good way for channel owners to monetize their efforts.
- Ads are one option among several. He names donations and subscriptions as alternatives, and says Telegram is also working to offer those.
- The aim is to give channel owners a choice of monetization strategies and not a single mandatory one.

## Building a financially sustainable creator class

- Pavel's stated end goal is a new class of content creators who are financially sustainable and free to choose the strategy that is best for their subscribers.
- He argues traditional social networks have "exploited users and publishers for far too long with excessive data collection and manipulative algorithms."
- His message: "It's time to change this."
- Strategy pattern: align creator income with subscriber trust by giving creators tools (ads, donations, subscriptions) without surveillance or algorithmic manipulation.

## Answering criticism of a planned monetization move

- Pavel rebuts the critical article point by point with three numbered reasons: no ads in chats, no data-based targeting, and fixing ads that already exist.
- He ties the answer to a December announcement in which he had already outlined that ads were being considered only for large channels.
- The tactic is to narrow the claim precisely (which surfaces, which data, which users) so "Telegram is going to introduce ads" is shown to be misleading.
