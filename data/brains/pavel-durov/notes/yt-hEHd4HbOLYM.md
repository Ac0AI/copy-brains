## Wikipedia as the most important digital project

- Pavel calls Wikipedia "arguably the most important digital project created so far by humankind" and says it is "always underestimated."
- His evidence is personal: 7 out of 10 of his Google searches end on a Wikipedia article, because it holds structured, objective information.
- He contrasts it with VK: VK is profitable with a clear, predictable financial future, while Wikipedia's financial future is not predictable. He still considers Wikipedia the far more important service for the global community.
- He argues that humanity evolves culturally and scientifically (not biologically) by passing knowledge to the next generation, and he sees Wikipedia as the best large-scale effort to accumulate that knowledge. He describes it as the memory section of the "global brain" idea his partner Yuri Milner talks about.
- He says Wikipedia does more to educate young, middle-aged and older people than some governments, so it is unfair that it must raise contributions over several months each year.
- At DLD12 he pledged $1 million to Wikipedia and called on other successful attendees to support it more broadly.

## Information vs. communication: which web products last

- Pavel sorts web services on an axis from pure information (gathering and structuring knowledge) to pure communication (facilitating interaction between people).
- Services on the information side become more useful over time as they accumulate widely useful, searchable content.
- Services on the communication side are more prone to fashion. His example: "You can use one instant messaging system today and then switch to some other instant messaging tomorrow."
- Facebook sits in between: mainly communication, but with a vast store of profiles, social graph and interest graph.
- His conclusion is that information-heavy products are more robust and longer-term.

## Greed vs. altruism: why users must not feel used

- Pavel adds a second axis, "greed": how greedy or altruistic users perceive a platform to be. He is explicit that more greed does not mean more profit; in most cases it is the other way around.
- Because most web services rely on user-generated content, users "must not feel offended" or "used" by the service. They must feel the service cares about them.
- Examples he gives of greedy platforms that declined:
  - Classmates.com, one of the first social networks, charged users for sending private messages.
  - StudiVZ, a famous German social network, leaned too hard on advertising and lost user loyalty (he notes several factors were at play).
- He notes Google Plus had no advertisements at the time, which made it the most altruistic of the networks he plotted.
- Combining both axes, he places Wikipedia in the best spot: high on information and highly altruistic, which he links to long-term survival.

## VK's position and growth at the time

- VK (VKontakte) grew from a website Pavel created five years earlier into the largest social network in Russia and Ukraine and other countries. Its international brand is "vk.com."
- Figures he cites (January 2012): 33 million daily unique visitors, about 77 million monthly actives, and roughly 160 million registered users.
- He cites comScore as ranking it the fourth most viewed website among Europeans, and says it was among the reasons Facebook was not doing well in Russia.
- He notes VK grew fast around the New Year, and that this is "probably one of the reasons why we banned advertisements promoting alcoholic beverages on our network."
- He jokes that VK is the most efficient tool for wasting your time.

## Open internet and regulation (response to the Wikipedia blackout)

- Pavel says any web project should be grateful that Wikipedia took a stand on SOPA/PIPA.
- He admits US laws did not directly affect VK, and that VK had no real copyright problems in the Russian legal system. He still says such a law would affect VK "in some future" if it passed.
- He agrees with the idea that US internet regulation inspires laws elsewhere, so poorly designed laws are bad for the industry everywhere.

## Entertainment industry and the VCR analogy

- Pavel recounts learning the day before that 30 years earlier the movie industry opposed the VCR, with claims it would destroy the industry and cost huge numbers of jobs. The video cassette business went on to yield huge profits for movie makers.
- He argues the internet is similar: a disruptive technology that "will eventually result in creating more profit for movie makers in case they learn how to use it."
