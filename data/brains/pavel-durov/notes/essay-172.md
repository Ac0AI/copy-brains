## Telegram's ad model: Sponsored Messages without user data

Pavel launched Sponsored Messages in November 2021 as Telegram's first official ad product, letting anyone promote channels and bots. His central claim: "Online ads should no longer be synonymous with the abuse of user privacy."

- Telegram's stated aim is to "redefine how a tech company should operate" by being a self-sustainable platform that respects users and content creators.
- At launch the product was in test mode and not available to everyone.

## Targeting by channel topic, not by user data

Pavel's privacy principle is that advertisers never get private data and none is mined to pick ads.

- Ads are matched solely to the topic of the public channel where they appear.
- No user data is analyzed or used for targeting.
- Pavel contrasts this with other apps: "unlike other apps we will not use your private data to display ads."
- He notes WhatsApp already shares user data with advertisers even though it shows no ads itself, while on Telegram "advertisers will never get your private data."

## Where ads appear: keeping the core messenger ad-free

Pavel draws a hard line around the personal messaging experience.

- No ads in chats. Sponsored messages can't appear in the chat list, private chats or groups.
- They show only in large public one-to-many channels with 1000+ members.
- Anyone using Telegram "the way you use WhatsApp" will never see a single ad.
- He argues channels are a feature added several years after launch (the messenger launched in 2013), so ads sit in a separate layer from the original product.
- He predicts that if WhatsApp adds a similar feature, it will likely show ads there too, as its parent company already does on Instagram and Facebook.

## Unobtrusive ad format

Pavel limits the format so ads stay light.

- Maximum 160 characters of text.
- No media and no external links.
- At most one sponsored message per channel.
- Shown only after the user has finished reading any new posts.

## Cleaning up the existing ad mess

Pavel frames the product as a fix for something already happening.

- Some channel admins already post ads as regular messages.
- He hopes Sponsored Messages become a more user-friendly and less chaotic way for people to promote their channels and bots.
- In effect, a native, constrained ad product replaces ad-hoc paid posts.

## Monetization and revenue sharing

Pavel sequences the economics: cover costs first, then share.

- Once the product is fully launched and covers Telegram's basic costs, he plans to share ad revenue with the admins of channels where the ads display.
- Costs he names: equipment and data centers used by channel admins to deliver content to "hundreds of millions" of users.
- His rationale for sharing: "because it is fair."
