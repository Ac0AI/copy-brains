## Message deletion: control over your own digital footprint

Pavel announced on 2019-03-24 that every Telegram user can now delete any message in a private conversation from both sides. Core idea: you should have control over data you created, even when it sits in someone else's inbox.

## The problem Pavel is solving

- Pavel has used private messaging for 23 years and built his own services for 16.
- Over 10-20 years, each person exchanges millions of messages with thousands of people. Most of those logs sit in other people's inboxes, outside the sender's reach.
- Relationships end, but chat histories with ex-friends and ex-colleagues "remain available forever."
- He expects the volume of private data held by chat partners to "easily quadruple" within a few decades.
- Despite progress in encryption and privacy, he argues users have "very little actual control of our data" because they cannot go back and erase things for other people.

## The risk he names

- An old, forgotten message can be taken out of context and used against you decades later.
- His example: a hasty text sent to a girlfriend in school could come back in 2030 when you decide to run for mayor.

## What shipped

- Any user can delete any message in a private conversation from both sides.
- It works regardless of who sent the message and when it was sent.
- Users can wipe out a whole conversation from both sides.
- "No trace will be left on either side."

## How he weighs the trade-off

- He acknowledges concerns about misuse of the feature and about the permanence of chat histories.
- The team "thought carefully through those issues" and still concluded that control over your own digital footprint "should be paramount."
- He sets user control above the objection that deletion could be abused.

## Product principle

- Privacy is framed as ownership of your own history, not just encryption in transit.
- He tests the feature on himself: looking through his own inbox, he finds little he would want to delete, yet says he feels "truly free and in control" for the first time in 23 years of private messaging.
- The value of the feature lies in having the option, even if it is rarely used.
