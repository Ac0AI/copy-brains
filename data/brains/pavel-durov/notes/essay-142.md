## Why Telegram must monetize and stay independent

- As Telegram nears 500 million active users, Pavel says a project of its size needs "at least a few hundred million dollars per year" for traffic and servers.
- He covered Telegram's expenses from personal savings for most of its history.
- He sees two options at this scale: earn money to cover costs, or sell the company.
- He rejects selling: "We are not going to sell the company like the founders of Whatsapp."
- His reasoning: Telegram must stay independent so users are respected and service quality is high, and he argues that is impossible once a company becomes part of a corporation.
- He wants Telegram to serve as an example of a tech company that strives for perfection and integrity.
- He frames Telegram as long-term: development began over 8 years earlier for personal use, and he says "This journey has just started."

## Monetization principles: non-intrusive, started next year

- Telegram will begin generating revenue starting in 2021, in line with its values and pledges from the previous 7 years.
- He argues that current scale makes it possible to monetize in a non-intrusive way: "Most users will hardly notice any change."
- The stated goal is funding that keeps Telegram independent, innovating, and able to grow to billions of users for decades.
- He wants to redefine how a tech company should operate while staying true to its values.

## Free tier stays free, paid features for power users

- Everything currently free stays free.
- New paid features will target business teams and power users.
- Some of these features need more resources, so premium users pay for them.
- Regular users keep Telegram "for free, forever."
- Pricing logic: charge the heavy users whose usage costs more, not the mass of regular users.

## Messaging stays ad-free

- All messaging parts of Telegram remain ad-free.
- He considers ads in private 1-to-1 chats or group chats "a bad idea."
- His principle: "Communication between people should be free of advertising of any sort."

## Ad Platform for public one-to-many channels

- Telegram has a social networking side besides messaging. Public one-to-many channels can have millions of subscribers each and work more like Twitter feeds.
- Channel owners in many markets already display ads, sometimes via third-party ad platforms. Those ads look like regular messages and are often intrusive.
- His fix: Telegram's own Ad Platform for public channels that is user-friendly, respects privacy, and covers server and traffic costs.
- Ads are confined to public broadcast channels, not private communication.

## Sharing revenue with the community

- His principle: if Telegram earns money, the community should benefit too.
- Ad Platform example: owners of large public channels monetized through it receive free traffic in proportion to their size.
- Premium stickers example: if Telegram introduces premium stickers with additional expressive features, the artists who make them get a part of the profit.
- Goal: "We want millions of Telegram-based creators and small businesses to thrive, enriching the experience of all our users."
- He calls this approach "the Telegram way."
