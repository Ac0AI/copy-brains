## Telegram's origin and the lightweight, secure protocol (2013)

- Pavel traces Telegram to 2013, when the team built a custom lightweight encrypted data protocol that could send messages securely over the weakest network connections.
- To keep Telegram "the fastest means of communication" everywhere, they set up a distributed server infrastructure all over the world.
- Secret chats, also launched in 2013, were "an instant hit" because they offered encryption and self-destruct timers, which mattered in the wake of Edward Snowden's revelations.
- He frames this as groundwork: security and speed came first, and later features were built on top.

## Letting usage lead product direction (2014)

- In 2014 Telegram reached broader audiences. Pavel noticed users loved cross-device synchronization and built-in cloud features.
- Users began moving activities from email and other messengers to Telegram, using it for business tasks as well as family and friends.
- The team responded by adding features that supported that use:
  - Instant search across millions of messages in chat history.
  - Strong laptop and tablet support.
  - Unlimited, very fast file transfer.
  - Massive group chats, kept manageable with replies, mentions and hashtags.
- His claim: all of this was added "without making the app more complicated." He says Telegram is still the easiest and simplest messaging app on the market.

## Keeping the app simple by building a platform

- Pavel's answer to "how do we add features without adding complexity?" is to build a platform and give third parties open APIs, so they create specific tools for specific audience needs.
- Telegram has been open from the start: in 2013 its API was 100% open and free, and he calls it probably the only massively popular messaging app with that property.
- In 2015 the team opened the bot platform. Third parties could build small apps inside Telegram using the familiar message interface as the UI.
- Thousands of developers used Telegram's social graph. Bots cover gaming, dating, custom services and integrating third-party applications.
- Forbes example: Forbes launched a Telegram bot letting users subscribe to news categories and search its database. It took Forbes only 3 days to set up and needs no extra human resources to maintain.

## Growth: 100 million users with zero marketing

- Telegram announced 100 million active users, up from 62 million announced in May of the prior year.
- Pavel says 350 thousand new users sign up every day (the transcript renders it as "350").
- All growth is "100% organic": "we have a zero marketing budget" and spend nothing on ads or other user acquisition.
- Growth comes from people recommending Telegram to friends and colleagues as a more efficient, reliable communication tool.
- Telegram has users in more than 200 countries, with at least a few million users in many of them.
- Engagement: 15 billion messages delivered daily, a huge increase over the previous year. He reads this as people using Telegram as their primary communication tool, not just one of several messengers.

## Channels: direct broadcast and why creators switch

- After bots launched, Pavel saw media and bloggers using them to broadcast customized messages and news to mass audiences.
- In September 2015 Telegram launched channels, a tool to publicly broadcast to an unlimited number of recipients, giving media and businesses a direct line to readers and customers.
- Scale cited: some channels have reached over 100 million recipients, and channels are viewed 400 million times a day. BBC has used channels since September to reach over 300,000 users, and the Pope's Lent messages were reported to reach thousands of members the same day.
- Why bloggers move: it creates a direct relationship with the reader. Pavel, who founded a social network 20 years earlier, says in traditional social networks "your voice could be easily lost in the chaos of news feeds," while in a messaging app it is "much more straightforward and direct."
- Channels are widely used in emerging markets as well.

## Release cadence and the bet on messaging

- Pavel believes in short production cycles and tries to launch new features at least every month. He says the update released that day was "literally built" within the last week.
- Each update is one in a continuing series, aimed at improving channels and bots.
- His forecast: "this could be the decade of messaging and things, products and services built on top of messaging."

## Privacy stance: Apple versus the US government

- Asked about Apple and the US government, Pavel says he would "definitely side with Tim Cook."
- His argument: building tools to unlock a PIN-locked iPhone slightly raises the risk that a stolen phone's private photos and data could be used for blackmail.
- He calls this "extremely dangerous" because hundreds of millions of people use iPhones.
