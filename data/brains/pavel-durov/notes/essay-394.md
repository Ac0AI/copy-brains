## Competition and public rankings drive excellence in education

- Pavel argues China's secondary education system is superior to the West's at producing outstanding performers in math and science.
- Evidence he cites: Chinese students have long outperformed others in math and programming at international olympiads.
- Pavel says China fosters fierce competition among students, a principle borrowed from the highly efficient Soviet model.
- He contrasts this with most Western schools, which discourage competition and prohibit public announcements of students' grades and rankings.
- He grants the rationale ("to protect students from pressure or ridicule") but says the measures predictably demotivate the best students.
- His core principle: "Victory and defeat are two sides of the same coin. Eliminate the losers — and you eliminate the winners."

## Motivation comes from visible rankings

- Pavel says many students are motivated to excel by treating high school as a competitive game, striving to rank first against strong opponents.
- Removing transparency in student performance can make school feel meaningless for ambitious teenagers.
- He observes that many gifted kids now find competitive gaming more exciting than academics, because in video games they can see how each player ranks.
- Takeaway for product and community design: visible leaderboards and rankings are a motivator for ambitious people, and removing them demotivates the strongest performers.

## Reality has public grades and rankings

- Pavel argues that telling all students they are champions regardless of performance may seem kind, but reality will shatter the illusion quickly after graduation.
- In his words, reality "does have public grades and rankings — whether in sports, business, science, or technology."
- He treats public benchmarks as the real-world equivalent of school grades, and says "more are coming."
- Example he gives: the AI benchmarks that demonstrate DeepSeek's superiority are a public ranking.
- Implication for builders: markets and benchmarks rank products and companies openly, so teams should expect to be measured against strong competitors rather than shielded from comparison.

## Why China caught up in AI, and the outlook

- Context: following the success of the Chinese startup DeepSeek (post dated 2025-01-29), many were surprised at how quickly China caught up with the US in AI.
- Pavel's view is that China's progress in algorithmic efficiency "hasn't come out of nothing"; it is rooted in a long-standing talent pipeline built through competitive math and programming education.
- His prediction: unless the US secondary education system undergoes radical reform, China's growing dominance in technology seems inevitable.
