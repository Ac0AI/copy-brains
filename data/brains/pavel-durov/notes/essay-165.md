## Individual freedoms are declining, and Big Tech controls the choke points

Pavel argues that on individual freedoms, the world is getting worse rather than better, despite the common assumption of yearly progress. He says most studies show humanity is now less free than several years ago.

- His comparison point: 20 years earlier (from his 2021-08-30 post), the Internet was decentralized and banking was relatively unrestricted.
- Today, he says, "Apple and Google censor information and apps on our phones while Visa and Mastercard limit what goods and services we can pay for."
- His summary of the trend: "Every year we give up more power and control over our lives to a handful of unaccountable corporate executives we didn't elect."

## Platform gatekeepers: app stores and payment networks

For Pavel, the control points are the layers every product depends on to reach users and get paid.

- **Distribution:** Apple and Google decide which apps and information appear on people's phones.
- **Payments:** Visa and Mastercard decide which goods and services people can pay for.
- He describes these as concentrated, unaccountable corporate power, with executives nobody elected setting the limits.
- Implication he draws: a builder's reach and revenue depend on a few intermediaries, and that dependence keeps growing.

## Surveillance and attention capture

Pavel criticizes the personal-data business model behind much of the consumer Internet.

- Most people "willingly carry tracking devices", meaning their phones.
- Corporations use users' private data to target them with content that keeps them distracted with low-quality entertainment.
- He also notes the spread of surveillance cameras, which in countries like China use AI "to make sure nobody can hide."
- His position is that tracking and ad targeting are tools for capturing attention rather than serving users.

## Authoritarian growth as a competing model

Pavel points to China's economic rise as a driver of the freedom decline.

- In 2017, China overtook the US as the largest economy in the world by purchasing power.
- He says this showed the world that "individual freedoms are not required for economic development."
- His claim is that, looking at China's success, more countries become authoritarian, curbing essential human rights such as freedom of speech, movement and assembly.

## Where talent is going, and who will fix it

Pavel asks who will reverse the trend, and he is critical of the answer.

- The most active and creative minds of his generation are "too busy playing in the rapidly shrinking sandbox called 'free enterprise'", or producing digital content that keeps everyone else glued to their devices for longer.
- Everyone else, he says, is too distracted by abundant cheap digital entertainment to assess the trend critically and act.
- He frames this as a talent-allocation problem: ambitious builders are optimizing for engagement and operating inside a narrowing space, instead of defending freedoms.

## Legacy framing

Pavel closes with a challenge to his generation about what it will be remembered for.

- Option one: "those who let free societies turn into dystopian nightmares."
- Option two: "those who defended the freedoms that previous generations fought so hard to win."
- This frames his own work, building a platform outside the gatekeepers' reach, as part of a larger duty to defend freedoms rather than only a product choice.
