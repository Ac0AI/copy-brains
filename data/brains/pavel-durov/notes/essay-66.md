## Freedom of speech as a core value

- Pavel treats freedom of speech as a human right Telegram defends alongside privacy: "You already know Telegram has your back when it comes to privacy. Another human right we feel passionately about is the freedom of speech."
- He says Telegram has defended it for 11 years (as of 2017): first in Russia, then globally.
- In some countries Telegram is the only popular platform for independent sources of information.
- Example he gives: in Iran the BBC is blocked, yet its @bbcpersian Telegram channel has been active for years and attracted almost a million followers.
- He notes there are thousands of large Telegram channels critical of their governments.

## Refusing political censorship

- Pavel states that "Telegram has never yielded to pressure from officials who wanted us to perform political censorship."
- Telegram's stance is presented as a long-standing, consistent position held across countries, not a one-off.
- The product's value in restrictive markets comes from this: users and independent media can reach audiences that state-controlled or blocked platforms cannot.

## Where the line is: Pavel's rule of thumb

- He describes the line as "pretty straightforward" and applies it whenever moderating public channels.
- Acceptable:
  - Criticizing local authorities
  - Challenging the status quo
  - Engaging in political debate
- Not acceptable:
  - Promoting violence
  - Calling for actions that can harm innocent people
- The test is about harm and violence, not about whether the speech is political or unpopular with governments.

## Rules for channel admins and enforcement

- Telegram's Terms of Service prohibit publicly promoting violence in any form. Channel admins must respect this.
- Enforcement scale he cites: in October 2017 alone, Telegram blocked over 8,500 channels related to terrorism, with more stats published in @isiswatch.
- Enforcement applies to public channels. The post frames the rules as applying to promotion of violence, not to political content.

## Case study: the Iranian vandalism-contest channel

- Pavel describes an Iranian channel that urged members to throw stones into windows of public buildings and vehicles (schools, temples, buses) and film it.
- His process before blocking:
  1. Telegram contacted the channel admins.
  2. It asked them to stop the "vandalism contest."
  3. The admins ignored the request and escalated.
- The escalation: the channel launched another competition urging its 100K+ users to burn mosques by throwing Molotov cocktails into them and film it.
- Outcome: "we were left with no other options but to block their channel."
- Takeaway: Telegram tries direct contact and a warning first, and blocks only when admins refuse to stop and escalate the calls for violence.
