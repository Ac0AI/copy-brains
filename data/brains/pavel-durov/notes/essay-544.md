## What the French case reveals: selective enforcement against Telegram

Two years after his Paris detention (held 3 days, the longest police can hold someone before charging), Pavel argues the French case is losing credibility. He calls it unprecedented: "French authorities accused the head of a major platform of crimes committed by its users." He says the investigation is still ongoing and "makes less sense with each passing year."

His core argument is evidence-based. Over two years, he says, extensive evidence shows Telegram was neither worse than other popular platforms at moderation nor the worst at cooperating with authorities. So the question becomes why Telegram was singled out.

## The pressure-then-smear pattern across countries

Pavel describes a pattern he has seen in multiple countries, mostly authoritarian ones:

- Telegram is quietly pressured to grant political favors, such as illegal censorship or surveillance.
- When Telegram refuses, local media and "non-governmental" organizations launch orchestrated campaigns.
- These campaigns portray Telegram as "a cesspool of crime, from child pornography to terrorism."
- The goal is to instill the idea that Telegram should be persecuted or restricted.

He maintains that Telegram's moderation is no worse than other major platforms'.

## Selective concern about crime and child protection

Pavel argues that officials suddenly care about crime and protecting children, but only on platforms that reject their secret political demands. Platforms that accept such deals "get away with almost anything," and he claims this includes "literally selling ads promoting child pornography." The implication is that moderation enforcement is used as political leverage rather than applied evenly.

## Is the French investigation political?

Pavel says the French case "certainly fits the pattern we see elsewhere" and "definitely raises questions." He predicts that "this investigation may itself be investigated," especially now that Macron's "war against free speech" is facing pushback.

## Resistance to free-speech restrictions as a signal

As evidence of that pushback, he cites that this month (August 2026) France's Constitutional Council struck down Macron's social media ban for children under 15, on freedom-of-expression grounds. He closes with the stance that "freedom and truth will prevail."
