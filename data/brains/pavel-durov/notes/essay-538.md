## Apple removed Telegram from the App Store over planted content

- On 2026-08-04, Apple briefly removed Telegram from the App Store because a single user had planted illegal pornographic content in a public group chat.
- Telegram was restored within hours.
- Pavel wrote the post to "warn other app developers and help protect online communities from similar attacks."

## How the attacker hid the content from moderation

- Telegram removes illegal content from public groups quickly, using "all kinds of moderation tools."
- To get around this, the attacker edited an old message in an active group chat and inserted AI-modified illegal content.
- Because the edit was backdated, the content was effectively hidden from the group's members. They could not see it, so they could not report it promptly.
- Pavel treats the need for such tricks as evidence the system works: "The fact that attackers must resort to backdated, effectively invisible content and other technical tricks proves this."
- His view: illegal pornographic content in Telegram's public groups is not a systemic problem, and Telegram's moderation is effective.

## Takedown extortion: the attacker's business model

- The attacker was a "takedown extortionist": someone who demands ransom from group owners in exchange for not targeting their communities.
- The method:
  - Automated accounts plant illegal content in public groups.
  - The extortionists report it directly to Apple.
  - The aim is to trigger the removal of legitimate communities whose owners refused to pay.
- Pavel notes the tactics are evolving, which puts communities across social platforms at risk.

## Platform risk: Apple removed the app before contacting Telegram

- Extortionists have found a way to manipulate Apple into overreacting.
- Apple removed Telegram before contacting the company.
- Pavel's argument: "If an app used by more than a billion people can be removed from the App Store without prior warning, any app can be."
- He calls this a potential systemic risk for every mobile app that hosts user-generated content.
- Takeaway for developers: dependence on a single app store is a distribution risk, since a single coordinated report can cut off even a billion-user app.

## Defending communities against coordinated reporting

- Telegram has extensive experience identifying the tricks used by coordinated reporting gangs and protecting legitimate communities.
- Pavel says Telegram does this even when it risks its own app being temporarily removed from the App Store, showing he prioritizes protecting communities over avoiding store-level risk.
- He warns that other platforms may not be equally prepared.
- His closing advice to developers and community owners: "Stay vigilant!"
