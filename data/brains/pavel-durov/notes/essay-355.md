## How Pavel built VK solo as a way to stay in touch with classmates

- Pavel launched VK (VKontakte, "in contact") on October 10, 2006, his first large company.
- The origin was personal: after graduating from Saint-Petersburg University in summer 2006, he wanted to keep in touch with classmates. He knew that would be hard without a site where everyone could find each other.
- In late August 2006 he set a goal of building a social network for university students and graduates in four weeks.
- It took six weeks instead of four. He calls the plan "overconfident", but says the result was worth it.

## Build everything from scratch, alone

- Pavel decided not to use any ready-made third-party modules. He built profiles, private messages, photo albums and search himself.
- His coding background: at 12 he built web games with vector animations and sound effects, and at 13 he taught Pascal to older kids in summer camps for programmers.
- Being the only team member minimized time spent on internal communication.
- Because he knew every line of the code base by heart, he could find and fix bugs faster.
- He tried to make each section flawless. He admits that obsessing over details did not help him hit the four-week target.

## Split a big task into small ones and sequence them

- The task initially "seemed too large to grasp". His brother Nikolai, a mathematician and algorithmic programmer then working on his thesis at the Max Planck University in Bonn, advised: "Write the code for user authorization first. You'll get through."
- Pavel started with a login page that generated session IDs. Sessions identified users, showed their profile pages and let them edit them.
- He deferred sign-up entirely. He prepopulated the database entries for the first few users manually.
- His lesson: "Every complex task is just a combination of many simple ones." If you split a big project into manageable parts and arrange them in the right order, you can get anything done.
- He adds that in practice you also hit technical obstacles that test your persistence.

## Extreme focus during the build

- In September 2006 he typically coded 20 hours in a row, ate one meal, then slept 10 hours.
- His food was a bucket of boiled pasta with a generous amount of cheese. He says no other food was required.
- He did not care whether it was day or night, and social connections "stopped existing". All that mattered was the code.

## Launch, early growth and a seeded audience

- The beta went live on October 10, 2006.
- Users invited from his earlier project, a students' portal he had been building since 2003, signed up by the thousands and started inviting friends.
- He kept adding new features quickly, and competitors struggled to catch up.
- A few months after launch he hired a second developer. By then VK already had a million members.
- Within seven years VK reached 100 million monthly users. At that point the VK board fired him, and he left to focus fully on Telegram.

## Why being a one-person product team shaped his career

- As sole member of the product team he did the work of front-end developer, back-end developer, UX/UI designer, system administrator and product manager, all at once.
- He learned the basics of each of those jobs and "the tiniest details of how a social network works".
- He says this experience was so valuable that it defined his career.
- His closing principle: "there are no complex tasks in this world, only many small ones that look scary when combined". Split a big task into smaller parts, organize them in the right sequence, and "you'll get through".
