## Dustin's framing of the Phia accusation

- Dustin describes the allegation as a shopping app that "opens a hidden tab on your phone, forces a click, and bills the merchant for a sale that it didn't earn."
- The alleged scheme ran quietly for seven months, right before the holidays.
- He says that if the accusation holds up, "this is not your usual affiliate fraud story."
- He treats it as a major event for the affiliate space and calls it a scandal for the whole affiliate marketing community.

## Manufactured clicks vs. stolen credit (Phia vs. Honey)

- Dustin separates two kinds of affiliate misconduct.
- **Honey-style credit theft:** software allegedly took credit for another publisher's click, so a legitimate publisher loses a commission they earned.
- **Phia-style manufactured commissions:** the app allegedly created clicks that never happened, so the sale was never driven by any real referral.
- In the Honey model the victim is the publisher who earned the click. In the Phia model the merchant ends up as the victim, because they pay a commission on a sale the app didn't earn.
- This is why he frames Phia as a different and arguably worse category than Honey.

## How to spot a forced-click scheme

- One of the tells Dustin flags for forced-click schemes is an implausibly high click-through rate.
- The other is an implausibly high conversion rate.
- Traffic that clicks and converts at rates far above what real shopper behavior would produce is a signal that the clicks are being generated, not earned.
- He plans to cover these tells in detail in the live session and ties them to what Ben Edelman's hands-on testing found.

## Where networks draw the line

Dustin lists how the major networks handle this kind of behavior as a topic for the panel:

- **Awin:** the "soft click" approach.
- **Impact:** a per-program paid PQ (partner quality) check.
- **CJ:** an NQ team (network quality) and a software ban in subaffiliate programs.

He positions the comparison as a way for merchants and publishers to understand what protection each network actually offers.
