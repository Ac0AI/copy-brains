## What B2B affiliate programs look like and how they differ from B2C

Dustin's guest, Adam Glazer, runs Partner Commerce, an agency that works only with B2B advertisers.

- Dustin says B2B is an underserviced corner of affiliate marketing.
- He sends B2B leads to Adam's agency and lists it among his preferred agencies.
- B2B is not coupons, discounts, cash back or toolbars.
- B2C programs can be "robust" with 30-40 affiliates and a few big names, run by junior staff.
- B2B is a "much longer tail" of partner types, so it is more partner development and less classic affiliate marketing.
- Low-value coupon and cash-back partners are avoided in favor of high-value, incremental, net-new customers.
- Dustin notes that nobody searches coupon sites for which business to buy from, so the B2C coupon and loyalty game does not carry over.

## The onboarding game plan: discovery, go-to-market plan, implementation

The first deliverable is a deep discovery phase that feeds a go-to-market plan.

**Discovery covers:**
- Who the customer is and what their buying journey looks like.
- The unit economics of the business.
- Any history with affiliate or partner marketing and what was learned.
- Other marketing channels, plus the KPIs and benchmarks that matter.
- Appetite for testing different payment models, including pay-for-play.

**The plan then defines:**
- A commission structure.
- Which partner personas to prioritize.
- What each partner type needs (discounting, co-branding) and the SLAs and requirements on both sides to deliver it.

**Timeline:**
- Planning takes 2-3 weeks.
- A technical spec and implementation take another 2-3 weeks.
- The program is live in market by about week 6, with a focus plan for the first 90 days.
- After launch the agency handles recruiting, onboarding, campaign activation, performance monitoring, platform operations, brand safety, affiliate support and application review.

## Aligning on commission structure and testing budget up front

Dustin endorses locking down payment models before launch so deals don't bottleneck later.

- The default is a rev share, for example in year one.
- Some partners will require CPL or flat pay-for-placement, so agree in advance whether the brand will test those.
- Agree on when to test and how much budget to allocate, for example "three of these types of deals this quarter".
- Evaluate each deal through a calculator and proceed only at roughly 80% confidence that it backs into allowables.
- Early alignment lets the client communicate to leadership, finance, the web team and dev resources.
- Capabilities that can't ship at launch (such as a lead-referral form) go onto a strategic roadmap for Q2 or Q3.

## Platform choice

- The agency is platform-agnostic but works mostly on impact.com, PartnerStack and Everflow, which it considers best-in-class for B2B.
- Processes, certifications and economies of scale are built around those three.
- Newer platforms carry unknowns about whether they can do what the leaders can.
- If a client has no preference, the agency audits and helps them evaluate.
- Dustin likes all three platforms and the people who work at them.

## B2B partner personas to recruit

Adam calls the approach "modern partner marketing", centered on incrementality and quality. Segments include:

- **Influencers:** primarily YouTube and LinkedIn.
  - Macro influencers with millions of followers are an awareness play handled at brand level.
  - The affiliate focus is micro influencers, say 50,000 followers in a very specific niche; there are thousands of them, so it is a scale motion.
- **Agencies:** advisors and intermediaries who steer small-business decisions. Adam cites a Forrester figure of about 76%, nearly two-thirds, of small-business spend going through an intermediary.
- **Content sites:**
  - Endemic B2B publishers such as Forbes, Fit Small Business and small-business advice portals.
  - Niche blogs and trade publications aimed at a vertical (medical practices, restaurants) or at a solution (a site about expense management suits fintech clients).
- **Member groups:** banks, co-working communities, associations, chambers of commerce and gated networks, where members get perks and deals.
- **Creators:** course and template builders on Coursera, Udemy, Udacity or LinkedIn Learning, or in Notion or ClickUp.
  - Example: search Udemy for courses on scaling an HVAC business that recommend moving from spreadsheets to a CRM.
  - A creator might reach only 900 people a year, but those buyers paid $200-500, trust the creator and onboard with templates and a playbook, so they stick and succeed.

## Card-linked offers and influencer attribution

**Card-linked offers (CLO):**
- Adam is enthusiastic about CLO and says it can reach "phenomenal" volume.
- Blocker one: clients want to incentivize net-new customers only, but many card offers can't exclude existing customers.
- Blocker two: large brands (Google, TikTok) see a perceived need to share customer data, which means legal and infosec review; smaller SaaS brands can move faster.

**Influencer attribution:**
- Influencers sit high in the funnel and often don't drive a direct click.
- A viewer may watch a YouTube video, then search Google or click a retargeting ad, so other channels win last-click credit.
- The fix is educating clients on how to evaluate these campaigns properly.

## Tools and operations

- HubSpot serves as the agency's CRM, "the brain of our organization".
  - It tracks every partner and all correspondence, plus deal pipeline waterfalls.
  - Example list pull: influencers in Brazil in the HVAC vertical.
  - A team of about 30 can check whether a colleague is already talking to a partner before outreach.
- Notion handles project management, process documentation, a repository of AI hacks, task tracking and a client-facing portal.
- The agency also uses outbound email warm-up and campaign tools.
- Custom AI tools are used to query client program data and assist with recruiting.
