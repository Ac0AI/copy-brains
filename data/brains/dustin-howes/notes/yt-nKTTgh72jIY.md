## Prediction: small, AI-run affiliate programs will beat big teams

- Dustin's bold prediction (also voiced by Matt Fra, whom he echoes): "the next great affiliate program is not going to be built by the biggest team." It will be built by the smallest, most efficient team of AI experts within 18 months.
- A one-person program running AI will outrecruit a 10-person team running spreadsheets. "Headcount stops being the moat."
- He expects the days of huge affiliate teams to end: "small, nimble, and extremely profitable."
- AI is not a traffic channel like social, mobile, influencer or toolbars. It is a layer underneath everything: how partners get found, how work gets done, how buyers decide.
- He frames the industry as a "partnership operating system": affiliate is a pricing model that partners with every channel, not a channel itself.
- Winners "automate the work and elevate the judgment"; losers "automate the judgment and keep doing the work."

## Where affiliate value is moving: LLM visibility and flat fees

- Publishers are losing click traffic to AI overviews and LLMs. A cited test showed hiding AI overviews raised organic clicks by almost 40% and cut zero-click searches by 35%.
- Publishers are already pivoting from chasing traffic to communicating the value they bring to brands, and pushing for flat-fee deals instead of CPC or CPA, because LLMs feed off their content.
- Dustin sees new payment models emerging. He cited a conversation with Mero about paying affiliates on LLM searches where the brand shows up, which he expects "is going to blow up."
- The panel's view of the mechanism: LLMs personalize results per user and source from trusted sites, authors and creators, so affiliates with trusted authors and creators are positioned to win information retrieval.
- Most LLM answers produce no clicks, so measurement lags. Visibility tools exist, and users often jump from an LLM to Google or Bing, which makes the influence partly visible.
- Local businesses win in LLMs the same way they won in SEO: real reviews (Yelp, Google), testimonials, content and inbound links. "Go be a good business and the LLM will pick you up."

## Attribution, incrementality and fraud

- The real long-term threat is last-click thinking. Affiliate gets bucketed with paid search and paid social, yet partnerships that add the most incremental value rarely get last-click credit.
- Brands refusing to pay for what they "can't measure" will kill the ecosystem. Example from an agency audit: a program that put branded paid search above affiliates and refused to pay partners on branded-search clicks.
- Attribution will likely improve as LLM platforms monetize and want to show sponsors results (source=chatgpt on outbound clicks, Shopify integrations). The open question is incentive: platforms grade their own homework.
- On fraud and toolbar scandals, he agrees with the panel: if the last click gets the money, people will game the last click. Programs must be actively managed with expert judgment and tools, not set and forgotten.

## Keep human judgment in the loop

- Volume is not the key; judgment is. Check whether a human reviewed the content, the outreach and the attribution rules.
- Self-driving analogy: AI is cruise control and cooled seats, but he still wants to "feel the road."
- Don't send AI-written outreach or content blindly. Panel consensus: audiences, especially younger ones, bounce from obviously AI-written pages and partners will be burned by sloppy AI replies.
- Vibe-coded site changes can open security holes (form-to-system leaks, injections). Have a developer or expert check them.

## What AI frees affiliate managers to do

- Affiliate managers spend an estimated 95% of their time pulling reports and logging into systems, leaving little for strategy and relationships. AI should reclaim that time for human work: strategy, client communication, partner relationships.
- His company's tool Alfie (XP Flow) automates affiliate recruitment and outreach: identifying niche content partners, sending over 1,000 outreach emails a month automatically, saving managers an average of 14 hours a week without adding headcount.
- Practical setups from the panel: a morning agent that briefs you from email, calendar and call notes and drafts follow-ups (never sent unreviewed); a Notion workspace connected to frontier models; sub-GPTs per partner that store their repeat questions; local-drive agents that run gap analysis.

## Monthly action items for program managers

- Do: connect AI to your own tools (email, calendar, call transcripts, workspace) so it handles reporting and admin.
- Do: set up background monitoring to flag affiliates whose traffic dropped, pages that were removed, whether your merchant ID is still in their source code, and what top performers have in common.
- Do: clear data use with legal and prefer the local desktop version over the web version for security.
- Stop: communicating blatantly with AI. Stop writing all content with AI only; people want to know who the real people are.
- Stop: treating affiliate as set-and-forget last-click.
