## Alfie's origin and the problem Dustin says it solves

- Dustin has 17 years in affiliate marketing, much of it "working out of spreadsheets, guessing at domains and firing emails into info@ boxes that nobody reads."
- He says this describes most affiliate managers' jobs, current or past.
- He presents Alfie (from XP Flow) as the tool that replaces that manual recruitment work, and says he has "never been so excited about a product."
- He frames the goal as freeing partnership managers to be more human: relationships and strategy instead of reporting and spreadsheet labor.
- He stresses the tool "is an enhancer, not a replacement" for affiliate managers and agencies, and that it is not meant to replace the networks (tracking and payments).

## Why recruitment stayed manual and what has changed

- Dustin's co-presenters' framing, which he endorses by hosting and agreeing, is that affiliate is relationship driven rather than "coin operated" like paid search or programmatic.
- AI now handles the messy parts: sifting millions of affiliates, structuring unstructured data, finding contacts, and drafting personalized outreach.
- Old workflow he describes: three screens open at once, with separate tools for finding contacts, checking traffic, finding similar sites, reading the site for placement ideas, and writing the email.
- Alfie puts all of this in one interface.
- Data brokers who used to sell spreadsheets of finance affiliates at conferences are made obsolete by on-demand matching.

## Setting up a recruitment campaign from just a URL

Live demo steps, run on a blank install using Robinhood as the brand:

1. Add an advertiser by entering only the brand URL. Alfie analyzes the site and generates a recruitment prompt, keywords (for example "best apps for beginners"), categories and verticals. This replaces hours of bullpen brainstorming.
2. Optionally add targeting to the prompt: US audience, gender skew, age.
3. Answer onboarding questions: affiliate network (the tool is network agnostic), signup link, commission (demo used 20%), cookie duration (90 days), extras like seasonal bonuses and creative refresh cadence, and an email signature. These become merge fields in outreach templates.
4. Launch the campaign. It uses a prompt, up to 10 keywords (add your own), IAB categories (700 available), affiliate types (content sites, review and comparison sites, influencers, and so on, toggled on or off), geo, age range, audience size and gender.

- Dustin says multiple campaigns can target different affiliate types or traffic, so different promos can go to different groups.

## How discovery, verification and fit score work

- Results come from a verified data lake of roughly 75,000-80,000 affiliates, plus live web curation that finds long-tail content sites not visible in the networks.
- Dustin points out that network marketplace data is only as good as what affiliates entered themselves and goes stale when owners leave. Alfie's data is verified contact and site data.
- Every result includes an affiliate drawer with a summary, why it was selected, best contact (multiple contacts can be chosen), traffic and authority metrics (otherwise pulled manually from tools like SimilarWeb or SEMrush), geo reach (for example 96% US), and audience age, gender and income signals.
- A verified check mark means Alfie detected affiliate links and disclaimers. Unverified content sites can still be good fits but require a different conversation (teaching them about affiliate programs).
- Fit score is out of 100 (the demo showed a 90, "exceptional match"). It reflects data completeness, keyword and category match, and geo and demographic criteria.
- Dustin: after 10 years with tools in this space (he names FiveIQ and MediaRails), "this is the most sophisticated search functionality that I've ever seen," and the fit score is "not something you can easily replicate" with Claude or ChatGPT.
- Rejecting an affiliate with a reason trains Alfie, and rejected affiliates can be restored later.

## Outreach sequences and the affiliate workflow

- The pipeline stages are Outreach, Engage, Deploy and Convert.
- Approved affiliates go into an autoresponder-style sequence with prefilled templates and merge tags (bonuses, benefits).
- Cadence in the demo: first email, follow-up 3 days later, third email 6 days later. The sequence stops automatically on reply.
- Emails send from an Alfie mail domain by default (name plus a number tied to the campaign and affiliate). Custom DNS can make mail come from your own domain.
- Engage: reply from an inbox, or have Alfie draft replies that you can edit or ask to make more casual.
- Deploy: send links, creatives and IO, set postbacks. Convert is currently a status field, with optimization suggestions planned.
- Once running, the work happens in the background so managers can focus on strategy.

## Current limits and roadmap

- Currently strongest on content affiliates. Social and influencer affiliates are on the roadmap.
- No export, API or CSV import yet (an MCP is in the works and CSV import is planned).
- It does not yet optimize publisher revenue or score on activation. Network integrations (for activation data and automatic deploy stages) are coming.
- Organizations and teams for agencies are "days away," and AI-personalized sequences are in design.
- It works for B2B and SaaS too, with Alfie's own program as the example.
