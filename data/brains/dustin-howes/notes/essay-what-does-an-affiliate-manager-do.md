## What an affiliate manager is and why the role matters

- Dustin defines an affiliate manager as the person who manages an affiliate program on behalf of a merchant.
- The role covers the people, tools and systems responsible for bringing in revenue from the program.
- Affiliate marketing is a performance marketing strategy: you partner with independent marketers who promote your product in exchange for compensation.
- He cites a projection that US affiliate marketing spend will reach $15 billion by 2028.
- His bottom line: "Affiliate programs will not magically grow on their own." A manager optimizes the program, drives relevant traffic, builds relationships with the network and creates effective recruiting and marketing campaigns.

## The six core responsibilities of an affiliate manager

1. **Recruiting new affiliates.** Build target lists, find contact information, craft an outreach email sequence, accept applications and follow up with high-potential affiliates.
2. **Activating affiliates.** Having hundreds of affiliates on the roster is not enough; the job is keeping accounts active by giving new and stagnant affiliates information, incentives and tools.
3. **Building relationships.** Includes support, payouts and friendship (see the relationship section below).
4. **Monitoring compliance.** Protects the brand, saves money on payouts and keeps affiliates within government regulations and program rules.
5. **Optimizing the program.** Analyze performance data and find the most effective channels.
6. **Directing affiliate creative.** Help affiliates produce effective content for their sites, since content decides whether they succeed at promoting the product.

## Recruiting and activating affiliates

- Recruitment takes up a huge chunk of the manager's time because program success depends on growing the network.
- Dustin says every manager should "fill their pipeline by recruiting new partners every week."
- Recruitment tasks: target lists of potential partners, hunting contact info, outreach email sequences, accepting applications, following up high-potential affiliates.
- Activation means motivating both new and stagnant affiliates to promote.
- A welcome email sequence is a "clutch time-saver" that helps affiliates "hit the ground running."
- Grovia is his go-to solution for recruitment.

## Relationships: the four hats an affiliate manager wears

- **Human resources:** managing affiliates, collecting payment info, ensuring accurate, on-time payouts.
- **Customer support:** helping with coupon codes, dashboards, links and troubleshooting.
- **Brand liaison:** educating affiliates on brand guidelines and positioning.
- **Friend:** building long-term relationships and professional networks.
- When you help partners succeed, you become vested in their journey and they in yours. Dustin says he has made hundreds of friendships this way and stays in touch after his time managing a program ends: "This type of networking is priceless."
- A good manager must be a people person who can empathize with both the affiliates and the brand they represent.

## Compliance and optimization

- Compliance protects brand image, saves payout money, keeps affiliates within regulations and program rules, and creates a safe environment for advertisers and publishers. He calls it integral to program longevity and success.
- Optimization means monitoring performance, analyzing data and discovering the best marketing channels.
- Four goals of optimization:
  1. Increase the efficiency of the affiliate manager
  2. Improve the quality of traffic affiliates send
  3. Increase conversion rates of landing pages and offers
  4. Drive more sales and revenue
- Hitting and exceeding revenue goals makes executives more likely to invest additional budget in exploratory campaigns.

## Three ways to staff an affiliate program

**1. Hire in-house.**
- Best for companies that want to really invest in program growth.
- Pros: dedicated to your brand, reacts quickly to promo strategy shifts, easy collaboration with the marketing team, 40+ hours a week on program growth, can help affiliates with product questions.
- Costs: salary of $40,000 to $80,000, equipment, office space if onsite, benefits, and training to keep up with industry trends.
- Works best when you already have a growing network generating revenue that can sustain the hire.

**2. Hire an agency.**
- Best for brands that have already had an in-house manager and want to scale.
- Pros: faster initial growth, expertise in recognizing big players in your niche, access to advanced tools, an extensive affiliate network, cheaper than a full-time manager, and frees your time for the core business.
- Cons: agencies charge $2,000 to $10,000 per month and may require a minimum six-month retainer; they are not fully integrated into your business, so collaboration and execution can be slower.
- Dustin partners with Grovia and praises its affiliate recruitment work.

**3. Train a junior marketer, intern or VA.**
- Aligns with your culture, values and mission, and the person learns your product well enough to craft tailored campaigns.
- He calls it one of the most cost-effective options and "an investment you'll benefit from for years."
- His Performance Marketing Manager course offers eight hours of conference-style video training with over 50 tips, tricks and hacks, covering time management, recruiting the right affiliates and building profitable partner relationships.

## Skills to look for, pay, and how to find good managers

**Skills:**
- Strategic thinking
- Detail orientation, especially with data, analytics and income reports
- Organization and multitasking
- People skills (trust, relationships, motivation)
- Basic HTML/CSS for emails that display properly on all devices
- Graphic design for banners, buttons and promotions
- Newsletter formatting, as newsletters are central to campaigns and affiliate communication
- Content creation and monitoring, including SEO-optimized content

**Pay:**
- He cites Payscale's average base salary of $54,046, varying by experience, location and industry.
- He recommends adding an incentive bonus or commission structure.

**Finding good managers:**
- Search LinkedIn for "affiliate manager"; good ones have a strong network and profile.
- Look for diversified experience across agencies, merchants and publishers.
- Years of experience don't always translate into greatness, so don't pass on junior managers: "You will want creative thinkers to run your program."

**Becoming a good one:** get proper training from a teacher or mentor, since there are many facets to master.
