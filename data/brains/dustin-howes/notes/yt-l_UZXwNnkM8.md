## Evaluating an inherited affiliate program

- Dustin's guest (Erica) starts with a look "under the hood": program size, partner quality, where partners came from, and how new partners get in.
- She defines health as "sales active", not email-responsive. Affiliates are busy, so the test is whether they consistently publish content and drive sales.
- A big roster of inactive partners is a weak program; she saw that at Get Response as the opportunity.
- On arrival she sent a few intro emails to build legitimacy, promised no changes that would hurt existing partners' commissions, and applied any changes to new partners only.

## Choosing and migrating from an in-house program to a network

- She made an external platform a condition of taking the job, rather than inheriting an in-house tracking setup.
- She chose PartnerStack over Impact for B2B because of its network (about 18,000 affiliates and partners) and because "for B2B, I find that everyone is on PartnerStack or at least most of the best ones." Impact has more reach for B2C.
- Migration was for new partners only; legacy partners could opt in to the new commission structure but weren't forced.
- Main work was internal education for leadership and developer work on the integration, with her as liaison to the platform. Rollout took about eight weeks.
- Lesson: she wishes she had asked in the interview for budget for non-traditional or experimental deals (sponsorships, hybrids).

## Recruiting inside a network marketplace

- She uses the network's weekly email allotment and maxes it out every week.
- She filters by region (for example US, Germany, Poland), and uses the platform's AI recommendations of relevant partners.
- Result: roughly a 15% join rate from invites sent, which she attributes to network members being quality people who understand the space.
- Tooling: PartnerStack discovery/recruitment heavily; Conneto to find publishers writing about the brand and its competitors (a prompt to reach out and say "we're here too, and here's why we're different").

## Recruiting outside the network

- LinkedIn is the main channel for off-network outreach; many publishers aren't on it, but influencers increasingly are.
- Passion Fruit is used to connect with influencers, though influencers are a small part of the program.
- About 90% of recruiting comes from the PartnerStack network.
- She tailors recruiting to company campaigns: for a "simplify your tech stack" promotion, she targets writers on that topic and consultants who simplify tech stacks.
- Campaign activation includes an ebook, landing pages for partners, and PPC alongside the affiliate push.

## Outreach message and commission terms

- Principle: "be the easiest person for people to work with." Make the email clear so partners don't have to decipher it.
- She states the commission openly (40% for 12 months, going up from there) instead of gatekeeping, with a join-now button and links to content resources.
- She disagrees with "take it or leave it" programs: that doesn't work in 2025, and managers need flexibility.

## Negotiating CPA, hybrid and sponsorship deals

- Every negotiation is backed by data. She sets a target CPA (example: $250), takes the partner's traffic and conversion numbers plus her own conversion rate, and sees whether she can back into the target.
- If the math lands near $1,000 instead, she declines.
- She has done a few CPA/CPL deals and works with leadership on guardrails so she can say yes quickly.
- Trend: influencers who asked "$5,000 for a video, no questions asked" a few years ago are now open to hybrid structures and video packages.

## Landing pages, coupons and trends

- Landing pages work when they echo the content that sent the visitor ("you read about us on so-and-so's blog, here are the main points, start a free trial"). She did more of this at previous roles; at Get Response bandwidth limits it, as a one-person team.
- Coupons stay in the program for brand reasons, but they drive few conversions and her biggest affiliates aren't coupon sites. She prefers individual promo codes (for example 15% to help close one affiliate's audience).
- She is a believer in Cart-level/CLO-style loyalty offers when done right, citing a B2B client success at her agency (via Cartlytics).
- 2025 trend: everyone is an "AI expert" with an AI blog or newsletter, so she is deciding whether to be listed everywhere or be selective. No standout AI-guru partner yet.
