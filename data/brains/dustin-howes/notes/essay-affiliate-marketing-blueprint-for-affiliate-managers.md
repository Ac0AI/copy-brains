## Treating the audit as the foundation of an affiliate program

Dustin starts every new affiliate program with a thorough audit. It positions the affiliate manager as the "expert" in the client's eyes and gets the manager familiar with the program. He documents notes on each area in the final audit deliverable.

- **Sales funnel audit:**
  - Examine product detail pages, landing pages and presells for optimization opportunities.
  - Check bounce rate and time on page for sales pages in Google Analytics.
  - Read the reviews on product pages.
  - Use tools like Mouseflow for user experience and behavior analytics.
  - Walk the checkout flow to spot friction points.
- **Product audit:**
  - Identify best sellers or hero products.
  - Learn product margins, especially which products have the highest margins.
  - Find out which products are seasonal.
  - Find out which products have inventory or return rate issues.
- **Partnership audit:**
  - Check which publisher types (content, loyalty, technology, etc.) are missing from the program.
  - Check the state of coupons and trademark bidding.
  - Review creative assets.
  - Visit top referring pages and look for non-affiliate links or broken links.
  - Run a short gap analysis against other clients.
  - Review the current offer strategy (dynamic commissions, term templates).

## Defining partner personas and scrubbing unproductive partners

Dustin builds partner personas: detailed evaluations of the best partner fit for a brand. For each persona he works out what type of partner they are, their job title, how to find them and their motivation to partner. Once the ideal persona is identified, he builds a list of who they are.

He calls total affiliate count a vanity metric and argues managers should focus on partner productivity instead of recruiting as many affiliates as possible.

- **Productive partners:** they drive traffic, or traffic and sales. Keep them and maximize their output. A few usually produce the lion's share of traffic and revenue.
- **Dormant partners:** they produce no traffic or revenue. Some have potential to become productive and some do not.
- **Action:** the manager decides which dormant partners have no potential and removes them from the program.

## Using abandonment and retargeting partners to bridge the waiting period

New partners can take weeks or months to produce valuable traffic and sales, which frustrates clients who are investing but not seeing returns. Dustin onboards abandonment and retargeting partners during this period.

- **Abandonment partners:** they detect exit intent (hovering over the tab's "X", switching windows, hitting back) and deploy pop-ups offering a discount or free shipping, or capture the email.
- **Retargeting partners:** they show ad units to previous visitors, via paid social or publisher sites, to keep the brand top of mind.
- **Examples named:** Upsellit, Revlifter, Addshoppers, Retargeted AI, Klickly, NMPI and Cybba.
- **Why:**
  - They generate more revenue attributable to the affiliate channel.
  - They produce results quickly and need little upkeep.
  - They bring incremental value while content, deal, mobile app and influencer partners ramp up.
  - They scale proportionately as traffic grows.

## On-site partners and conversion optimization

Dustin calls conversion rate optimization the affiliate program's best friend. Affiliates who see a high-quality, converting site send more traffic. On-site technology partners run in the background and need little effort after implementation.

- **Raise AOV:** partners like Revlifter and Increasingly offer discounts at spend thresholds, bundle products and suggest recommended products. He sees them as helpful in fashion, cosmetics, technology and tools.
- **Chat and assisted shopping:** companies like Envolve Tech add virtual shopping assistants that answer questions and guide shoppers to products through question-led funnels.
- **Trademark plus (TM+) partners:** they bid on "Brand Name" + a term, e.g. "Nike Shoes".
  - The affiliate world debates their value, and some brands ban TM+ bidding.
  - If the client permits, partners like Savings.com and Bravo Deal can save brands CPC budget on branded searches.
  - They are scalable because they work through Google, Bing or other ad networks.
  - Attributable revenue rises whenever the brand's search volume rises.
- **Tracking technology:** he notes not all tracking attribution platforms are equal, and names Inflektion.ai as his favorite solution of 2024.

## Landing review articles and finding the right publishers

Review articles are the toughest placement to win. Dustin says managers should care less about vanity (placement count, publisher name recognition) and more about direct results.

- A premium publisher (Forbes, Business Insider, CNN) is a benchmark, but niche review sites with product reviews, round-ups and gift guides may be more valuable.
- Premium does not mean authoritative for a given industry or product.
- To find the right sites, research which sites rank highest for the client's relevant consumer searches, using Semrush, Similarweb, Ahrefs and Spyfu.
- What matters most is how much traffic a site drives to the client's program. "High trust and high relevant traffic will lead to high performance."
- Evaluate a publisher's messaging, appearance, credibility and professionalism. Bounce rate and time on page indicate how much consumers trust it.

## Paid search publishers and outsourced media buyers

Some publishers buy paid search traffic to their review pages. If a publisher's earnings per click from commissions exceed the ad cost per click, it makes sense for them to keep buying that traffic. For the brand, this accelerates traffic and revenue.

- **Examples named:** buyersguide.org, bestreview.guide and consumerrating.org. Managers should comb their reviews for client fit.
- **Premium publishers do it too:** The Spruce bids on "best vacuum cleaner" and around 100 other keywords. Semrush estimated about 8,800 monthly visits via sponsored listings, which he puts at around 400% more than the page gets organically (estimates not exact).
- **Outsourced media buyers:** they specialize in Meta, Google Paid Search, Google Shopping, native, TikTok and similar channels. They audit past campaigns, performance and average CPA, and usually work on commission (percentage of spend, revenue or cost per action).
- **Fronted ad spend / CPA model:** the partner invests its own ad money so long as the CPA it is paid stays profitable. Example: if the brand's Google Paid Search CPA is $40, the partner might charge $50 per customer, keeping $10 per referral for its investment and risk. NMPI and Imwave work this way, as do several partners on the Squaredance marketplace.
- **Non-traditional partnerships:** he defines affiliate as a model where a promoter gets paid for driving outcomes. Look beyond the affiliate network for traffic sources: app stores, advertising marketplaces like Nift, DTC blogs, podcasts and innovative brands in the industry.

## Social and market listening, reporting, and ongoing learning

Dustin has managers set up listening alerts to spot recruitment and placement opportunities.

- **Search terms:** product, competitor and market terms, plus publication, influencer and industry voice names. For a vacuum client: "Dyson", "best vacuum cleaners".
- **Tools:** Google Alerts (free, web pages only, daily email); Awario, Brand24 and BuzzSumo for social platforms like X, Instagram, YouTube and TikTok.

On reporting, he wants less time number crunching and more time growing the program.

- Use reporting tools (Affluent, Google Data Studio, Tableau) plus network reporting.
- Affluent only works with affiliate network data, so use an extension such as Schedule and Send Email in Spreadsheets for outside data like profit analysis in Google Sheets.
- Customize reports to the metrics the client cares about: customer lifetime value, ROAS, CPC, CPA, earnings per click.
- Questions to ask the client: top three metrics; new customers vs. repeat purchases; target CPA; publisher types wanted; month-over-month growth target; seasonal periods; target share of total revenue from affiliate.

He also tells managers to keep learning through network newsletters, webinars, courses (Google, Datacamp, Udemy) and agency development teams, using as many resources as bandwidth allows.
