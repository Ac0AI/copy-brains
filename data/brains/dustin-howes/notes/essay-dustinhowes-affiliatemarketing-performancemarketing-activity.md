## Soft-pitch outreach to selective publishers (the Wirecutter example)

- Dustin describes Wirecutter as a premium publisher that is very selective about which brands and products it publishes content for, and notes it gets "hundreds of emails every single day."
- He backs the approach that landed Wirecutter as a top partner: a data-led, human, soft pitch instead of a hard pitch.
- Steps he describes:
  - Research first: check whether the publisher already has content in the brand's vertical or niche.
  - Acknowledge there are people on the other side who get blasted all day.
  - Lead with data, not just "here's the commission, can you join our program."
  - Include the brand, the product, what already ranks well for their site, how it converts, EPC for similar content, and where else it ranks.
- Research tools for the pitch: Ahrefs and SimilarWeb, plus a simple search on competitors' sites to see how many articles they have listed.
- His take: "It sounds like it's such a basic thing, but it actually worked." Having a brand the publisher was interested in also helped.

## Deal structures: independents, big publishers and whitelisting

- Deal flexibility depends on publisher size. Independent publishers are easier to work with and more flexible (hybrid, CPC only).
- Larger publishers are harder to get into and have flat-fee demands up front.
- Dustin points to a way in for the big mass-media publishers: whitelisting deals brokered by certain companies, which his team has launched for some clients in the last month or two.
- How he positions whitelisting to a publisher who is losing Google traffic:
  - Brands spend paid traffic against the publisher's content, so it's another traffic source: "you're losing Google on this side, but you're gaining meta on this side."
  - The content can be published at production cost or even free, with a set amount of spend directed at the page.
  - The publisher recoups commissions on that page long after it goes live.
  - Fee structures vary: commission payments only, a monthly handle/access fee, or another fee.
- For independents, he suggests being creative with the structure and thinking about keeping the relationship rolling long term so they earn passive commissions.

## Conversion rate and honesty as leverage

- Dustin says conversion rate matters more than anything in the vertical and niche context. Working with many telehealth brands, he sees listicle sites that normally have flat fees or CPC deals bypass those terms when the vertical is lucrative and the brand converts well.
- "The conversion rate is supreme" because publishers know they will make money on it long term.
- On proving a claimed conversion rate: you never provide proof. It rests on trust, because publishers are smart and know the numbers, and if you lie you burn the bridge for any future relationship.
- Rule: "Reputation is everything. You do business from a place of honesty."
- If the offer has a weak conversion rate, don't oversell it as "the best thing since sliced bread." If it converts well, go for the big publishers.

## AI visibility data: layer it and pitch with it

- Dustin's position (alongside what his team saw in their own data) is that a small set of domains, around 5 to 8, drives the vast majority of AI citations in a category. That data can be used to pitch those publishers.
- Deal progression he uses: start with a hybrid model to get in, then shift to performance as visibility and trending improve, while acknowledging there is always some sponsored element.
- Don't rely on a single AI visibility tool or dataset. Layer multi-touch attribution, AI visibility and SimilarWeb data, build a story, and constantly challenge the strategy. A single dataset can diverge sharply.
- Be skeptical of tools selling "prompts per dollar." Check whether prompts are neutral or have a persona behind them, because LLMs learn from user behavior and answer differently per audience (probabilistic models).

## Prioritizing publishers: Google rankings first, then AI citations

- Workflow he describes using Profound:
  - Build non-branded comparison prompts (and incremental prompts) for the brand.
  - Load them in to see which publisher URLs are cited most (top 10, 20, 30, 50).
  - Examples of frequently cited publishers in his space: Healthline (often #1 or #2) and Fortune.
- AI citation rank doesn't always match SEO rank: a page may be #1 on Google but #10 for AI.
- His prioritization: from a revenue perspective, go after the publishers ranking on Google first, then work on AI citation targets afterward, because AI attribution is harder to show in an affiliate program unless you give secondary reports.

## Dustin's own affiliate position on LLM visibility

- As an affiliate of productivity, SaaS and affiliate marketing tools, he is writing content intentionally to rank in LLMs.
- His current experience: he gets visibility, but LLMs consume his content and display it without sending clicks.
- He hopes affiliate marketing catches up on crediting AI-driven influence and says he needs to figure out how to earn credit in that channel, as many affiliates are trying to.
