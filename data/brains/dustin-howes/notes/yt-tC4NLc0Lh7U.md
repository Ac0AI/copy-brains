## The affiliate partnership funnel and where time goes

Dustin uses an eight-step partnership funnel, honed over the years:
- Discovery, outreach, express interest, call scheduled, negotiation, applied and aligned, content active, revenue active.
- The goal is to move every affiliate down this funnel.
- About 80% of an affiliate manager's time is absorbed in the top steps (finding contacts and affiliates).
- Junior managers work the top; senior managers work negotiation and relationship steps and must train the next generation for those calls.
- Dustin wants the top part automated so more time goes to relationships. His line: don't fear AI; "AI isn't going to be replacing the human side of affiliate marketing. It's actually protecting it."
- He argues managers who adapt to AI fastest are the ones who survive layoffs in a tough job market.

## Strategy: ICP, partner personas and three target lists

- Start from the ICP and work backwards: where does the ICP hang out online?
- Build a partner persona sheet per partner type (job title, motivation, ideal content such as a "best of" listicle vs. full product review, subtypes like lifestyle or mommy bloggers).
- Build three lists rather than one undifferentiated blast:
  1. **Wish list:** 25 dream partners he never stops pursuing until he gets a hard no. Research them heavily and aim for touchpoints with about five a week.
  2. **Keyword list:** sites ranking for the keywords the brand wants to rank on, aligned with buyer intent.
  3. **Competitor traffic list:** sites ranking for competitors' keywords. They already have the traffic and can switch to promoting you with the right pitch.
- Prioritize non-branded terms ("best sleeping product") over branded terms. Still monitor branded terms so bad actors don't bid on trademarks and steal credit for sales you'd get anyway.
- Rebuild lists regularly (a VA can do it) because people change companies fast and a list goes stale within a year.

## Discovery channels and tools

- **Content sites:** drop in around 50 keywords from the SEO team and find who ranks.
- **YouTube:** descriptions give an easy click path; YouTubers are great affiliates.
- **LinkedIn and social:** a "hidden gem." Creators who ignore DMs on Instagram (even with 100,000 followers) respond on LinkedIn because they're trying to network and grow there.
- **Closed communities:** for Facebook groups, befriend an admin and ask what top-of-group placement costs for a month. It may be about $100 and is "totally worth it."
- Tools he named, with his stated prices:
  - Semrush for keywords (~$140/month)
  - Similarweb for site metrics (~$200/month)
  - Channel Crawler for YouTube (~$60/month, includes fake-follower checks)
  - Favicon for LinkedIn discovery and traffic value (~$160/month)
  - Hunter.io for contact enrichment (~$50/month)
- He estimates the old manual method takes at least 10 minutes per spreadsheet line for someone good, 20 for someone not.

## Outreach: spray-and-pray vs. personalized

- **Spray and pray** is for low-priority, long-tail affiliates: a five-email sequence over about two weeks. Hitting a 20% response rate means "you're absolutely winning." Sending about 50 emails a day gets results.
  - Email 1: short and punchy, ends with a question. Thursday is a good start day.
  - Email 2: quick follow-up.
  - Email 3: longer, with program context and how you'd work together.
  - Email 4: almost always uses a gift or joke. He uses the "Hello" gift from Lionel Richie and says it gets more responses than his other emails because it makes people laugh.
  - Email 5: the hard ask: "give me a yes or a no... tell me to kick rocks."
  - Suggested spacing: 3, 5, 7, then 6 days between sends; cadence is flexible.
- **Personalized** is for the high-value 25: reference specific content and hand-craft both the first email and follow-ups.
- Build a separate template per persona (YouTubers, bloggers and media buyers shouldn't get the same pitch). Test one variable at a time (subject, opener, ask), measure replies, keep winners and retire the rest.
- Use Claude to draft templates and generate many variants. Use Instantly (~$50/month) for sequencing.

## Bargaining chips that get attention

- As an affiliate himself (dustin.com), he gets about a dozen partnership emails a day. Emails saying "this is our affiliate program, come join" get deleted; ones saying "we're open to running a relationship how you want" stand out.
- Chips to offer:
  - Sponsorship money (gets attention)
  - Backlinks (gold for bloggers)
  - Free product
  - Co-branded landing pages. At Inflection he built a page showing the prospect's own branding for high-value partners and got incredible responses.
  - Audience exchanges or collaborations
  - Deeper discounts
- The goal is to offer something immediately rather than just point to the program.

## LinkedIn networking and fast response handling

- He keeps LinkedIn Sales Navigator (~$120/month, subscribed about 10 years) and uses Sales Connector to automate outreach; he connects with at least 100 people a week.
- Combining LinkedIn and email lifts response rates. He gave a figure of 43% but admitted it was made up from experience, so treat it as anecdotal.
- When someone responds:
  - Reply fast ("speed equals professionalism") and match their tone and length.
  - Thank them, get to the point, answer questions, and immediately drop a Calendly link to kill scheduling back-and-forth.
- He uses SaneBox (~$10/month) for inbox organization and HubSpot or HighLevel as CRM/PRM.

## Discovery calls, negotiation and nurture

- **Before the call:** research the person in Sales Navigator and LinkedIn, run their site through Claude for a brief and goals, and find something personal to connect on.
- **On the call:** help first, since "no one really cares about what you want." Spend about five minutes on small talk, ask questions, share creative ideas, close with action items. Record with Granola (or Fireflies) and push notes to the CRM, with Claude drafting follow-ups.
- **Negotiation** should be win-win. Pure performance-only deals put all the risk on the affiliate and "you're starting off on the wrong foot."
  - Ask for their media kit; it's a door opener and signals a sale.
  - Hybrid is almost always right: a flat fee for their time plus commission.
  - A small paid placement (e.g. $100 newsletter slot) gets your foot in the door for commission work later.
  - Everything is negotiable; verify claims with screenshots, Google Analytics or conversion stats.
  - Off-peak rates are cheaper, notably Q1 and Q3.
- **Nurture after approval:** a five-day drip: welcome and product info; "my door is always open"; all assets in one place; "anything else I can do for you?" Evergreen content is what drives long-term revenue, and it won't happen overnight. Do favors, check in more often with more important partners, and document content wins to report to executives.
