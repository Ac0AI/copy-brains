## Choosing and booking a conference stay

Dustin advises against staying at the conference hotel because of the cost. For CJU 2026 in Santa Barbara he booked a small hotel near the venue instead:

- Small hotels around the venue are "just great enough to put your head at night" for roughly $100 to $200 a night.
- He booked two days before the event and was still able to get a place a block from the beach.
- The venue was about a mile's walk away, which he treated as a bonus for getting exercise in.
- Check-in was quick, with no line.

## Using live interviews as a networking tool

Dustin took his Affiliate Nerd Out show on the road to CJU 2026 and filmed live interviews throughout the three days.

- He approached people at the event, on the beach, and at the party, and asked the same short question: what do you think of the vibes here?
- He interviewed network staff (Jon Thollander of CJ), agency and partner-side contacts (Matthew Wood of Hello Partner, Jake Fuller of JEBCommerce, Richard Jackson of Pion, Dan Sweeney of Coleman Digital Media, Matt Frary of XPFlow) and publishers (Allison Cirincione, Shawn Cowher, Marie Flores of Goloot, Beth Baratte of ShopHer Media).
- He published the footage as a multi-part series: Part 1 of the walkthrough, and Part 2 with his final score for the conference.
- He publicly thanked everyone who appeared, and framed the series as an honest look at "the good and the not-so-good."

## Why affiliate events are worth attending

His takeaways on the value of CJU's format:

- It is networking-led. Attendees are relaxed and "you don't feel pressured to go meet everybody"; you can walk up, grab a coffee, and meet someone.
- The people you want to meet are mostly in one place: agencies, publishers, and network staff.
- Compact logistics help. One end of the conference to the other was under a 3-minute walk.
- Social events do real work. He called the beach party probably the best party at any conference he has attended, because people network with their feet in the sand.
- On day one he took roughly 10 scheduled meetings plus about 10 more informal conversations on the side.

## What he sees on the CJ side of the market

From his conversation with Jon Thollander of CJ (a 20-year industry veteran), Dustin relayed the themes dominating the event:

- Marketers are worried about AI visibility and zero-click journeys.
- CJ is leaning into "connected commerce": an integrated approach that looks at the full funnel, how affiliate affects other channels, and how other channels affect affiliate.
- CJ's pitch to brands is that it sits at the intersection of these shifts, backed by Publicis and Epsilon data management and large funding.

## Critique of CJU 2026: where the event fell short

Dustin gave a candid assessment of the weaker points:

- **Event app:** the biggest hit to the conference. It was clunky, not user friendly, and made scheduling meetings difficult. Attendees were frustrated and could not use it effectively.
- **Meeting pace:** too aggressive, with no time between meetings and no guidance on where to go next.
- **Content:** sessions were "pretty decent," but nearly all speakers came from CJ or a CJ sponsor. He wanted more outside voices.
- **Education focus:** the content centered on things within CJ rather than helping program managers get better at their jobs.

## How he runs the day

- He works the schedule hard on day one, then goes to the evening party to keep mingling.
- He skips late-night events like karaoke to get a fresh start the next day, even though he hears they are a strong networking opportunity.
- He thanks sponsors and hosts of the evening events (such as Slick Deals) publicly at the end of the night.
