## The 15-step affiliate partnership lifecycle

Dustin frames affiliate management as a long, hands-on process in which a manager has to "hold the hand" of a high-potential affiliate. He walks through 15 steps, grouped into four phases, and says knowing how much time to spend on each step is crucial.

- **Phase 1 (steps 1-4): getting the affiliate into the program.**
- **Phase 2 (steps 5-9): research, content and monetization.**
- **Phase 3 (steps 10-12): ranking, clicks and sales.**
- **Phase 4 (steps 13-15): connection, negotiation and a new campaign.**

He stresses that none of this happens instantly and that these steps do not complete on their own without the manager's involvement.

## Phase 1: recruiting the affiliate and getting them to join

- **Step 1, find or be found.** Dustin calls this "a dance." The manager has to get out and recruit so affiliates can find the program. The affiliate may already be searching a niche or running a site about it, and either they find you or you find them.
- **Step 2, review.** The affiliate has to review the program and agree to its terms. The manager can entice them with bigger commissions, landing pages or free products. The affiliate checks that the product and program are a good fit.
- **Step 3, join.** The manager either sends a link or talks the affiliate into signing up on whatever network or platform the program runs on. The affiliate accepts the terms agreed in earlier discussions.
- **Step 4, a separate stage.** The transcript does not name this step clearly, but he says it is "not an easy task" and "these kinds of things don't happen instantaneously."

## Phase 2: how a good affiliate researches and builds content

Dustin describes what a good affiliate does after joining, so a manager knows what is happening behind the scenes.

- **Step 5, keyword research.** The affiliate researches what is currently ranking on Google, YouTube or wherever they publish.
- **Step 6, content research.** They review all the content about the target keywords. They find a winner to go after, see which content actually works, then replicate it and make it better. He says a lot of research goes into this step for good affiliates.
- **Step 7, create the content.** They decide the format and length, such as a 500-word or 10,000-word blog post, or a one-minute video versus a 15-minute tutorial. Everyone's process differs. The goal is content that produces clicks and sales.
- **Step 8, optimize.** They work out how to rank at the top of Google. He says this takes time and strategy: "can't just slop this together."
- **Step 9, monetize.** They place affiliate links in the content. They are probably promoting more than one brand at a time, so monetizing each piece matters and takes time.

## Phase 3: ranking, clicks and sales

- **Step 10, rank.** The content has to rank on Google or other platforms before clicks come through. Good affiliates know how to rank faster and have domain authority greater than most content already out there. Some buy their placement at the top of Google.
- **Step 11, clicks.** Clicks must arrive in large volume.
- **Step 12, sales.** Clicks have to convert. Depending on the medium, conversion rate could be 2% or 15%.

## Phase 4: connecting, negotiating and repeating

- **Step 13, connect with the affiliate manager.** Managers should use reporting to watch their up-and-comers and reach out to the ones starting to bring in sales. The goal is to replicate what is working, so he suggests getting on a call.
- **Step 14, negotiate.** Topics include landing pages and commission rate, anything that gives the affiliate more exposure to the program. He calls it a give and take: affiliates "ask for the moon," the manager gives a little, and both meet in the middle.
- **Step 15, create a new campaign.** Once the content-ranking-sales formula works, repeat it with the same affiliate because the relationship is established. For that affiliate, go back to phase 2 and repeat steps 5 through 9. For new affiliates, start from step 1.
