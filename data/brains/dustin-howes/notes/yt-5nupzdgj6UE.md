## Affiliate recruitment with AppyStash

- Dustin and Vic Giurgiu (his business partner on AppyStash, which Vic builds) say the most time-consuming affiliate manager tasks are recruitment, sending emails, spotting fake followers and onboarding new partners.
- The recruitment workflow in AppyStash starts from a brand URL, which the tool turns into a list of potential affiliates and influencers. The brand's keywords, target audience and description are extracted automatically.
- AppyStash supports 10 platforms, including social platforms, and the AI Partner Finder takes just a partner type and a platform. Dustin's view is that this removes the need for separate keyword analysis or expensive SEO tools.
- The Lookalike feature finds more affiliates similar to a good one. Dustin says it is the feature he uses most: "I'm in there almost every day. If I find a good affiliate and I want to replicate it, that's the thing I use."
- Dustin says he spends a little extra time giving thumbs up to the results he wants, to train the algorithm toward similar results in future.

## Building a 150-lead YouTuber list in under five minutes

- The "800 YouTubers" lead-gen list was built from repeated manual-search exports in AppyStash, using the YouTube search with keywords such as "best products for better sleep" and "sleep product review".
- The search tool allows multiple keywords at once, exclusion keywords and hashtags for social platforms.
- One search returned 150 results, all ranking for the target queries, which Dustin calls "really good quality publishers".
- The CSV export includes the source, contact example, URL, contact email and referral link.
- Referral-link types (Genius Link, ShareASale, Amazon links, Bitly) signal existing affiliate activity.
- Only emails that are public can be scraped, so sorting A to Z puts the leads with emails first and leaves the rest for manual outreach.
- Dustin's comparison: the usual pain with YouTubers is the number of clicks (and a CAPTCHA) needed to reach an email address, and this skips it. A list takes "literally under five minutes" to get to a CSV ready for outreach.

## Email outreach automation tools

- Dustin and Vic point to Instantly and Smartlead for bulk affiliate email outreach. Instantly was used internally and "worked really well", and both are described as very affordable.
- Instantly allows AI-personalized icebreakers. The workflow: put each prospect's content example into a custom variable, then prompt a GPT model to compliment their video.
- Example icebreaker: "I saw you have a video about fast and efficient car chargers... I was just looking for one for my new car."
- The point of the approach is that it sounds far better than bulk "join my affiliate program" or "can you review this product" emails, because the pitch shows the creator's content helped you and offers them a chance to earn.

## Spotting fake followers with HypeAuditor

- Vic's favorite tool for fake-follower detection is HypeAuditor, and he says fake followers are a big problem in influencer marketing. Its "check any influencer" feature is free.
- Engagement rate is the first signal, since it usually indicates suspicious follower activity, and HypeAuditor compares it to the industry average.
- Subscriber growth rate is the second. A big spike followed by much slower growth can mean bought followers or views, unless it was a genuinely viral video that can be checked.
- It also shows total video views and average views.
- AppyStash shows similar audience data (views, likes, audience interests), but Vic recommends running candidates through HypeAuditor for checking stats.

## Onboarding new affiliates

- Vic suggests an onboarding email sequence for each new affiliate, and Dustin confirms that is standard practice. For email, Vic recommends MailerLite as super cheap and affordable, and Mailchimp for a more enterprise solution.
- Vic says to keep three things in mind: emails, onboarding materials and affiliate support.
- Boost Box (built by Ru from Australia) trains an AI chatbot on a program's materials, past work and policies. Affiliates ask it questions first and escalate to the affiliate manager or support team only if needed.
- This takes repetitive support emails off the manager's plate.
- Dustin's caveat: it sits "on that cusp of how much is too much automation", since the last thing partnership marketing should lose is the face-to-face relationship, but he finds its effectiveness valuable.

## Advice for young entrepreneurs

- Dustin's view is that you should not work so hard that you burn out. He tells Vic to get life experiences and says he does not want him "living behind a computer", because you only get one childhood and one set of college years.
- Vic's tips, which Dustin endorses with "great points": outsource as much as possible, build a quality team, focus on growth over profitability early, and pay for help (social media, features) if the budget allows.
- Vic also advises learning the fundamentals of business from the start, taking breaks, traveling, and surrounding yourself with the right people, who may even work for little or nothing to be part of a bigger mission.
- AI lets a high-school student ship features, new products and bug fixes faster than would otherwise be possible while balancing school and work.
