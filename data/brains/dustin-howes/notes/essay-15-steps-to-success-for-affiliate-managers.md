## The 15-step affiliate growth process Dustin follows

Dustin frames affiliate programs as slow-build channels: "You can't just turn them on like Facebook ads and see instant results." The manager has to be "the right mix of aggressive and patient" with high-potential affiliates, and the job is never done because there are always more affiliates to recruit.

He lays out 15 steps, split between what the manager does (recruit, enable, negotiate) and what the affiliate does (research, write, rank).

### Recruiting and onboarding (steps 1-4)
1. **Find the program.** Good affiliates mostly have to be found and convinced, not just discovered via the network. Dustin sends media kits and one-sheeters, offers incentives to join and drive volume, and asks engaging questions.
2. **Review the program, product and site.** The goal is to get the affiliate excited. Give them free product, share reviews with great selling points, show which landing pages convert best, and share how much other affiliates earn. "Do what it takes to get your foot in the door."
3. **Request to join.** Send an official offer, usually with special terms and VIP commissions.
4. **Get accepted.** Harder than it sounds: affiliates forget to accept terms or move on. Follow up as much as necessary and supply all marketing materials.

### Affiliate-side content steps and the manager's role (steps 5-10)
5. **Keyword research.** The affiliate finds high-volume keywords with tools like SEMRush. Provide free trials and product so they can test and pick a main keyword.
6. **Content research.** The affiliate studies what ranks and reads positive and negative reviews. The manager recommends where products fit on their site, with example evergreen posts, competitor-style posts and upcoming promos.
7. **Create the content.** A review of 500 to 10,000 words requires product knowledge, so the manager supplies "all the ammo": the best selling points.
8. **Optimize the content.** Skilled partners know how to rank. To go further, offer to edit the first draft and give feedback.
9. **Monetize the content.** Affiliate links, call-to-action buttons and banners go in. Give the affiliate the latest closing coupon to round out the article.
10. **Start ranking on Google.** Rankings take time and don't happen overnight. Managers and executives "need to be patient here and believe in the process."

### Traffic, conversion and optimization (steps 11-12)
11. **Bring in clicks.** Reach drives early clicks. Ask the internal social team to share strong content or include it in the next affiliate newsletter.
12. **Convert clicks to sales.** Use click data to see what converts and tweak content. Landing pages may need to adapt to the affiliate's audience, so make sure deep linking is enabled for tracking.

### Relationship and expansion (steps 13-15)
13. **Connect with the affiliate's manager.** Someone on each site owns affiliate revenue. Find them and reach out by email, Skype or phone. Dustin uses Calendly to save time scheduling calls.
14. **Calls and negotiating commissions.** Trade more commissions or bonuses for more exposure. It "takes finesse" to get a partner to promote you over competitors, and every conversation is unique.
15. **Create new campaigns.** Once a winning formula exists, repeat steps 5 through 9 with the same partner instead of starting over. "Convincing someone with money in their pocket of doing more is a much easier conversation than starting from scratch."

## What an affiliate manager actually does day to day

Dustin lists the recurring daily and weekly tasks:

- Review program applications.
- Find affiliates that fit, hunt down contact info, send recruitment and follow-up emails.
- Protect brand trademarks with tools like BrandVerity.
- Answer detailed emails and zero out the inbox for themselves, affiliates@ and support@.
- Hold a weekly team call and one-on-ones with the boss.
- Run week-over-week and month-over-month reports for forecasting.
- Give strategy and recommendations to top affiliates and message them on Skype.
- Post group Slack updates for the marketing team.
- Review new partner sales for fraud.
- Plan and write next week's newsletter.
- Report all wins to executives weekly.

## Time management is the deciding success factor

Dustin says the biggest success metric for an affiliate manager is time management, not years of experience or percentage of revenue growth (though growth helps job security). Each day the manager must choose where to spend time among daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual tasks, including executive reporting.

His answer to where time should go: "Spend time nurturing high potential partnerships." He says the execution is the hard part. The manager's job is to recognize high potential in a partner and "do whatever it takes to make magic happen." Without knowing where to spend effort, he argues, a program never reaches its full potential.
