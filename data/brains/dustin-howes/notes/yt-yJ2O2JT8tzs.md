## Partnership marketing is business development at scale

- Dustin's guest, Robert Glazer, frames the industry as "business development supported by technology."
- Dustin's view of the opportunity: partnership marketing is business development at scale, which he calls a multi-trillion dollar market. The question is how to scale all the Business Development Partnerships in the world.
- The name "partnership marketing" is a change for the better. "Affiliate" is confused with "affinity" and implies a loose relationship.
- The term has also widened the tent to include influencer, B2B and other partnership types enabled by technology.

*Note: the opinions in this source are mostly Robert Glazer's; Dustin is the host and asks the questions.*

Because the source's substantive views belong to the guest, not Dustin, I'm cutting the output down to what Dustin himself says or clearly endorses.

## What Dustin says about his own path and podcast

- Dustin built an affiliate marketing course after hearing a talk advocating it. He says six years later he wishes he had never done it, because "beating my head against the wall trying to get people to buy a product that they don't think they need" is hard.
- He started his podcast (this was episode 80 of it) to drum up business for his course and consulting.
- It turned into two benefits: educational upskilling for himself, and a large growth in his connections across the industry.
- He says it is very time consuming, and he is winding it down because he can't do that much content alongside everything else.
- He prefers to stay in his lane: "on the phones and talking with people" doing partnership development, not compliance.

## Dustin's view on where affiliate managers are headed

- Dustin favors a partnership-first skill set: relationship building, recruiting, and open-the-door deal making.
- He is waiting for AI that takes a brand, lists every affiliate in a database that fits its exact niche, and reaches out with their contact information automatically.
- He notes Robert's point that this is machine learning rather than generative AI.
- He admires people who sustain long-running projects (Robert's 17 years at his agency, 330 podcast episodes, 425 newsletters) and asked how they stick with it.
- He also highlights John Labruto's move into a "director of affiliate marketing" title, and says that title is rarely seen in the industry.

## Publisher advice Dustin endorses: lead with passion, not monetization

- Dustin agrees that the less you push a podcast, or any content effort, for other business goals, the more it helps.
- Robert's framing, which Dustin responds to as matching his own experience, is that successful publishers loved a topic (barbecues, cell phones) first and monetized after earning trust.
- Starting from "what do I get out of it" instead of "how do I give value" rarely works.
