## Defining the Ideal Affiliate Profile and partner personas

- Dustin says no ICP means no IAP: if you don't deeply know your Ideal Customer Profile, you can't define your Ideal Affiliate Profile.
- He writes five partner personas, like customer personas, that fit the category and goals.
- For each persona he lists:
  - **Who they are:** blogger, listicle site, YouTuber, LinkedIn creator, newsletter owner, community admin, media buyer, emailer, or coupon/loyalty/extension closer.
  - **Audience fit:** keywords or topics they rank for, platform, niche relevance.
  - **Motivation:** fee vs. free product vs. commission, backlink, co-marketing, traffic support.
  - **Your pitch:** what you can offer (EPCs/conversion rate, exclusive deal, content ideas, tracking, creative).
- He argues quality beats quantity. Total affiliate count is a vanity metric; revenue concentration is real, and a tiny fraction of partners drives most sales.

## Why affiliate recruitment feels hard

- Dustin: "Too many brands expect affiliates to just show up, grab a link, and go. Recruitment and activation end up last on the task list—yet they're the engine of the program."
- Three root causes he sees repeatedly:
  1. **Mis-set expectations.** Networks pitch "set it and forget it," but outbound recruitment is what wins. Recruitment must be an ongoing system, not an afterthought.
  2. **No ICP, so no IAP.**
  3. **Underinvested "exploratory" budget.** Competitive partners, especially creators, rarely engage without some combination of fee, free product, and commission.
- Common management mistakes he lists:
  - Treating recruitment as a one-time task instead of ongoing pipeline work.
  - Chasing celebrity macro-influencers before testing a dozen micro/niche partners.
  - Over-collecting info on applications, which kills signups.
  - No clear rules on paid search, brand bidding, coupons, or extensions.
  - Onboarding with nothing: no assets, no examples, no contact means no activation.

## Where to find high-quality affiliates

Dustin prioritizes incremental partners who influence buyers before checkout:

1. **SEO content owners** (bloggers, reviewers, listicles) already ranking for your target keywords.
2. **Media buyers**, who can scale once messaging is dialed (stay on-brand, review copy closely).
3. **Emailers/newsletters** with curated, responsive lists.
4. **B2B and SaaS:** LinkedIn creators and niche reviewers, lower volume but high intent.
5. **Communities** (Slack, Discord, Facebook, Reddit) via group admins and mods.
6. **Lower-funnel closers:** coupon, loyalty, and extension partners, used with intent and rules.

Tools he and Rewardful's CEO recommend for discovery and outreach:

- Affistash: discovery, keywords, contacts
- Upfluence: influencers
- PhantomBuster: export competitor followers
- Semrush and Similarweb: research
- Hunter.io: emails
- Ahrefs: authority/links
- Waalaxy: LinkedIn outreach
- Matt McWilliams's affiliate email swipe copy

## Writing outreach that gets replies

- Skip the mass blast; send targeted, human messages.
- The first message has three parts:
  1. **Proof you looked:** call out a recent post, video, ranking page, or newsletter issue.
  2. **Value fast:** what the product does and what's in it for them (commission/EPC, free product, backlink, co-marketing, traffic buys).
  3. **One question:** "Would this fit your audience?", "Want to try it?", or "Open to a quick test?"
- Ways to make yes easy:
  - Even a modest exploratory budget dramatically lifts response rates.
  - Always offer a free product or account, especially to creators.
  - Share concrete EPC/conversion-rate ranges and top-performer case studies.
  - Offer a backlink when pitching content sites.
  - Try a triple-touch (DM, email, LinkedIn) over a few days, polite, not spammy.
- Over-templated AI emails are obvious (he cites "dip/dive/delve" and random emojis). Use AI to draft, then humanize.

## Vetting and approving applicants

- Keep the application short: name, company, URL, socials, how they'll promote.
- Green flags:
  - Content and audience match your niche/ICP.
  - Real site/socials, quality content, clear CTAs.
  - Transparent traffic/engagement and a specific promotion plan.
- Red flags:
  - Irrelevant sites (a "cat blog" for a B2B tool) with no plan.
  - Impossible stats, unclear business model, generic copy/paste answers.
  - Patterns tied to fraud or brand bidding: approve cautiously and monitor.

## Onboarding and activating new affiliates

- He treats activation like product onboarding: the "aha" moment is their first commission.
- A 5-email welcome sequence over the first 5 days:
  1. **Welcome + link:** intro, unique link, how to reach you.
  2. **Assets:** logos, banners, deep links, screenshots, demo video, UTM guidance.
  3. **What works:** top content angles, best-converting pages, proven CTAs/titles.
  4. **Max earnings:** promo calendar, exclusive codes, suggested placements, do/don't list.
  5. **Open door:** office hours or Calendly, reminder of perks, invite questions.
- Give them a single home base (Notion or portal) with all of the above, plus case studies, competitor comparisons, media kit, and contact/booking link.
- Incentives that move the needle:
  - **Hybrid deals:** small fee plus commission for tests, especially for creators.
  - **Volume bonuses/tiers** and time-boxed boosts for new promos.
  - **Traffic support:** consider buying ads to a partner's high-ranking article for your key term.
  - **Newsletter shout-outs** that spotlight partner wins as social proof.
  - **Promote the program** on social, since your biggest fans may become long-tail partners.

## Commission structures and sponsorship-only creators

- Which structure fits depends on LTV, upsells, and sales cycle, so he supports both:
  - **Rev-share:** aligns incentives; many programs cap recurring at 12 months to balance risk.
  - **CPA:** pegged to roughly the first 2-3 months of revenue, or a tested fixed amount.
  - **Hybrids:** use them to win competitive recruits and negotiate lower fees.
- For "sponsorship-only" B2B and SaaS creators:
  - Lead with relationship and value: product access, data they can use, co-marketing (webinar, case study, newsletter feature).
  - Share proof, e.g. a similar creator's conversion rate and estimated earnings on your commission plan.
  - When budgets are tight, be patient; friendship and genuine support often beat a one-off fee.
- He recommends measuring and tiering: focus on the few partners who drive the most while steadily testing new recruits.
