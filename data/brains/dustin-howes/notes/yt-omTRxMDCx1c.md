## Commission structure: performance-based tiers that reward ongoing engagement

Bob Sparkins (Leadpages) describes a restructure Dustin endorses and teaches as a model for SaaS programs.

- Old structure: a flat 30% lifetime commission. Problem: affiliates who promoted in 2014-2015 stopped promoting, moved to competitors, and still received checks.
- New structure: 10% base rate, 40% if the affiliate brings at least $49 in new sales in a rolling 30-day window, and 50% for super affiliates above $3,000 in new sales in 30 days. The higher rate applies to renewals as well as new accounts.
- The qualifier is based on new sales, so affiliates must keep engaging to hold the higher tier.
- Two goals when setting commissions: attractive enough to get people out of bed, and profitable enough to protect cash flow.
- Dustin's take: a commission structure that is hard to understand needs a short explainer video in the first onboarding email.

## Why lifetime value and no CPA

- Dustin and Bob favor rev share with retention over upfront CPA for quality: affiliates have to bring customers who renew into year two and three.
- Leadpages offers annual plans and pays within about five weeks (sales in one month are paid by the 13th of the next).
- CPA carries fraud risk. Dustin: if an affiliate gets a $200 payout and only has to pay $30 for a subscription, "they're going to do that all day long until you catch them."
- Rev share lowers fraud risk, because self-submitted sales at 40% don't make sense logistically.
- To answer partners who ask for CPA, show them lifetime value: average months retained times the 40% tier shows they can earn several times a flat CPA.

## Changing a program: communicate and gather feedback

- Before the change, Leadpages surveyed top affiliates by email, group chat and one-on-ones. Some said they were out, some all in. Expect to lose some and gain others.
- Give several months of lead time and over-communicate the deadlines and effective dates.
- Dustin: asking for feedback makes affiliates feel heard and creates more interaction with partners.
- Dustin's warning: keeping a commission structure stale is one of the worst things you can do, since competitors change payouts constantly.

## Ideal affiliate and understanding their side

- Best partners for Leadpages teach audiences how to do business online: course creators, workshop and live-event hosts, and coaches who build the tool into a module ("go get your leadpages account, then your email provider").
- Other fits: YouTubers who showcase use cases and PPC affiliates who know the math, but the focus is course creators.
- Dustin's advice: build your own landing page and try being an affiliate yourself so you can empathize with partners and improve outreach.
- Use varied incentives, since different people respond to different rewards.

## Recognition and engagement

- About 90-95% of Leadpages affiliate revenue comes from the top 5-10% of affiliates.
- Leadpages sends periodic leaderboards (a top 15 for the quarter) mainly to acknowledge and thank top performers rather than run contests.
- After a top affiliate asked for a badge, Leadpages created quarterly badges: number one, top ten, and top one percent. This also incentivizes those outside the group.
- Dustin likes leaderboards because affiliates are competitive and want to know where they rank. He had never considered badges but found the idea appealing.
- Leadpages has run roundtables but no in-person events yet, which Bob calls a shortcoming.

## Migrating affiliate platforms

- Bob's first ten checklist items: don't do it unless you have substantial reasons. Leadpages outgrew a platform built around CPA and e-commerce and moved to PartnerStack for a cleaner dashboard and support for the commission structure.
- Give about six months' notice and explain why.
- Make sure the new platform can import historical data and referrals so no one is lost.
- Run both programs in parallel (Leadpages ran about seven months, paying both platforms' fees) and send private outreach to affiliates still sending traffic to old links.
- Always use a redirect (such as Pretty Links) instead of raw affiliate links, so links can be changed in one place when programs move.
- Dustin's view: a migration pauses growth, and most of your time goes to begging people to move over; affiliates don't love change.
