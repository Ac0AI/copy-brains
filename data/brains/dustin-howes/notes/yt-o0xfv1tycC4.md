## Why affiliate compliance monitoring matters for brands

Dustin hosted Joe Campanella, CEO of Comply, for an "Affiliate Nerd Out" SaaS review. Dustin's first job in the industry was working compliance at CJ, over 15 years ago. He notes that affiliate compliance "is usually ignored until something actually breaks." He frames the core idea this way:

- Affiliates are an extension of the brand's marketing and sales team.
- The brand is ultimately held responsible for what affiliates say, not the affiliate.
- Most programs vet affiliates before onboarding but do little ongoing monitoring.
- Manually checking even a couple hundred affiliates' social accounts is too time consuming for affiliate managers.

## Where compliance risk actually sits

Dustin treats compliance as more than disclosures and cleanup. The risk is in what is being said, and the FTC looks at the intent of the claim.

- Tracking links and gaming of the system are separate issues from what can get a brand sued.
- Implied earnings claims count: suggesting a product lets people "make money online" or generate a set revenue amount is a potential earnings claim.
- Health claims need substantiation: a claim like "reduce wrinkles by 22%" needs a study or evidence behind it.
- Express performance claims such as "guarantees results" are not allowed.
- False scarcity ("only five spots remaining today") is a violation.
- Trigger words the FTC watches for include "step by step" and "easy." Combined with a line like "so easy even a 5-year-old can follow," they become a problem.
- The point of tooling is to mitigate risk, since a business can never remove it fully.

## How an affiliate compliance monitoring workflow runs

The platform Dustin reviewed (Comply's ACM) works as follows:

1. Upload the affiliate list, including each affiliate's referring URL.
2. Add brand name, primary website, YouTube and TikTok channels, and custom brand keywords.
3. The tool crawls the web, social accounts, YouTube videos, websites and sales pages for each affiliate.
4. It scores each piece of content out of 100 (the demo showed 44/100) and flags violations in red.
5. It tracks original versus current score and trends over time in an alert center.
6. It generates a PDF report listing each warning, why it is a violation and how to fix it.

Dustin's takeaway for affiliate managers: this saves hours of manual review and turns monitoring into something that runs continuously.

## Automating affiliate outreach when a violation is found

The tool lets a brand act on violations without manual follow-up:

- One-touch email to the affiliate, with the PDF report attached.
- Reusable email templates, created from several factors.
- Automation settings: scan weekly, every two weeks or monthly.
- Automatic emails when a warning or violation triggers, with the PDF optional.
- Exclusion of select affiliates, such as your top five, from automated outreach.
- Monitoring of paid ads to catch affiliates bidding on trademark or brand keyword terms.

## When to start compliance, and what onboarding looks like

- Dustin asked whether a brand needs to reach a certain size first. The answer given: start now.
- The FTC can look back three years, so a brand doing $500,000 a year today can be investigated for earlier periods when it assumed it was under the radar.
- The FTC has gone after small companies for $22,000 and large ones for hundreds of millions.
- Cost of an investigation, from Joe's own experience at a digital publishing company: multiple millions in fines and legal fees, plus time shut down.
- Onboarding: about a week from signed agreement to use, 15-25 minutes of account setup, then alerts appear after 24-48 hours of crawling across Facebook, TikTok, Instagram and LinkedIn.
- Follow-up: an onboarding call, a check-in a couple of weeks later, then roughly monthly touch points.
- Dustin's own advice to affiliate managers: it is never a bad time to start monitoring, since disclosure monitoring gets harder as a program grows.
