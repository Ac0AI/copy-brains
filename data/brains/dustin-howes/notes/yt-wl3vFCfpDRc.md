## Getting thrown an affiliate program and learning it fast

Erica's story is the case study here, and Dustin frames it as common: many managers are handed an affiliate program with no background, or run a massive network as a one-person show.

- Erica was asked to take over affiliate at a startup in March 2020, with no prior knowledge. She read articles, watched YouTube, and listened to podcasts at the same time.
- Dustin points to acceleration Partners blog posts as a core resource (partner type definitions, CPA vs CPC, first click vs last click). She printed them into a binder.
- Her network rep at PepperJam gave her hour-long calls explaining how to find partners.
- Dustin's takeaway: you can bring a lot of value without knowing everything, and being willing to ask for help prevents many mistakes.

## Networking, asking questions, and not caring what people think

The "subtle art of not giving a duck" theme is applied to fear of looking dumb. Dustin and Erica both argue the fear is mostly in your own head.

- Dustin says he has "no shame whatsoever" and doesn't mind feeling stupid when asking follow-up questions in communities.
- Erica's rule: if anyone thinks you're stupid for asking, shame on them, and you think about it more than they do.
- Her first question to a well-known community figure was the difference between coupon and loyalty partners. It felt scary then and is basic now.
- At conferences: walk up and say "I've seen you on LinkedIn, I like your work, nice to meet you."
- After about 70 episodes and growing branding, Dustin had 20+ strangers approach him at Affiliate Summit. He wants to be friends with and mentor exactly those people.
- He and Erica share an "ask forgiveness rather than permission" outlook, but not in your first three months at a company, to avoid getting fired over something ridiculous.

## Continued education: why veterans should keep learning

Dustin describes a pattern in affiliate managers.

- At 2-3 years of experience, many feel they know everything and stop learning.
- By year six they see how little they knew at year three. By year 14 he still feels there is plenty he doesn't know, which is why he started a podcast and invites people smarter than him.
- Erica's current education mix: trial and error with data, learning from teammates on an agency team, impact.com's PXA course, webinars and podcasts.
- Dustin's own communities: MTech Record, his own community and inner circle, and the PMA. He calls community essential to continual learning and to not falling behind day to day.

## Running programs solo: organization, data, and confidence

How an affiliate manager (AM) works on an island, using Erica's experience at TruthFinder, where she managed a roughly $5 million monthly affiliate budget at about 25 years old and six months into affiliate.

- **Organization drives efficiency.** She uses task management tools, notes, and spreadsheets, with daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly task lists. Impact.com has a spreadsheet template that breaks out AM tasks by cadence, and a QBR template listing talking points for quarterly business reviews.
- **Data backs decisions.** She made a friend in the business intelligence director's team and used data to justify spend, for example backing a proposed spend into an implied CPA from existing conversion rates.
- **Confidence.** She had to remind herself she was in the seat on her qualifications rather than assume someone with a master's should be there. Dustin notes acting like you know what you're doing, and confidence in the program's vision, matters in the role.
- Dustin's observation of her: very efficient with a chill attitude, unlike many successful people in the industry who are neurotic. She was named employee of the year at a 100-person company at 25.

## Staying motivated and educating the business as an in-house AM

- Erica's motivation is largely intrinsic: she takes pride in her work even though she doesn't "give a duck."
- Discouraged days usually signal she needs help with something, so she identifies it and asks. She used a 20-minute call feature with acceleration Partners account reps for questions.
- In-house, a large part of the job is educating higher-ups and advocating for affiliates. One bad actor in a program can make leadership hostile to every affiliate.
- Dustin's addition: AMs often work from home and are the only affiliate person at the company, so a community outlet (friends or virtual) is essential. Erica said MTech tends to surface network outages or updates before anyone else.

## Tools for partner research and recruitment

- Erica's favorite tool is SimilarWeb. She uses it to find new partners, monitor where top affiliates send traffic, see what share goes to competitors, and catch who is bidding on branded keywords when they shouldn't be (compliance).
- Dustin notes that his own tool, AffiStash, integrates a SimilarWeb API, so users get the same stats in the tool for $97 a month, and that it is positioned as an easy way for agencies or brands to recruit affiliates.

## Getting value from conferences and defending "be valuable, not successful"

- Erica's best conference connections are often unplanned: chatting next to someone ordering a drink at a Top Golf event, or at a women-in-affiliate meetup where she met a new partner who will drive traffic to her brand.
- She says she will stop whoever is walking by and say hi, and it always leads to a positive outcome. Dustin's summary: "just be nice to people, have a fun time, network."
- Defending her old LinkedIn post: "the goal is not to be successful, the goal is to be valuable. Once you're valuable, instead of chasing success it will attract itself to you." She still believes it, adding that you can be valuable without knowing everything and that success comes with time and hard work.
- Dustin's point on small voices: "a whole bunch of little old mes make up our whole affiliate community."
