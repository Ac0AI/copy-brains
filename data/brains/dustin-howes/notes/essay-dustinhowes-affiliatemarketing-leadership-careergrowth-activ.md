## Moving from affiliate manager to executive leadership

Dustin hosts the conversation with Viktorija Ratomskė (founder of Partner Gap), and his own framing is that affiliate professionals build skills across SEO, paid media, content, influencers and analytics, yet are still introduced as "the affiliate person" when executive roles open.

- The fix is to stop speaking "affiliate" and start speaking C-suite.
- He frames affiliate experience as a launchpad, not a ceiling.

## Why affiliate managers make good CMOs

Dustin agrees that the most underrated thing affiliate managers do is develop a wide understanding of every marketing channel.

- Partners are so different (SEO, paid, content, newsletters) that the manager has to understand all of them.
- He says the education comes from partners, not formal courses.
- His own view: "my partners in the past have taught me the most out of all of the marketing channels."
- He notes that paid courses can cost money and some "will turn you down the wrong road."

## Learning from partners and your own team

Dustin endorses the habit of asking the people beneath you to challenge you.

- He calls "I don't know anything" a great stance for a leader.
- Team members research and find new avenues, so keep learning from them.

## Relationship management and adaptability

Dustin treats networking and adaptability as core affiliate-manager skills that carry into leadership.

- Adaptability to an environment is "one of those keys to a really good affiliate manager."
- The networking never stops.
- Being able to negotiate and get everyone in a room to reach the same agreement transfers directly to executive work.

## Keeping emotion out of business

Dustin agrees with the principle of never taking things personally, especially in negotiations.

- He admits he has been emotional at points in his career.
- He says keeping it professional is key in this environment.
- He rejects the idea that holding back versus speaking up is a gender issue. He has held his tongue plenty of times and voiced his opinion plenty of times, and says you have to pick and choose your battles.

## Explaining intangible value to executives

Dustin raises the problem that relationship and networking work produces results that are not directly visible in numbers.

- His question to guests: how do you show executives you are productive when the output isn't an obvious number?
- The approach he draws out: make the intangible tangible with data, for example showing that long-standing partner connections are the ones now driving traffic and revenue for the brand.

## What affiliate managers should do to reach the C-suite

Dustin endorses staying broad rather than narrowing to one channel.

- Be an expert on traditional affiliate marketing while staying open to new channels.
- Don't specialize narrowly into only media buying, content or newsletters.
- Build a wide outlook and understanding of all channels.
- Network widely. He suggests going to Affiliate Summit West to see the many different ways people drive traffic, and calls educating yourself through networking "a really great practice."
