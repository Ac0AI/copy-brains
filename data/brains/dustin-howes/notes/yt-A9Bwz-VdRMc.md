## Why affiliate partners don't activate, and how Dustin gets them moving

- Dustin's core problem: an affiliate goes from one cold email to promoting a product with a big gap in between. They have no time to evaluate random products, so the manager has to bridge it.
- He says "making it as easy as possible for them to get the education" is essential.
- Free samples for physical products and free trials for SaaS let partners educate themselves.
- Short videos about what the product does help.
- A personalized video is better. Share your screen and show where your brand could fit on their site or in an existing YouTube video.
- This is his quickest route to activation.

## Paying for a partner's time

- Dustin says money talks, because a publisher's time is valuable.
- Ask what an hour of their consulting costs. Buying an hour at $200 to $300 can be a good investment.
- Use the hour to ask how they would promote the product, how they would approach others, and what their expertise is.
- It builds loyalty and may lead to a real partnership.
- He has traditionally paid for these hours out of his own pocket.
- His order of operations: get information and promotion for free first, then offer sponsorship opportunities, and if the partner is out of budget, ask "can I buy an hour of your time, what does that cost?"
- He charges $200 an hour himself.
- Some people don't recognize their own hourly rate. His cheapest call was $25 an hour, and he'll happily pay $25 for an hour of someone's information.

## Handling too many low-quality and fraud applications

- Dustin calls a high application volume "a good problem to have", because it means the program is doing something right.
- He says he enjoys going through applications every day, since there can be "real gold" in there.
- He cites a peer who reviews about 50 applications a day, even with filters.
- Auto-decline certain countries that traditionally have low affiliate value.
- Put a line in the decline message: if a mistake was made, reply to your email for a second look.
- Train a virtual assistant to review applications.
- Reviewing is 15 to 20 minutes a day, about one application per minute, and he says to shift your mindset to liking it.

## "Lazy" or unresponsive affiliates

- Dustin says managers often don't recognize what affiliates go through with content calendars.
- Getting into a calendar means talking someone into pausing something else.
- A publisher with high traffic, high value and strong domain authority is not lazy, just not motivated enough.
- The fix is motivation: pay for their time, offer sponsorship, or buy an hour of consulting.
- He says "they are not lazy, you just have never been in their shoes."

## Finding long-term, consistent partnerships

- If you can't find long-term partnerships, Dustin says you're likely not deep enough in content.
- Lists and rented traffic are flashes in the pan.
- Content affiliates matter, even the long-tail ones making one or two sales a month. A hundred of them make the difference.
- Finding content sites is a consistent battle.
- Joint ventures and list sharing can be powerful short term, but long-term value is in Google and SEO rankings.
- Tactic: use Semrush to find who ranks for your target keywords and reach out to them.

## Relationship building on calls

- Dustin dislikes using a headset and microphone on one-on-one partner calls, because it takes away from the personal conversation.
- He says building a connection is an intricate part of being a good partner manager.
- He uses a mic for podcasts for audio quality but avoids it in one-on-one conversations.
