## Defining the publisher side as the underserved opportunity in affiliate

- Adam Weiss's consultancy is built on a gap Dustin agrees with: many agencies manage advertiser programs and many technologies support advertisers, but the publisher side is "a little bit underserved."
- Dustin says he builds his courses to serve the do-it-yourself advertiser before they reach the agency level, and that he rarely hears of anyone servicing publishers.
- Newcomers to affiliate need a map of the space: whether to work with every network, what a sub-network is, which agency to call, and when to call an advertiser directly.
- A consultant with that map can speed up a publisher's entry into the space.

## Validating a consulting niche by calling people first

- Adam's approach, which Dustin highlights as the way he found his niche: before launching, he called people he knew from his years at a network and described what he was thinking.
- He asked about their pain points and the problems they wanted solved, all on the publisher side.
- That feedback shaped the offering, and he says he is still "refining and tweaking" it years later.
- Starting simple also worked: he did not agonize over the company name or website. A friend told him to go get business and learn, and the main name criteria were an available domain and LLC name.

## Publisher consulting is more than affiliate revenue

- Dustin and Adam frame publisher problems as three layers that must work in lockstep: the revenue piece (partnerships and economics), the product itself (content site or technology), and customer acquisition (traffic, readers, newsletter subscribers).
- Partnerships create no value without audience, so a consultant who fixes only the revenue stream is not enough.
- Dustin cites Adam Enfroy's approach: publish genuinely helpful content with organic links first so Google sees you as an authority, then monetize once page visits reach a certain level.
- Adam's principle, which Dustin endorses: focus on doing the right thing for the customer and let monetization follow. Non-monetized helpful links are fine.

## Sub-networks versus direct partnerships

- Sub-networks (the Skimlinks and Sovrn type) give publishers an easy entry, with tens of thousands of potential partnerships and the fragmentation across networks aggregated for them.
- Fragmentation is the core publisher pain: an advertiser sets up on one network, but a publisher may need to join Impact, Rakuten, CJ, Awin and more.
- Rule of thumb: manage roughly your top 20% of partners directly, and let sub-networks cover the long tail, unless you have resources to run everything directly.
- Tools Dustin and Adam name for publishers: Affilimate, Trackonomics and FMTC.
- Dustin uses Geniuslink-style link management; his own affiliate links are standardized and redirect through dustinhowes.com/affiliate, and he stopped using Affluent's tool because of limits.

## Using AI and keywords in affiliate

- Dustin's current publisher workflow: build content on high-volume, low-difficulty keywords, then look up "best [keyword]" lists from other bloggers and join the affiliate programs listed. He expects someone to build a better brand-matching product, possibly with AI.
- Adam's view, which Dustin engages with: AI can help networks surface partner recommendations from benchmarks of publishers that worked for similar advertisers, and help optimize economics.
- On the publisher side, AI can flag content gaps and stale posts for operational efficiency and margin, but not replace first-hand content.
- Dustin's tactical advice to advertisers: stuff relevant keywords into your network profile page, because publishers search networks by keyword and affiliate managers too often neglect this.

## Pitching and matching brands with publishers

- Adam leads with goals rather than a publisher pitch: company goals, affiliate program goals, what is working, which publisher mix they want, and where they invest or don't (for example, coupon sites or influencers).
- Brands should likewise share who they are and what they stand for so there is alignment on messaging and values.
- Sometimes it is not a fit, and that is fine.
- Dustin says these basics are what he plugs in his course, since people with a couple of years of experience often forget them.

## Publisher landscape map and when affiliate is the right time

- Dustin praises Adam's quarterly Performance Marketing publisher landscape: a visual map of publishers by category, which Dustin calls a one-of-a-kind resource.
- His use: when he lands a new client or job, he goes to it to build a hit list of potential affiliates.
- Adam's takeaway for advertisers: there is a lot of quality distribution available, and roughly 20% of advertisers are not in affiliate today.
- Advice on timing, from Adam: the right time is a recognizable brand with traffic seeking alternate monetization, or a funded startup with a growth plan. Put operational infrastructure in place before traffic arrives so you are not scrambling.
- On perfectionism, the advice Adam took from Mike McNerney: publish it, because "it's good."
- Dustin also stresses community: martech record's Slack, the PMA (he cites its $9.1 billion industry size figure) and conferences, where people make introductions and help each other.

## Why network jobs are a breeding ground for talent

- Dustin's first industry job was at CJ, and it took him three positions and five interviews to get hired, since he had no industry expertise.
- Both agree networks are the interchange of the ecosystem: advertisers, publishers, tracking, payments, product feeds and reporting all pass through, giving a "30,000 foot view" that few other roles give.
- Alumni go on to brands, publishers and agencies, or start their own agencies.
- Dustin points newcomers to performancemarketingmanager.com, his online course on creating and scaling an affiliate program.
