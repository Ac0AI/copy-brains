## Audit and activate before you recruit

- Dustin hosts the panel (Nancy Harnett, Victor Moen, Ed Seballos); his own contributions are the framing and the emphasis on practical, tested advice. He says B2B affiliate is among "the most scalable and lowest cost acquisition channels in the entire marketing mix."
- He agrees that auditing an existing program is "an absolute must" before jumping into recruitment. Recruitment is necessary and you want early traction, but the audit comes first.
- Vanity metrics such as partner count are not the goal. He backs the panel's view that the aim is revenue-driving partners, not a big roster.

## Recruitment: volume, persistence and fast response

- Dustin highlights a four-email drip campaign he received from Victor's team as a model of good recruitment outreach. He liked three things:
  - It presents what the possibility is.
  - It gives a time frame to get into an exclusive opportunity.
  - It ends with a question aimed at getting him on a call.
- He was "really impressed by the persistence" of that sequence. He says recruitment is a volume game, and that a 10% response rate is excellent: "10% is a good response rate" given he gets a dozen outreach emails a day as a small-time B2B affiliate.
- He sees recruitment as one of the biggest time drains for affiliate managers. A tool that sends over 1,000 outreach emails a month automatically can save an average of 14 hours per week (this is the sponsor's claim, Alfie by Everflow, which he promotes).
- When an affiliate replies and asks for a call, jump on it immediately. He shared a bad example: a brand replied "to save us both time on a call... we're not looking for paid placements at the moment." He calls this "a very bad way to respond to an email to an affiliate" and says to take the call with "no questions asked."
- He works by talking to people directly. His own preference is a 15-minute call to confirm the audience, product and offer fit before committing.

## Conversion rate and co-branded landing pages

- Optimizing the conversion rate for your affiliates is "absolutely optimal" for the program.
- Build partners a co-branded landing page: "that's going to convert better and you're going to be in a much better spot."

## Different affiliates sit at different funnel stages

- Dustin stresses that every affiliate is a little different and "treating them all the same way is the wrong answer."
- Some affiliates are top of funnel and some are bottom. Find the right way to pay each and give top-of-funnel partners the credit they deserve, same as bottom-of-funnel ones.
- Attribution and commission structure get complicated, so he recommends bringing in experienced people to build the commission structure properly.
- He notes Everflow is extremely helpful for building out commission structure: a brand can come with an idea and the team will slow it down and ask what will actually appeal to the affiliates being targeted.

## Coaching affiliates is underrated

- He calls coaching "a big part of affiliate management that people don't recognize how important it is."
- Spending time with affiliates is "extraordinarily valuable" to them and to the program, provided it is done the right way.

## Paying creators who want upfront fees (his take as a creator)

- Dustin's advice to a brand facing creators who want flat fees instead of pure revenue share: beg for an exploration budget from your team.
- Pitch it internally with a concrete number, for example "just give me $2,000 a month to check this out."
- He argues a performance deal alone is slow with creators: "if you pay me up front I am much more likely to get things done immediately" than "I'll get to it when I can."
- Frame the proposition to leadership as more content, faster, than a performance-only structure would deliver.

## Practical filters on the audience's questions

- On the stat that only 16% of affiliates run B2B programs, Dustin said he wasn't sure of the source and couldn't confirm it, though it "sounds right." He treats the thin B2B pool as a reason to seek out the right partners rather than a barrier.
- He fields audience questions on auditing dormant partners and tracking events in the funnel, and says tracking all events across the affiliate journey is the real goal.
