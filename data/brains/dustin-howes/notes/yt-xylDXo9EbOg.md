## Background and path into affiliate marketing

- Dustin spent six years in the Marine Corps, then college (psychology degree), then worked as a poker dealer and a professional poker player for a year before going "dead broke."
- His first affiliate job was in the fraud department at Commission Junction (CJ). Poker's fraud awareness, Marine Corps discipline and the psychology degree made it the best fit when he had zero affiliate experience.
- Fraud work let him audit how affiliates making six and seven figures operated, including "the really dirty parts of affiliate marketing." That gave him a sharp eye for fraud when later running merchant programs.
- That led to roles in sales and managing programs for merchants. He has worked for networks, agencies, merchants and publishers, and now owns his own publications, agency and brands, with 14 years in the industry.
- He worked at WP Engine (hosting) and at Volusion before it.

## Why he built an affiliate management course and community

- At WP Engine the program was doing well, but he wanted to get better and found conferences too unstructured: no clear path to success, and they cost thousands to send a representative to.
- He put everything he knew about running affiliate programs into an online course: roughly 200-300 slides and eight hours of video in step-by-step modules. He has sold it for five years.
- Pricing ladder:
  - Course: $300 entry fee.
  - Mastermind: another $700 to join, or $100 a month as a membership.
  - Done-for-you affiliate program management: price points of $2,500 and $15,000.
- Every course buyer is a candidate for upsell into the next tier. He notes many course creators use the course as lead gen for higher-priced services.
- The course became a community of affiliate managers helping each other. He calls the recurring membership his "inner circle" and mentors members when they need help growing a program.
- He also runs the podcast Affiliate Nerd Out, created to pick other people's brains.
- He owns a SaaS product, Affstash, that uses AI to find affiliates to recruit based on search keywords, and doubles as a tool for finding backlink opportunities on high-domain-authority sites, with contact information.
- On courses: he says there is "nothing passive" about owning one. You are constantly updating content and marketing it. Passive only describes waking up to overnight sales after the hard work. He has "pounded my head against a wall" for five years working out why people are not buying, and would not advise building one without a real need and a large email list.

## Core principles for affiliate managers

- **Relationships first.** Affiliates are "not just a number on a page" with dollar signs; they are people. The more love you give, the more they give back.
- **Stay organized and prioritize.** Pay more attention to your favorites at the top of the list, without ignoring the people at the bottom making barely any sales. Where you spend your time each day is a staple of his course.
- **Keep learning.** In year three managers think they know everything, by year six they realize how little they know, and at year 14 he still learns from others. Find mentors and communities.
- **Spot potential in lower-tier affiliates** and work with them to become big ones later. He says this is the hardest thing to teach.
- **Reporting.** He doesn't enjoy data, but it is necessary. He teaches what to look for and how to present it to executives so they see the program is working.
- **Don't be a ghost.** Managers can't help affiliates who don't talk to them. Affiliates work alone, so he urges finding communities (he names PMA, MarTech Review's Slack, and his own inner circle), which he says is a social necessity after 60 hours a week at a computer.

## The affiliate-turned-manager path: Adam Enfroy

- Adam Enfroy was Dustin's first student in the affiliate management course. He had digital marketing and partnership skills and had just taken the job at BigCommerce.
- He increased affiliate channel revenue by over 200% in his first six months and got promoted in six months.
- Seeing the affiliates, he built his own sites, went from 40 to 80 hours a week, reached $10,000 a month on the side, quit, and a year later was making six figures a month and netting about a million dollars a year.
- Dustin says it is a slow burn for most people with full-time jobs and not a get-rich-quick scheme. The people with Lamborghinis and tigers worked very hard early. He calls courses that sell it as a scheme "pretty irresponsible."
- He recommends Adam's website SEO and YouTube SEO course, which he has taken himself. Dustin himself has never maximized his own affiliate efforts and describes affiliate income as "my retirement plan."

## His affiliate marketing playbook

- **Focus.** Pick one brand and one medium (writing and SEO, or on-camera video and YouTube) and get good at it before moving on. Lessons transfer: replicate what worked and cut what didn't.
- **SEO is a long game** measured in years, with a small cost (hosting and your time).
- **Content authenticity.** Only recommend things you actually believe in; lack of passion shows. He warns Google can recognize AI-generated content.
- **Amplify everything.** Use a tool like Buffer or eClincher and post every piece of content to free places such as Reddit, and anywhere you can get a link back to your site, especially content-relevant sites.
- **Backlinks.** Hire virtual assistants to get backlinks to both transactional and informational articles.
- **Paid amplification.** Spend $50-$100 to boost a good YouTube video. Or pay around a quarter per click on a transactional keyword that sends traffic to a listicle blog post. The extra traffic helps Google see you as an authority, which lifts rankings.
- **Every link is an opportunity.** Use short links via the Pretty Links plugin on his own domain that redirect to affiliate links. When people ask what tools he uses for podcasting, he sends a short link for Opus (clip tool), which is word-of-mouth marketing combined with affiliate marketing. Anyone with a full-time job can be an affiliate for tools in their own workflow.
- **Channels.**
  - Quora can work if you stay active.
  - Reddit does not allow affiliate links, but he knows affiliates with a secret workaround.
  - Pinterest is "still a monster" for affiliates (memes, pictures, suggestions, videos).

## Email marketing and lead capture

- Email is the cheapest route to conversions at every company he has worked for. Affiliate is often number two, and when done right has a lower cost per acquisition than paid social ads.
- His regret: he wishes he had collected emails much earlier. He has 13,000 LinkedIn followers he is trying to convert to email, and an email list in the low 10,000s that "should be easily at 50" had he started sooner.
- He says lead capture is one of his weakest assets, but describes a four-step process:
  1. Offer a downloadable lead magnet on the site that requires an email address, valuable enough to lead to a later purchase.
  2. Put the lead magnet in the right-hand column of the blog.
  3. Add popups: exit-intent, time-on-page or scroll-based.
  4. Build a second piece of content and A/B test which brings in more subscribers; keep the winner, test a third against it, and repeat.
- Final step: place topic-specific lead magnets on matching pages (a cat page gets the cat lead magnet, not the dog one).

## Marketing innovation, courses and AI

- **Innovation.** Make something that works first, then tweak it. Go to conferences to learn what worked "last year," and look for the next thing. Copy the latest from people like Frank Kern, make it work for you, then adjust it slightly.
- **Promoting others' courses.** Easier than making your own. Course creators can pay affiliates 20% to 90% of revenue because overhead is low and they hope to upsell. He suggests only promoting a course you have taken and believe in, and building your own splash pages to drive traffic.
- **Weakness of course affiliate programs.** There's no affiliate network that caters to courses, and good affiliates don't like joining the internal programs in Kajabi, GoHighLevel or ClickFunnels. That makes it hard for creators to run a profitable affiliate program.
- **Free education.** Take free courses from big merchants (he says SEMrush's SEO course taught him much of what he knows). He notes free courses bundled with paid courses may be upsell-driven.
- **AI.** Embrace it or lose your job; learn tools beyond ChatGPT (he mentions Jasper and Opus). He says AI content isn't top-notch yet and calls out words like "dips, dives and delves" as AI tells. He uses Speedy Brand, edits heavily to put in his own voice, and expects AI to get much better within two years.
