## Network settings that matter for e-commerce programs

Dustin's guest (not Dustin) did most of the talking on network setup, but Dustin endorses and adds the following requirements when choosing or configuring an affiliate network:

- **Flexible commissions.** "Anything that has a flat fee that you can't change is not a network for me." Coupon sites should earn much less than content sites, so commission must be adjustable per partner type.
- **Vanity code (voucher) tracking** is required to measure coupon-driven sales.
- **Flexible locking periods.** For native-ad buyers he trusts, he wants a 10-day lock instead of 30 days so they are paid faster.
- **Two-tier commissions.** He wants to recruit affiliates into a program and be paid in perpetuity on their activity, and to let affiliates invite other affiliates. He calls it "a big motivator" and says not every network supports it.
- **Multiple networks as a workaround.** He dislikes running several networks, but sometimes it is the solution: coupon sites on one network and content sites on a CPA model on another. He accepts some overlap and possible double commissions on a sale.

## Product feeds are an essential, not just for shopping sites

Dustin says product feeds are making a comeback and are no longer only for comparison and shopping platforms.

- Content affiliates now use feeds to pull products and calls to action into articles.
- Tools like affiliate.com and Gizmo let affiliates build content from feeds, so a data feed with strong calls to action matters to them.
- YouTubers can put feed-powered links in video descriptions, which makes the call to action "so much hotter."
- His rule: he does not work with networks that lack product feed capability, because he finds it "super necessary in e-com."

## Where he stands on the affiliate-manager skill mix

Dustin cut his teeth as an affiliate manager and says you should keep doing the hands-on work.

- If you are not working on strategy and technology yourself, "you shouldn't be qualified to be talking about it."
- He still recruits affiliates for the programs he runs himself rather than delegating all of it.
- He finds strategy the fun, easy part and the grunt work the hard part.
- He is a relationship and outreach person, not a data person ("analytics and me are not great"), so he pairs with a data specialist.
- His most successful program, WP Engine, was run with a partner (Oswin) who was the data head and who talked to executives about results, while Dustin did relationships and outreach.

## Eight types of affiliate manager and how to staff a team

Dustin wrote a blog post breaking affiliate managers into eight types:

1. Relationship architects (people like him who are good at talking to people and likable)
2. Organizational types
3. Knowledge fountains
4. Problem solvers
5. Data heads
6. Strategists
7. Partner supporters
8. (He listed these as his categories; the transcript names seven explicitly in the list above.)

His staffing guidance:

- A good affiliate manager needs roughly four of these skills. Having all of them is rare.
- You do not have to be best at everything. Hire a complement: if you hire a relationship guru, also hire a data head to fill the gap.
- Pairing a relationship person with a data person makes "one really good affiliate manager."

## Hiring, outreach and gender observation

- Dustin says that in his experience female affiliate managers are often better than male ones, and that if he were to hire another affiliate manager it would be a woman. His reasoning is that likability and building connections come easier.
- He has hired virtual assistants to do outreach on his behalf and bring in deals, and says female VAs consistently get better response rates.
- He notes only a finite number of men in the industry are good at the job, and he does not consider himself a very good affiliate manager overall, only strong in certain aspects.

## Training and documentation for agency teams

- As an agency owner he says you must train everyone in "the ways that are working right now."
- Good documentation for turning a young marketing specialist into an affiliate manager is "an art."
- He has done this for years through his Performance Marketing Manager course.
- He says these skills develop over time and only through experience plus the right training.

## Boutique agencies and award shows

- Dustin defines "boutique" as being dialed into what you do and serving clients well, and says it is not a negative regardless of size.
- He favors boutique agencies, saying they give "the most bang for your buck."
- He agrees with the guest's critique that awards are often not merit-based because small agencies doing strong work cannot afford entry fees.
