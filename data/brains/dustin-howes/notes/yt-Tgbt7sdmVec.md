## How Dustin builds a "Perfect Partner 500" affiliate recruitment list

- **Six-step process per brand:** (1) learn the brand, (2) audit the existing program, (3) build partner personas, (4) run a gap analysis of which affiliates the program is missing, (5) build a list of 500 ideal targets with contact info, (6) write email templates for each persona.
- **Learning the brand:** the brand fills out a survey of about 30 minutes for someone on the marketing team. It covers the marketing campaigns that work for them.
- **Live demo brand:** Truly Free, organic non-toxic cleaning supplies (fabric softener, cleaning products). The top target is moms who are cautious about chemicals, so "mommy bloggers" are a priority. The brand already sends influencers a free product pack.
- **Where he sources targets:**
  - Competitor backlinks (Semrush for the backlinks, Afy Stash for contact enrichment).
  - Existing content that doesn't use affiliate links, found with manual YouTube searches.
  - Brand followers, competitor followers and existing customers, using Phantom Buster to pull followers from a competing brand's Instagram and Influencer Club to enrich them.
  - Content sites found by keyword.
  - Influencers via Upfluence.
  - Podcasts via PodMatch.
  - His own lists of high-potential big publications from past work.

## Mining competitor backlinks for partners already on performance deals

- In Semrush, he enters a competitor's URL and opens the Backlinks report.
- He filters to the "Sponsored" link type first. Sponsored links show the competitor is partnered with that site on some performance basis, so he can see which affiliate partners they work with.
- He downloads the list and takes the source URLs (his example from another client, Backbone: PC Gamer is a great fit).
- He pastes the domains, or uploads a CSV, into Afy Stash's bulk contact finder.
- Afy Stash returns roughly 5 to 10 contacts per company, with LinkedIn and email.
- He picks the right person (for example a writer or editor) and adds them to the list.
- A competitor with few backlinks and weak domain authority is a weak source. He moves on to find a better-established competitor, since low traffic means little to spy on.
- Competitor discovery tip: Semrush's organic competitors are "hit or miss". A plain Google search such as "[brand] competitor" and checking the first non-sponsored results works too.

## Finding content sites by ranking keywords

- In Semrush's Keyword Magic Tool, he searches a keyword the brand would want to rank for (example: "organic cleaning supplies").
- He exports the first 100 ranking results to CSV and uploads it to Afy Stash to get contacts.
- He strips out results that aren't recruitable: Walmart, Amazon, YouTube, Target and similar. Anything already ranking and not a content partner is eliminated.
- He downloads the contacts as a CSV, or stars them to push them into the CRM.
- Afy Stash counts a credit for every contact downloaded.
- He also looks at the brand's organic keywords with the brand name removed, to see what the brand wants to rank for and is missing.

## Finding influencers with Upfluence

- Dustin calls Upfluence "incredibly powerful" but "pretty expensive". He thinks it's about $500 a month for 500 credits, and says he could be wrong on that.
- Filters he uses:
  - Platform: Instagram.
  - Followers: 20,000 to 200,000.
  - Engagement rate: 2% as a baseline, higher if you want.
  - Keyword set as a must-have in their content (example: "safe cleaning products").
  - Show only creators with emails.
  - Country: United States.
- A keyword filter can cut millions of creators down to a handful, and with looser filters he could pick from hundreds or thousands.
- He likes the "area of expertise" filter because creators may still work on a performance basis with just gifting.
- Caveat: some creators are represented by agencies and are hard to land on a real sponsorship.
- Upfluence shows an estimated price range per creator. It's negotiable but gives a baseline.
- He reviews each profile, adds good fits to a selected list (for example a creator with a dryer ball in her content and 21,000 followers) and exports.
- The standard export has no emails; the email export is what he uses. Upfluence gives about 50 extra columns he doesn't need. He keeps URL, subscribers, views, engagement rate, engagement growth, price suggestions, blogs and visits.

## Scoring and prioritizing the list

- He scores each prospect from 1 to 5 based on partner type, category fit, follower range of 20,000 to 200,000, and US location.
- Only 4s and 5s go on the sheet. A 3 won't make the list of 500.

## Partner personas and client alignment

- He sorts partners into two top-level types: influencers and content sites.
- He doesn't work with coupon or loyalty sites. He is "strictly looking on content creators".
- Each type gets subcategories (for example gaming news, Minecraft influencers, toys), with an example of each.
- He takes the personas back to the client and asks whether they agree, which to pursue and how much time to spend on each.
- Prioritizing matters. Example: build 50 Minecraft influencers and only 20 toy influencers.
- For content sites, the list is what's already ranking online that the brand could be part of on a listicle. It includes the people at each company and their contact information.
