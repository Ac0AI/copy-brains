## Remote work removed the structure that held a workday together

- Dustin argues that the digital industry went fully remote and "quietly removed every external structure that once held a workday together."
- The office had built-in scaffolding: fixed hours, people around you, visible expectations. Remote work stripped these out without replacing them.
- He frames this as a structural problem rather than a personal failing. People for whom external structure matters most were hit hardest.

## Nobody was trained to work remotely

- According to Dustin, no one was trained for remote work. Employers simply had to trust that people were responsible enough to manage themselves.
- That trust assumed self-management came naturally. For people with ADHD, it often doesn't, so the setup worked against them.

## Productivity apps and ADHD: "a recipe for failure"

- Dustin notes that apps were built to make people more productive, but ADHD people ("ADHDoers") were "thrown into a world with a recipe for failure."
- His point is that tools alone don't replace the structure that was lost. They assume a level of self-direction that ADHD brains may not have by default.

## Why this matters for people in affiliate and digital marketing

- Dustin positions this topic as relevant to anyone in the digital space who may be struggling quietly with the lack of structure.
- He frames the topic around a guest, Kyrus Keenan Westcott, who holds a full-time job and an influencer side hustle with an ADHD brain.
- The purpose of the episode, as Dustin describes it, is to share what is working for him in practice, plus tips for others, as part of the Affiliate Nerd Out #nerditorium series.
- The post is an introduction to the episode, so the specific systems and tactics the guest uses are not covered here.
