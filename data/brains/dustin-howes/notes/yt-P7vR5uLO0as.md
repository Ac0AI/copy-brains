## Affiliate Summit's evolution and where new affiliates get lost

- Dustin asks long-time industry peers what Affiliate Summit was like in its early days versus now, and the answers shape his view of how the industry changed.
- Early on the industry networked through conference calls hosted by one company that had a conference line, with about 20-25 people on them.
- The first Affiliate Summit was roughly 150 people, one day to a day and a half, all panels in one room, with networking over lunch and dinner.
- Today the show is far bigger, and newer content producers and small affiliates get lost in "the sea of CPA networks."
- Dustin's takeaway: the relationships between merchants, agencies and newer publishers are where value sits, and communities like the PMA (Performance Marketing Association) and brunches at the show help fill that gap.

## Pitching PPC affiliates to merchants (no-risk positioning)

- Dustin's guests pitch PPC affiliates to merchants as a risk-free channel, and he engages with and endorses the framing.
- The argument: if a merchant runs PPC in-house, they carry the risk of not knowing what they're doing and of spending money with no positive ROI.
- In the affiliate channel the merchant only pays commissions on actual sales, so they aren't fronting ad spend and the channel "should always be a positive ROI."
- Dustin's use: send merchants who want PPC to PPC publishers rather than having them front the media budget.

## Three topics the affiliate industry must level up on

- Dustin draws out a guest's view of what is coming and treats it as the agenda for affiliate managers.
- Compliance: confirming partners are compliant and that it's tracked.
- AI and automation: conversational search (ChatGPT, Bard) now gives product recommendations, and the industry has to figure out how that is tracked and credited. Younger buyers may use comparison sites, blogs and newsletters less.
- Integration of affiliate with influencer and creator marketing: Gen Z and Gen Alpha lean on TikTok, Snapchat and video, and affiliate tracking hasn't yet figured out how to convert and attribute in those channels.
- Dustin's own stance on AI: "AI is not going to come and take our jobs as AMs, but the people that are using AI will." He frames the real risk as tracking, conversions and crediting contribution to a sale.

## Using ChatGPT for affiliate managers' prep and content

- Dustin agrees ChatGPT is "magical" for content creation: brainstorming, drafting talking points and doing research.
- Meeting prep: if you run out of time before a meeting, ask for 10 bullet points on a brand's value propositions, its main competitors, or a publisher's audience.
- Campaign matching: ask it to match your campaigns to a particular brand and tell you which to pitch, so you arrive with a stronger, targeted offer.
- Caveat he endorses: you need someone who knows how to use it well.

## Impact's creator and affiliate platform and manager organization

- Dustin discusses a platform that handles both affiliate and creator programs in one place, with a toggle between the two.
- The problem it solves: creators already sit inside affiliate programs but want things affiliates traditionally didn't get, like free products, referrals, flat rates and sponsorships.
- Organization tools he draws out and endorses for managing a big team: calendar discipline ("if it's not on the calendar it doesn't exist"), Gmail snooze and Slack reminders, Lattice for one-on-ones, shared Drive folders, and Wiki/Confluence/Seismic to document processes so people don't recreate the wheel.
- Managers should meet each person, including indirect reports, at least quarterly and keep notes in one centralized place tied to goals and feedback.

## Mentorship and "help first" as a career approach

- Dustin describes his own approach, learned from a mentor, as "help others first": sit down with people and ask how you can support them.
- He asks every guest "how can I help you?" and treats helping as the default way to build relationships.
- He uses the show itself as a relationship tool: short five-minute guest conversations to spread the word about affiliate marketing, with guests invited via dustinhouse.com/nerd.
- His stated long-term goal is to move to the other side of the equation and be an affiliate full-time.
