## Real talk with an affiliate manager: network failures that cost clients thousands

Dustin recounts two cases from a conversation with affiliate manager Christen Evans in which affiliate networks failed clients.

- **Eight months of broken tracking.** A client's tracking was down for eight months. The network billed $7,000 in estimated commissions plus $1,500 to reinstate tracking.
- **Why the bill was unreasonable.** The estimates used general conversion rates rather than the client's actual data, so they were nearly impossible to verify. Dustin says that estimating commissions for a short outage might make sense, but "eight months is excessive."
- **The network's failure.** He argues the network should have flagged the tracking problem much earlier instead of blindsiding the client with a large invoice.

## Removed affiliates can still earn via "bookmarked links"

Christen removed an affiliate from a program in February and found that same affiliate credited with two sales months later.

- **Cause:** a network feature called "bookmarked links" lets affiliates keep earning commissions even after removal from a program.
- **Network response:** the network's proposed solutions were unhelpful, so Christen had to reverse the sales manually.
- **Lesson Dustin draws:** this shows why you must stay hands-on with your own data rather than trusting network defaults.

## Takeaway: monitor network data closely

Dustin's first of three takeaways is to stay hands-on with data.

- Network quirks such as "bookmarked links" and estimated commissions can cause major headaches.
- Diligent monitoring, rather than assuming the network will catch problems, is the protection.
- Early detection matters: a tracking outage caught in days is a minor fix, while one left for eight months became an $8,500 problem.

## Standing out in a job hunt with a video and a landing page

Dustin describes how he approached a competitive job search while moving toward a more stable role at a major agency, keeping affiliate marketing as a side hustle.

- **Tactic:** he made a three-minute YouTube video and a dedicated page explaining why he would be a great hire.
- **Result:** it got him in the door and landed the crucial first interview; he was in the final stages when he wrote.
- **Principle:** going the extra mile with a personalized, creative approach helps you stand out in a crowded market.

## Seasonality: the summer slump and the Q4 rush

Dustin and Christen discussed industry timing.

- **Summer** felt like a "graveyard," with many people out of office and little response to outreach.
- **After school restarts**, emails get responses again.
- **Q4:** with Black Friday approaching, everyone scrambles to secure placements and finalize strategies.
- **His advice:** prepare for Q4 early. Lock in placements and finalize strategy before the holiday rush hits.
