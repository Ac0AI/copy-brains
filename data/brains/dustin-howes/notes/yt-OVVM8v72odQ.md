## Gigi Robinson's creator-first view of paid partnerships vs. affiliate

- Dustin hosts Gigi Robinson (speaker, creator-preneur, founder of Host of Influence) on "Affiliate Nerd Out". Gigi is the one making these points; Dustin's own contribution is framing influencers as top-of-funnel.
- Dustin agrees influencers sit at the top of the funnel for brand recognition and getting a customer in the door. Other affiliates act as "closers" who retarget and close the deal.
- His view is that every influencer and creator deserves to get paid for their time and effort, and that affiliate marketing can be unfair on that point.
- He flags that Gigi is not a fan of affiliate marketing and that this is fine with him. He wanted her perspective so affiliate managers can learn how creators think.
- Dustin promotes Inflection as the sponsor: personalized funnels that help influencers convert more, with co-branded landing pages, discount codes for the influencers, and a quote from them about why they like the product.

## Why many creators decline commission-only deals

These are Gigi's arguments, which Dustin treats as a valuable creator perspective:

- Link clicks do not equal conversions. Hundreds or thousands of story link clicks may produce only a handful of purchases.
- Her example: five people buy a $45 Stanley bottle at a 3 to 5% commission, which does not compensate days or hours of production time or travel.
- Not every creator is a converter. Many share wins and never post links, so brands should check tracking data and know who they are targeting before pitching an affiliate deal.
- She only considers affiliate for high-ticket products aligned with her niche (air and water purifiers, tech she uses). She says fashion and home creators are where affiliate money is made.
- Dustin's takeaway for managers: match the deal type to the creator's niche and audience behavior.

## Hybrid models and the "Honeygate" risk

- Dustin raises Honeygate, where the Honey browser extension is accused of taking last-click commissions from influencers who drove the sale.
- Gigi's response: this is a reason to value guaranteed paid partnerships over performance-only income.
- Hybrid models (flat fee plus affiliate) are good in principle, but she often sees flat fees of only a couple hundred dollars.
- She describes agencies that boost a creator's organic content containing affiliate links and split the income with the creator 70/30, 60/40 or 50/50. This suits hardware or fashion creators, not her.

## How brands should approach creators

Answering Dustin's final question for affiliate managers, Gigi's guidance, which he summarizes as "budget and creative freedom":

- Lead with budget. A couple thousand dollars is enough to build something productive.
- Let the creator pitch the idea. Her example: telling her own story of using Squarespace since age 10 and how she built her business on it, tied to a product launch, rather than reading a script.
- Scripted ads perform worse because audiences realize it is just an ad. Some creators now tell audiences "I have an ad coming, please engage" so they can keep creating.
- Collaborate on ideas that align with the creator and comply with FTC guidelines.

## Running brand deals smoothly (creator workflow)

- Gigi works inbound and outbound deals the same way and says it makes no difference; what matters is reaching the negotiation stage.
- Hold a Zoom call before production to align on the brief and prevent endless back-and-forth.
- Dustin's addition: record the call and keep notes as proof of what was agreed if the brand later asks for changes.
- Bake in edit clauses limiting revisions to one or two rounds.
- Creators are not commercial producers with one shot on location, and brands should not assume anything can be redone on a whim.

## Storytelling and authenticity framework

- Gigi's background in fine arts taught her "show, don't tell". She defines authenticity as transparent storytelling and vulnerability, not just posting outfits of the day.
- Use a hook, know the target audience, and choose what resonates (photo, video, headline). Put the detail in the caption.
- She says roughly 94% of creators earn under $100,000 a year, which is why many take deals for the money.
- Her stance: the creator should use the product and give an authentic review. She focuses on long-term brand building rather than going viral.
