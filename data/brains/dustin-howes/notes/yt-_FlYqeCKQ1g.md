## Why affiliate programs fail and what the "fish" lesson means

- Dustin says most affiliate programs fail because they are "set up poorly and just ignored", not because the product is bad. He compares them to raising kids: nurtured programs treat you well, ignored ones "turn into a turd".
- Good affiliates "don't just show up" and make magic happen. They are fickle, get excited about a niche, and drift away if they lack resources or a good onboarding experience.
- Any program not set up by a trained manager has affiliates "falling through the cracks".
- He argues setup alone won't grow a program. The manager must be taught the functions, per the "teach a man to fish" adage.
- His payoff claim for doing it right over 12 months: up to 20% additional revenue, an "army of sales reps" on commission, a cost per acquisition cheaper than any other channel except email, and the highest-quality customers with the best lifetime value.
- His background: 13+ years in affiliate, including Commission Junction, impact, publishers, agencies and merchant programs. At WP Engine he grew the program about 60% year over year consistently.

## Step 1: Research and the 30/60/90/180/365 plan

- Run a competitive analysis chart: which platform competitors use, their offers, their application process, and their commissions. Sign up for their programs and note what they do right and wrong.
- Calculate your standard and maximum commission rates. Stay competitive, and work out what you can afford to give partners while the company still profits. If you can't out-pay competitors, find something else to offer.
- Take demos with at least three tracking platforms before choosing.
- Decide in-house vs outsourced (agency) management.
- Build partner personas, like customer avatars, to define who you're targeting.
- Write a 30, 60, 90, 180 and 365-day plan with growth expectations and accountability for the affiliate manager. Do this before hiring the person or company to manage it.

## Step 2: Program decisions (payouts, visibility, CRM, platform)

- Pick a payout model: cost per click, lead or sale. He calls CPA-style models the easiest and most efficient.
- Define the standard payout and the absolute maximum you'd pay premium partners. Set a base rate and a VIP rate, plus any bonuses or volume/sale incentives.
- Decide whether the program is public or invite-only.
- Choose a CRM for partnerships ("you will need a CRM"). You can piggyback on the company CRM or separate partnerships into their own.
- Choose a platform: a big network gives big publisher pools and strong reports; a small platform saves money but means you do all the recruiting yourself.

## Steps 3 and 4: Build the program, then announce it

Build everything before announcing, because the program is "an extension of your brand".

- Application responders (automated replies, some platforms allow only one or two).
- Welcome email flow that sets the tone, explains where assets are and how to grab a link.
- Branded signup page: the first impression.
- Banners and links library with landing page variations.
- Program terms and conditions.
- Partner assets folder with banners, links, blog posts and examples of affiliates already doing good work.
- Recruitment email templates per persona, as a message sequence.
- Third-party tools to expand reach.

His bar analogy: an unstaffed bar with no bouncer or bartender attracts dishonest people and makes honest ones cautious. A greeted, served guest becomes loyal.

Announcement tactics:
- Company newsletter, blog, social and forums.
- Invite customers, since they're evangelists.
- Put the affiliate program link in the site footer, "the first place I look when I'm an affiliate".

## Step 5: Recruitment

- Build a partner list: use Semrush to find sites ranking on Google, Hunter.io for contact emails, and look at competitors' traffic sources and backlinks.
- Email with a goal of 50 per week and scale up, then schedule calls.
- Come to calls flexible, with talking points on why the program is worth promoting.
- He mentions using a tool called Rona for recruiting and time management.
- Most programs fail by not dividing time between recruiting and activating interested affiliates.
- Good managers look for potential before the affiliate makes sales and nurture the relationship, acting as the leader who helps the troops succeed.

## Step 6: Ongoing program management

- Update assets, approve or deny applications daily, and initiate the automated welcome sequence with a first link.
- Send a newsletter at least monthly to stay top of mind.
- Run incentive campaigns that motivate new content and sales.
- Give VIP treatment to top performers: "every teacher has favorites".
- Keep banners and links fresh, with links, landing pages and banners for every new product.

## Step 7: Reporting KPIs for executives

Because the manager's work has many intangibles, build reports that show effort and results.

- Daily: transactions, program performance, traffic spikes. Investigate anything out of place.
- Weekly: week-over-week results and best-selling days.
- Weekly/monthly: top links and banners, best-selling SKUs, best coupons, partner conversion rates vs average.
- Also track lifetime value and AOV per partner to identify who drives the best traffic.
- Investigate dips and spikes per partner versus the prior month.
