## Why founders should learn from operators, not courses

Jake's core argument: anyone trying to build a profitable app has two options. One is to spend hundreds of hours researching ideas and bingeing coding and marketing tutorials, then quit because they don't know where to start or whose advice to trust. The other is to study people "actually in the arena", meaning those shipping apps, running profitable companies and posting what works in real time. He says following a few of these operators is "more valuable than any course or YouTube video you'll consume."

His practical recommendation: pick any two of the seven operators he lists, read everything they've published, and you'll know more about building a profitable app than 95% of people calling themselves founders. His framing is that the barrier to building has never been lower, and the barrier to learning from the right people has just dropped too.

## Eric Seufert: mobile advertising and paid acquisition economics

Jake recommends Eric Seufert as the "marketing economist". Seufert ran growth at Wooga and Rovio (the Angry Birds studio), wrote the book *Freemium Economics*, and built and sold a marketing analytics company.

- His blog Mobile Dev Memo explains how mobile advertising actually works after the privacy changes.
- Jake points to him for founders asking why their paid acquisition isn't converting and what to do instead.
- Jake calls him one of the deepest thinkers in the space on this topic.

## Jacob Eiting: pricing, paywalls and subscription conversion

Jake calls Jacob Eiting "the subscription whisperer". Eiting is founder/CEO of RevenueCat, which powers in-app subscriptions for 30,000+ apps. That gives him a view of what is making money across the whole App Store.

- Jake says to listen to him on pricing, paywalls and conversion because he is "reading from the largest subscription dataset on earth."
- Distribution lesson Jake highlights: Eiting grew RevenueCat from $400/month to $2M ARR by writing content that answered the exact questions developers were Googling. Jake calls this "an elite distribution lesson in itself."

## David Barnard and Adam Lyttle: indie app playbooks and portfolio systems

**David Barnard, the indie veteran.** Barnard has been building apps since the App Store launched in 2008. He has shipped 30+ projects, sold four, and survived every algorithm shift.

- He is RevenueCat's Growth Advocate and hosts the Sub Club podcast, where he extracts tactical playbooks from the founders behind big apps.
- Jake values his war stories, including launches that flopped badly and how to actually A/B test.

**Adam Lyttle, the portfolio machine.** Lyttle went from $200K in debt to a reported $800K+ across a portfolio of 50+ simple apps. Jake sees this as proof that you don't need to build the next Instagram to win.

- His approach is a repeatable system: find a niche, build simply, optimize for App Store discovery, repeat.
- He posts ASO tricks, rapid-build sprints and revenue numbers openly.
- Jake calls him the most actionable follow on the list for a solo builder.

## Thomas Petit: App Store Optimization and Apple Search Ads

Jake calls Thomas Petit "the distribution mechanic". Petit is an independent growth advisor who has consulted for 20+ apps including Deezer and Babbel, and won the 2022 App Growth Award for contribution to the community.

- Jake values him as someone who tells hard truths others won't.
- Example Jake cites: even when you target hundreds of keywords in Apple Search Ads, most of your downloads come from just five to ten of them.
- Jake's pointer: if App Store Optimization and paid search are your bottleneck, Petit is the operator to study.

## Josh Mohrer: solo-building an AI app with AI tools

Jake describes Josh Mohrer as "the zero-to-code solo founder". Mohrer helped build Uber's New York operation from zero to billions in revenue. He then taught himself to code using ChatGPT and solo-built Wave AI to $4M ARR in eight months, with no employees or co-founder.

- Jake sees him as living proof of the new "solopreneur" reality, where one person with AI tools and business sense can build a real company.
- Jake says to follow him for the playbook on shipping an AI app with a tiny team.

## Sylvain Gauchet: onboarding and early user experience

Jake calls Sylvain Gauchet "the insight miner". Gauchet runs growth at Babbel and distills tactical insights from hundreds of growth podcasts, webinars and talks into his Growth Gems newsletter.

- His specialty is onboarding and the early user experience, which Jake calls "the exact moments that determine whether someone churns or becomes a paying subscriber."
- Jake's framing: he is a curated filter that turns thousands of hours of content into "the 5% that actually moves revenue."
