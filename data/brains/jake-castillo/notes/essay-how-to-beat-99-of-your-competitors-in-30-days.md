## Critical thinking is the one skill AI can't replace, and Jake treats it as something to practice and protect

- Jake describes AI as probably the greatest technological advancement in human history, and says he is "as AI-pilled as they come."
- He also says using it has made him "dumber to a degree." It can give a feeling of being more productive while he wastes time prompting AI for something he could have "cranked out in 20 mins myself."
- His main takeaway: "you need to practice and protect your ability to think critically at all costs."
- He argues that having original thoughts and articulating them intelligently may be the most important skill to master going forward.
- Evidence he points to for the value of human thinking: people still buy physical books, go to TED talks, and pay financial advisors.
- He notes that as everyone offloads thinking to chatbots, it has never been easier to stand out in the traditional job market.

## Beating competitors in any sector with one month of deep learning

- Jake claims you can use AI to your advantage to beat out competition "in ANY sector in just 30 days," though "maybe not the way you're expecting."
- The edge is not more AI output. It is that most people are offloading their thinking, so one person who locks in for a month of real, intense learning can pull ahead.
- This applies to any high-income skill: understanding distribution for apps, email marketing, creating ads, or even learning guitar.
- He expects you to be bad at first and says that is the point: "You're going to suck at it at first, but that's the point."

## The First 20 Hours: why 10,000 hours is not required

- Jake recommends *The First 20 Hours* by Josh Kaufman for standing out in any crowded market (he says it is not sponsored, and that reading summaries is fine).
- Kaufman's main thesis, as Jake relays it, challenges the idea that you need 10,000 hours to truly master a skill.
- Jake's takeaway is that a short, focused block of effort can "catapult yourself into a whole new realm of possibilities."

## "Where you fear is, there your task is": using fear as a signal for what to work on

- Jake quotes Carl Jung: "Where you fear is, there your task is."
- He applies it when stuck on a task or dreading trying something new, knowing he will be bad at it.
- Example he cites: Michael Smoak (HigherUp Wellness on Instagram) was terrified of public speaking and making social media content about 3 years ago, so he forced himself into a 30-day challenge of recording one 60-second video per day.
- Three years later, Smoak has a multi-million-follower audience and has inspired over 100,000 people to complete the same 30-day public speaking challenge.

## Jake's 30-day challenge: the step-by-step protocol

1. Pick up Josh Kaufman's book, or read summaries of it.
2. Write down what has been stressing you out or making you anxious over the last 30 days.
3. Lock yourself in a room for an hour every day for a month and "DO THE WORK."
4. Don't offload all your critical thinking to AI.

- He asks readers to email him after 30 days with what they were struggling with, what they did to overcome it, and where they are now.
- The core mechanic is a small, fixed daily time block (one hour, or one 60-second video in Smoak's case) aimed at the specific thing that scares or stresses you, repeated for 30 days.
