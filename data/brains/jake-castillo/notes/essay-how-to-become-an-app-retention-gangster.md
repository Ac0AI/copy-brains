## Ecom brands are the best source of app retention ideas

Jake argues app founders sit in an echo chamber of other app founders on X and LinkedIn, especially on retention. He says the most aggressive retention playbooks are in ecommerce, and apps should copy them and adapt the principles.

- Ecom is "a knife fight": CPMs and CAC keep climbing and every brand sells a version of the same product, so every funnel step matters.
- He expects apps to head to the same place: paid acquisition gets too expensive to waste a single customer.
- He quotes "Good artists copy, great artists steal" and says there is a lot worth stealing in ecom.
- Study how big ecom brands run their emails after you buy from them: welcome flow, winback flow, upsell and cross-sell emails.
- His method: follow a few DTC operators, sign up for their subscriptions, then try to cancel and screenshot every step. He says this yields more retention ideas than hours on YouTube.

## AG1: make the subscription the default

Jake cites a YouTube video about changes AG1 made to its main product page that raised LTV and average order value.

- The only option above the fold is the subscription button, so it looks like the only way to buy.
- The one-time purchase option appears only if you scroll down.
- His app translation: make the annual plan the hero of the paywall.
- He asks founders: is the annual plan the obvious default, or are you showing three plans side by side and "asking a stranger to do math"?

## Olipop: treat subscription as a later stage in the relationship

Jake describes Eli Weiss (@eliweisss), who ran CX and retention at Olipop and Jones Road, as taking the opposite approach to AG1.

- Olipop doesn't default anyone into a subscription and doesn't market it to first-time buyers.
- Eli treats the subscription as a stage the customer opts into once they already love the product.
- His rule of thumb, as Jake relays it: remove the friction first, then add flexibility (skip a delivery, swap a flavor, change the cadence, anything that keeps someone from hitting cancel).
- Jake says Eli grew Olipop's subscriptions 3x in a year this way.
- Jake's conclusion on AG1 vs Olipop: both are right. "AG1 wins the first sale. Olipop wins the hundredth." The move is stealing insights from both.

## Generation vs preservation, and cutting churn

Jake cites Nick Shackelford (@iamshackelford), co-founder of BREZ and Structured, who has managed hundreds of millions in ad spend.

- Nick's team reduced BREZ's subscription churn from 24% to 17% and is chasing sub-5%.
- He splits the business into two buckets: generation (ads and content) and preservation (email, community, and keeping the customers you already paid for).
- Jake's observation: most app founders he talks to have a full team on generation and "one intern fumbling around" on keeping customers from leaving.

## Translating ecom tactics into app versions

Jake says ecom tactics don't translate 1:1 (you can't, or shouldn't, ship a free t-shirt for subscribing to a single-use utility app, and you can't put a "subscribe and save 20%" badge on an App Store listing). The work is to take the principle and find the app version.

- Default the subscription becomes: make the annual plan the hero of your paywall.
- Reorder reminder (for a product that lasts 30 days) becomes: a push notification right before the habit breaks.
- Cancellation save offer becomes: "pause instead of cancel" in the manage-subscription flow.
- Unboxing experience becomes: the first 10 seconds in the app.
- Also borrow from ecom email: welcome, winback, upsell and cross-sell flows from brands you've bought from.
