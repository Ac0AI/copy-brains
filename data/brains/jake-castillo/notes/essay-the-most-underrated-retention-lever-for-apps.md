## Email is the retention lever most app founders ignore

Jake argues that app founders pour effort into ads, creators, App Store conversion, onboarding and the paywall, then stop marketing the moment someone downloads. The only emails many users ever get are billing updates and password resets. He calls this "actually insane" and says email gives another avenue to deliver value, educate customers, create upsells and bring people back. After consulting on over 100 apps, he says most have no system for winning back customers or preventing them from leaving.

## Plan around 15% annual retention for consumer apps

- Jake's working benchmark for consumer apps is 15% annual retention.
- Even a viable app therefore has a large group of people who will eventually stop using it or fail to renew.
- Without a retention or winback system, founders let the relationship deteriorate, then spend more money and time acquiring new customers just to maintain MRR.

## The product is still the biggest retention lever

- "Email cannot save a product people don't want."
- Before building a "37-email automation monster", fix the product.
- Look at the behaviors retained users repeat, find the feature they use most, then make that experience faster, easier and more valuable.
- Emails should pull people back toward a strong product, not "drag or beg" them to keep using a weak one.
- Once the product foundation exists, the email strategy can stay simple.

## The 2:1 lifecycle email ratio

Jake ran an email marketing agency and usually recommends this starting ratio:

- Two emails that add value.
- One email that is offer based.

**Value emails** should help the customer make progress toward the outcome they downloaded the app to achieve. Ways to do that:
- Teach them something useful.
- Show them a new feature or highlight additional resources.
- Give them a reason to return today instead of vaguely planning to come back later.

**The offer email** is for an offer, an upgrade or a relevant upsell.

## Monetization does not end at the first paywall

- Many apps think monetization begins and ends at the first paywall and leave money on the table.
- A customer who already understands the product is often the most logical person to see the next useful offer.
- Keep upsells relevant to what the customer already uses.

## Winback: lapsed users did not necessarily hate the product

- Not every lapsed person disliked the app. People get busy, priorities change, or they forget why they downloaded it.
- A useful email lets you re-enter the conversation without buying that person's attention again through ads.
- If the answer to "what happens after someone stops using the app?" is "nothing", the app has no winback system at all.

## Weekly lifecycle email audit checklist

Jake suggests auditing lifecycle email this week by answering:
- What does a new customer receive after signup?
- How are you helping active users get more value?
- Are you educating people about useful features?
- Do you have a relevant next offer for your best customers?
- What happens after someone stops using the app?

His summary: "Acquisition gets someone through the door, your product gives them a reason to stay. And email gives you another chance when life pulls them away."
