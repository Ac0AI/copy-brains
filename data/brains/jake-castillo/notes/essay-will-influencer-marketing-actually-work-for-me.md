## Influencer marketing didn't stop working, it stopped being cheap

- Jake still says yes to "does influencer marketing work?", but the real question is whether you can still get a good deal on it. His answer as of August 2026 is "mostly no".
- Creator quality didn't drop. RPMs didn't drop either. The revenue side looks the same as when he was signing creator deals in 2024-25.
- What changed is CPMs, which "jumped waaaay up".
- His explanation: people saw profitable brands scaling with influencers (including his own operation) and decided influencer marketing was the move. Everyone now bids for the same creators, and the creators have caught on.
- His takeaway, in his words: "Influencer marketing didn't stop working. It just stopped being cheap."

## How creator pricing shifted: from mid-tier sweet spot to everyone overpriced

- The old market had two distortions:
  - Giant brands overpaid at the top.
  - Small influencers overcharged companies with no influencer deal experience.
  - So the best viable option was mid-tier creators.
- Now every influencer is overpriced in general, including the "bottom of the barrel" creators. Jake says this is "killing margins like crazy".
- Concrete benchmark: his team retained 300+ creators at one time two years ago. He says they would not have been able to do that under today's pricing structure.
- He has an X thread on how to price influencer deals (linked in the post) for anyone who still wants to run them.

## Why Jake is looking hard at UGC right now

- Three main distribution channels for consumer apps, as he frames them: paid ads, influencer deals, and UGC.
- UGC "hasn't been exhausted YET" and isn't overpriced yet.
- He compares UGC today to where influencer marketing sat a couple of years ago, "right before everyone started trying to copy the Cal AI playbook".
- He frames this as a window of opportunity that is likely to close as more people pile in.
- He points to an X article by @jammer3k on running a UGC program the right way (he notes it is not sponsored or affiliated).

## Sourcing UGC creators: SideShift and Trybe

The hardest part of UGC is sourcing high quality creators. Jake's recommendations (he states he is not sponsored on either):

- **SideShift** for mass UGC campaigns
  - Has an abundance of creators.
  - Faster than a VA scrolling TikTok and Instagram all day.
- **Trybe** if you mainly run paid ads
  - Creators make the content with nothing paid upfront.
  - You run it as an ad, and the creator takes a percentage of the spend on the video they made.
  - You pay out only on performance.
- **Staffing**: one VA doing manual outreach on TikTok and Instagram (what Jake did himself), plus one VA searching SideShift or Trybe, gives the best odds of finding creators to work with.

## Q4 paid ads: don't try to outbid anyone

- Meta and TikTok CPMs climb "through the roof" in Q4 because of Black Friday and Christmas. Every e-commerce brand fights over sales to finish the year strong.
- His advice for consumer apps: don't try to outbid anybody.
- Accept early that you won't scale insanely fast or profitably on paid in Q4, and move that time and energy into organic channels.
- This Q4 effect is a second reason, alongside the bloated influencer market, to prioritize UGC now.

## When UGC will not work: check your fit and funnel first

Jake warns that you can source great creators, get the content made, and still wrongly blame the creators or format when it doesn't convert. UGC will fail if:

- You don't have solid marketing angles and scripts.
- Your product is hard to understand in 2-5 seconds.
- Your budget is too small to test enough before one of your videos goes viral.
- Your entire funnel isn't congruent.
- Your onboarding isn't optimized.
- Your paywall isn't optimized.

His verdict on those cases: "that's on you". He has a separate X article on onboarding and paywall optimization (linked in the post) for anyone who needs help there.
