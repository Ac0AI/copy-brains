## Why creator tier decides influencer ROI

Jake says influencer marketing lives or dies on two tasks done well: picking the right niche, and identifying creators in that niche whose audiences are "primed for conversion." Fail at either and a brand burns budget, then claims the channel "doesn't work for them." His cheat sheet ranks creator tiers so operators can research the right partners first and avoid spreading cash in every direction "just to see what sticks." His ranking comes from evaluating over 10,000 influencers and signing too many creators that flopped.

## S tier: mid-level creators are where the math works

- Big enough for real reach, small enough that your deal still matters to them.
- Pricing stays reasonable because nobody fights over them: giant brands chase celebrities and small brands chase micro-influencers.
- Their audience still sees them as relatable, so the trust being paid for is intact.
- At Cal AI, the most profitable deals usually came from this level, and the winners paid for every deal that didn't hit.
- Caveat: you still have to run enough deals in this category to find your winning formula.

## A tier: micro-influencers are a volume play

- A creator with 30,000 followers feels like a friend to their audience, so a plug lands as a recommendation rather than an ad.
- They are cheaper and still convert, but no single micro-influencer deal will blow up downloads.
- The tier only works with a pipeline that signs and manages dozens of creators without the operator spending hours on logistics.

## B tier: nano-influencers are risky because per-deal work is fixed

- The work to close a deal is the same whether the creator has 7,000 or 300,000 followers: outreach, negotiation, contracts, reviews, and making sure posts go live.
- At nano scale, deals may only break even or leave you in a small hole.
- A cult-like audience can still lose money, so pick carefully.
- His rule: if you can't get this tier down to near-zero effort per deal, skip it.

## C tier: big creators charge for reach, and reach alone rarely converts

- Audiences know big creators do brand deals, so trust erodes as the paid partnership becomes visible.
- You pay premium prices for reach, which usually doesn't convert on its own.
- Jake's team broke this rule twice at Cal AI on very large bets that paid off, but he says that is not the norm.
- Signing someone at this level gives a "dopamine hit" but is often "an expensive way to feel important."

## F tier: celebrities have no borrowed trust left

- Jake's framing: "The entire product in influencer marketing is borrowed trust."
- Everyone knows a celebrity post is an ad, and their audience follows them for entertainment, not for recommendations on improving their lives.
- You pay the most where the key lever, real trust, is absent.

## Checklist before wiring money

- Know what you are paying for and whether it fits your current goals and revenue.
- Mid-tier creators worked best for Cal AI, but you still have to evaluate the quality of each influencer's audience yourself.
- Plan for volume: run enough mid-tier and micro deals to find the winners, and build a low-effort process for contracts, reviews and post tracking.
