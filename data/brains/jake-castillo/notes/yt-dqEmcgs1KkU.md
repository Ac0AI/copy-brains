## Zero to $10K/month with UGC creators: the overall playbook

- Jake Castillo co-founded Cal AI and served as CMO. Cal AI launched in May 2024, scaled to 15 million users in under 18 months, and was acquired by MyFitnessPal.
- His core claim is that an app can go from zero to $10K a month using UGC alone, without raising money or spending heavily on paid ads.
- Cal AI's primary growth channel was influencer marketing, and a big part of his job was "figuring out how to turn creators into subscriptions."
- The five-step system is: find creators, warm up accounts, pick a content format, analyze economics, then cut and scale.
- His closing advice: "it's not easy, but it is simple." No genius, technical depth or funding is needed. What matters is doing the boring work repeatedly: source creators, test content, measure, cut what fails, double down on what works, and keep at it "longer and with more discipline than everyone else."

## Why UGC creators instead of big influencers, and how to find them

- His principle: "good outputs start with great inputs, and creators are your inputs." The best scripts and app can't fix a delivery person who isn't compelling.
- Influencer marketing is more competitive because AI has increased the number of apps launched, so more companies chase the same influencers. Paying large influencers is expensive for a small startup or solo founder.
- A UGC creator is an everyday person who creates a brand new account dedicated to your product. What you pay for is reach.
- Where to find them:
  - Post a job on platforms like SideShift and get hundreds of applicants overnight.
  - Search "UGC creator" directly on TikTok and Instagram.
- Filter for talking-head content, because it converts better and feels more trustworthy.
- Signals of a good creator:
  - Natural camera presence, charisma and good energy.
  - Feels like they're FaceTiming a friend rather than reading a script.
  - At least one talking-head video with a million views ("they got the juice").
- Pricing structure:
  - Start with a small test: about 10 test videos at $10 each.
  - If the videos are good, move to a full campaign: 30 videos a month at $30 per video.
  - Add bonuses at view milestones such as 100k, 500k and 1 million views.
- UGC creators usually come to you, so respond quickly. For negotiating, he advises getting on a call as soon as possible, because "it is much easier to win a negotiation over a call than over email."

## Warming up creator accounts so they don't get shadowbanned

- Have each creator make a brand new account dedicated specifically to your app.
- Warm-up routine:
  - About 30 minutes a day watching content in the niche they'll post in.
  - Liking videos, commenting and engaging like a normal user.
  - Publishing a few videos in the niche without mentioning your brand.
- Reason: TikTok and Instagram know UGC creators make branded content that amounts to free advertisement. If they identify an account as a creator account, they shadowban it, keep it out of the algorithm and prevent real views.
- Only once a video breaks 1,000 views should the creator begin posting branded content.
- The warm-up also works as a test of whether the creator is willing to follow your process.

## Choosing a content format: magic moment vs. painful problem

- He calls content format "undoubtedly the most important part of the entire process" because it determines installs. He uses a simple decision tree.
- **Question 1: does the app have a magic moment?** This is a feature so visually impressive that viewers do a double take on seeing it on screen. Cal AI is his example.
  - If yes, maximize views. The creator makes the most viral story possible, finds a natural point to integrate the app, demonstrates the magic moment, and lets the product sell itself.
  - These videos get more views and a lower conversion rate. The extra reach should offset the lower conversion.
- **Question 2 (no magic moment): does the app solve a painful problem?**
  - If yes, maximize intent. Spend the start of the video making the problem feel real, so the viewer thinks "oh my god, like me," then introduce the app as the solution.
  - These videos get fewer views but convert much better.
  - If no, he would "seriously reconsider" the product or its positioning.
- He credits co-founder Blake Anderson with the idea of starting from the marketing angle first and building the product backwards from the viral content idea.
- On creative control: he lets creators cook within their domain of expertise. He gives general guidelines rather than dedicated scripts, because "I'm not a great creator, but I recognize that."

## Measuring whether creator content makes money: CPM, installs and RPM

Jake's worked example:
- Pay $900 for 30 videos that get 600k views, which is a $1.50 CPM.
- At 2 installs per 1,000 views, 600k views yields 1.2k installs.
- At a 10% install-to-paid conversion rate ("pretty standard"), that is about 120 paying users.
- At a $20 average order value, that is $2.4k in revenue from $900 of spend.
- Revenue per 1,000 views (RPM) is $4. Paying a $1.50 CPM to earn a $4 RPM means "I'm ready to scale."

Realism check:
- 30 videos a month at $30 each is $900 of spend, which most creators will accept.
- Reaching 600k views usually needs only one or two low-level viral videos, not even a million-view video.
- Two installs per 1,000 views should be the bare minimum. Below that, there is a content problem or an app problem.
- UGC is "not for the faint of heart." Accounts have no following, so 20 videos might all land under 5K views before the 21st gets 200K.

## Reviewing creators: assume you'll cut everyone

- He starts the analysis assuming he will cut every creator, then requires each creator's data to give a reason to renew them.
- Three things he checks:
  1. **Economics:** did the revenue from their views justify what was paid for those views?
  2. **Intent:** read the comment sections for people asking "what app is that?" or tagging friends ("you need to see this"). This shows real interest in the product.
  3. **Correlation:** cross-reference viral videos with App Store Connect data to see whether a spike in views matched a spike in downloads.
- These checks separate vanity metrics from real business results.
- Ideally, for every creator cut, bring in two replacements.
- He frames this as a different hat from building: growth economics and analysis, with constant testing of creators and content types.

## Scaling the creator program and what revenue to expect

- Scaling path: from 5 creators to 100, 200 and 300.
- At 5 to 10 creators you can manage it yourself. Around 50 active creators, hire a dedicated creator manager, at roughly one manager per 50 active creators.
- By that point the content format and frameworks are in place, and the hard part is organization. Many tools exist for managing creators, and he says to find a system that works for you and double down on it.
- Rough revenue by program size:
  - 5 to 10 creators: about $10K a month.
  - About 50 creators: roughly $100K to $200K a month.
  - 100 to 300 creators: can reach the millions.
- Returns scale asymmetrically "because that's how virality works."
- He shares more tactical detail in his newsletter, covering topics like onboarding, creating a good viral video, signing a first creator and reviewing creators.
