## Indie hacking's 2016-2023 cycle and why it "died"

- Pieter says indie hacking got big around 2016 and, on his 7-year cycle theory, was due to die by 2023. The post is headlined "R.I.P. Indie hacking, 2016 - 2023".
- His reading of the "death" is not failure. Indie hacking became "part of the normal, the status quo".
- Its principles are now something "any startup founder's mindset" will "at least consider".

## Indie hacking as the successor to the Lean Startup

- Pieter argues indie hacking has "replaced the Lean Startup of 2010", as an update to it.
- The key change is skipping customer interviews. Founders go "straight to lean prototyping".

## The indie hacker playbook, as Pieter summarizes it

- Launch fast.
- "Throw stuff at the wall and see what sticks."
- "Nuke it if it doesn't work" and go on to the next project.
- Broadcast the journey publicly and use that as marketing.

## Distribution through building in public

- In this model, public broadcasting of the journey is the marketing channel. There is no separate marketing budget or campaign.
- Each project launched and shut down publicly feeds an audience that carries over to the next project.

## The next generation of founders

- Pieter says new Gen Z founders are "indie hackers and nomads by default".
- They are also open to raising funding later, so indie hacking and venture funding are no longer opposites.
- Bootstrapping first and raising later has become a normal path rather than a rejection of funding.
