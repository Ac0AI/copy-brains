## How VC funding dilutes founder ownership by round

Pieter's core point: raising money is not free. "People don't give you money out of charity, they buy a little slice of your company every time they invest." Each round sells a share of ownership in exchange for cash, in the hope that the founder's smaller slice becomes worth more than the ownership given away.

He argues ~5% is probably the most common final stake for VC-funded founders when things work out, especially with a co-founder.

## Median founder ownership by round (data via Carta)

**Single founder:**
- Seed: ~56%
- Series A: ~36%
- Series B: ~23.5%
- Series C: ~16.5%
- Series D: ~10%
- Series E: <10%

**One co-founder (most startups):**
- Seed: ~28%
- Series A: ~18%
- Series B: ~11.75%
- Series C: ~8.25%
- Series D: ~5%
- Series E: <5%

**One of 4 co-founders:**
- Seed: ~14%
- Series A: ~9%
- Series B: ~5.875%
- Series C: ~4.125%
- Series D: ~2.5%
- Series E: <2.5%

## Payout at a $1 billion acquisition

Pieter runs the same stakes against a $1B exit ("$1 billion is a lot!").

- **Single founder:** Seed ~$560M, Series A ~$360M, Series B ~$235M, Series C ~$165M, Series D ~$100M, Series E <$100M.
- **One co-founder:** Seed ~$280M, Series A ~$180M, Series B ~$117.5M, Series C ~$82.5M, Series D ~$50M, Series E <$50M.
- **One of 4 co-founders:** Seed ~$140M, Series A ~$90M, Series B ~$59M, Series C ~$41M, Series D ~$25M, Series E <$25M.

## Payout at the realistic median acquisition (~$71M)

The median acquisition value for a VC-backed startup sits at approximately $71 million.

- **Single founder:** Seed ~$39.8M, Series A ~$25.6M, Series B ~$16.7M, Series C ~$11.7M, Series D ~$7.1M, Series E <$7.1M.
- **One co-founder:** Seed ~$19.9M, Series A ~$12.8M, Series B ~$8.3M, Series C ~$5.9M, Series D ~$3.55M, Series E <$3.55M.
- **One of 4 co-founders:** Seed ~$9.95M, Series A ~$6.4M, Series B ~$4.2M, Series C ~$2.9M, Series D ~$1.8M, Series E <$1.8M.

## Median expected outcome, with acquisition probability included

Two inputs: the median acquisition happens around Series A (quite early), and the median acquisition value is $71M. That gives the median outcome for a startup that does get acquired:

- Single founder: ~$25.6M
- One co-founder: ~$12.8M
- One of 4 co-founders: ~$6.4M

But "most startups are by definition doomed to fail," and only ~15% ever get acquired. Weighting by that probability:

- Single founder: ~$3.84M
- One of two co-founders: ~$1.92M
- One of four co-founders: ~$960,000

## Caveats and how Pieter frames it

- The numbers ignore taxes and liquidation preferences. Investors may be paid their preference before common shareholders receive anything, so founders would get even less.
- They also ignore founders taking money off the table in earlier rounds. Pieter says the two omissions "balance each other out a bit."
- He is not calling VC bad: "it's just how the VC game works but good to write it out and be aware of how it works."
- VC-backed startups "shoot for the moon." It is one of the few ways to get a crazy big payout and become an actual billionaire, which he says is very rare for a bootstrapped founder using their own money.
