## Core principle: start with your own problem and ship ugly fast

- Pieter says to "pick a problem you personally have." His test: "if you don't use your own product daily, quit now."
- He skips the pitch deck. He opens the code editor and ships "something ugly in a weekend."
- He argues the VC game is built to make founders think they need permission to start. His view: "you don't."

## Monetization: charge from day 1

- He says to charge money from day 1.
- His reason: "free users give you nothing but support tickets."
- Paying customers are the validation. Free users don't give a meaningful signal.

## Tech stack and hosting: boring and cheap

- He recommends boring tech: PHP, SQLite, vanilla JS.
- He calls frameworks "a trap that mass waste your time."
- He hosts on a cheap VPS at $5-20/mo, not AWS.
- His reasoning: "you don't need kubernetes for 1,000 users."

## Operations: do support yourself, automate the rest

- He does customer support himself because "it's the fastest product feedback loop that exists."
- He automates "everything you do more than twice."
- His rule: "cron jobs > employees."

## Distribution: build in public on X

- He grows on Twitter/X by building in public.
- His framing: "your journey IS the marketing."
- The essay itself reached 646,370 views and 12,660 likes on X, an example of the channel he describes.

## Financial independence: near-zero burn, no equity deals

- He keeps burn rate near zero so he never needs to raise. His phrase: "ramen profitable > series A."
- He says no to investors, cofounders and "advisors" who want equity for intros.
- He reports 10+ years of working this way with no employees, no funding and no board meetings.
