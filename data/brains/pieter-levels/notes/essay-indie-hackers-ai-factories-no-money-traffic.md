## Pieter's observation: indie hackers over-build and under-distribute

- Pieter shares a friend's report from an indie hacker meetup in June 2026. Almost everyone there was "super focused on development."
- They built "whole spaceships" that generate code, review it and produce reports and analytics.
- One attendee built an entire "factory": a list of ideas, with agents generating the landing page, the SaaS and the analytics, all pulled into one dashboard.
- The builders optimized all of it heavily and were comfortable doing so.

## The core problem: no money and no traffic

- The friend's key finding, which Pieter amplifies: "almost none of them have money or traffic."
- Nobody at the meetup knew where to get either one.
- Building is the comfortable part, so people keep polishing the system instead of facing marketing.
- The result is a lot of output with no audience and no revenue.

## Procrastination pattern: "marketing later, after my system is done"

- A common line from these builders: "yeah, I should probably do marketing, but first I'll finish my super system and then I'll start."
- The marketing plan is often just another automation project, such as building an agent that posts to Instagram automatically.
- This repeats the old trap in a new form. Marketing gets postponed behind more tooling, and tooling becomes a way to avoid talking to customers.

## AI makes the old programmer mistake worse

- Pieter and his friend compare this to the classic programmer, who spent a year writing code and tests and preparing for scale "in the basement" without showing anything to anyone.
- Their view is that it is now worse: "the amount of useless aislop nobody needs has grown massively."
- Because AI lets anyone build, building is no longer the differentiator.

## Takeaway: distribution is the real challenge

- Pieter links this to his related post "Everyone can now build apps with AI so now distribution is the real challenge."
- The principle: when building is cheap, the scarce resource is traffic and paying customers, not code or automation infrastructure.
- Automated idea-to-SaaS pipelines, code-review systems and dashboards do not solve the question of where users and money come from.
- Priority should go to showing the product to people and finding a source of traffic and revenue, rather than perfecting internal systems first.
