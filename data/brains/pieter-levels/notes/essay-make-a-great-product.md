## Great products push themselves; marketing alone has limits

- Pieter's core claim: "Make a great product. Chances are it will push itself before you even thought about marketing it."
- Marketing and putting a product on the market yourself "can get you far, but an absolutely great product pushes itself."
- He applies this beyond music, to products in general.
- Self-assessment of his own album: it was a great product, "but it could be a lot better." He believes that if he had put the time he spent on marketing and promotion into making the music better, it would have gone much further.
- You can market something to success, but "you can not force people to like" it.

## What the 2008 drum & bass album project involved

Pieter released a 12-track drum & bass album in 2008 and did everything himself:

- Music, mixing and mastering.
- Artwork.
- Writing the payment processing system.
- All promotion, plus folding posters.
- Becoming a publisher to collect royalties.
- Fulfilment: putting CDs into envelopes and cycling to the post office every week to send new orders.
- Result: he sold the entire batch of 1000 CDs and 2000 posters, all over the internet.
- He had an amazing time and calls independent release "a crazy experience."
- The campaign site was retakemanhattan.com, with a tongue-in-cheek pitch that the Dutch would "retake Manhattan" (New Amsterdam became New York).
- The project spun off into a very popular YouTube channel, the Panda Mix Show.

## You can't do everything alone, even when you can

- Pieter's lesson: "to be successful in music, you cannot do everything on your own. And even if you can, you don't want to."
- You will always need others, such as tastemakers and big DJs who push your name. The internet has not changed that.
- He argues he could have benefited from earning the respect of those people before releasing his own album. He did some of this, but not enough.

## Distribution comes from tastemakers, not labels

His advice to musicians:

- Be stubborn and do your own thing.
- Don't rely on labels to push you.
- Do rely on tastemakers, such as DJs or niche YouTube channels, to push you.
- People want to see you pushed up by significant people in a genre's scene before you go your own way.
- Significant people will find you when you make good music: "They will."

## Get good before you launch independently

- Don't release your own work independently too early.
- Get really good at the craft first.
- "Great music is like valuable information, it wants to be free and it will at some point."
- Priority order he implies: quality first, then endorsement from respected people in the niche, then self-release and marketing.
