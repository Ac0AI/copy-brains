## Building in the dark produces products nobody wants

Pieter's core claim, posted on 13 October 2021, is that "Building a product in the dark without letting people use it early on is the best way to make something nobody wants."

- The failure mode is building privately, with no real users touching the product in the early stages.
- He frames this as the most reliable route to a product with no demand, not as one risk among many.
- The post is tagged Entrepreneurship, Analysis and Creativity on levels.io.

## Early usage is the safeguard

The inverse of the claim is the practical rule: let people use the product early.

- Pieter's wording is "letting people use it early on", so the emphasis is on actual use, not just showing the product or collecting opinions.
- Early use is what separates a product people want from one built in isolation.
- The word "early" matters. The problem is delaying contact with users until the product is already built.

## Who this applies to

The post is addressed to anyone building a startup or product, including solo makers. Pieter links his book MAKE, about building startups without funding, as the broader context for this advice.

- The principle is general: it is not tied to one product type or channel.
- The source gives no specific tactics, numbers or examples for how to release early. It states the principle only.
