## Don't quit until side project revenue beats your job for 6 months

- Pieter says many people quit their job and go full time on a side project, and he wouldn't recommend it.
- His alternative is a two-step rule:
  - Build the side project while you have stable income from a job.
  - Consider quitting only when the side project's revenue has surpassed your job's income for 6 months.
- The core principle is to keep a stable salary while the project is still unproven, and let revenue, not enthusiasm, trigger the decision to go full time.

## The revenue trigger: exceed job income, sustained for 6 months

- The threshold is relative to the job: side project revenue must exceed what the job pays, not merely become "meaningful".
- The 6-month duration matters. A single strong month does not count; the revenue has to stay above job income for a full 6 months before quitting is even worth considering.
- Even at that point he only says to "consider quitting", not to quit automatically.

## Why he builds while employed rather than going all-in

- Pieter advises building the project "while you have stable income from a job", which keeps financial pressure low while you test whether the product works.
- This fits his wider bootstrapping approach, described in his book MAKE, of building startups without funding.
