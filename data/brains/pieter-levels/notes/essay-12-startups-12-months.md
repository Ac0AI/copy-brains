## Why Pieter set the 12 startups in 12 months challenge

- Pieter took inspiration from Jennifer Dewalt's "180 websites in 180 days". Unlike her, he could already build, so his goal was to practice finishing and launching.
- Goal (March 2014): launch one startup every month for 12 months. Each month covers picking an idea, developing it, and launching it to press.
- He described it as "a tiny personal hyperfast incubator" meant to change his habits and force him to finish what he starts.
- His expectation: after 12 months he would have more to show for his name.

## The two problems: finishing and launching

- **Finishing:** creatives (musicians, writers, developers) are perfectionists, and projects are never "just done". There is always one more feature or chapter. Near the finish line they drop the project and move to something new, because "we like the feeling of starting something 'new', we despise the feeling of finishing something 'old'."
- **Launching:** fear of failure keeps builders from launching. They hold a perfect fantasy of press coverage and signups that reality will not match, so they under-launch or never launch. His reasoning: "what if anybody would actually use what we built? They might hate it!"
- **Result:** a vicious cycle of no progress. He had seen startups with great ideas and long hours still sitting on the same landing page collecting beta emails a year later.
- He had suffered from both problems himself for years, and he saw them killing good ideas in others too.

## Why small projects count as startups

- Pieter argues a startup does not have to be a "world changing high impact $1B+ company from the start". He calls that a myth.
- Examples he cites: Airbnb started as a company selling Obama-themed cereal. Dropbox started as Drew Houston building a graphical user interface for rsync as a side project. The big vision came after they found market fit with their MVPs.
- Waiting for a big vision while staying idle is the wrong approach. Doing something puts you ahead of most people, and "you'll probably do the wrong thing. But that's not the point."
- You learn by exposing yourself to the world and its market forces: "By doing nothing, you figure out exactly nothing."
- Especially if you are inexperienced, start small first and slowly build bigger things.

## His definition of a startup and the one-month test

- He combined Eric Ries's definition (a human institution delivering a new product or service under extreme uncertainty) with Paul Graham's (a company designed to grow fast, where growth is the only essential thing).
- His minified definition: "A startup delivers a new product and grows it fast."
- Delivering a new product is the easy part; growing it fast is the hard part. It gets tougher if there is no market fit after the MVP.
- His rule: see whether a project gets market fit within one month. If it shows no signs of being somewhat in demand, he considers it failed and moves on.

## What he shipped as the progress report

His list of projects at the time of writing:

1. **Play My Inbox:** collects music from people's inboxes and playlists it in a visual music player.
2. **Go Fucking Do It:** forces you to reach goals by a deadline, and charges your card if you do not.
3. **Tubelytics:** a real-time analytics dashboard SaaS for YouTube publishers and media brands, tracking hundreds of channels, videos and networks.
4. **NomadList:** a live index of the best cities to live and work remotely, scored on cost of living, weather, internet speed and crowdsourced metrics. It sells city guide books and nomad kits (coworking day pass, SIM card, hotel stay).
5. **NomadJobs:** a job board for remote workers, featuring only 100% distributed startups such as Buffer, Zapier, Sqwiggle and Automattic.
6. **GifBook:** upload animated GIFs and order printed flip books made from them.
7. **#nomads:** a chat community for digital nomads. It had over 2,000 members, meet-ups from Berlin to Bangkok, and a Product Hunt feature.
8. **Remote | OK:** a daily aggregator of remote jobs. It does not accept job postings and is not curated. It aims to win on volume, indexing jobs from other boards and using sentiment analysis to decide whether they are remote. It reached #1 on Product Hunt.
