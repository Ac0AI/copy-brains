## Relationships and a small circle

- Sam says never to put family, friends or a significant other low on the priority list. He prefers "a handful of truly close friends to a hundred acquaintances".
- He advises keeping up with old friends, having parties, and occasionally staying up until sunrise talking to people.
- Tell parents you love them more often and visit home as often as you can.
- Don't burn bridges or screw people, pick battles carefully, forgive people, and don't judge others too quickly because "you never know their whole story".
- Go out of your way to help people and be nice to strangers, "even when it doesn't matter".
- Don't maintain relationships with people you don't like, and cut negative people out. "Negativity is really bad."

## How to succeed

- Sam's formula: pick the right thing to do ("critical and usually ignored"), focus, believe in yourself (especially when others say it won't work), develop personal connections with people who will help you, learn to identify talented people, and work hard.
- Picking what to work on is hard because "original thought is hard".
- He quotes Paul Graham: "People can become formidable, but it's hard to predict who". Don't let yourself get pushed around. Aim to be confident, not arrogant.
- Be a doer, not a talker. Don't chase status, because status without substance doesn't last and is unfulfilling.
- Ask for what you want.

## Work and career

- It is difficult to do a great job on work you don't care about, and hard to be fulfilled if you dislike your work.
- Work very hard, though a surprising number of people will be offended by it, but not so hard that the rest of life passes you by.
- Aim to be the best in the world at what you do professionally. "Even if you miss, you'll probably end up in a pretty good place."
- Build your own productivity system. Don't waste time being unorganized or working at suboptimal times.
- Take career risks, especially early. Most people pick a career fairly randomly, so think hard about what you like, which fields will succeed, and talk to people in those fields.
- Don't waste time. "Most people waste most of their time, especially in business."
- Minimize cognitive load from distractions that don't matter. He says it is hard to overstate the importance, and most people are bad at it. Build strong ways to stop unwanted tasks piling up and eating mental cycles at work.

## Goals, planning and opportunities

- Have clear goals for every day, year and decade.
- Planning is valuable, but if a great opportunity comes along, take it. Don't be afraid to do something slightly reckless.
- Working hard makes good opportunities show up, but it is still up to you to jump on them.
- Think for a few seconds before acting, and for a few minutes if angry.

## Risk, regret and doing new things

- Most people are too risk-averse, so most advice is biased toward conservative paths. "Things in life are rarely as risky as they seem."
- If you think you'll regret not doing something, probably do it. Most people regret what they didn't do far more than what they did.
- Do new things often. Sam says it seems to slow the perception of time, raise happiness and stop people calcifying in how they think.
- Aim to do "something big, new, and risky every year" in both personal and professional life.
- Learn voraciously.

## Surrounding yourself with the right people

- Go out of your way to be around smart, interesting, ambitious people. Work for them and hire them.
- One of the most satisfying parts of work is forging deep relationships with really good people.
- Spend time with people who are either the best in the world at what they do, or extremely promising but totally unknown.
- "You become an average of the people you spend the most time with."

## Money

- Whether or not money buys happiness, it buys freedom, and lack of money is very stressful.
- Having enough that you don't stress about rent does more for wellbeing than having enough to buy your own jet.
- Keep your personal burn rate low. This alone gives you many opportunities.
- Making money is often more fun than spending it. Sam has never regretted money spent on friends, new experiences, saving time, travel and causes he believes in.
- In your 20s, a "Give me financial discipline, but not just yet" attitude is OK. "All the money in the world will never get back time that passed you by."

## Time, attention and wellbeing

- "Life is not a dress rehearsal—this is probably it." Time is limited and goes by fast.
- Do what makes you happy and fulfilled, and don't make excuses for not doing what you want. Don't do things you dislike just because others want you to.
- Talk to people more, read more long content and fewer tweets, watch less TV, spend less time online.
- Exercise, eat well, sleep, and get out into nature regularly.
- Most things are fine in moderation and almost nothing is fine in extreme amounts.
- Existential angst is part of life, especially around major events or after career milestones, and affects smart, ambitious people. Sam thinks some people work hard to avoid thinking about it. Nothing is wrong with you for feeling it.
- Be grateful, keep problems in perspective, don't complain much, and don't hate others' success. Some people will hate yours, so learn to ignore it.
- Humans can adjust to almost anything given time. "This too shall pass."
- His closing line: "The days are long but the decades are short."
