## Early-stage ingredients vs. finishing ingredients

- Sam says things "get started" through optimism, obsession, self-belief, raw horsepower and personal connections.
- Things "get finished" through cohesive teams, the right mix of calmness and urgency, and unreasonable commitment.
- He notes long-term orientation is in short supply. He advises not worrying about what people think in the short term, which gets easier over time.
- He says working with great people is one of the best parts of life and that you should get back up and keep going after setbacks.

## Picking goals and focusing resources

- A team finds it easier to do a hard thing that really matters than an easy thing that doesn't. Audacious ideas motivate people.
- He says to concentrate resources on a small number of high-conviction bets, which is "easy to say but evidently hard to do."
- He adds that "you can delete more stuff than you think."
- He treats incentives as superpowers and says to set them carefully.

## Org health: communication, bureaucracy, outcomes

- Communicate clearly and concisely.
- Fight bullshit and bureaucracy every time you see it, and get other people to fight it too.
- Do not let the org chart get in the way of people working productively together.
- Outcomes are what count. Do not let good process excuse bad results.

## Recruiting and evaluating people

- Spend more time recruiting.
- Take risks on high-potential people with a fast rate of improvement.
- Look for evidence of getting stuff done in addition to intelligence.
- Superstars are even more valuable than they seem, but evaluate people on their net impact on the organization's performance, not just individual output.

## Speed, planning horizon and bias to action

- Fast iteration can make up for a lot, and it is usually fine to be wrong if you iterate quickly.
- "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks."
- Inspiration is perishable and life goes by fast. Inaction is a particularly insidious type of risk.

## Scale, compounding and business physics

- Do not fight "the business equivalent of the laws of physics."
- Scale often has surprising emergent properties.
- Compounding exponentials are magic. He says you particularly want to build a business that gets a compounding advantage with scale.
