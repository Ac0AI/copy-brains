## The merge is already underway and gradual, not a single moment

- Sam notes Silicon Valley debates what year humans and machines merge, with most guesses between 2025 and 2075.
- He thinks "singularity" fell out of use partly because it implies one moment, while the merge looks gradual, and "gradual processes are hard to notice."
- He believes the merge "has already started, and we are a few years in": "Our phones control us and tell us what to do when; social media feeds determine how we feel; search engines decide what we think."
- The algorithms behind this are no longer understood by any one person. They optimize for what their creators tell them to, in ways no human could figure out. They are what seems like sophisticated AI today and "will seem like child's play" tomorrow.

## Co-evolution between humans and AI

- Sam describes the current phase as co-evolution: "the AIs affect, effect, and infect us, and then we improve the AI."
- The loop he points to is that people build more computing power and run AI on it, and the AI then figures out how to build even better chips.
- He argues this probably cannot be stopped, since scientific advancement eventually happens if the laws of physics do not prevent it.
- Unless humanity destroys itself first, he expects superhuman AI, genetic enhancement and brain-machine interfaces all to happen.
- He calls it "a failure of human imagination and human arrogance" to assume we will never build things smarter than ourselves.
- On intelligence, he argues that self-worth is so tied to intelligence that people believe it must be singular. He suggests it sits on a continuum, and that AI might see the gap between us and bonobos as barely worth discussing.

## Why a merge is the best-case outcome

- His logic is that if two species both want the same thing (to be the dominant species on the planet and beyond) and only one can have it, they will come into conflict.
- He concludes: "We should all want one team where all members care about the well-being of everyone else."
- A merge can take many forms, from electrodes in the brain to simply becoming "really close friends with a chatbot."
- Humans will be "the first species ever to design our own descendants." He sees two paths: be "the biological bootloader for digital intelligence" and fade into an evolutionary branch, or work out what a successful merge looks like.

## Why it may come sooner than expected

- Hardware is improving exponentially, and the number of smart people working on AI is also growing exponentially. He warns that "double exponential functions get away from you fast."
- The most surprising thing he has learned working on OpenAI is "just how correlated increasing computing power and AI breakthroughs are."
- He argues the world should take this much more seriously now, because worldwide coordination is slow and is needed for this.

## Attention hacking as a personal and societal risk

- Sam says he has a very hard time resisting what the algorithms want him to do, and found himself getting extremely addicted to the internet until he made a real effort to combat it.
- He predicts "attention hacking is going to be the sugar epidemic of this generation."
- He reports personal effects: he can wistfully remember having an attention span, and he is angry and unhappy more often.
- He says he channels that anger into productive change less often, instead chasing "the dual dopamine hits of likes and outrage."
- He observes that friends' young children do not know an attention span is something they should miss.
