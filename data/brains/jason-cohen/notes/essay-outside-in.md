## Say it on the outside only if you can prove it on the inside: let prospects test your support themselves

Jason cites "Brandon's Rule": "If it's on the outside, it's not on the inside." Examples are the People's Republic of China (not a Republic), Smucker's "100% Simply Fruit" spreads (30% juice), and "Scrubs will be right back after these messages" (no more Scrubs after the final break).

- When something is true, you don't need to announce it, convince people of it, or draw attention to it.
- Claims like "We put customers first," "We treat customers as partners," and "We have unmatched customer support" are the kind of thing that signals the opposite.

## Why great support can't be sold with words

Jason says support quality is something a customer notices right away: whether the phone gets answered quickly, whether the person on the other end genuinely helps, and whether the account manager keeps providing service after the sale.

- He has heard a C_O say countless times that "What distinguishes us from our competitors is better customer service."
- In theory good service is a competitive advantage, the primary way to build solid relationships, a driver of word-of-mouth advertising ("the best kind"), and can win a sale even after an unfavorable first impression.
- But "you can't convince people you have good support by telling them so. In fact, telling them so might turn them off."

## Demo tactic: invite prospects to trial your tech support

In demos, Jason tells prospects: "While you're trialing our software, also trial our tech support. Give 'em a call and test it out. Make sure we're responsive and helpful."

- Steps: (1) make the invitation during the demo; (2) point them at the real support line; (3) let them judge responsiveness and helpfulness themselves.
- It works because the claim is verified by the prospect's own experience rather than asserted by the seller.

## Honest, verifiable claims leave a mark

Jason's line: "When you hit someone over the head with honesty, it leaves a mark."

- People are used to hearing "We have unbeatable customer service" and "Customer service is priority number one," so the words are "water off a duck's back." Even when true, they're meaningless.
- Inviting a trial gets amazing reactions. One prospect told him: "I've never had a vendor actually encourage me to call them for support."
- Jason replied: "Well, I could tell you we have great customer service, but you wouldn't believe me." The prospect answered: "I don't have to call. I already know you guys are going to rock."

## Build the relationship during the initial demo

Jason asks when you last built that kind of relationship with a potential customer during the initial demo, and answers that it comes "just by being honest."

- Principle: don't say it, do it. Encourage potential customers to notice, and let them come to their own conclusions.
- Applies beyond support to any quality claim (service, quality, responsiveness): demonstrate it rather than announce it.
