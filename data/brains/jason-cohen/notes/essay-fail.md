## Why "fail" is the wrong word

- Jason argues "fail" is almost never the right word for what is happening. It is "so final, so negative, and it's a dead end: What do you do after a failure? Mope?"
- He says words should do two jobs: explain what is really going on, and suggest what to do next. "Fail" does neither.
- He rejects "fail fast" and "fail forward" too: "It's not fail fast. It's not fail forward. It's not 'fail' at all."
- His fix is to pick a more precise word for the situation, so you understand it better and are pointed at the next step.

## Exploring new territory: it's an experiment, not a failure

- Building a business or product is like solving a maze (he links the "idea maze" concept). When a path doesn't work, you haven't failed, you've backtracked.
- This usually takes the form of an experiment. The result is evidence for or against the hypothesis, and neither outcome is a failure.
- A/B testing is a good practice even though most tests won't find an improvement.
- Words to use instead: experiment, test, bet, learning, investigate, trial, retry, probe.
- He cites Fermi: if the result confirms the hypothesis you've made a measurement, and if it contradicts it you've made a discovery.

## Revolutionizing: you haven't found the breakthrough yet

- If you're building something nobody has done before and it doesn't immediately transform the industry, you haven't failed. You just haven't found the breakthrough.
- Innovations often need many trials before the right solution appears. If the answer were obvious, it wouldn't be an innovation.
- Words to use: backtrack, iterate, trial, attempt, step, miss.
- He cites Edison: "I have not failed. I've just found 10,000 ways that won't work."

## Learning and iterating

- **Learning a skill:** stumbling, getting lost, or sliding backward is growth, not failure. No good teacher gets mad at a student for not learning instantly. The learning curve is well understood and is not a straight line.
  - Words to use: practice, cultivate, acquire, develop, learning curve, learning moment, progression, evolve.
- **Iterating on something that already works:** this is optimization. Not every improvement attempt has the hoped-for effect, but you shouldn't call yourself a failure when things are basically fine as they are.
  - Words to use: refine, calibrate, optimize, polish, hone, correct, fine-tune, dial-in.

## Solving problems: setbacks are normal

- When you hit a minor problem particular to your current circumstance and your high-level strategy and plan are still right, that is not failure.
- In a complex space, whether a one-person startup or a thousand-person organization, you cannot have innovation and speed and also no setbacks, complications or false starts.
- Words to use: iterate, adapt, adjust, course-correct, tweak, revise, morph, refactor, amend, fix.
- He endorses Churchill: "Success is not final, failure is not fatal: It is the courage to continue that counts."

## Major errors: name the nature of the mistake

- Jason grants that a complete misjudgment, a wrong strategic decision, or months of work by dozens of people proving useless is a big deal, and saying "we failed" may be justified.
- Even then, the word doesn't explain the nature of the problem and doesn't point toward a solution, which is the constructive thing to do.
- Words to use: pivot, evolve, migrate, overhaul, revamp, transform, reconstruct, repurpose, reimagine, redesign, recreate, reinvent, reengineer, remodel, rebrand, realign.
- He includes Sumner Redstone's line: "Success is not built on success. It's built on failure. It's built on frustration. Sometimes it's built on catastrophe."

## When "fail" is the right word

- Jason says there are moments of true, irrevocable, fatal failure, and then you should say so. His example: a company that has to shut down, especially before achieving meaningful success.
- Otherwise, find a better way to describe what's happening so you understand the situation and are pointed toward what to do next.
