## The "ship of Theseus" test for startups: what stays constant is the essence

- Jason argues that nearly everything about a startup changes over time: product features, marketing copy (molded by A/B testing), team composition, metrics (by orders of magnitude), logo, name, and office space.
- He uses the Tao Te Ching's "emptiness creates function" idea and the ship-whose-boards-all-rot-and-get-replaced puzzle to frame it. The "emptiness" a ship, a person, or a company creates is its essence.
- His example: is a company of 2 people and 150 customers the "same company" as one with 150 people and 25,000 customers? Tangibly no, but "in its emptiness lies the essence and purpose" of the people in it.
- The founder's key question: "What is constant? What doesn't change as we grow, and thus what binds us together in this journey?" Whatever the answer is "defines the startup and its purpose."
- That answer also resolves why anyone, employees or customers, should join the founder and share this "very personal responsibility."

## Core values as the constant: WP Engine's "inviolable constitution"

- At WP Engine, Jason calls the constants "values", described as an "inviolable constitution". When a tough decision comes up, the values determine the outcome.
- Concrete examples he gives:
  - If a choice is between losing money and doing what's right for the customer, WP Engine does what's right for the customer.
  - If someone is exceptionally skillful at their job but dishonest, or works for himself instead of the team, WP Engine will always fire that person.
- Values are the tiebreaker for hard tradeoffs, not decoration, so they must be able to override short-term money and individual talent.

## Values are how you empower everyone to act like an owner

- Jason says a clear agreement on core values "is how you empower everyone in the company to act like an owner."
- Example: can someone in their first week of tech support decide to give a customer a large refund, or spend extra hours going above and beyond to fix something? If the values clearly point to that being right, they can decide without approval chains, meetings, or debates.
- He treats this as the definition of empowerment and as part of the solution to scaling, since decisions no longer have to route through founders.

## Write the values down and get real buy-in

- Jason says it is "so important to encode your company's values on paper."
- At WP Engine they literally put the values on the wall and everyone signed it, like the US Declaration of Independence.
- Agreement must go beyond a condition of employment or values memorized because of an external requirement. The goal is that people "in their bones" already embody the values, so they naturally keep propagating them to those around them and to those who follow.

## Culture is the output of living your values

- Jason defines "Company Culture" as "the output of living your values."
- It's never perfect, for individuals or the organization. At any moment the company falls short on some aspect of its culture while aspirationally trying to live up to its own expectations. He says that is healthy.

## Financial success is the means, not the end

- Jason's first three companies, before WP Engine, were "successful enough", yet he never thought about culture or values and calls that a mistake. Two still exist and have kept growing for nearly two decades.
- His critique: without a higher purpose, a truly important mission for customers, and dedication to employees, "maybe who cares?"
- He questions what that success actually created: were people happy and empowered, mentoring and being mentored, building careers they're proud of, feeling their time wasn't wasted? He admits he doesn't know, and calls that sad.
- His position: "Financial success is the means, not the ends." Financial success is absolutely necessary because without it the company isn't sustainable, but money doesn't make you happy or other people happy, and doesn't lead to a fulfilling, meaningful life.
- He closes by saying you've done something important with your life, and for others' lives, if you answer what does create fulfillment through your culture and values, and he asks founders: "What is your emptiness?"
