## A top performer is infinitely better, not 10x better

Jason rejects the saying that a great developer is 10x as productive as a mediocre one. He argues the real gap is larger, because great people produce ideas and work that no number of average people would produce.

- His test: "You can't put 100 average designers on a committee and get a fabulous design, right?"
- A great developer or designer is "better than an infinite number" of average ones, since they come up with ideas and implementations that 100 others wouldn't.
- He calls this "universal, timeless, human rule" and says it holds in every role, not just engineering.

## The same rule applies in every function

Jason gives one example for each role, contrasting the great person with a crowd of average ones:

- **Writing (content marketing, social media):** a great writer versus 100 average bloggers.
- **Management:** a great manager who develops the careers of their people versus several average managers who are "glorified project managers."
- **Customer support:** a great rep thrilling customers and causing love on Twitter versus 100 others "answering tickets" who never evoke an emotional reaction.
- **Product management:** a PM who consistently drives brilliant output based on salability, instead of under-the-hood technical gymnastics.
- **Design and development:** the original case, where committees of average people cannot reproduce one great person's output.

He says that if you think any position at your company doesn't follow the "better than 10x" rule, "you've never worked with greatness at that position."

## Great salespeople are not revenue-acquisition robots

Jason pushes back on modeling sales as robots with a "zombie-like drive to make phone calls," where a slew of barely productive closers may be financially inefficient but still gets the job done. He says that if you see it that way, you shouldn't be responsible for building the sales group.

What a great salesperson does instead:

- Produces "cheerleader customers" who are actually thrilled and referenceable after the sale.
- Excites the whole company about what's possible with growth, "the engine that makes everything else possible."
- Brings along people at every position and shares the corporate culture, rather than building a separate roost that feeds on leads bagged by marketing.

He says that if you think sales can't work this way, "you are the one with incorrect expectations," and he cites WP Engine as living proof that it can.

## Hiring conclusion: you cannot afford to hire less

Because of this rule, Jason concludes that you can't afford to hire anyone less than great.

- If a competitor has the intent, the money and the story needed to hire great people and you don't, you must work out how your business will thrive anyway.
- He allows that this might be possible, but calls it "a special sort of optimization" and not one he would be excited to execute.
- His own preference is to work with the best, do amazing things together, and learn new things every day from them.
- His closing line: "Life's too short, and high tech moves too fast, to mess about with mediocrity."
