## Why Jason didn't register smartbear.com at launch

- Smart Bear began as a sole proprietorship. In Feb 2003 Jason converted it to a single-member LLC, "mostly just to see what it was like" (single-member LLCs and sole proprietorships have identical tax consequences: none).
- Code Historian and Code Reviewer were shareware at the time, and the company name didn't seem to matter. He owned `codehistorian.com` and thought that was better, so he never registered `smartbear.com`.
- He rejects every excuse he could offer: no money (but the domain was cheap), didn't expect things to go well (but it was cheap insurance), didn't think anyone was watching (but domain squatters monitor chamber of commerce records and grab whatever they find). His verdict: "There's no excuse."
- The lesson he draws is to act fast: hesitating on a cheap, obvious purchase cost him $2000.

## How the squatter ended up with the domain

- By the time Jason came to his senses, a squatter had taken `smartbear.com`. First the page was just broken, then it said the domain was for sale at $2000.
- Jason settled for `smartbearsoftware.com`.
- The cost of the workaround was practical: the name is awkward to say over the phone. He would have to explain "Smart Bear Software. One word. That's 'bear' as in the animal... You have to have the software."

## Negotiating and legal options against the squatter

- Jason offered the squatter $1000. It was rejected.
- He sent a lawyer letter, which was ignored.
- He asked the lawyer what actually pursuing the squatter would cost: a minimum of $2500. His takeaway: "(Oh. That's how the price is set.)" Squatters price just below the cost of legal action.
- He pointed out the Smart Bear trademark. The squatter replied that it wasn't a registered trademark.
- Jason started registering all the company's trademarks, but years later the process was still in its last stage and not finished.

## What finally made him pay

- The other Bears pushed him: "Just get the domain. We know it sucks. We know it's unfair. But stop making us type 'software' all the time like chumps."
- The final straw was a conversation with Chris Boyd, co-founder of Midas Networks, who told him, "you just have to pay those guys." Jason says Chris "always tells it straight," and that was when he knew he was sunk.
- On 2007-01-23 he bought `smartbear.com` for the $2000 asking price. He notes this was a matter of principle as much as money ("It's the principle of the thing though").

## Takeaways on domains and early decisions

- Register the company-name domain on day one, even when the product has its own domain and the company is tiny.
- The cost of registering early is trivial next to the later cost: the $2000 purchase price, plus the extra friction of a longer domain and the time spent negotiating.
- Don't count on trademarks or lawyers to recover a squatted domain: an unregistered trademark gives little leverage, and legal action costs more than the squatter's asking price.
- When a squatter holds a name you need and legal options cost more than the price, paying the asking price can be the rational choice.
