## Pricing on growth value, not savings: the 8x repositioning

Jason argues that pricing is "much more about positioning and perceived value than it is about cost-analysis and unconvincing ROI calculators." The same product can command a much higher price depending on how its value is framed. His worked example is a fictional tool, DoubleDown, that doubles the cost-efficiency of AdWords campaigns, so a marketer gets the same conversions and lead quality for half the ad spend. Repositioning it from "saves money" to "creates growth" lets him raise the price eightfold.

## The "saves money" pitch caps your price at a fraction of the savings

- Jason's baseline: a customer spends $40,000/mo on AdWords. DoubleDown halves that, saving $20,000/mo.
- The customer cannot be charged more than the savings, so he assumes a vendor can charge about 25% of the savings and still find many willing customers. That is $5,000/mo.
- The customer keeps 75% of the value ($15,000/mo) and the VP of Demand Gen can boast to the CMO about it.
- His footnote on the 25% figure: there is data supporting it. Charge less and you are not monetizing enough. Charge much more and you leave too much space for a competitor.

## Why growth is worth more than cost reduction

- Marketers' paramount goal is growth. Even brand, events and PR support growth indirectly.
- "Increasing growth is much more valuable than decreasing cost." He says it is "always 10x more valuable for a business to grow faster than it is for the business to save money."
- The contrast he uses, a CMO reporting to the CEO:
  - "I reduced costs 20% this year": the CEO is happy, then asks how to use the savings to grow faster.
  - "I increased growth 20%, but it also cost 20% more": the CEO is thrilled. Company value rises non-linearly, the extra revenue more than pays for the extra marketing cost, and the follow-up question is "Can we spend even more?"

## The repositioned pitch: spend more, get more at the same unit cost

- Same customer: $40,000/mo yields 200 quality leads, so $200/lead.
- New pitch: use DoubleDown to double leads to 400, still at $200/lead, for $80,000/mo total.
- The customer already accepts $200/lead, so it doesn't matter how the $80,000 splits between AdWords and DoubleDown. The goal is not "save money on AdWords" but "generate more growth at a similar unit cost."
- Value created becomes $40,000 instead of $20,000, and the customer is willing to hand a vendor 100% of it rather than 25%. Both numbers move up, which is how the price goes from $5,000/mo to $40,000/mo (8x).

## The rule: sell the value in the customer's own metric

- Both the amount of value created and the share the customer will pay are "a multiple higher" for the growth pitch than for the savings pitch.
- His instruction: when you want to describe your product as a way to "save time" or "save money" or "be more efficient", don't.
- Instead, work out how the product creates value in the way the customer already measures value, and position the product as a way to accomplish that.
- Check for the equivalent in any market: identify the metric the buyer is judged on (for a marketer, growth and lead volume at an acceptable cost per lead), then frame the product as producing more of it.
