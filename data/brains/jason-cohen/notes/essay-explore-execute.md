## Explore and Execute are two different business modalities

- Jason defines **Explore** as "figuring out what works, as quickly and flexibly as possible."
- He defines **Execute** as "becoming excellent at doing what works, while scaling."
- He prefers "Execute" to the standard term "Explore vs Exploit". Exploitation means extracting value at the expense of someone else. He wants capitalism to create more value than it consumes, so that both parties are better off.
- Execute means operationalizing what was discovered: making it repeatable, teachable, higher quality and automated, and building teams that improve and grow.
- After Product/Market Fit (PMF) the company needs a "radical shift in working style" from Explore to Execute. Applying the wrong modality fails in either direction.
- "Scaling too soon" is a common cause of startup failure. It means switching to Execute before exploration is complete, while you still don't know:
  - which customer segment to target,
  - what price to charge,
  - why someone should pick you over a competitor.

## Why founders resist the switch after PMF

- Founder arrogance peaks right after PMF. The logic runs: "We achieved our success *because* of the way we've run the company... we will continue to be successful because of those things."
- Jason says most honest founders are "scared shitless". He finds that founders who are deeply certain their success is assured are the most likely to fail, because they are disconnected from reality. "Reality wins."
- The company's needs change dramatically after PMF, and so must everyone at the company, including the founder.
- The result is tumult, frustration, chaos and sometimes irreparable disaster. He calls the period after PMF but before maturity the most difficult part of the journey. "Being hard doesn't make it the hardest. What's hardest, comes next."

## What breaks when growth outpaces the company

- The company suddenly grows faster than it can manage. For example, support requests exceed capacity, queues grow without bound and service quality plummets.
- Small-company care and personal attention is often a differentiator against larger competitors, and it is the first thing lost.
- WP Engine example:
  - PMF hit in January 2012.
  - Twitter feedback went from "Best support team in the world!" to "Wow, what happened? They're crap now."
  - It was 2013 before there were enough people and process, covering both the science of metrics and the art of culture and empathy.
  - Over the next decade WP Engine became known for the best service in the industry, winning many awards, with a CSAT of 98% and an NPS of 64.
- Hiring is slow. A person contacted today takes about six months to become productive: interview, notice period, a week off, training with no materials while everyone is overloaded, then ramp-up. This applies to engineering, design, product, marketing, sales and finance.
- Because it happened suddenly, "there's quite literally no time in which to fix it," and digging out makes it worse.
- Many facets of the business break at scale, not only software and infrastructure but also people, processes and workflows.

## The Explore vs Execute shift, item by item

| Explore | Execute |
|:---:|:---:|
| experiment | standardize |
| fail fast | fail never |
| speed | reliability |
| creativity | stability |
| learning | leveraging |
| gathering evidence | data extrapolation |
| manual labor | automation |
| discontinuous jumps | continuous optimization |
| question assumptions | leverage assumptions |
| short-term tactical planning | long-term strategic planning |
| low quality buys speed | high quality buys safety |
| new | valuable |

- **Explore** is for answering the pre-PMF questions:
  - the problem solved,
  - the target market and perfect customer,
  - why they buy,
  - which few features make them buy,
  - which distribution channel works,
  - the right price,
  - the biggest risks.
- In Explore you try, test and fail quickly, because you are running out of time and money.
- In Explore you look for big shifts rather than subtle changes that are too small to notice, since there isn't enough data to measure them.
- Explorers are "confident about nothing", question everything and seek difficult truths.
- Exploration follows no timeline and no single path, though well-researched patterns can improve the odds.
- Once PMF is reached those questions are answered. "All that work, all that worry... none of that is relevant now. In fact, it's getting in the way of what needs to be done, which is to execute."

## Execute mode: hiring, fixing shortcuts and de-risking

- **Hiring and managing, not building only.**
  - Executives move from building things to building teams that build them.
  - Anything important must be done by a team, for quantity of work and so that illness or departures don't halt a function.
  - Team building is its own skill set: organizational design, hiring managers rather than individual contributors, new communication methods, being "in command instead of in control", prioritization, coordination, and balancing organizational against individual needs.
  - Knowing how to code or design a website doesn't mean you know how to interview a manager, set priorities or run a meeting.
- **Fixing shortcuts, not new features only.**
  - The simple, lovable, complete (SLC) product and the tech debt taken on to ship fast was the right trade in Explore.
  - Customers now hit those bugs and UX issues. They contact support or click "Cancel Subscription".
  - The debt must be paid down. You also need automation, test suites, CI/CD, auto-scaling infrastructure and separate support prioritization and channels.
  - None of that counts as "new features". It is needed because of a growing product and a growing team coordinating work inside it.
- **De-risking, not new innovation only.**
  - Pre-PMF, the likely cause of failure was not having a working business model.
  - Post-PMF, the likely cause of failure is operations: bad tech support, a buggy product, load failures, marketing that can't keep up, hard-to-track finance, security emergencies, taxes and legal.
  - De-risking is a completely different mentality and skill set from adding features.

## Execute mode: scale, predictability and measurement

- **Rare things become common.**
  - A bug affecting 1% of customers didn't matter before. Now it causes 5 support tickets a day and angry Twitter complaints.
  - Rare problems are hard to detect and hard to solve.
  - Quality becomes a priority, addressed with requirements and processes. Those ideas are new to the company and likely counter-cultural.
- **Predictable processes.**
  - Hiring takes at least six months from deciding to open a position to a trained, productive person.
  - So you must know what the company needs in six to twelve months, which means the business has to become more predictable. This applies to support, sales, engineering and new departments like finance, HR and account management.
  - Predictability requires playbooks, documentation, measurement and processes that keep output steady under variable input.
  - These guardrails also help new people get up to speed in your new way.
- **Stalls in growth.**
  - You are underwater serving customers, hiring, lacking documentation, adding processes and changing culture all at once.
  - Meanwhile growth can stall because you lag on features, competitors and marketing.
- **Measuring success.**
  - Execute lends itself to measurement: sales leads, closed-won rate, sprint velocity.
  - Measuring Explore is difficult or impossible. If you expect to fail at least half the time, "we learned something" is hard to verify, and "learning" probably can't be measured.
  - Still, "an explorer who wanders for years and never finds anything, is not succeeding."
  - Teams that have only known Explore often resist the specific measurement of Execute.
  - Jason doesn't think sprint velocity should measure productivity. It becomes meaningless once management watches it, because teams subconsciously inflate point values. He says it should be a private team metric for honest self-management.

## People and culture have to change, including the founder

- Explore suits someone who doesn't want a map, happily throws work away when an assumption proves wrong, and ships at 50% quality and 50% completion.
- Execute suits someone who wants a clear goal and constraints, designs a way to complete the mission, then executes it.
- "These aren't the same personalities. It's probably not the same person." Some people can make the transition and some can't. This is hard to manage, especially when they were critical to early success.
- It is hardest when that person is the founder: "It's most difficult when that person is you. And it always is."
- The change is a fundamental shift in attitude, skill sets and company culture, not only daily tasks.
- Things you were proud of were right for discovering what to make, but now hold you back from scaling it. "It's time to be proud of something else."
- It means letting go of "the things that got us here", sometimes even the people who got you here, and sometimes realizing you yourself no longer fit. "This is going to be hard."
