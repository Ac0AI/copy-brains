## Treat the decision to persist as an unknowable bet, and optimize for finding out fast

- Jason frames two scenarios. In S1 you start an AdWords campaign, it's "obviously not working" at time B, you keep going, and by time C it works. In S2 you do the same, and at time C it's still failing and you've wasted more time and money.
- At time B the two are identical. Persevering is only wise in S1 and stopping is only wise in S2, and you can't tell which one you're in.
- He says the usual labels are convenient rationalizations: S1 becomes "success through perseverance" and S2 becomes "failure through obstinance." Both sets of platitudes (winners never quit; doing the same thing expecting different results is insanity) are backward-looking.
- It applies to AdWords, product design, a company's vision, and the market you hope to create around it. "It all looks the same at point B."
- Even venture capitalists, who decide this for a living, can't tell. He notes most VC portfolios lose money.

## Why stopping isn't the safe default

- Stopping at B can look smart, but "either way you've failed" to achieve something useful.
- "Stopping is sure failure while persisting is at least possible success." If you stop, you never create something great.
- He argues you also can't reliably say you "learned something" from the outcome. You made choices and observed results, and that may be all there is.

## Treat uncertainty as liberating

- Accepting that you can't know which scenario you're in lets you decide with less second-guessing in the moment and less guilt afterwards.
- It also means taking less credit when things go well.
- The job becomes to "find out which one it is as quickly as possible." In practice:
  - Stop dithering and make a strong decision.
  - Keep your eyes open and measure what's happening as objectively as possible.
  - Hope for S1 but allow for S2.
  - Don't feel guilty if you guessed wrong or cocky if you guessed right.
- His closing advice: "just shut up and get going," and when done, don't look back too much, "just shut up and get going again."

## Guiding questions for whether to keep going (reader-contributed, lightly edited and endorsed by Jason)

- **Timebox:** set a hard deadline for how much longer you'll work on it without seeing improvement.
- **Enjoyment and learning:** are you still enjoying the project and learning from it?
- **Rate of progress:** is it slowing or accelerating?
- **Existence proof:** if others achieved a similar outcome, don't stop, especially if their solutions are bad and they're still making money.
- **Noticeably better:** is the product better than market alternatives, or will it be soon? If no to both, consider stopping.
- **Run out of ideas:** if the team has run out of ideas and conviction, consider pivoting. If it has run out for pivoting too, do a full reset or quit.
- **12-month test:** can you see yourself doing this in 12 months? If not, you might as well stop now.

## Guardrails against self-deception

- **External sounding board:** use one to keep you honest. Numbers often won't say left or right, and even when they do, we dismiss them as "outliers" or "this time is different."
- **Sunk cost:** don't let it decide.
- **Fear:** don't decide out of fear. If the outcome scares you a little, stick with it.
- **Penny in the air:** take a long walk and listen. Often you already know but "don't want to admit it to myself."
- **Go back to "The Why":** if you now have more information about whether the original reason is credible, re-assess. If the information and belief are unchanged, press on.

## Weigh the stakes and alternatives

- **Opportunity cost:** are you missing opportunities because you're fixated on this?
- **Consequences:** what's the downside of not finishing, and what do you get if you succeed? Are there smaller wins along the way?
- **Worthwhile, fixable, energy:** is it worthwhile, is it fixable, and do you still have energy for it?
- **Self-checks:** am I enjoying it, do I have room to improve, am I willing to do the work, am I marketing it as well as I can, and would my time be better spent on a new project?
