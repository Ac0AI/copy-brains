## Zero-sum vs non-zero-sum marketing channels

Jason defines a zero-sum channel as one where one company winning a piece of it locks competitors out of that piece.

- AdWords: "the price is set by the highest (dumbest?) bidder." The few companies in the good slots get the vast majority of clicks. The rest cannot get enough clicks to materially affect their sales.
- SEO for head terms: the top three results get nearly all the traffic. Even holding 10 slots ranked 11-30 doesn't add up to a fraction of what the winners get.
- Counter-example: social media. If company P has an awesome Twitter feed, company Q can have one too. P's great content doesn't stop Q from earning attention the same way.

## Why winning a zero-sum channel is usually a bad deal

He acknowledges the tempting logic. Winning a zero-sum game has "double" value: you get the sale, and your competitors don't.

He argues the cost outweighs that, especially in auction-style channels:

- Winning means paying top dollar.
- Available inventory is limited (the top few slots in AdWords or SEO).
- So even if you win, growth in that channel is capped and expensive.

## Invest in non-zero-sum channels instead

His recommended alternatives:

- Social media
- Long-tail SEO
- Brand-building
- Content
- Value-added resellers
- Cold-calling

Because these channels aren't winner-take-all, "the limit of the channel is closer to the limit of the market itself, rather than the sliver of the market which might reach you via a single advertising channel."

## Control: success depends on you, not a platform or a competitor

Jason says that in non-zero-sum channels, "your success is dependent almost exclusively on yourself." It does not depend on:

- how an advertising platform works
- how much money a competitor is willing to lose

## Why this matters most for small companies

- Smaller companies lack the money, consultants and in-house expertise that large or funded companies bring to "gladiator-style kill-or-be-killed arenas of zero-sum games."
- In the other games, "there's nothing the big guys can do to prevent you from shining."

## Focus on what you control

Jason notes there are few things in startups a founder controls. He names three of them:

- The product
- Your culture
- Your voice online

His advice: "Take advantage of those when you can!"
