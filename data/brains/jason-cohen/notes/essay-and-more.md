## "And more" is lazy marketing: say what you are

- Jason argues that the phrase "...and more!" is "ubiquitous and less meaningful" than almost any other. He used to think it was a harmless waste of words, then decided he was wrong.
- His test: swap in absurd nouns ("Bed, Bath, and Robots!", "Bed, Bath, and Velociraptors!"). The suffix is so empty that anything fits.
- His conclusion: "'and more' is a weak declaration that your message is incomplete." A phrase that doesn't commit to anything doesn't get people interested, excited, or through the door.

## Most "and more" claims aren't even true

- Jason checked real sites using the phrase and found most were about one thing only.
  - "Pumpkins and More" is a site about pumpkins and nothing else.
  - "The German Way and More" covers German life and culture.
  - "Law and More" is a blog by and for lawyers, and "Cooking and More" is only about cooking.
  - "Tourism and More" is actually about Tourism and Security (and its company shield says "Security," not "more").
  - "Rain Barrels and More" is a company that makes only rain barrels.
- His point: the phrase is both useless and inaccurate, so it adds a false claim on top of a vague one.

## Replace "and more" with enthusiasm and commitment

- If a business does one thing, say so with passion instead of hedging. Jason's rewrites for the pumpkin site: "Everything Pumpkin," "Pumpkins Supreme," "Nothin' but Pumpkin," "Pumpkin Lovers."
- For the German site he suggests "The German Way Explained" or "The German Way in English."
- Specialization is a selling point: "When I need information about pumpkins, I want someone fully committed to pumpkindom!"
- On the rain barrel company: "How much more do they know about rain barrels than anyone else? How many stories could they tell?" He says "and more" dilutes "this otherwise incredible message" and douses a singular passion. He would rather buy from "passionate rain barrel people."

## When "and more" is true, it still doesn't work

- Jason concedes that Bed Bath & Beyond really does sell more than bed and bath goods, so "and Beyond" is at least informative.
- The case for it: the listed items attract some people, and "and beyond" tells the rest "we have more!"
- His rebuttal: "I don't buy it." Someone uninterested in bed and bath products is unlikely to care about air fresheners and cutlery, and simply saying "and beyond" won't stir them into action.
- He gives the name a pass partly for the alliteration, but says non-committal phrases generally don't get people interested.

## Takeaway: own what you are

- "Forget about 'more.' Just say what you are. Own it."
- It should be possible to convey the purpose of a business in a phrase without ambiguity.
- He ties this to knowing your ideal customer: make sure "Carol gets it" (his ideal customer persona) and the rest follows.
