## Why frameworks aren't maps: "A compass is not a map"

Jason argues that startup advice tells you how to move, not where to go. A compass "tells you **which way is north, not whether you should be heading north**." He says there is no map to startup success, and that this is fine: "There's no map. That's OK. Keep moving."

## Frameworks and famous startups are not laws

- Jason treats Lean Startup, 37signals' habits and the origin stories of favorite startups as examples, not rules.
- He points out that most startups failed before Lean Startup and most still failed after it, so he questions whether it is strictly necessary.
- When an entrepreneur said he disliked Lean Startup and asked if he was cut out for startups, Jason noted that Elon didn't use Lean Startup to build Tesla, though he added that most founders aren't manufacturing cars.
- He criticizes founders who justify every decision with "37signals does it" without explaining why it applies to their situation, or whether 37signals did it when they were still the founder's size.

## For any advice P, the 2x2 is full

- For any piece of advice `P`, there are many successes and failures among startups that did `P`, and many successes and failures among startups that did the opposite.
- He gives examples: one startup got 300 customers from a TechCrunch launch article while another got 0; some built loyalty on Twitter while another grew revenue 20% month-over-month without having reserved their Twitter handle; some credit spectacular design while another successful startup has "never hired a designer (and it shows)".
- His question to the reader: if all four boxes are full, what does that tell us about `P`?

## Why startup "rules" fail: survivors are anomalies

- Besides survivor bias, the core problem is that "by definition the successful ones are anomalies."
- "Trends don't predict outliers." Statistics show trends, but they can't determine the outcome of an individual case, in startups or anywhere else.
- Nothing predicts which startup becomes an outlier. A successful startup looks a lot like an unsuccessful one at the start.

## Lessons are not traction

- Jason questions pitch decks that boast about mistakes made and lessons learned when those lessons haven't produced paying customers.
- The open question he poses: is it time to give up, or does that knowledge and introspection position the team for rocket-ship growth? He doesn't claim a rule settles it.

## How to use startup advice

- Use advice "for sparks of inspiration, for brainstorming, as a candy store where every item has its own merits but you must pick and choose what to put into your basket."
- Don't confuse frameworks, dogma or your favorite startup's origin story with laws.
- Find your own way: "Decide what makes you the best version of who you already are."
