## Legacy as the ongoing impact of ideas, not the survival of a name

Jason distinguishes two kinds of legacy. The old human one is "second death" avoidance: Egyptian monuments, Kaddish, Greek "glory", Día de los Muertos, donors' names on buildings, discoverers' names on theorems and particles. The one he has become "enamored with" is "the on-going impact of ideas."

- His examples: religious texts have mostly unnamed authors yet shaped the worldview of tens of billions of people. Darwin's ideas would have echoed even if published anonymously.
- "If we erased all names from all discoveries, they would not cease to be discoveries, and their effect on us would not diminish."
- Romeo and Juliet predates Shakespeare in Pyramus and Thisbe, Layla and Majnun, Tristan and Isolde and others, so ideas travel wide in geography and deep in time without a traceable origin.

## The idea-stone and ripples model of how ideas spread

- The creator "lobs an idea-stone into the lake of humanity." Waves move in all directions, potentially infinitely far across time and space, with the original stone forgotten or never known.
- Waves fizzle without support. They grow when "whipped up by winds of repetition," and when they inspire other creators who remix them into new stones.
- Most ripples become indiscernible on the surface, but they were still part of the force that eventually reaches "a distant shore of a distant mind of a distant human."
- Practical takeaway: influence compounds through repetition and remixing by others, not through the originator being credited.

## Reframing plagiarism of his own SLC as pride

- Jason's SLC (Simple, Lovable, Complete, his alternative to MVP) is now repeated widely. Consultants and agencies present it as their own idea. He links more than a dozen blog posts, tweets and a video, and at least one outright plagiarism.
- His first reaction was being "indignant, maybe even bitter, maybe even angry."
- His reframe: these are "ripples on the lake" and "winds of repetition with no reference to the originator." If the ripples are the legacy, they can be met "with gratitude and pride."
- This is an emotional stance for creators whose ideas get copied, not a claim that copying is fine.

## What this means for content strategy

- He argues people should worry less about "content" for the sake of "eyeballs" and more about "creating ideas that are worth repeating": ideas "so important and true and compelling and repeatable that people *wish* to repeat them, even if our names aren't attached."
- Worry less about attention to yourself and more about how to positively influence others and "help others become better versions of who they already are."
- Name ideas with self-descriptive names so they spread: SLC, Binstack, Needs Stack, Talk/Walk, Fairytale Strategic Planning, Brittle Points, Pivot Points.
- Other ideas he left untitled or credited to others: Roadmap for PMF, Love/Skill/Need, Love/Utility/Coercion, "Is there a problem", Unpredictability, Fermi Estimation.
- He has been publishing ideas on his site for 19 years, "unwittingly" answering the question of what he wants to spread without branding it with his name.

## Caveats: attribution, money and privilege

- Jason admits this is "easy for *me* to say": he has a "modicum of fame" from writing about 26 years of building companies, and the article went to 65,000 newsletter subscribers.
- He made enough money from building companies that he "was never compelled to monetize content," which he calls "a rare luxury."
- Attribution still matters: "Artists should be paid for their work. Fame and money matters while we're alive." Proper attribution is ethical and "plagiarism is theft."
- His point is additive: alongside accumulating fame and money, ask "And what else?"

## Questions to decide which stones to drop

Jason poses these as a prompt for creators and founders:
- Which of your ideas deserve repetition?
- Which of others' ideas deserve repetition, with your own take, style and exposition?
- What will you do with your influence?
- What behaviors do you wish to encourage in others?
- What lessons do you wish humanity would heed?

He argues you must drop some stones, because the alternative is a nihilistic view of life as a flicker between about 13.77 billion years of near-nothing before and a far longer span after, in a universe roughly 880 billion billion billion meters wide. His response: "it _does_ matter. To be human is to defy the uncaring universe by caring deeply anyway," about loved ones, creations and legacy.

## Meaning of life and how people live on

- His stated meaning of life: "To discover who we are, to be driven by what we were meant to do, to create beloved and useful things, and to love and help others."
- His father died 20 years earlier while Jason held his hand. A vivid dream weeks later left him with the idea that as long as you remember someone, and their words or ideals are reflected in what you say, do and value, "that part of them is literally and physically living on, in you. And you to the next."
