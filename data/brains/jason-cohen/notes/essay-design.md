## Design is a strategic choice, not a universal requirement

Jason redesigned the WP Engine website in 2011, going from a site with database errors and CSS blowups to a sleek, themed design (a grayscale 1950s automotive motif built with CSS3/HTML5). He used the result to ask whether design really matters, and concluded that it depends on whether design is core to your strategy.

## The WP Engine redesign did not move the metrics

- After the redesign, customers emailed things like "Thank God you fixed that horrible website. I was embarrassed when referring you to my clients."
- The team said they were "soooo glad" the site was fixed, because they had been embarrassed by it.
- But the same customers were still customers and were still referring clients.
- Jason checked Google Analytics before and after the change. Home page bounce rate, average time-on-site and sales conversion rate (weekly signups) all showed no visible change at the redesign date.
- His reading: "Looks like the data are objectively saying 'design doesn't matter.'"
- He still values the soft evidence. Customer emails and team morale matter even when the dashboards are flat. He is glad they did it, "if not for objective data, then for the subjective feelings."

## Design as a signal of maturity in sales

- After the redesign, WP Engine landed a large customer who said they could tell from the website that, among competitors, WP Engine was more mature and ready to handle a bigger customer.
- Jason notes that WP Engine was not objectively particularly more mature, and that this customer would probably have been fine with a competitor.
- He says the design "could be" what gave the edge, and "Didn't hurt, anyway."

## Companies that won with bad design

- **Smart Bear:** an "eye-melting" website, yet the company doubled revenue and profit every year for half a decade.
- **ITWatchDogs** (his second company): an old homepage with calliope-colored menubar, two broken images above the fold and a chaotic layout. It grew every month, made millions of dollars, took business from competitors with billion-dollar market caps and professional-looking sites, and had a successful exit.
- His conclusion: this does not prove design doesn't matter, but design may not be the deciding factor.
- At ITWatchDogs, the culture and message were "low-cost, friendly, approachable, regular people, who understand exactly what you worry about, exactly what your budget is, and we nail it." Competitors had slick corporate sites; the messy site matched customers' own offices, full of "assorted hardware corpses and solder stains."
- He rejects the retort that "that's design too," since no designer would have created that site because it looks "relatable."

## Counter-examples where design is the business

- **Hipmunk:** does the same thing as Orbitz or Travelocity. The only difference is design, which makes flight search pleasurable and usable. It lacks better data, branding, name, SEO or money, so design was its quantum-leap advantage.
- **Amy Hoy's time zone tool:** Jason uses and recommends it only because it is "just nice to use and look at."
- **Mint:** many credit its success to design. Given how many features were broken for how long, Jason finds it hard to argue otherwise.
- **37signals:** documented with data that Highrise signup page design and headline changes produced more credit-card-swiping customers.

## How to decide: pick a position and act consistently

- Decide whether design is core to your startup's identity, then act consistently.
- If design is how you win (how you acquire and retain customers, earn referrals and stand out in the market), it can be the most important thing, as with Hipmunk.
- If design is core, build a consistent culture that values design "through-and-through" starting now.
- If design is not important to you, decide what is, and "nail those things all the harder." Competitors will use superior design to cover up weaknesses in those areas, so your superiority "must shine through regardless."
- It is not strictly either-or. WP Engine did fine before and after the redesign.
- Editor's note added in 2025: with the consumerization of enterprise software and product-led growth, design has moved to the fore in business software, and consumers will pick and pay more for great design, so it is no longer clear one can hold that "design doesn't matter."
