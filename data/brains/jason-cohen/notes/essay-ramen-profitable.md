## "Profitable" means the business can pay for the founder's job

- Jason argues most "profitable from day one" claims are bullshit. His example: a SaaS with one $49/mo customer, $40 of AWS and $1.72 of Stripe fees, "profitable" only because the founder's own cost is ignored.
- He says anyone with experience knows this, so the claim makes the founder look like a bullshit artist or just ignorant.
- The main error is ignoring the cost of yourself.

## Five tests for "not profitable yet"

- Your savings go down every month.
- You make less than minimum wage.
- You couldn't afford to pay someone else to do your job.
- If someone bought the company and hired people (even below market rates), they'd have less money in the bank each month.
- You need another job to pay the bills.

His key word is "yet." Businesses aren't profitable on day one; they're profitable later, if ever. That isn't failure.

## A practical definition: replace the salary you quit

- Profitable means you take out of the business the same money you made at the job you quit.
- His reasoning: the market already decided your time and skills are worth at least that, so if the startup replaces it, calling it profitable is fair.
- He notes that most small business owners earn about the same as they did as employees, without the extra stress and hours, and most earn less than the average CEO salary.

## Ramen-profitable is the minimum bar

- At minimum, be ramen-profitable: eat as cheaply as possible, not be malnourished, and not burn savings.
- Lifestyle and location change that income requirement dramatically.
- It is easier to start when young, unattached and childless, with few material needs, possibly living abroad for a while.

## The strongest definition: sell it to a good operator

- Imagine selling the business to a high-quality operator who hires people to do all the work.
- Founders wear many hats, so some roles are part-time. You may be excellent at some (the new owner must hire great talent at market rates) and poor at others (the new owner can skimp).
- The company is profitable if it would still be profitable in that scenario.
- In his words: profitable "on its own power," not "it + a founder pouring their heart and soul into every waking hour to the limit of their endurance and ability, for free."

## Slow starts are normal, including his own

- Almost no company explodes out of the blocks, including all the successful ones in all industries. A slow start is expected, even for the next 37signals, Smart Bear or WP Engine.
- WP Engine looked like this for its first 9 months, then was chaos for two years.
- At Smart Bear it took 2.5 years before he could hire one employee, and even then at 1/4 of the salary the person deserved (and later earned). Eventually the company made millions of dollars a year in profit, but not for years.
- His advice: stop saying "profitable from day one," drop the feather-fluffing, face your strengths, challenges and resources, and set goals to make the business profitable for real.
