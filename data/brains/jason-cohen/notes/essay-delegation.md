## Delegation does not scale a company; hiring people better than the founder does

- Jason argues that "delegation" does not scale a business. What does is "creating a team that is better than the current team. Including you."
- Founding a company is a selfish act, fueled by youth and energy. Founder skills (coding, design, salesmanship) are "probably right enough" early on.
- Those skills do not help build a 100-engineer team balancing quality and speed, an international sales team, a consistent global brand, or cash-flow management once the P&L is "(in millions)".
- Jason's job definition for any founder or manager: "hire people who are better than you at every position," because only then is the organization increasing its strength and abilities.

## Why the "do it myself, then hire someone to do it my way" model fails

- The common founder pattern Jason says he followed himself when young and naïve:
  1. First I'll do it myself.
  2. Then I'll understand it.
  3. Then I'll hire and instruct a new person to do it the right way (my way).
- The result is "a team that is not materially better than the founder, at anything." The company is then never very good at more than one or two things.
- That is bad for the company, employees, customers, and ultimately the founder.
- He ties this to the idea that if you think a position isn't useful, it is because you have never worked with greatness in that position.
- Hiring someone who knows what you know and works like you work gets you a substitute for your own not-world-class performance. You are lucky to even get that; you might hire worse.

## Micromanaging hires is always bad management

- Jason's logic: if you hire and then micromanage, or constantly "fix" what others do, you are a bad manager.
  - If the person is incapable, you hired the wrong person.
  - If the person is capable, you are getting in their way and wasting your time.
  - If the person isn't better than you, you aren't improving the organization.
  - If the person is better than you, micromanagement destroys that improvement, their morale, and your schedule.
- In every case, "it's bad management, and it's your fault."
- He quotes Steve Jobs: "It doesn't make sense to hire smart people and then tell them what to do. We hire smart people so they can tell *us* what to do."

## Delegation vs. team-building

- Delegation: you still own the outcome, and someone else does the work. You assign away lesser jobs so you can be even more heroic, so it is still about you.
- Team-building: the team is trusted to own the work and its obligations, figures out and executes the details, and is responsible for raising its own expectations of itself.
- Jason's line: "You're still in charge, but the team is in command."
- At larger scale the goal is building teams that themselves grow and create greatness. The founder is not attracting, identifying and retaining greatness alone; the founder builds teams who do that. Jason calls this the best definition of "team-building."
- His conclusion: "Delegation isn't team-building, and thus it doesn't lead to scale, nor to greatness." Hiring "up" creates greatness and the space for scale.

## Bottlenecks and resilience

- Under delegation you are still the bottleneck, just with a slightly wider neck.
- Team-building removes the bottleneck because the team can be as wide as needed. The best teams measure their own necks and decide how and when to widen further.
- It also derisks the company by moving it from brittle to resilient:
  - With delegation alone, if one person gets sick a deadline is missed, and if someone leaves a strategy is not executed.
  - With team-building there is group knowledge, so illness or departures are baked into the plan.

## Applying the rule to yourself, including stepping down as CEO

- No one is exempt. A technical founder will readily agree she is not the best person to build a global sales organization.
- The harder test: even where she is excellent, it is still her job to find people who are more excellent, not just at individual tasks but at building entire teams.
- Jason did this multiple times at WP Engine, including no longer being the CEO.

## Managing people who are better than you

- Jason acknowledges the consequence: leaders manage a set of people, all better qualified than the leader to do their jobs.
- He admits this is difficult (how you argue with them, how you earn their respect and confidence), but says it is the leader's job, because anything less "is by definition holding the company back."
- His bar: if you can't handle that, don't be a manager, a leader, or the CEO. The company and its employees deserve a real leader.
- The end state: convert the selfishness and egocentrism needed to start a company into an "egoless, outward facing, empowering, team-growing organization," where you are never the most knowledgeable and experienced person in the room because you've surrounded yourself with greatness, who each do the same.
