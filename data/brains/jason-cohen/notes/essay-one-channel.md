## Go deep on one marketing channel you already understand

Jason argues early startups win by concentrating on a single marketing channel, not by spreading across many. Founders who spread thin rarely get off the ground.

- Successful founders almost never say they ran many channels that each "worked a little bit" and added up to a river.
- Spreading "precious money and even more precious time over many channels doesn't get a company off the ground."
- The usual pattern is that a company finds **one channel that really works**, then plumbs it to its maximum inventory before layering on additional channels.
- The goal on that channel is to double the business, not to dabble.

## How to pick the one channel

Jason says "spray and pray" is a poor way to find the answer. Start instead with the handful of channels you personally use and understand.

- If you are a Twitter person, spend a few months building a corporate Twitter presence and buying Twitter ads. This holds even if you rarely use Facebook and can't see the appeal of Pinterest.
- If you think Twitter is "all trash and politics" but find Pinterest full of appealing visuals, build shareable images and buy Pinterest ads.
- Test the channel for a few months, focused on one channel at a time.

## Why personal fit with a channel matters

Jason's reasoning is that success in social marketing channels comes from resonance between your personality and the norms and language of that channel.

- The best chance of resonance is when it arises naturally.
- Resonance doesn't come from A/B testing a persona. His example is agonizing over whether to be "more humorous and sarcastic or more serious and profound" on Twitter.
- If a channel's culture baffles you, you won't just jump in and develop a presence that resonates with people you are "predisposed to despise at worst and just not understand at best."
- His example is someone who finds Facebook culture fake: people pretending to be "thoughtfully happy and thoughtlessly witty" while privately feeling they fall short of their friends.

## Stay in your comfort zone, but don't spread thin

Jason's summary rule: "stay with what you know, where you're comfortable, and focus on doubling your business on that channel."

- "Sometimes, getting outside your comfort zone is wrong."
- "Spreading yourself thin is even more wrong."
- Add further channels only after the first one is exhausted (see his related essay on low-budget marketing and maximum inventory).
