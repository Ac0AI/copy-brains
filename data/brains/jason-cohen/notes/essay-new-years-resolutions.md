## Self-questions beat resolutions, and the questions differ by company stage

Jason argues that New Year's resolutions don't work (he cites studies saying so). His alternative is to ask yourself good questions you haven't taken the time to ask, and to match the questions to your stage. He posed them in a December 2014 essay.

## Bootstrapper questions: double take-home profit without new hires

- Core question: "How could you double your take-home profits without additional hires?"
- His hint for the usual answer:
  - Increasing price, not cutting costs.
  - Increasing retention, not expanding to new markets.
  - Better marketing, not more features.
- Saving money is wise, but not so much that it hurts the business. Ask what the single most impactful thing is that you could spend non-trivial money on.
- Example: you may be able to spend more than you think on paid marketing.
- Thought experiment: what would you do with an extra $50,000 in cash?
- If the answer is truly game-changing, consider making it real through crowd-funding, customer prepayments, or an angel investor "who won't drive you nuts".

## Consultant questions: force a doubling of rates

- Core question: "If I forced you to double your rates, what would you have to do to justify it?"
- Prompts he gives:
  - How would your website be redesigned or rewritten?
  - Would you become more impersonal/corporate or more personal/boutique?
  - Would the service itself change, in what you deliver and when?
- Follow-up options:
  - Make some of those changes and actually raise rates.
  - Make some of them without raising rates, and win more business.
- Some changes may be relatively easy and enough to raise your rate by 50%.

## Brand-new company questions: validate with money, honestly

- Core question: "How can you get 20 people to give you $50?" He says to make sure you're asking that now.
- Be completely honest about what the market is saying, including price in the discussion.
- Work out the key thing that makes people buy.
- Consider whether you can position the same product better.
- Decide whether you're going broad/cheap or deep/expensive, and think through the implications.
- Check whether you're aggressively going after the first few customers or hanging back doing the easy stuff (product, coding, social, events).

## Post-product/market-fit, pre-scale questions: one focus and retention

- Are you focused on the single most important thing that gets you to early scale, and do you know what it is?
- Is the whole company aligned to it, with no exceptions? His test: ask any employee what the one thing is that the company must do to succeed, and they should all give the same answer.
- Have you clarified your target customer and aligned marketing, sales, and product around it?
  - That may mean saying "no" to other interesting opportunities, including one-off features or consulting work.
- Are you aggressively discovering and removing barriers to sale?
- He adds that you should probably be doing something to increase retention.

## Scale-phase questions: what breaks in six months

- Focus on the most important things. What is minor today will be trivial in three months. What is important but only a minor irritant today will become critical and massive in three months.
- Identify the "Rocks" that will move the needle and organize teams around them.
- Are you spending a seemingly disproportionate amount of time sourcing and hiring, finding "10xers"?
- Are you driving toward the unit economics needed to eventually be profitable?
- Ask: "What is going to be broken in six months and thus needs to be addressed today?"
