## Cris's line: "If your own story doesn't inspire you, it's time to change it"

In a Thunder Substack post, Andreea Popa writes that Cris Pop shared a sentence that changed her mindset: "If your own story doesn't inspire you, it's time to change it." She says she can't recall exactly when he said it, but she remembers how it landed, "like a moment that shifts your perspective entirely." Before that, she writes, she had never asked herself whether the story she lived inspired her. She was comfortable in her own bubble and accepted that "this was simply how things were." The rest of the post is her own reading of Cris's line, not something the source attributes to him.

## Why the stories we live by shape identity and choices

Thunder's Substack argues, through Andreea Popa, that everyone lives by a story about who they are, where they've been and what they're capable of. That story shapes identity, choices and ultimately life. The problem comes when the story "no longer inspires us," when it feels too small or too limiting. Popa lists the self-limiting lines people repeat without noticing how much they hold them back:

- "I'm just not good at that."
- "I'm not the kind of person who..."
- "This is just who I am."

Her claim is that these outdated narratives work quietly and that most people don't realize they are repeating them.

## Your story is not set in stone: you are not your mistakes

Andreea Popa writes that a person is not the mistakes they've made, the failures they fear, or "the version of yourself that settled, played small, or got stuck." She illustrates this with her own padel experience (her anecdote, not Cris's). She obsesses over missed shots, and the good shots and won points fade into the background. She saw her playing partner do the same: after one missed shot the partner fell into frustration and forgot how well she had played until then. Popa reminded her not to let one shot define her game.

Her lesson is that we replay the misses and downplay the wins. We carry one moment "as if it defines the whole match," but it doesn't and shouldn't. Stories can evolve, and if the current chapter feels uninspiring, "you have the power and the responsibility to rewrite the next one."

## Five reflection questions for rewriting your story

Under the heading "Turn the page," Popa offers questions to ask honestly in order to begin changing your story:

- Do my words about myself make me feel stronger or weaker?
- What parts of my story do I keep repeating that no longer serve me?
- Am I following my own path or what others expect?
- What would my story sound like if I believed in myself more?
- What beliefs about myself do I need to challenge or let go of?

She advises letting the story "sing with possibility, rooted in your values and expanding toward the future you envision."

## An uninspiring chapter means readiness for growth, not failure

The post argues that an uninspiring story does not mean someone has failed: "It means you're ready for growth." Popa frames the choice as staying stuck in a story that drains you or rising into one that empowers you. She closes with two forward-looking questions:

- What story do you want to tell a year from now?
- What needs to change today for that to happen?

The post treats self-reflection and personal narrative as a leadership starting point. It places the responsibility for rewriting the story on the individual, rooted in their own values rather than other people's expectations.
