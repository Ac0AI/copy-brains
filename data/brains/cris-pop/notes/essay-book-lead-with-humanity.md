## Leadership as a capacity in every person: the "Lead With Humanity" chapter

Thunder's leadership book, "Leadership Before It Has a Name" (2025, written by Andreea Popa and coordinated by Cris Pop), opens its "Lead With Humanity" chapter with the claim that "every human being carries something inside that makes them capable of leading." The chapter calls this "a spark," "a conviction," and "a quiet instinct to lift, build, and protect." It says the job of leadership is to find that spark, feed it, and fire it, "hour by hour. Day by day. Human by human." The chapter treats leadership as something already present in people, not a title or a position. It adds that every story that broke the ordinary "started with humans". These were people who "chose to be real in a world that rewards pretending" and who "stood up before they felt ready."

## Start now: leadership is something you do, not something you wait for

The chapter argues that leadership "won't come by itself. You have to do it. Now." It tells readers to stop thinking and dreaming about leadership and to start doing it ("Get after it."). It asks them to commit to the process "not because it's glamorous, but because it's yours." In this view leadership is built through repeated practice:

- Every setback sharpens you.
- Every small win adds weight to your steps.
- Every hard conversation and every course correction is "leadership being built."

The book frames growth as cumulative and ordinary. It does not rely on a single defining moment.

## The leadership behaviors the chapter lists

The chapter says to lead "with intention. With presence. With a willingness to get it wrong and grow anyway." It lists concrete behaviors:

- Show up before you feel ready.
- Make decisions with care.
- Share what you're learning.
- Ask questions.
- Offer your hand.
- When the moment asks for it, step forward.

The book argues that readiness is not a precondition for leading, and that being willing to be wrong is part of the work. It also stresses sharing and helping others as part of leading, alongside stepping forward. It ends this passage by saying legacy begins "inside ordinary days, shaped by extraordinary integrity."

## "We're rooting for you": Thunder's stance toward people trying to keep their humanity

Thunder's leadership book closes the chapter with a direct address to readers. It says that "for everything you do to keep your humanity intact, we're rooting for you." The authors note that people often need someone in their corner to offer a vote of confidence, but "it rarely comes." The chapter sets out to be that moment: "you'll always have us."

The book is open about the limits of this support. It says "We may not be able to support you in the ways you need most, like offering a job or a leadership role," but promises to be there as a friend. It tells readers to reach out through social media, email, or the team's personal accounts, and says "we're listening."

## Mentorship, the newsletter, and paying it forward

The chapter points readers to two Thunder resources for continuing the journey:

- **A mentorship program**, which the book says is "designed to help you grow as a leader by focusing on your foundation first."
- **A newsletter**, which will keep sharing insights on the leadership path.

The chapter's line "Don't fight alone" frames leadership development as something done with others. It then asks readers to pay it forward: "Someone near you is waiting to be invited into their own courage. Quiet revolutions start this way." The book presents leadership as something passed from person to person through invitation and encouragement. It closes with: "Keep going. We see you. We're with you. And we're always rooting for you."
