## Trust over control: how Cris builds leaders instead of workers

Cris's core claim is that if the goal is building leaders, the method cannot be control. He frames his role as "not about drawing boxes around people" but "breaking the walls that confine them." He says the aim is recognizing raw potential and nurturing it "even when it takes us places we didn't plan to go."

- "Micromanagement builds workers. Trust builds giants. And the world is starving for giants."
- "People grow in freedom. Trust is the ultimate accelerant."
- "When people feel trusted, they rise. They don't just deliver, they soar."
- He argues that trust produces things "no amount of micromanagement could ever manufacture."
- He says expectations placed on people are "usually too small to begin with."

## Saying yes to out-of-role requests: the Sabina example

Two interns, Mălina and Sabina, joined Thunder's dev internship program about two months before the essay (September 2025). A few weeks in, Sabina asked to write and lend her voice to Thunder's story, which is outside a dev intern's role.

- Cris says he did not see this as stepping out of role. He saw "a spark beginning to be ignited" and said yes.
- Sabina wrote a personal first-person piece (her doubts, fears and returning hope) that he published in the essay.
- His takeaway: if he had focused on job descriptions and staying in lanes, or "managing instead of listening," her voice "would have stayed buried" and the story would never have been told.
- He asks the leader's question: how many people sit in meetings, follow processes and complete tasks while carrying stories like this inside them?
- The principle: bet on potential instead of policing boundaries, and you get transformation.

## How Thunder hires: warm interviews that look for the person

Sabina's account shows the interview process Cris and his team run for the internship.

- The technical interview was built to be warm. Two interviewers (one she vaguely knew from a lab, one a stranger) were kind, spoke to her "not as a candidate, but as a person," offered patient smiles when anxiety made her stumble, and did not pounce on weaknesses.
- They asked about her passions and her vision for the future, and listened.
- The final interview was with Cris. She says it "wasn't about my qualifications, it was about my essence." His questions were: what makes you tick, and what lights that fire in your soul?
- Cris entered "not as a titan of industry, but as a person," and smiled.
- Sabina's reaction to being hired was pressure as well as relief ("Now I had to be worth the chance they took"), which shows that the trust placed in someone also creates motivation.
- Thunder also recruits through visible community presence: she found it by slipping into their talks and labs and saw a community before applying.

## Onboarding: give permission to learn at your own pace

Sabina describes the first week as the thing that changed her relationship with the work.

- The team started interns with familiar faces from the interviews and gave them "permission": to take their time, to learn at their own pace, to ask, to question, to be imperfect.
- They took interns back to the bare bones of web development, stripping away the academic pressure that had smothered her passion.
- Result by her account: she went from dreading the morning alarm to looking forward to the next challenge, and her old love for coding returned.
- Her day-one instinct was to be flawless and give nobody a reason to regret hiring her. Removing that pressure through explicit permission is what let curiosity replace fear.

## Culture: an ecosystem, not a zero-sum corporation

Cris publishes Sabina's description of Thunder as the culture he has built, and endorses it as "our story."

- Contrast with the zero-sum world she came from, where you don't help the person next to you for fear they'll take your spot.
- Thunder's operating idea is that "we all grow better together." Every person is a pillar, and their wins are each other's wins.
- Knowledge sharing is treated as the only way to build something that lasts, not as a weakness.
- Safety is the precondition: Sabina says she could be honest only once she felt "safe enough," and Cris says that is when people bring their whole selves to the table and create "what they create will be unforgettable."
- The outcome Cris points to: someone who arrived demoralized now wants to "help build more places like it," so the culture reproduces leaders who want to build the same kind of place.
