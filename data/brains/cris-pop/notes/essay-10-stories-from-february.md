## Group coaching and the rules-versus-guidelines question

In a Thunder Substack post ("10 stories from February"), Andreea Popa describes Thunder's first group coaching session with Tünde, focused on deepening relationships, being more connected and intentional with one another. In the coming months the team planned to split into two groups to share perspectives on communication, friendship at work, and how stronger relationships shape both work and life.

Cris also began 1:1 coaching with Tünde to find areas where he can grow as a leader. In one session she asked him: "Would you rather set rules or offer guidelines?" Andreea Popa writes that this question "opened something up." Her reading: rules make people stop feeling they own what they do, so work becomes compliance, "ticking boxes and staying out of trouble," with no heart in it. Guidelines trust people to find the path; the framework is given and they fill in the details, which creates ownership. People then choose how to show up, and the focus shifts from getting the job done to "who they're becoming in the process." Her takeaway: "When you give people guidelines instead of rules, they take ownership, and in doing so, they work on their own becoming."

## Transferring recruitment interviews to the team

Thunder's Substack describes February as the month recruitment ownership was "fully transferred." Interviews, which had mostly been Cris's territory, were run by Florina, Iuli, and Cosmin, who decided who belongs on the team. Andreea Popa notes the role reversal: not long before, they had been the candidates. Cris trusted them to do it well.

Cosmin said the experience strengthened his own understanding of key concepts, since explaining ideas to candidates forced him to structure his thinking and revisit fundamentals ("Teaching, in a way, becomes learning"). The post traces Florina's path at Thunder: intern, contributor, developer, initiative owner, interviewer, and mentor to colleagues such as Mălina. The author's view: growth like this is "built through trust, ownership, and consistency. You can't manufacture it. You can only create the conditions for it, and then get out of the way."

## The hackathon as a hiring process

Thunder's Substack describes a recruitment hackathon held February 20-21. Out of nearly 200 applications, nine participants were selected to spend a weekend building a mission-driven software product. They split into three teams, and most projects centered on skills and meaningful exchanges between people.

Cris opened the day by sharing Thunder's culture, values, and the thinking behind how the company works. Florina guided the technical side so each team had clarity and support. The tone mixed deep focus with lightness: pizza, laughter, conversation about hobbies between coding sessions. Some participants stayed until 4 AM, and Sergiu worked continuously until 10:30 PM the next day without sleeping. On day two, after breakfast and lunch together, each team presented.

Andreea Popa writes that what stood out was the thoughtfulness of the ideas, how the teams worked together, and "care in what they built." Denis and Andrei joined Thunder part-time, a decision based on long-term fit, team dynamics, and shared values. For others, the door stays open, with Thunder aiming to support all participants through future initiatives, tools, and mentorship.

## Culture as stepping into a colleague's world

In a Thunder Substack post, Andreea Popa describes a team outing to BT Arena on February 11 to watch U-BT play basketball. It mattered because Anda, a huge basketball fan and former player, was there, and seeing the game through her eyes made it feel more alive. The author writes that there is "something powerful about stepping into someone else's world for an evening."

The stated culture goal: someone's passion doesn't stay theirs alone but becomes something everyone experiences together. "We weren't just there for the game. We were there because it matters to her, and that's reason enough." The post's closing invitation extends this: think of a colleague or friend, identify what lights them up (a sport, hobby, or passion they rarely get to share), and show up for it.

## Bookclub, podcast, and ceasing to wait for the right moment

Thunder's Substack reports that bookclubs returned on February 12, with four people presenting four books from four different worlds; some of the group continued to a screening of Hamnet, Gabi's pick. Andreea Popa shared her own journey, from authoritarian systems to growing inside a workplace that puts people first, and the shift from seeing leadership as a title to understanding it as "a lifelong process of becoming," which she says sits at the heart of Thunder's book.
