## Learning only counts once it changes how you live

Cris argues that consuming insight is not the same as becoming someone different. "Thinking that learning = becoming" is the trap. He says becoming takes "integration, not just inspiration." We pile new knowledge on top of old knowledge "like a to-do list we'll never touch," and our lives don't shift.

His test: how much of what you've learned actually shows up in how you live?

## An insight without a system is a ghost

- Ideas look good in note apps and make you feel wise, but until you put them to work they are "expensive decorations in your brain."
- Cris does not rush to the next book or podcast after finishing one. He won't move on until he has "squeezed it for all it's worth."
- He rereads the same book a few times and rewatches podcasts, because most things don't land deeply the first time. He sticks with a resource until it stops giving him something new.
- His rule: "If it doesn't live in your day-to-day life, it doesn't live at all."

**Tactic: one idea, one week.** Build something around a single idea. Options he gives:
- Turn it into a question you ask every time you get the chance.
- Make a small template.
- Teach it to someone (he jokes: "your cat").

## Cut inputs that don't serve a problem you have now

- You don't need more input; you need fewer mental and emotional tabs open and fewer layers between you and what matters.
- Delete noise "until the signal rings clear."
- Be ruthless, even with good content, "especially the good stuff that keeps you comfortable."
- Filter question: "Is this solving a problem I'm facing right now?" If not, shelve it or burn it.
- His summary: "Clarity needs subtraction." It is less about what you know and more about what you're willing to drop.

## Don't teach what you haven't lived

- "Truth wears boots, not bullet points." It walks, stumbles and scars before it teaches.
- Recycling someone else's genius doesn't make you wise; insight without experience is "just noise in a nicer font."
- Three checks before sharing an idea:
  - If you haven't tested it, don't preach it.
  - If you haven't failed with it, don't share it.
  - If you don't feel it in your bones, you're not ready to pass it on.
- His line: "Teach from the scars, not the scripts."

## Listen diagnostically before you teach

- Cris wants listening "like a thief in a museum": the job is not to respond but to steal the essence of what the other person can't quite say.
- He rejects "active listening" as a phrase hugged to death by mediocre leadership books and calls his version diagnostic listening: hearing the silence and tuning to "the frequency of what's unsaid."
- Why it matters for anyone who teaches, sells or builds: you can't distill knowledge if you don't know what people are hungry to understand, and you can't teach with precision without first listening "with obsession."
- Sequence he advises: before you craft the insight, stay quiet long enough to hear the need; before you offer clarity, find where the confusion actually lives.
- "Knowledge is not something you just teach. It's something you extract." Extraction starts with listening deep enough that their unsaid thoughts "start whispering back."
- Resource he recommends: Charles Duhigg's TEDx talk "The science behind dramatically better conversations."

## Be an integrator, not a collector

- Cris calls for "braver integrators": people who stop collecting and start translating, stop consuming and start creating, stop nodding and start changing.
- "Everything you've ever needed is already in your bookmarks folder. You just haven't done anything with it."
- To understand why some ideas stick and others vanish, he recommends *Made to Stick* by Chip Heath and Dan Heath.
- He was giving a 5-minute talk on the topic at the Offbeat event (2025).
