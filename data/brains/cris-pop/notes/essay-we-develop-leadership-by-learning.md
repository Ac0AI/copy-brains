## Thunder's Substack: leadership starts with leading yourself

In a Thunder Substack post on The Thunder Way (published 2025-09-05), Andreea Popa argues that "we develop leadership not by holding a title, but by practicing the activities that teach us to lead ourselves." The post is written by Andreea Popa of the Thunder team, not by Cris Pop. It profiles Manu, a Thunder team member who is also a runner and Spartan race competitor, as an example of "quiet leadership that inspires growth." Her approach to code, collaboration and races is presented as one way of moving through challenges: excellence as a habit, not a single act.

## Thunder's Substack on discipline: have a plan and stick to it

Thunder's Substack post "We develop leadership by learning to lead ourselves" shares Manu's discipline practice, in her words. Her "secret is having a plan": when she sets a goal, she creates a plan and sticks to it. "Rain, snow, or scorching heat doesn't matter, if I've scheduled four runs in a week, I complete them." She adds a personal rule: never skip more than two consecutive days. In her words, "the voice saying 'not today' disappears because there are no excuses." Andreea Popa frames this as showing up regardless of conditions, mood or excuses, and as the way discipline becomes freedom: "progress is built step by step."

## Thunder's Substack on pushing past perceived limits

The post's Q&A with Manu covers the biggest mental barrier she has faced. She describes moments in training and races when she felt she could not go any further. Her lesson: "when you feel like you can't, you actually can go a little more. Our bodies are stronger than we think, and our minds just need a small push to keep moving." Andreea Popa lists this as the first of six elements of leadership in the post: "the courage to take one more step." Her message to her younger self is "Believe in yourself, you're capable of more than you imagine," which the post calls "the belief that shapes all other beliefs."

## Thunder's Substack on community over competition

Manu describes her first Spartan race as full of emotion and uncertainty. She had trail-running experience, but Spartan races differ because "it's not just about you; it's about community." After racing in her own country and abroad, she now sees them as a team experience, where people offer encouragement or "a hand over a difficult wall." Her words: "Nobody competes against each other; we all share the same goal: to finish the race together." Andreea Popa treats this as the post's version of true strength, which "lives in lifting others up." The post concludes that leaders practice "lifting others as we climb."

## Thunder's Substack on endings as beginnings (the infinite mindset)

Asked how she avoids feeling low after a big goal such as a marathon, Manu explains that she has never felt discouraged after reaching one, because there is always something to improve: a longer distance or a faster time. Last year she ran her first half marathon, and then decided to aim for a full marathon. "Every achievement opens the door to a new challenge." Her closing advice: "There's no finish line for ambition. Every goal you reach is just the beginning of the next challenge. Enjoy the journey, the progress, and the person you're becoming with every step you take." Andreea Popa calls this "the infinite mindset where endings become beginnings" and "the continuous act of becoming." The post's subtitle is "Every finish line is simply the start of a new journey."

## The six-part summary of leadership in the post

Andreea Popa distills Manu's story into six elements of "leadership distilled to its essence":
- The courage to take one more step.
- The commitment to show up regardless of conditions, mood or excuses.
- True strength that lives in lifting others up.
- The belief that shapes all other beliefs: believing in yourself.
- The infinite mindset, where endings become beginnings.
- The continuous act of becoming.

Her conclusion is that these practices, "showing up when it's hard, pushing a little further than we think we can, lifting others as we climb, and seeing every ending as the start of something new", are the steps that "turn discipline into freedom and action into quiet, lasting leadership."
