## Utopia as balance: Thunder's Substack on leadership as the harmony between light and darkness

In a Thunder Substack post, Andreea Popa writes that leadership, like utopia, is "not perfect, not free of struggle, but a balance between courage and vulnerability, vision and reality, ambition and care." The post frames utopia as the balance of joy and sorrow, the ordinary and the extraordinary, rather than a distant ideal. Thunder's Substack argues leadership invites deeper questions: what it means to live authentically, how to grow into who we are meant to become, and how to find possibility in everyday moments. This is the author's reflection and an interview with her colleague Cosmin, not words from Cris Pop.

## Leadership as becoming "the kind of human others want to be around"

Andreea Popa writes in Thunder's Substack that through emotions and the music we listen to, "we begin to see leadership differently. Not as a set of techniques to master, but as the art of becoming the kind of human others want to be around." She describes three traits of such a person:
- Someone who gives emotions permission to be real.
- Someone who finds balance between light and darkness.
- Someone who uses their passions to grow into who they're meant to become.

The post presents this as a counter to technique-driven views of leadership: the emphasis is on character and authenticity rather than methods.

## The leaders who stay with us are "brave enough to be human"

Andreea Popa writes that the leaders who have truly stayed with her were the ones brave enough to be human. They helped her see possibilities she couldn't see before and made her feel she had permission to be herself. She names her colleague Cosmin as an example of this kind of leader. The Substack post positions permission to be yourself, and the ability to open up new possibilities for others, as the lasting marks of leadership, more than results or authority.

## "Harmony we create together": people over task

According to Andreea Popa, Cosmin often says he would rather do work he doesn't love with people he cares about than the other way around. She reads this as a reminder that "leadership is less about the task itself and more about the harmony we create together." Thunder's Substack uses this to argue that the quality of relationships on a team matters more than how enjoyable the work itself is.

## Cosmin's interview: music as emotional permission and authenticity

In the interview, Cosmin says music "takes your emotions and gives them permission to come into reality." He says the version of himself that listens to music the loudest is the truest, allowing him to be brave and loving, and to shed tears, which he rarely does. He adds that without music he would likely lack his confidence and his personal style, and might never have formed the purpose of "bringing light into people's lives." Thunder's Substack uses this as the basis for the first of its leadership lessons, the courage to feel authentically.

## Cosmin on utopia: balance, ordinary moments, and growth

Cosmin explains that his idea of utopia comes from Travis Scott's album *Utopia*, which treats light and darkness, happiness and suffering, as combined. He argues that laughing until you cry would be impossible "if life was only funny," and that "utopia is a balance of all things, take some steps back, you might be living it some days," such as a cozy night at home with a partner or friends. He also says music pushes him to grow: he travelled to several countries to see favorite artists live, which "forced me to be the type of person that can afford that," and he wants to become "the type of human those people would like to meet." It brings him close to believing "that anything in this life is possible."

## The symphony of leadership: five lessons from Cosmin's lens

The Substack post summarizes Cosmin's reflections as five leadership themes, each tied to a quote:
- **The courage to feel authentically:** music gives emotions permission to become real.
- **Shaped by growth:** being pushed to become the kind of person your idols would want to meet.
- **The freedom to imagine and create:** "Music is anything you want it to be and can be used as a tool to achieve whatever you'd like."
- **Utopia in the ordinary moments:** balance of all things, possibly already lived.
- **The boundless self:** the belief that anything in this life is possible.

The post closes by asking readers whether they are listening to what this teaches about "who we could become."
