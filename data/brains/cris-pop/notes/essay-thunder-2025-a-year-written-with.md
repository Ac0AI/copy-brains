## The Thunderometer: a shared compass of principles for when things feel unclear or rushed

In a Thunder Substack post reviewing 2025, Andreea Popa describes the Thunderometer. Thunder created it because "some things can't be measured in numbers, but they still need attention." The team wanted a shared way to check in with themselves, with each other and with the work.

She describes it as a set of principles the team returns to when things feel "unclear, heavy, or rushed." Thunder uses it to:
- stay honest with itself
- notice when it is drifting from its values
- catch moments when "the pace gets louder than the purpose"
- catch moments when "good enough starts replacing care"

The post says it works on three levels:
- **Relationships:** lead with trust, listen with intention, uplift one another.
- **Work:** craft with meaning, decisions made consciously, responsibility for what you leave behind.
- **Personal:** self-leadership through owning your choices, your energy and your standards.

Popa calls it "a compass." It doesn't tell people what to do. It helps them sense whether they are moving in the right direction: toward work they're proud of, relationships that feel real, and a way of being that stays true "no matter how fast we grow."

## Lessons from the 2025 work: self-leadership in development, presence in marketing

Andreea Popa writes in Thunder's Substack that every piece of Thunder's 2025 work "carried the trust we placed in each other and the daily decision to show up fully, to care about both the work and the people we share it with." She draws one lesson from each side of the company.

**Development.** Colleagues took on complex systems, unfamiliar technologies and higher expectations with "a strong sense of self-leadership." They understood that "quality is something you take responsibility for." Time spent thinking before building paid off, and many of the best solutions came from asking the right questions. Her takeaway: "Lead yourself through the work. Take ownership beyond your tasks, hold your standards high, and build with intention. Create work that carries value beyond yourself."

**Marketing.** The focus moved closer to people. Popa writes that digital presence has its place, but "real connection still happens soul to soul." Being part of the community meant being present, listening and organizing events where ideas grew stronger by being shared. Her takeaway: "Show up where people are. Pull chairs to the same table, invest in gathering together, and build community the timeless way."

## Collaboration over competition: RebelDot, BitStone, Linnify and the student tickets

The post says Thunder has "never bought into the idea of competition." In 2025 it connected with Cluj companies that share its vision: RebelDot, BitStone and Linnify. The stated aim is "coming together and contributing to a community worth being part of, side by side."

Popa singles out that these companies invested in students by buying tickets to The Thunder Way. She says this kind of faith in the next generation gives students "trust, wings, and the courage to chart their own path."

The collaboration with Linnify led to two events in New York:
- **AI beyond the BUZZ**, with speakers from Endava, Siena AI and TypingDNA.
- **A panel of Romanian tech leaders and business owners.** Cris Pop took part as founder and leader of Thunder, alongside Simona Vasile (BDO), Andrei Papancea (NLX) and Gabriel Păunescu (Naologic).

Popa says the events sharpened the need to stay connected to tech communities: "Technology doesn't evolve in isolation, and neither should the people building it." Thunder wants to share what it learns and stay close to ecosystem conversations.

Thunder also spent the year building a platform to broadcast events at Teatrul Nației, the cultural hub founded by Dragoș Pătraru. Popa says it reminds the team that "the work we do is only as meaningful as the people it serves."

## The Thunder Way event: beginning before certainty

Popa writes that the Thunder Way idea lived quietly inside the team for more than a year, "paired with doubt, questions, and the feeling that it might be too big." In 2025, "instead of waiting for certainty, we chose to begin." The event took place at Cinema Florin Piersic.

She says it was born from a line the team keeps following: "before a path exists, there is always a pulse. You just have to listen to it, and step in fully, body and soul."

The post frames the year as a lesson in momentum: it "comes not from having everything figured out, but from showing up fully, believing in what you're creating, and trusting that the people around you will meet you there."

Speakers included Dragoș Pătraru (simplicity and responsibility toward each other), Andra Pintican (work doesn't have to hurt to have meaning), Marius Balo (resilience and inner freedom) and Alexandra Crăciun (curiosity and authenticity). Popa says Cris Pop's talk reminded the audience that "when you lead with belief in others and a commitment to service, you lift a vision that carries the weight of many hearts and minds."

## Thunder Talks, Bookclubs and student events: community built around people

Thunder's Substack describes how Thunder ran events "for people, by people" in 2025. Thunder Talks included voices from outside the team:
- Tudor Ciuleanu on collaborative entrepreneurship and honest communication at work.
- Cristina Frei on drawing your own path.
- Tünde Macarie on saying less but meaning more.
- Mădălina Hotca on trusting yourself and fighting for your vision when no one else does.

According to Popa, Cris "contributed to each conversation with his own stories and struggles, always seeking the next layer of truth and searching for the questions that would take us all deeper."

Cris also guided the final event of the year, with Cluj Startups at the opening of Founders Hive. The question was "what does leadership look like before it has a name?" He drew from the book and from years of learning "what leadership looks like when it's stripped of the polish."

Bookclubs continued as a space where people "came thirsty for meaning and left feeling less alone." Marius Balo joined to share his book "Păturica roz."

On students, Popa argues that the education system often leaves young people "at the edge of the real world, unprepared." She says "preparing them for careers just feels hollow" next to helping them understand who they are and who they can become, and know they have a community behind them.

## "Leadership Before It Has a Name": the book as a call back to being

Popa writes that the book began as a simple guide to capture Thunder's philosophy and what leadership looks like "when you strip away all the polished theories." As they wrote, it grew into a book, one the team "never planned to write."

She says the book is proof of "how essential humanity is in everything we do." It is a reminder of how easy it is to get caught up in "the doing, the building, the achieving, and lose sight of the being," and it calls the team back to "who we are beneath all the roles we play and the masks we sometimes wear."

Its core claim, in her words: "leadership is not about titles, nor about authority. It will always come back to showing up, fully human, fully present, and leading from that place of authenticity." She calls it a promise that the team will never drift too far from who they are and are becoming, however far they go.

## Welcoming interns and gathering as a team

Popa writes that two interns from the AI web internship, Mălina and Sabina, joined Thunder in 2025.
- Mălina is described as dedicated and dependable, with a warm, positive presence.
- Sabina arrived with "a fire for tech and a hunger to learn." She wrote candidly about her journey before and during the internship, and the team found her words "profound, honest, and moving."

Popa says they "arrived as two, but worked as one," leaning on and challenging each other in a way that "raised the bar for everyone around them."

The team also travels together. In 2025 that meant a winter trip to the mountains with skiing and snowboarding, Amsterdam, New York, and a closing gathering in a cabin near Cluj-Napoca to reflect on the year.

For 2026, the post names events in Berlin, The Thunder Way in Bucharest (June) and Cluj-Napoca (November), one stop in Zurich and plans to return to the United States. Thunder Talks will likely drop to two or three "with the same depth and intention." Thunder will keep running student events and Bookclubs.
