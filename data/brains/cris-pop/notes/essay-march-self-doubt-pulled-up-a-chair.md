## Thunder's Substack on organizing events abroad: the Austin SXSW empty room

In a Thunder Substack post titled "March: Self-doubt pulled up a chair," Andreea Popa writes about Thunder taking its community-building work to the United States, which she calls unusual for a small company from Cluj. The stated belief is that exposing the team to different cultures and ways of thinking is how Thunder breaks "the ceiling of what we think is possible." The first two New York events went well, so the team felt confident going into Austin during SXSW.

At Austin, 70 people signed up, many on the same day, yet only seven walked in. Cris, Deni and Gabi stood in a room that was supposed to be full. The post says none of them knew what to say, because none had been there before. The lesson the team drew: during SXSW people sign up for dozens of events and attend only a few. This was "a cultural nuance, and a local rule nobody told us," learned only by being in enough rooms. The post's takeaway is "You learn the room by being in it."

## Thunder's Substack on how Thunder reads the Austin setback

Andreea Popa writes that the empty chairs "don't tell the whole story." Thunder organized the Austin event with Linnify. Cătălin and his team helped find the venue and connect with people, and then took the Thunder team to live concerts and around the city, creating space for informal conversations that, the post says, are "the foundation of any good long-term relationship." Through Cătălin the team met Mir, a founder running a business that makes millions, described as humble and as someone who leads by investing in people first. The post says meeting him in the middle of what felt like failure showed that a company can grow and still stay grounded in what matters.

The post also credits Cris, Deni and Gabi for staying late, joining meetings and working on projects alongside the event, noting that being in the States "comes with a lot of hard work behind the scenes."

The wider point: growth is not a straight line. "Sometimes you win first. Sometimes you lose twice. The order doesn't define the outcome." Thunder's stance: "We'll keep trying. We'll keep failing. We'll keep doing it."

## Thunder's Substack on the Berlin event and "culture is behaviour"

Thunder's Substack describes a Berlin event on the theme "Culture under pressure," run with Irina, founder of Brightroom. Thunder brought its book, as it does wherever it goes. Unlike Austin, people signed up and most showed up. The post notes a ticket price, which it says created a different kind of commitment. Engagement ran deeper in the panel, the conversations after and the smaller groups, and discussions moved into real workplace situations, challenges and unfinished decisions.

The idea that resonated most with the team: you can't separate culture from behaviour. "Culture is behaviour. How you show up, how you speak, how you treat the person next to you, that's the culture." The post's closing reflection repeats it: "If you want to understand the culture of a team, just look at how people actually behave." Thunder says it is still learning how to build community across cultures but is more convinced it is worth figuring out.

## Cris's reframing of self-doubt at Thunder Talks (15th edition)

At the 15th Thunder Talks, with guest Iulia, a long-time member of the Thunder community, the post says Cris opened the conversation with a perspective on self-doubt. It is an emotion you don't need to fight but can learn to understand differently. People usually treat it as a problem that blocks or pulls them back. Seen from another angle, the post says Cris suggested, self-doubt can mean you care, are paying attention, are willing to prepare better and go deeper, and are ready to take responsibility for what you are about to do.

Andreea Popa adds that self-doubt "doesn't disappear, and maybe it's not even supposed to." What changes is the meaning, depending on how you relate to it. If you stay involved in your own process, it becomes part of how you grow.

## Iulia's reframes of self-doubt, shared at Thunder Talks

The post summarizes Iulia's talk. She recalled first feeling self-doubt as a child, and how it kept returning in different forms through her life. The post lists unspoken ideas many people grow up with: if you're scared, maybe it's not for you; you're either good at something or not; life happens to you; if you're not sure it will turn out well, don't try. Iulia didn't deny them but looked at them differently, with these reframes:

- "I want to do this and I'm scared."
- "(Almost) everything is a competence I can build."
- "Life is happening and I'm creating it."
- "If I can't guarantee the result, I will guarantee my involvement and my character."

The post connects this to Thunder's long-held intention for Thunder Talks: a space where people grow and invest in who they are becoming.

## The month's theme and practice: let go of the outcome, hold on to the work

Andreea Popa frames March as being about continuing: staying in the process when things don't look as expected, and being willing to understand, adapt and learn from different contexts. She notes the team seemed to live the self-doubt lesson before putting it into words. A near-empty room in Austin could have been read as a sign to step back, but the team went on to Berlin and then home. "At times, you don't first understand and then go through the experience. You might go through it first, and only after... you start to see what it was actually teaching you."

The post offers a small practice: when doubt shows up, ask whether it is fear telling you to stop or a request to prepare. Then change the sentence from "I want this but I'm scared" to "I want this and I'm scared," and see what changes. Another reflection carried forward: "Technology comes from humans. Don't forget to invest there first."
