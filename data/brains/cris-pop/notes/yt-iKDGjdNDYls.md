## Change starts with becoming, not with fixing the world

- Cris's core thesis: stop waiting for change from employers, society or other people. The change is "already here" and within reach, in who you choose to become.
- His rule of thumb: whatever is missing or frustrating in the world exists because "cineva, undeva" (someone, somewhere) chose not to become the kind of person who could have done it, and waited for someone else instead.
- His closing line: stop trying to change the world and focus on becoming ("să ne oprim din a căuta să schimbăm lumea și să căutăm mult mai mult să devenim").
- He treats your own person and your own growth as the project, not society.

## Choose the harder path when it is the one that puts people first

- Story from high school (informatics track, Baia Mare): he and his desk-mate Călin had an arrangement to help each other on tests, and a teacher exposed it publicly.
- Quitting informatics would have been the easy exit. He chose the harder path.
- His test: if he doesn't pick people over a small failure or scandal now, why would he pick them next time, or why would anyone else pick him over material gain?
- Lesson he draws years later: the feeling that it was impossible was a "lie" built from emotions he couldn't yet process. The ability to become better was always within reach.

## When you have no skills, find a master and offer to be an apprentice

- Situation: fresh out of university in Cluj, confused, with a degree that didn't prepare him for the local IT industry.
- Common advice was "get a corporate job: it's comfortable, pays okay, pays well once you learn." He found that boring. He wanted interesting work, people who care, and to pull toward something bigger than his own coding skill.
- His father's saying about the importance of being an apprentice guided him. His reasoning: it is hard to be an apprentice alone, so he needed a person to learn beside.
- Tactic: skip the beaten path and target smaller companies, going person to person to find a leader to apprentice under.
- He found Adi (the founder) through a friend who worked there. The company wasn't hiring and had no open roles. Cris pushed for 1-2 weeks, messaging or calling the friend daily until Adi agreed to meet "that otherwise I won't get rid of this guy."
- His pitch was not an interview: he offered to come in the morning, leave in the evening, "spăl pe jos, mătur" (mop and sweep), asked for no money, and was honest that Adi wouldn't give him eight hours a day of teaching.
- Adi had little to lose and said to come and see. That started his programming career.
- He wanted to develop as a person, not only learn code, and was curious about everything (design, history, culture) at once.

## Refuse toxic corporate tactics; the effort compounds even when it's slow

- He saw industry culture as toxic: competition where people matter little, and advancement comes from stepping over others, never admitting mistakes, and staying guarded.
- He refused to use those tools. His reasoning: work behavior gets carried home to your partner, family and friends, and you can't be cold at work and warm at home.
- Result: as he grew as a person, he became a better engineer precisely because he could understand and work with people and be there for them.
- On timing: results often don't show short term. One took about 3 years. His claim: "munca niciodată nu minte" (work never lies); he has never felt an effort was wasted.

## Keep the promise to your team: he went unpaid before laying anyone off

- He founded Tander to build a company with a healthy culture "about people," a lifestyle. He describes it to colleagues as a lifestyle company.
- Low point: the company was in its worst moment while he was in his worst personal moment, having recently lost his father.
- Standard advice from everyone: lay off half the team and carry on with the money you have, "you're an entrepreneur."
- His logic: he became an entrepreneur to build a context with good people, not to make money. He had promised the team it would be about them, not about him or profit.
- His rule: before firing anyone, fire yourself first. He did.
- For almost 1 year he kept working on client projects while effectively removed from his own company's payroll, taking no money, so the whole team could stay on board ("barca").
- His framing: the company is a boat, and it's about everyone in it, not what falls off the side so he can take it home.
- He knows this contradicts entrepreneurship books but says: "eu nu îmi optimizez viața pentru profit, eu îmi optimizez viața pentru cum simt că trăiesc zi de zi" (I optimize my life for how I feel living day to day, not for profit).
- He presents it as proof that his promise to people was real, "not marketing, not PR."
