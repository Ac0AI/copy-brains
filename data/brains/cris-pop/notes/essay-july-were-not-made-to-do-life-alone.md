## Thunder's Substack on why growth starts with the people beside you

In a July 2026 post on The Thunder Way, Thunder's Substack, Andreea Popa argues that "we're not made to do life alone." The post says Thunder was built around that idea from the start: "building relationships that go beyond the work itself" and finding communities around the world to contribute to or join. It says the people around us make good moments better, hard ones easier to carry, and keep pushing us to grow, often without either side noticing. The post is framed as small July proofs of that idea.

## Thunder's Substack on bringing in people who fit the culture, without a job posting

Andreea Popa describes two people who joined Thunder in July to learn, practice and "figure things out together." The post says Thunder never announced an opportunity: "We just crossed paths." Its stated approach is that when Thunder meets people who feel right for the culture it is trying to build, it doesn't like "letting the moment pass," and instead brings them closer to see what becomes possible. One was David, Iuli's brother, a student at the Technical University who came to start building his way into engineering. The other was Bianca, who studied photo and video in Timișoara, lived in Germany, and came back home looking for a place to put more of herself into the work she loves. Andreea writes that Thunder is always looking for people with "hunger, discipline, and willingness to work for the life they want."

## Thunder's Substack on raising the bar for the people around you

Andreea Popa uses David and his sister Iuli to make a point about example-setting. David spends his days at Thunder full-time learning to become a better engineer, and also delivers food for Wolt by bike to earn extra money. The post says he already understands that the life you want later is built through the choices you make now. It credits Iuli with setting that example "probably long before either of them realized how much it mattered." She is described as ambitious, as having carried high-stakes projects, and as having repeatedly shown what it looks like to take responsibility and deliver outstanding work. Andreea writes that one of the most powerful things we can do for each other is "to live in a way that raises the bar for the people around us."

## Thunder's Substack on doing new things scared, but not alone

The post tells how Bianca and Deni went to Linnify for a photo shoot with its team. It was new for both of them, which meant nerves, and Thunder had just brought Bianca in to help tell more of the stories happening around Thunder through visual storytelling. Deni wrote that she couldn't have done it without Bianca, who showed her things about lighting. Bianca wrote that she was convinced they would figure it out together, helped by talking beforehand about their approach, and praised Deni's ease with people, which created a relaxed atmosphere. Andreea's reading: Bianca knew her way around light and Deni around people, and "the unknown got a little smaller because neither of them had to step into it alone." She notes the messages said "Look what we did together," not "Look what I did."

## Bianca on her first three weeks as an intern at Thunder

In a section written by Bianca herself, she describes her first three weeks as a mix of peace, surprise, gratitude and some frustration. She says colleagues took the time to explain how things work, with special thanks to Deni, who she works with most closely and who answers all her questions. She reports that coming to the office gave her energy on days she wasn't feeling right, and that she can take breaks in the downstairs living room with people who "feel like a breath of fresh air" and already feel like "more than colleagues." She also names a tension: she values autonomy and independence, so balancing openness to others' opinions with feeling that her creative view counts can be hard, and adjusting to others' work pace as an intern is another hit to that. She says she is glad there is "always space to talk about these things."

## Andreea Popa on three years at Thunder and Cris's reminder that no one fights alone

Andreea marks three years at Thunder, the longest she has stayed at any company. She writes that Adi told her, three months in, that she acted like she'd been there forever, and that Thunder made belonging feel easy by welcoming "all the parts of me." She describes the 23 July all-hands in the lounge: she arrived tired after a full week, including work on the website rebrand, and people shared ordinary stories that left a mark on them. By the middle of it she wasn't tired anymore, which she says shows people can refill you just by being there, sharing and making you laugh. She is candid that the three years haven't been all easy; she overthinks and pressures herself. She writes that "as Cris always points out, we don't have to fight our battles alone," and that reaching three years made her hear that differently. She names Cris as the person "who reminds me what I'm capable of when I forget," alongside colleagues who give her energy when hers is gone. Her conclusion: "It really does take a team to build one person's dream," and the task is to notice who has carried pieces of the journey with you and let them matter.

## Thunder's Substack on caring about people beyond the role that brought them in

Thunder hired a cook, Alexandra, a friend of Deni's who is also a singer and songwriter and wants to become a musician. Andreea writes that Thunder hopes to help her get closer to that dream while she's there, possibly through opportunities along the way or by having her perform at a future event, or something not yet thought of. She states the principle: "We want to care about the people we bring close beyond the role that brought them through the door. To know what they dream about, what they hope to build for themselves, and, whenever we can, make a little more room for those dreams to grow."
