## Small, unseen work is the real culture-building

Cris argues that most meaningful change at Thunder happens in the background, and that leaders should deliberately notice and name it.

- He contrasts cultures that celebrate "big thinking. Bold visions. Moonshots." with the quieter reality that real change is "someone choosing to do something well, even when the only witness is their own integrity."
- At Thunder, the team looks for small details on purpose, because "the way small things get done (or don't) says everything about who we are."
- Care in small things compounds. He calls this "the quiet math of impact."
- He quotes James Clear's Atomic Habits: "It's so easy to overestimate the importance of one defining moment and underestimate the value of making small improvements on a daily basis." Small daily improvements, made with care, become the culture and the work.

## Example of unprompted initiative: the Instagram links page

Cris uses a concrete Thunder example of the behavior he wants to reinforce.

- Anda, Thunder's designer, noticed the company's Instagram links page. Nobody asked her to check or change it.
- She reorganized the links into thoughtful categories so people could find what they were looking for more easily.
- He calls it a "small touch, but one that was needed," and says this kind of thing happens a lot at Thunder.
- What he values is the act itself and also how colleagues respond to it.

## Recognition: tell people you saw it

Cris treats specific acknowledgment as a cheap, high-value leadership habit.

- Even good, creative, thoughtful people forget to say "I saw what you did, and it mattered."
- It is easy to point out what is missing (a missed deadline, a bug, a rough draft). When something just works, people assume the person knows, and the team moves on.
- Behind competence there is still a person hoping their extra care was not invisible. Noticing "doesn't cost much, but it gives a lot."
- At Thunder, comments on shared writing are not only edits but "little notes of recognition," tiny signals that what someone did mattered.
- Principle: "invisible work, done with care, often creates visible impact."

## Feedback should include what someone got right

Cris describes the kind of feedback culture he is building.

- Feedback covers "not just what to fix, but what you got right."
- A good idea is celebrated even when it did not come from the top.
- Trust is earned by how people show up for each other "again and again, in the little ways."

## What work is really building: belonging

Cris reframes what a company produces beyond tasks and calendars.

- On the surface work is "a job," "a to-do list," or "a calendar full of check-ins and strategy calls."
- Underneath, he says the team is building belonging: a space where people feel safe enough to care and where small things are not forgotten.
- Psychological safety to care is the precondition for the quiet initiative he praises.

## Practical habit for leaders and teammates

Cris closes with a simple daily action.

- Ask: "What invisible work is happening around you, right now? And who needs to hear that you saw it?"
- Then act: "Say the thing. Send the note. Leave the comment."
- His framing: "Build the culture you want to be part of."
