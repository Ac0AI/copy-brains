## Starting from an honest inventory

Cris left school with a lot of information and almost nothing he could use. He says education is "built to fill our heads, not change our lives": it teaches people to store facts, not to become someone who can build, create or solve real problems.

- He was mediocre early in his career despite an IT degree, because he "didn't know much that actually mattered".
- His first framework step is a "brutal inventory of potential": where could he have been versus where he is now.
- He checks four questions to take his pulse:
  - Am I on the right road?
  - Am I actually following my goals or just talking about them?
  - Do I have the activities I crave?
  - Am I spending time with people who elevate me or drain me?
- He admits this can't be measured perfectly, but says you can feel the difference when you ask.

## Building the foundation: forgive, accept, become a scout

Cris builds a foundation before any plan or productivity system. He says you "can't build greatness on a foundation of self-criticism".

- **Forgive yourself** for past decisions he isn't proud of.
- **Accept your past**: those decisions are exactly why you may not be where you want to be today, "and that's okay". This lets you move forward "with clarity instead of shame".
- He warns that if you're not good with yourself, you'll sabotage every self-help book and every chance at growth "from the inside out".
- **Develop a scout mentality**: question your beliefs "like your life depends on it". When you find that 1, 2 or 5 beliefs are wrong, have the courage to seek new ways of seeing reality.
- He contrasts the soldier, who fights to prove everyone else wrong, with the scout, who is willing to be wrong if it means finding the truth.

## Focus attention with structure and small daily steps

After the foundation, Cris focuses attention with intention.

- Develop a clear plan.
- Set limits: spend less time on the phone and on time-wasting activities.
- Invest in what he actually wants "with small, intentional steps every single day".
- He closes by saying the gap between who you are and who you could be "will never close on its own". It takes three things:
  - brutal honesty about where you stand
  - the humility to unlearn what doesn't serve you
  - the courage to take small, intentional steps daily

## Personalize your road and own your learning

Cris says only you know your specific problems, fears, needs, dreams and the destination that will make you feel alive, so the road has to be personal.

- Everything you know can change, including what you think is possible for your life.
- Don't wait for schools, workplaces or the world to hand you the tools you need.
- Learning starts when you take ownership of it, when you stop treating knowledge "like something to collect" and treat it "like something to live".
- He refuses to set a finish line for his potential. He sees it as something that evolves with every challenge and every moment of doubt he pushes through.
- His line on mediocrity: "Mediocrity is crowded, and it's time to stop accepting it."

## How Cris got through his early developer years

Cris became a developer without a passion for it. He didn't like mathematics, informatics or algorithms.

- **Put passion in on purpose.** He allowed himself to put passion into the work, because "without intention and fire in your work or learning, you'll extract nothing but disappointment".
- **Commit to a trial window.** On his first day coding he wanted to quit because it was too hard and confusing. He promised himself he would give it everything for 1-2 years and then see if he was up for the challenge.
- **Hunt for mentors.** He sought people willing to help, because when you're truly paying attention you can learn from anyone walking a similar path. He extracted every drop of wisdom, applied what felt right for him, and eventually became a mentor himself, "paying it forward".
- **Keep learning outside work.** He spends much of his free time on history, philosophy and physics, learning from people online who teach them in ways that spark curiosity.

## Turning the framework into a habit

Cris built the framework because what he learned at university and what he needed in the real world were "separated by an ocean of irrelevance".

- Through constant use, it became part of him and shaped how he approaches learning anything new.
- He frames the goal as becoming "the kind of person who can grow endlessly".
- His definition of real education: "not passing exams, but continually passing the test of becoming".
