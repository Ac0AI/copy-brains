## Thunder's Substack on why US trips raise ambition: absorbing a culture where failure is expected

In an April post on Thunder's Substack, Andreea Popa describes Cris Pop and Anda spending three weeks in the US (San Francisco, Las Vegas, New York) making connections and chasing opportunities. The post argues that "exposing yourself to other people and places opens new ways of thinking." Its core claim: "If you're open to being inspired by others, you can lower your own threshold for taking action." The author contrasts San Francisco, where failure "has crossed the threshold of just being accepted. It's expected" ("Fall down, get up"), with Eastern Europe, where founders start businesses already asking "If this doesn't work out, what's my backup?" Thunder's Substack summarizes the contrast as "Falling upwards, that's the whole philosophy" versus a mind that "goes downward before it ever looks up."

## Thunder's Substack on the New Narratives retreat: trust and co-creation feed audacity

Andreea Popa writes that Cris began his trip at the New Narratives retreat in California, organized by Renee Lynn Frojo for entrepreneurs building intentional, human-centered businesses. It was small by design, with conversations of real depth on marketing, business strategy, team dynamics, productivity and solopreneurship, all told through lived moments rather than abstractions. The post argues that stories stick because seeing a principle applied by a real person with real stakes gives it "texture. Nuance. Color."

The author says the retreat's openness came from people who wanted each other to succeed. Questions like "Have you ever considered doing it that way?" or "Are we missing something here?" carried no criticism, though in Eastern European culture they can easily land as such. The post concludes that "that culture of trust and co-creation is what feeds the audacity," and quotes Renee's newsletter: "If we want to create the change we want to see in the world, we have to stop thinking we can do it alone." Cris also noticed a pattern: most attendees had left corporate life, tired of hierarchies and processes that slowed everything down, to build one-person businesses on their own dream.

## Thunder's Substack on Las Vegas and padel: a city built from desert by commitment

The post describes Las Vegas, setting aside the casinos, as a city that "rose from nothing," built by people with "a vision, a strategy, and the nerve to follow it all the way." Andreea Popa writes that whether or not you like it on a moral level, it is hard not to be humbled by what committed people can build.

The same energy followed Cris onto the padel court. He plays regularly back home, and in the US he joined games with strangers and connected with them instantly. Through those matches he met people who believe ideas only matter if executed boldly, including founders of a New York-based startup using AI-powered cameras to record padel matches, analyze performance and improve the player experience. They were in Vegas setting up for the biggest padel tournament in the USA.

## Thunder's Substack on New York: reconnecting and strangers offering support

According to Thunder's Substack, back in New York two weeks later, Cris and Anda reconnected with people they first met in November, people who "share our story and our book" and believe in Thunder with the conviction they bring to their own dreams. Cris also met people with no reason to care about what Thunder is building who nonetheless "pulled their chairs closer and offered to support our vision."

The author reads this as people who had decided "scarcity was a lie, and that helping someone else go further costs you nothing and might just change everything." She says the journey felt "less like pushing against the world and more like moving through it, with the right people appearing at the right time."

## Thunder's Substack on young Cluj founders: ambition close to home

Andreea Popa writes that audacity also exists a few streets from Thunder, in Cluj. She describes Vic, 19, who built two startups and sold one. He started at 13 following a love for computers, and at 16 opened his own company under his father's name and built his first product with friends; that is the one he sold. He then created Listener, a startup helping podcasters track their efforts, now based in San Francisco, where he is head of engineering.

Vic is also part of Cluj House, a co-living and co-working space that began when Bantu proposed hanging out with friends while each worked on their own projects; about 12 young people now live there. The Thunder team visited them and discovered another product they build, Anara, an AI workspace for scientists, students and research teams. The following month, the Thunder team hosted them at the author's home. The post says the more such people they find, "the more we believe that a different culture is being built. By all of us together."

## Thunder's Substack takeaways and monthly commitment: pick an environment that expands your sense of possible

The post closes with four takeaways:

- "We're so busy avoiding the fall that we forget to look at how far we could go."
- "The culture you're in shapes the ceiling you believe exists."
- "When people move in the same direction and commit to the path, even empty ground can become a city."
- "Every connection you make is a door that hasn't revealed itself yet. Go further and meet more people."

The monthly commitment draws on a thought from Anda: traveling to places that feel more evolved, among driven people, changes you, and you return "more awake and ambitious." Thunder's Substack asks readers to seek out at least one place, community or environment that makes them feel that way, let themselves be inspired by others, take action, and then ask others to join in.
