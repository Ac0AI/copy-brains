## Thunder's Substack on travelling to Tallinn and Helsinki to learn from other startup scenes

In an August recap on Thunder's Substack, Andreea Popa writes that Thunder people have been showing up in different corners of Europe and America. The stated reasons are curiosity and "that familiar Romanian feeling of, 'if they can do it, why can't we?'" The post says some cities are ahead of the curve and have livelier ideas, so Thunder goes to see them in person. Cris and other colleagues go to observe how people build, what they talk about, and what they do differently, then bring ideas back to try in Cluj. The post frames this as checking your "North Star": you may not be lost, but watching how others work can teach you about your own path.

## Thunder's Substack on what Cris took from Tallinn and Helsinki: being consistent and braver about exposure

Andreea Popa writes that in Tallinn and Helsinki, Cris left with the sense that Thunder is "not really missing much." The post lists what Thunder already has: the right people, lots of experience, good work happening every day, and hard-earned lessons. What it needs, per Cris's takeaway as the post reports it, is to be more consistent and a bit braver about getting outside its usual places, to find out how far it can go and to get more exposure for its work.

Thunder's Substack describes a shift already under way. For a long time, in the community space, Thunder focused on its own culture and did not share its work, the product-building knowledge it gained, how it navigates change, or the opinions it formed. Now some people give talks, moderate discussions and meet new people, becoming more visible voices for Thunder. Andreea Popa argues this is also practice in talking about what you know, being seen, and saying yes when you don't feel completely ready. She adds that when things go well, networking and sharing feel non-urgent, but tough times are a reminder not to take good ones for granted.

## Thunder's Substack on Cris's long-term dream of Thunder as a place to start projects

Thunder's Substack reports that while looking at the startup scenes in Tallinn and Helsinki, Cris considered a long-term dream: that one day Thunder might become a place where people not only come to work but also come to start new projects, meet other people, get mentorship, and find support while their ideas are still taking shape. The post says this dream is still far off but that Thunder is working towards it. For now, the near-term step it names is to use what Thunder has more consistently and share it with the world.

## Thunder's Substack on Estonia and how success becomes permission for the next success

Andreea Popa writes that Gabi, a Thunder colleague on the trip, tends to study an ecosystem or country and "come back talking about us as people", returning "with a principle for the inside." His reflections on Estonia, as quoted in the post:

- Estonia has around 1.3 million people yet produced an extraordinary number of startups and unicorns for its size. Infrastructure, regulation and capital matter, but the story starts earlier.
- After regaining independence in 1991 it had to reinvent itself almost from scratch, and a young generation of reformers built new institutions.
- Skype became proof that something built in a tiny country could matter globally. Its first employee, Taavet Hinrikus, co-founded TransferWise (now Wise). Alumni of Skype, and later Bolt and Pipedrive, became founders, mentors and investors.
- Success creates capital and knowledge, but also confidence. "One success becomes permission for another. And over time, confidence becomes cultural."

## Thunder's Substack on inner authority and difficult conversations on the trip

Andreea Popa writes that travelling together gave the Thunder team uninterrupted time with one another, which led to some profound and at times very difficult conversations that "brought us closer" rather than creating distance. She connects them to Richard Rohr's idea of inner authority from *From Wild Man to Wise Man*.

Her reading of it: inner authority is the capacity to trust your own perception and experience without constantly needing outside validation. Without it, people look for heroes and authorities, and when those fail the projection, admiration turns quickly into judgment and resentment. Developing it does not mean being certain you are right; it means being secure enough that another person's perspective no longer threatens yours. "I can tell you honestly how I see something without requiring you to agree with me. You can challenge me without becoming my enemy."

The post says the team said hard things, heard hard things and stayed in the conversation, and that relationships deepen by discovering "that the relationship can survive truth." It contrasts two kinds of confidence: an ecosystem's cultural confidence built from successive wins, and the quieter personal confidence of no longer needing every difficult conversation to end with a winner and a loser.

## Thunder's Substack on team members representing Thunder at the Festival of Thinking

According to the post, a friend met in New York invited Thunder to the Festival of Thinking in Bucharest, a festival for young creative entrepreneurs with sessions, events and labs where people who have been there before can poke holes in ideas and help them grow. Cosmin joined as a mentor and gave a masterclass. Andreea Popa describes him as someone who gets involved beyond the work: speaking at events, contributing to things around Thunder, and caring about the spaces the company is part of. Cosmin said he valued meeting inspiring people and the chance to share his thoughts, noting that "having the feeling that you might have given somebody a boost of confidence is even better."

Bianca went along to capture the festival, her first time covering an event like this solo with no one to guide her. She said she gave a lot of herself while a voice asked whether it was enough, and that she is trying to do her best "while still being gentle with myself." She returned wanting to go deeper into video and writing. Deni, separately, made her first vlog-style video of the Tallinn and Helsinki trip, and Thunder's Instagram is where she shares what she sees.

## Thunder's Substack on care at Thunder Home and the "time travel" closing idea

Andreea Popa credits Alexandra, who joined Thunder's office, with taking care of the team: trying new recipes, cooking nutritious food, asking what people like, and putting "a little bit of herself" into what she makes. The post says the team has been "doing a little better" since she arrived.

The essay closes on its title idea. Andreea Popa argues we already time-travel through memory, revisiting and reinterpreting moments, wishing we had acted differently. We cannot change the beginning, but "we can start where we are and change the ending." Changing the course of things often needs only a decision in the present, and then fighting for it and giving it everything so it becomes the best decision we could have made. She also notes that the book on Thunder's philosophy, which began as a document about her own distance travelled, is soon to have an audio version.
