## Cris's view: work with people, not for them

- Cris frames collaborative entrepreneurship as the future of building companies, in contrast to "toxic competition" and the "zero-sum game." At Thunder Talks he welcomed people from RebelDot, a company that could be seen as a rival, and traded stories and lessons openly instead.
- He endorses Tudor Ciulean's (RebelDot co-founder) principle "work with, not for": stand shoulder-to-shoulder with people instead of above them.
- When you work with someone, you share the weight of uncertainty, and their wins and struggles become yours.
- Cris endorses the idea that it is not enough to dream together; "we have to be willing to fail together." Shared risk and a shared stake in an uncertain outcome turn colleagues into collaborators.
- Every conversation is a choice that either builds or diminishes trust.

## Honest communication as the foundation of workplace relationships

- Cris argues most workplaces run on "polite half-truths and carefully curated feedback." He backs the principle of truth even when it is uncomfortable.
- Lasting business relationships are forged in the vulnerable kind of honesty, the kind that risks rejection to invite trust.
- He treats honesty as a practice, "a way of working, leading, and showing up for one another," and says that when you are building something meaningful, "transparency is everything."
- The chain he endorses, quoted from Tudi: "Transparency builds trust. Trust fuels ownership. Ownership generates care and sparks creativity."
- Most companies do the opposite: they hide struggles, micromanage the process, and then wonder why people are disengaged.

## Case study: how RebelDot was built on radical transparency

Cris holds up RebelDot as proof that this approach works.
- Vision: "A thriving society built on fair relationships." Mission: "We bring value through technology with honest partnerships."
- Origin: as co-founder of a software company, Tudi was asked to fire good people simply because a client hadn't paid its bill. That was the "rational," safe business decision. He refused, walked away, and built a new company with the people he was meant to let go.
- Early days: the company was losing money. Tudi told people the uncomfortable truth and gave them the choice to stay or leave. Some left; others believed in the mission and stayed.
- He made no false promises and kept everyone informed about the reality they faced together. Over time this produced a team that felt genuinely invested in the outcome and understood "not just what they were doing, but why it mattered."
- Result, per Cris: one of the fastest growing tech companies in their area, "not because they had the best rules, but because they focused on people and values," and because everyone understood what they were fighting for, not because of a perfect strategy.
- Lesson: give people not just the how but the why.

## Integral factory culture versus a meritocratic culture

- Cris describes most companies as having an "integral factory culture": everything systematized, standardized and optimized for efficiency, so people become interchangeable parts. He says this is why innovation dies and talent walks out the door.
- He also stresses the gap between the culture a company actually creates and the culture it thinks it is creating.
- Collaborative entrepreneurship instead rests on a meritocratic foundation: the best ideas win regardless of who suggests them, whether CEO or newest hire.
- Merit-driven decisions cultivate trust, and trust compounds.

## What trust and transparency produce for a business

Cris lists the compound effects of a merit- and trust-based culture:
- Quality, because people care about the outcome.
- Speed, because there is no bureaucracy slowing things down.
- Flexibility, which leads to real performance and partnerships that last "decades, not quarters."
- Requirement: complete transparency and clarity. "No politics. No 'need to know' basis. Everything in the open."
- His claim: collaborative entrepreneurship "is never the reason we fail, it's what prevents failure."

## Entrepreneurship with human values and civic responsibility

- Thunder's mission from day one, per Cris, was "entrepreneurship with human values and civic responsibility": work can be meaningful, done alongside people you care about, pursuing goals that matter both personally and collectively.
- He argues consumers and business builders have more power than they realize: every company supported, partnership formed and hire made is a vote for the kind of world we want to work in.
- He says the open question is not whether this approach works (RebelDot proves it does) but whether more people have the courage to choose it.
- He is writing a book called "Leadership before it has a name," on why self-leadership is the most important leadership, to be launched at The Thunder Way.
