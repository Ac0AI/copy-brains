## Determination as the top founder quality

- Paul says that when YC started, the team expected intelligence to be the most important quality in founders. That's "the myth in the Valley."
- His view after funding many startups: as long as a founder is over a certain intelligence threshold, "what matters most is determination."
- Founders hit many obstacles, so they can't be the sort of person who gets demoralized easily.
- Example: Bill Clerico and Rich Aberman of WePay run a finance startup, which means endless negotiations with big, bureaucratic companies. When a startup depends on deals with big companies, it often feels like they're trying to ignore you out of existence. Paul's point is that when Clerico starts calling you, "you may as well do what he asks, because he is not going away."

## Flexibility: determination without stubbornness

- Paul does not want the kind of determination in phrases like "don't give up on your dreams."
- Startups are so unpredictable that founders must be able to modify their dreams on the fly.
- His best metaphor is a running back. He is determined to get downfield, but at any moment may need to go sideways or even backwards to get there.
- Example: Daniel Gross of Greplin applied to YC with a bad ecommerce idea. YC said it would fund him if he did something else. He thought for a second and said ok. He then went through two more ideas before settling on Greplin.
- He had worked on Greplin only a couple of days when he presented to investors at Demo Day, and he still got a lot of interest. Paul says he "always seems to land on his feet."

## Imagination: the kind of intelligence that matters

- Intelligence matters a lot, and the type that matters most is imagination.
- What counts is not solving predefined problems quickly, but coming up with surprising new ideas.
- In startups, "most good ideas seem bad initially." If they were obviously good, someone would already be doing them.
- Founders therefore need the kind of intelligence that produces ideas with "just the right level of craziness."
- Example: Airbnb. YC thought the idea was too crazy and couldn't believe large numbers of people would want to stay in other people's places. YC funded it because it liked the founders so much. They were in as soon as YC heard the founders had been supporting themselves by selling Obama and McCain branded breakfast cereal. The idea turned out to be on the right side of crazy.

## Naughtiness: the piratical gleam

- The most successful founders are usually good people, but they tend to have "a piratical gleam in their eye." They're not "Goody Two-Shoes type good."
- Morally, they care about getting the big questions right, not about observing proprieties.
- Paul prefers "naughty" to "evil." They delight in breaking rules, but not rules that matter.
- He notes this quality may be redundant, since it may be implied by imagination.
- Application question: Sam Altman of Loopt, one of YC's most successful alumni, was asked what question to put on the YC application to find more people like him. He suggested asking about a time they had hacked something to their advantage, meaning beating the system, not breaking into computers.
- That question became one of the ones YC pays most attention to when judging applications.

## Friendship: founder relationships

- Empirically it is hard to start a startup with just one founder. Most of the big successes have two or three.
- The relationship between founders has to be strong. They must genuinely like one another and work well together.
- His line: "Startups do to the relationship between the founders what a dog does to a sock: if it can be pulled apart, it will be."
- Example: Emmett Shear and Justin Kan of Justin.tv have known each other since second grade and "can practically read one another's minds." Paul says he has never sensed any unresolved tension between them, though he assumes they argue like all founders.

## Summary of the five qualities

- Determination, flexibility, imagination, naughtiness and friendship are what YC looks for in founders.
- Determination is the most important, and it must be paired with flexibility.
- Imagination is the form of intelligence that matters most.
- Naughtiness may be implied by imagination.
- Founder friendship matters because startups strain the relationship.
- Paul wrote this essay in October 2010 for Forbes. The print version cut the last item (friendship) for lack of room.
