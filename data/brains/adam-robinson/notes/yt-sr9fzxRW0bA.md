## Founder brand content as a distribution engine

Adam says a founder who isn't building a personal brand is "leaving millions on the table". His LinkedIn audience helped scale RB2B from zero to $1M ARR in six weeks.

- **Common mistakes:** sounding like a big professional company, sharing only wins, and avoiding vulnerability out of fear of competitors.
- **What he does instead:**
  - Be clear about who you are. His identity is "a bootstrapped SaaS founder who builds in public".
  - Stay consistent on that identity across every piece of content.
  - Be vulnerable and authentic in ways big companies can't. He calls that the competitive advantage.
- **His breakthrough came from a public failure.** In 2023 he predicted on LinkedIn that retention.com would triple revenue from $15M to $45M ARR. It grew only about 40%, and he had to fire his entire BDR team after outbound crashed. He shared all of it. Other founders were going through the same struggle but nobody was talking about it, which gave him his most engaged audience.
- **Leverage:** each post reaches at least 50,000 people, sometimes 100,000 or more, and his videos get thousands of views. This drives thousands of inbound leads a month to RB2B at no cost except his time.

## Inbound-led outbound: following up with website visitors

Adam says this hybrid took RB2B from zero to $1M ARR in six weeks. Traditional cold outreach gets less effective every day, so he combines inbound and outbound.

1. Drive traffic to your site through content, podcasts and partnerships.
2. Use RB2B to identify which visitors fit your ideal customer profile.
3. Follow up with them the way you would with cold prospects. They aren't cold anymore, because they've already shown intent by visiting.

He reports conversion rates 10 times higher than traditional cold outreach, and time spent on high-intent prospects instead of a wide net.

**Cold email workflow for these visitors:**
1. Install RB2B to identify anonymous visitors.
2. Connect RB2B to Clay to enrich and process the data.
3. Use Clay's AI to write a personalized email for each visitor.
4. Push everything to Instantly or Smartlead and send with domain rotation.

He says campaigns like this convert at over 10%. The same list also works for LinkedIn connection requests, where acceptance and response rates are "through the roof" because people just visited your site.

## Cold email at scale: lists, verification, domains and copy

Adam says outbound still works if you have the right list. RB2B sends over 200,000 cold emails a month.

**List building**
- Pull from five or six data providers, not one, because coverage differs and you want to cover your total addressable market.
- B2B leads: Apollo, Instantly, Clearbit-style tools such as ZoomInfo, ListKit, Seamless AI, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, UpLead, Wiza, Lead411.
- Vertical-specific B2B: Ocean.io and Adapt.
- Venture: Crunchbase and PitchBook.
- E-commerce: StoreLeads and Weo.
- Local businesses: D7, Leadfinder, Lead Scrape.
- Always validate emails with NeverBounce or LeadMagic. Invalid emails "will absolutely destroy" deliverability, and he calls this non-negotiable.

**Sending infrastructure**
- Tools: Instantly and Smartlead. Both work, and the team switches depending on the moment.
- Inbox rotation across hundreds of domains.
- Don't send from the main domain. Use variants like try-, the-real-, get- prefixes on rb2b.com. Buy the domains through Instantly or Smartlead.
- Create two inboxes per domain and send 2 to 10 emails a day from each. He says this gets past Gmail and Outlook spam filters.
- Keep volume high but list quality high, with multiple verification steps on every email.

**Messaging**
- Short and punchy, no corporate speak, direct benefit in the first line, one simple call to action.
- One of his best performers: "We launched a tool that lets you know when individuals, not companies, visit your site. We send you their LinkedIn profile in Slack, and we don't charge you a dime. Respond back with yes if you'd like me to send you a link to sign up."

## LinkedIn outbound: connect, value, follow-up

Adam calls LinkedIn outbound one of the most underutilized strategies. Connection requests with no message get high acceptance rates, and once accepted the person starts seeing your content in their feed.

- **Automation:** HeyReach or La Growth Machine.
- **Volume:** 10 to 20 connection requests a day to people in your ICP. More than that risks a LinkedIn ban.
- **Sequence:** connect, then value, then follow-up.

**Step 1: connection request.** One relevant sentence, no pitch, no links. The goal is the connection, not a meeting. Example: "I saw you're hiring two SDRs. I work on making new reps' first 30 days easier. I'm happy to share what's working."

**Step 2: value message after acceptance.** Something simple they can act on today. Example: "Thanks for connecting. If you want to close the loop on ABM, try identifying site visitors at the person level rather than the company level. I can show you how to set it up if it's helpful."

**Step 3: follow-up drip using the rule of three.** Send three messages, then stop.
- Message 1: a quick reminder.
- Message 2: a concrete offer, such as a template, the copy he used, and the signup link.
- Message 3: the final ask or close, such as "Do you want person level ID or should I close this thread?"

He says it works because there are no attachments, no walls of text and no fake urgency.

## Paid ads as an accelerator, with the creative formula

Adam says paid ads "still work, but they're not a growth machine anymore". They are an accelerator, not the foundation, and other channels must be working first.

**How he uses them**
- Retargeting on LinkedIn, Meta, Google and Twitter. Install each platform's retargeting pixel and stay in front of people who already engaged.
- Matched-audience ads to ABM lists: upload the list built from Apollo or ZoomInfo and run ads directly to decision makers at target accounts. Works on LinkedIn, Meta, Google and Twitter.
- Lookalike audiences from your paying customer list, uploaded to Meta and Google.

**Creative script:** Russell Brunson's who, what, why, how.
- Who: "Hi, I'm Adam Robinson, the founder of RB2B."
- What: "We identify your anonymous website visitors at the person level, free forever."
- Why: "You're spending money on ads, content, and outbound to drive traffic to your site. But 97% of visitors leave without taking action. You have no way to follow up with them."
- How: "Click the link below and start identifying your website visitors for free today."

**Production:** the best-performing content is authentic. He takes selfie videos on his phone, with no fancy production, actors or agencies, talking into the camera about a real problem and how the product solves it.

## User-generated content and creator communities

Adam calls UGC "the current superpower in marketing". It is authentic, free or very cheap, builds trust branded content can't, scales beyond paid marketing, and the best pieces can become ads.

- **B2C:** focus on volume, with thousands of pieces.
- **B2B:** focus on community, with deep relationships with fewer creators.

**RB2B's B2B program**
- They work closely with Clay agencies that use the product.
- It is run by one person on the team, who tracks mentions and stays in touch with creators.
- Incentives:
  - Recognition as market leaders. These are usually agencies growing their own business, so visibility matters to them.
  - Access to other business owners and the internal team. Their network strength equals their business strength.
  - Revenue through the partner program, which pays 20% commission for life on anyone they send.
  - Physical perks like custom figurines with their face on them. He says Clay is the best in the world at sending gifts that make people feel special.

**Key lesson:** people don't create content because they care about your company, they do it to grow their own business. Align incentives with theirs: "Help them make more money and they'll create content for you forever."

**Growth loop:** users post about RB2B on LinkedIn, the content drives traffic, RB2B identifies those visitors and converts them to customers, and the new customers create more content.

If the product doesn't naturally generate content, build content creation into the product or offer something free that people want to share. Make it "stupid simple" to create and share.

## Word of mouth and product-market fit come first

Adam says word of mouth is the hardest channel to get but "the only thing that actually matters". With it, everything else works. Without it, "nothing you do will work". It can't be bought, faked or forced. It comes only from product-market fit.

- **Definition:** the perfect product for the perfect group of buyers at the perfect time. Strong fit builds a massive company. Weak fit means a decades-long grind.
- **Common mistake:** adding paying customers and starting to growth hack with ads, SEO, influencers and newsletter sponsorships. None of it works without word of mouth.
- **His rule:** do things that don't scale. Until you have verifiable product-market fit, skip ads, SEO, influencers and partnership programs. Talk to users one-on-one yourself and build fast feedback loops that iterate on the product.

**Signs you have product-market fit**
- Very strong word of mouth and high retention.
- Doing nothing would still produce exponential organic growth.
- People will prepay even though the product isn't built yet.
- It feels like the market is pulling the product out of you.
- Customers would be livid if you took it away, and cash is piling up in the bank.

He says it takes longer than you think. Getting impatient and growth hacking too early "dooms" you to a mediocre business, and once you have fit, the other marketing strategies work much better.
