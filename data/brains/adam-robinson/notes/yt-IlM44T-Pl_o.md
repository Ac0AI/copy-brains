## Building a LinkedIn personal brand: the story and the prolific zone

Adam says a personal brand on LinkedIn is "the fastest, most efficient, and highest leverage way to grow a startup that has ever existed." He went from 0 to 140,000 followers in 2 years. Over 18 months he grew RB2B from 0 to $6M ARR with no funding, and reached $4M ARR using LinkedIn alone.

**Your story is a character you create in your market's mind. It rests on three C's:**
- **Clear:** be unambiguous about who you are. The market knows him as "a bootstrapped SaaS founder who builds in public with radical transparency."
- **Consistent:** he does not talk about his personal life or hobbies, only that one identity.
- **Constantly reinforced:** repeated brand impressions across more channels build trust, and trust leads to purchases.

**Find your prolific zone:**
- Mainstream takes are noise because everyone is already saying them.
- Take positions far enough out that, say, 80% of one side loves you and 20% of the other side hates you. Too far out and you are treated as a lunatic.
- Polarizing takes inside that zone are what cut through.

## Vulnerability as an unfair advantage on LinkedIn

- Big companies have to talk like big companies, and small players are afraid to share their biggest mistakes. LinkedIn is a professional network where people try to impress, so almost nobody admits real work problems. Doing so puts you in the under-1% of creators.
- Example: Adam posted about a layoff. His executive team told him not to and begged him to soften it. They wanted him to cut the parts saying the company had fallen out of product-market fit and had been unable to fix its churn problem for the prior 18 months. He refused.
- His reasoning: the brutal honesty is why his content resonates, and employees understanding the real situation leads to better long-term decisions.
- He calls this an extreme example. With investors or a co-founder you probably can't write this way.
- Rule of thumb: "If you can say things on LinkedIn that you might only say to your spouse behind closed doors, it will do better than anything any larger company could ever post."
- Tip from Devon Reed: people behave on LinkedIn like they behave at work, so being a bit inappropriate stands out.

## Optimizing your LinkedIn profile to convert

Adam's profile alone drives hundreds of inbound RB2B leads every month. Each change is small but the cumulative effect is large.

- **Headshot:** professional, looking straight at the camera, solid background, smiling.
- **Banner:** branded, with a clear value proposition, an offer and a call to action. Canva's free version works.
- **Headline:** clearly states what you do and who you help, maximizing professional credibility.
- **Link:** in the top section, via a link or custom button to your website. A page built to convert cold traffic into a front-end offer is better.
- **About section:** very detailed. Be clear about who you help and how. Cite specific accomplishments with numbers. Put maximum credibility in the first line, above the fold. Don't be vague or talk about who you aspire to be.
- **Featured section:** direct-response calls to action (website, newsletter, podcast). Don't feature your best-performing post. Send people somewhere that moves them closer to converting.
- **Roles, education, recommendations:** detail every role, including adviser and investor roles. List education, degrees and certifications, which still matter for entrepreneurs. Get 3-4 recommendations, using swaps if necessary.
- **Warm leads:** with LinkedIn Premium you can see who viewed your profile. Opening conversations with them is much easier than with cold contacts.

## Finding post ideas

Mindset: write for your audience, not the algorithm. The algorithm is designed to serve the audience, and the only answer to any change is better content. The best posts answer questions people have about your niche.

His process, using cold email as the example:
1. Study the top creators in the niche. Spend several hours finding their best posts of the last 90 days, bookmark the ones with far more likes than usual, and log their topics in a spreadsheet.
2. Pick topics where you can offer a prolific, polarizing take. Never copy their ideas; write your own version.
3. Ask Gemini (powered by Google and YouTube search): "What are the most common questions people have about cold emails?"
4. Search Reddit threads on the niche and read what people ask.
5. Between these sources you have enough ideas for your first 30 days.

## Writing posts: swipe file, hook, template and close

**Swipe file:** spend 2 hours straight on the best LinkedIn posts from influencers in your space. Look at anything with over 1,000 likes and apply your own facts to the template. Save favorite hooks, templates, closers and calls to action in one place.

**Hook:**
- The hook is everything before the "see more" button, and it is the most important part of the post. Its only goal is to get the click.
- Write as if no one has heard of you, and build maximum credibility with something bold enough to keep people reading.
- He uses numbers heavily because "LinkedIn loves numbers."
- Examples he uses: "I spoke to 20 CEOs over the past 30 days" (brokering information reads as credible) and "I bootstrapped 20 million ARR in 3.5 years."

**Template:**
- His common format is a list of bullet points with about two sentences of explanation under each. Sometimes he uses sub-bullets or numbered lists.
- Lean on templates until you master posting. Put your story on top of a template that works for others.
- Every time: (1) agitate a specific pain point, (2) offer a solution, (3) tie it together with a strong takeaway. This is a mini hero's journey in one post.

**Close:**
- End with something clever and cozy that gives a "dopamine hit" after the pain and the solution.
- Tip: write the post, paste it into Claude, and ask it for five cozy closes that give the reader a dopamine hit. Pick the one that fits your tone.
- His example close: "Here's a few lessons for my fellow bootstrappers… Keep building."
- Never close with "let's continue the conversation in the comments" or a generic question.
- Experiment, but stick with one approach for a while to build consistency before mixing things up.

## Connect campaigns and the 60-day rule

1. **Build a prospect list** of 100 to 250 of your best prospects.
2. **Connect** starting at 10-20 per day, with no message. Accept rates are very high and rise as you grow your audience. You can work up to 50 per day, but automation technically violates LinkedIn's terms and risks a ban.
3. **RB2B tie-in:** installing RB2B on your website sends the LinkedIn profiles of visitors to Slack so you can connect with them. He calls these practically the warmest leads you'll have.
4. **Wait before messaging.** After a connection is accepted, they start seeing your content. He waits about 60 days before messaging. By then they think you're a celebrity, and he reports an 80% reply rate. He says this makes LinkedIn "the most effective conversation opening vehicle in the entire world."
5. **Be patient.** People who wait 48 hours or 5 days still count as impatient. Don't pitch right after a connection. The wait builds a parasocial relationship and trust.

**Inbound once you've grown:**
- Accept only ideal-customer-profile requests, and decline people trying to sell to you, so your audience concentrates in your ICP.
- After accepting, message: "Hey, first name, what caught your attention?" then follow up with "How are things going at [company]?"

**Consistency example:** his friend Megan Bowen has manually connected with 20 ICP people a day for four years, reaching virtually all her prospects (thousands). Combined with strong content driving inbound requests, she has over 36,000 followers and a trust machine producing leads, partnerships and investment opportunities for her agency.

## Building community through comments

- The ideas you post about become the shared values that bring people together in your comments. This works on any platform, and you can later move the community elsewhere.
- Adam answers every comment on his posts, typically 100 or more per post. At live events he meets many people he already knows online.
- **Pricing example:** RB2B had 3,000 free signups and only 13 paying customers. He posted about the problem non-stop and commenters helped him fix the pricing. The result was a 12.5% touchless free-to-paid conversion rate, and growth from $0 to $6M ARR in 18 months with just three people.
- **When you're starting out:**
  - List influential people in your space and comment on their posts for 15 minutes a day.
  - Don't use AI to write comments that regurgitate the post and end with a question. He hates it and so will they.
  - Thoughtful, consistent comments get you remembered by the creator and attract their audience.
  - Spend as much or more time commenting each week as you spend writing.
- Also connect with 10-20 ICP people every day, no matter what.

## Adding video to LinkedIn posts

- Video isn't required to start, but LinkedIn keeps pushing video and it may become necessary. Getting people to watch and listen to you say something intelligent raises your status in their minds.
- His process:
  1. Write the post first, which gets the topic in his head.
  2. Record a free-flowing monologue, looking at the post's bullet points and elaborating on whichever are most interesting.
  3. The video and post end up slightly different, which he considers a net positive.
- He recommends holding off on video until you've found your voice and get strong engagement on text posts, because video adds a lot of extra work.
