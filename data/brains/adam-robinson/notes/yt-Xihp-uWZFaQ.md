## Why Adam went aggressive in 2023, and what he learned

- Adam shared an office with Dave Rogenmoser and the Jasper AI team. Both companies had been stuck at about $2-3M ARR for two years. Jasper then went from 0 to $50M ARR in 12 months with a tiny team and raised heavily.
- Seeing that, Adam thought "well I can do that too." He took the free cash from retention.com and put it into hiring and trying to build a "unicorn company" around a tighter ICP, hoping to fix churn.
- Churn was not solved. He says he "really lost my religion during this period."
- He was glad he never raised money. Retention.com had been at $12M ARR with six employees (about $2M revenue per employee), and he recommends running that way if you can.
- He calls the pressure to look bigger "everything": "What do you mean you only have six employees?" It takes a lot of ego to resist vanity metrics. He expects he could lose the discipline again.

## Retention.com P&L and margins (January 2024)

- Run rate of about $6M net income at the time. He says the business is "making a lot of money profit."
- Gross margin was 87%, above the typical 75-80% for SaaS.
- His explanation: it is a data company. You compile a lot of data at SaaS margins, and if you structure it right the data cost becomes fixed, so COGS does not grow with scale.
- G&A was $346K in January, mostly executive salaries (six executives).
- Retention.com made about $1M of profit in the year discussed. It is 85% of revenue and 120% of profit, so RB2B's losses are funded by it.
- Retention.com revenue was flat to slightly declining. Adam says stacking RB2B on top is what shows growth again.
- To keep the team motivated on a flat product, he tells them it is "the fuel behind everything we're doing." Something growing from 0 to 1 feels more exciting than a profitable business that is not growing, "like a baby is more exciting than an 80-year-old."

## Lawsuit marketing: the cease and desist post

- A vendor Adam used for comparison research, six months before the contract expired, told him it would triple his price. That annoyed him enough to write a LinkedIn post describing what they did.
- They sent a cease and desist. The old playbook is to apologize and delete. Adam saw it as "the single greatest PR opportunity for a company that is 3 weeks old" because it would take him from "LinkedIn guy" to getting every customer of that vendor to learn what RB2B does.
- Risk check: his lawyer said the worst case was a suit costing a few hundred thousand dollars ($300-400K) over about three years. The lawyer said he would never recommend it but understood the logic. Adam judged it worth it.
- Result: about 1,600 likes, 913 comments and several hundred thousand impressions.
- His broader point: founders say they want a founder-led brand, but then everything goes through a PR machine and ends up as fluffy content nobody relates to. Authentic, blunt stories resonate.

## LinkedIn content strategy and pre-launch lead gen

- Very little of his content is sales content. Roughly one in ten posts gets a hard CTA at the bottom.
- Most of the conversion comes from a "visit my website" link in his profile. People see interesting, transparent posts about the journey and wonder what he does. The algorithm then keeps serving them more of his posts.
- His view: "people don't want to be sold to on social media." Organic content brings people along on a journey, builds community around ideas and builds relationships through comments.
- He admits he cannot fully say why it works so well, but it works well enough that he keeps doing it.
- Pre-launch, the CTA was a free Google Form link dropped into the post. Variants of the post ran three or four times and collected 1,600+ leads. He did not wait for a perfect tech stack or tracking.
- It also produced hundreds of discovery conversations. He and his cofounder were on the phone with about 10 people a day between them for around six months, so they knew what to build at launch. He cites *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*.
- His comparison: instead of a Product Hunt launch, this yielded more leads and more customer conversations.

## RB2B funnel, pricing and conversion numbers

- RB2B launched March 1, 2024 with no revenue before then. It went from about $5K to about $200K MRR, adding about $60K of new MRR per month on average over the prior three months. About $10K of new MRR came in during the first six days of September.
- Aug 8 to Sep 6: about 3,000 new trials, with almost 10% converting to new customers. Typical free-to-paid is 3-5%.
- He attributes activation and conversion to content that targets a very tight audience of the right people on LinkedIn. Installing the script converts at about 35% of signups, which others call a dream.
- Pricing is credit-based (usage-based, not transactional):
  - Premium: 200 leads/month, delivering LinkedIn URLs into a Slack channel, starting at $99/month (integrations and email included at that tier).
  - About 1,000 leads/month costs roughly $300/month.
  - Average new customer pays about $270/month.
- About 100 expansions in the period, which he describes as "right-sizing" rather than growth.

## Churn problem and plan to fix it

- Churn is very high, about 10% a month, with a roughly 9-month LTV. An agency cancelled about 80 accounts and consolidated them under one, so it looks even worse than 10%.
- Adam expects churn to be "a disaster" to solve. His cofounder Santos (formerly at Apollo and ZoomInfo) is not worried because the product is so young.
- He cites Nathan Barry's ConvertKit as a benchmark: churn was about 10% at a similar revenue level, then went down over time.
- Adam is unsure he can get there without adding a system-of-record product. The Clearbit Reveal team told him churn was their biggest problem and the only fix was getting other departments using other products.
- Plan: add other products, especially for salespeople (RB2B is currently a marketer's tool), and get more teams using the platform. He prefers building over buying because he is scared of the M&A and integration process.
- He also wants to borrow Russell Brunson's tactics (content, courses, community) to turn free marketing users into paid users and lower churn. He notes Brunson grew ClickFunnels to hundreds of millions after churn stalled at 17%.
