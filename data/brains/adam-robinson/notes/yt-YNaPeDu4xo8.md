## Manual list building beat database platforms at Get Emails

Adam scaled his SaaS Get Emails (retention.com) from zero to $13M ARR in 36 months with six employees. He did it with a lean, manual list-building operation run by five people in the Philippines for less than $3,000 a month.

- His co-founder Diana saw that most companies bought Apollo or ZoomInfo and blasted automated emails.
- Everyone else uses the same databases and the same addresses, so "those email addresses get absolutely smashed." Open and reply rates are poor because you compete with every company hitting the same prospects.
- Manually researched contacts were fresh and not hammered daily. Adam says this produced meetings at about 10x the conversion rate.
- This worked in 2020 and 2021.

## The four-step manual list-building process

1. **Pick target companies.** Use BuiltWith to find companies running complementary or competitor software. For Get Emails that meant Shopify stores using Klaviyo for email or Attentive for SMS. Verify traffic with SimilarWeb. The sweet spot was Shopify Plus customers with over 10,000 monthly unique visitors.
2. **Find 3-5 people per company.** Use LinkedIn Sales Navigator. Always include the CEO, plus the VP Marketing, head of e-commerce, email marketing manager and director of growth. Copy all of them on the same email to raise the chance of an open.
3. **Find emails manually.** Find the person on LinkedIn first, then look up the address in Scrap.io, then Hunter.io if Scrap has nothing. If neither has it, move on. Adam says this two-tool check gave much better deliverability than Apollo alone.
4. **Send manually.** Every email in 2020-2021 was sent one by one from individual Gmail accounts, by humans, under several sender names (for example the head of sales and a sales assistant) with multiple address variations. "Our emails looked completely human because they were completely human."

## The 4-email sequence

- **Email 1, direct value prop.** First name, then the product line: Get Emails identifies up to 35% of anonymous site visitors and gives you their email addresses. Cite customer results ("Vital Proteins sees a 9.25x+ ROI") with a case study link, and a calendar link for a 10-minute intro.
- **Email 2, gentle reminder.** Bump the first email, ask for a short intro, and mention capturing more first-party data. Calendar link again.
- **Email 3, problem agitator.** Shoppers show purchase intent and leave without identifying themselves. With recent Apple updates "you may never get them back." Repeat the 35% claim, note it is CAN-SPAM and CCPA compliant, and repeat the case study.
- **Email 4, final summary.** Recap who it helps (Shopify Plus customers), say the addresses are verified as engaged and deliverable with open rates matching the in-house list, and offer a trial discussion.
- **Timing:** first email, follow up 3-5 days later, then a third email a week after that.

## Team structure and management philosophy

- Five people in the Philippines: a team leader (Jim) at $1,000/month and four team members at $500/month each. Total about $3,000/month.
- Jim hired his wife, his friends and his friends' cousins.
- Shifts: 8pm-midnight Eastern for lead generation, 8am-noon Eastern for prospecting.
- A US BDR at the time cost $50-70K a year plus benefits. Adam spent about $36,000 a year for what would have cost around $300,000 in the US.
- Philosophy: "We loaded them up with activity, not problems to solve." No complex decisions, and the process was fully repeatable (find companies in BuiltWith, identify 3-5 people on LinkedIn, find emails with Scrap and Hunter, send email 1, follow up, send email 3). They worked from home and set their own hours within the shift windows.

## LinkedIn outreach: connect, value, follow-up

The same list was worked on LinkedIn at the same time as email, so prospects got both together. Connection requests without a message got very high acceptance rates, and once accepted, prospects start seeing your content in their feed.

1. **Connection request:** one sentence about something relevant, no pitch, no links. Example: "saw you're using Klaviyo for email capture. You want to collect 10 times more emails from your site visitors." The goal is the connection, not a meeting.
2. **Value message after acceptance:** something simple they can act on today. Example: offer to get email addresses of anonymous website visitors who don't fill out a form.
3. **Follow-up drip using the "rule of three":** three messages, then stop.
   - Message 1: a quick reminder.
   - Message 2: a concrete offer, such as sending the template and copy.
   - Message 3: a final ask or close ("This is the last note from me").

Why it works: no attachments, no walls of text, no fake urgency.

## ABM ads layered on top of outreach

- Upload the target list to Facebook and Google so prospects see ads while also getting email and LinkedIn messages. This created the impression that Get Emails was "absolutely everywhere."
- Also ran ads to lookalike audiences of the core list, and retargeting ads to all site visitors.
- Also emailed those site visitors using addresses captured by the Get Emails product itself.
- **Ad script:** Russell Brunson's Who, What, Why, How. Who you are ("I'm Adam Robinson, founder of Get Emails"), what you have (identify 35% of anonymous visitors), why they need it (97% of visitors leave without filling out a form), how to get it (click the link).
- **Creative style:** authentic. Adam took selfie videos on his phone, talking to the camera about a real problem and how the product solves it. No actors, no agencies, no production.

## Why it worked in 2020-21, and what he'd change today

Conditions that made it work:
- Strong product-market fit. "If you don't have word of mouth... this will not save you," but with PMF, high-volume outbound can speed you up significantly.
- Shopify Plus buyers were responsive to cold email in 2021-2022. Response rates are much lower now, so the strategy would be harder.
- Senior employees wore multiple hats. His head of sales managed the Philippines team, closed deals and refined messaging.

What he'd do differently today:
- Still build lists manually, because database platforms are even more saturated now.
- Don't send from the primary domain. They never had major deliverability problems sending from getemails.com, but he calls that luck. Use multiple sending domains to protect the brand domain.
- Use Instantly or Smartlead instead of manual sending. Tech has improved enough to automate while keeping a human feel, and "the edge you're going to get from using humans to send is just not there today."
- Create organic LinkedIn content. A personal brand multiplies every other channel: prospects see your content, your ad, your email and your DM.
- Install RB2B on the website to identify visitors from your ICP and push their LinkedIn profiles to Slack, then add them to outreach immediately. Adam calls these the warmest leads because they are already researching and visited the site.
