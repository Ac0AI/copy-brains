## Distinguishing referral programs from affiliate programs

Corey separates two programs that people often blur together.

**Customer referral programs**
- The referrer is an existing customer recommending the product to their network.
- Rewards are one-time or limited.
- Trust is higher and volume is lower.
- They fit products with natural word-of-mouth, and lower-ticket or self-serve products.

**Affiliate programs**
- Affiliates may not be customers.
- The relationship is ongoing, paid as commission.
- Volume is higher and trust is variable.
- They fit when you want to reach audiences you don't own (creators, influencers, bloggers) and when the product is high-ticket enough to justify commissions.

Before designing either, he gathers four things:
- Program type: referral, affiliate or both; B2B or B2C; average customer LTV; current CAC from other channels.
- Current state: any existing program, the percentage of customers who refer, and which incentives have been tried.
- Product fit: whether the product is shareable, has network effects, and whether customers already talk about it.
- Resources: tools and budget for incentives.

## Engineer virality before building a reward program

Corey's first check is whether virality can be built into the product. He says this is often cheaper and more durable than paid referrals. He also says not to force virality where it doesn't fit.

He places the product on a Viral Potential Spectrum:
- **Natural (build for it):** collaboration tools, communication tools, and products with user-facing outputs, where every use exposes the product to non-users.
- **Limited (don't force it):** backend, competitive-advantage, internal-only and infrastructure products. For these, invest in referral programs, content and partnerships instead.

If the product sits at the natural end, he suggests product-embedded mechanisms before or alongside a reward program. His examples are Powered By badges, exposure loops, social sharing, embeds and watermarks.

## Designing the referral loop

Corey frames the loop as: Trigger Moment → Share Action → Convert Referred → Reward → (Loop).

**Trigger moments.** Ask at high-intent points:
- Right after the first "aha" moment.
- After the customer hits a milestone.
- After exceptional support.
- After a renewal or upgrade.

**Share mechanism, ranked by effectiveness:**
1. In-product sharing (highest conversion).
2. Personalized link.
3. Email invitation.
4. Social sharing.
5. Referral code (works offline).

He also says to reduce friction with one-click sharing and pre-written messages.

## Choosing the incentive structure

- **Single-sided rewards** (referrer only): simpler, and they work for high-value products.
- **Double-sided rewards** (both parties): higher conversion, with a win-win framing.
- **Tiered rewards:** these gamify the process and increase engagement.

Two rules on presentation and timing:
- Lead with the bigger-*feeling* number. On a low-priced product, say "$10 off," not "40% off."
- Reward at the aha moment or a milestone, not at signup.

## Diagnosing and optimizing a program

If few customers refer:
- Ask at better moments.
- Simplify sharing.
- Test different incentive types.
- Make the referral prompt prominent in the product.

If referrals don't convert:
- Improve the landing experience for referred users.
- Strengthen the incentive for the new user.
- Make the referrer's endorsement visible.

| Problem | Corey's fix |
|---|---|
| Low awareness | Add prominent in-app prompts |
| Low share rate | Simplify to one click |
| Low conversion | Optimize the referred user's experience |
| Fraud/abuse | Add verification and limits |
| One-time referrers | Add tiered or gamified rewards |

A/B tests to run:
- **Incentive:** amount, type, single vs. double-sided, timing.
- **Messaging:** program description, CTA copy, landing page copy.
- **Placement:** where and when the prompt appears.

## Measuring a referral program

**Program health:**
- Active referrers (those who referred someone in the last 30 days).
- Referral conversion rate.
- Rewards earned and paid.

**Business impact:**
- Share of new customers coming from referrals.
- CAC via referral vs. other channels.
- LTV of referred customers.
- Program ROI.

His typical findings:
- Referred customers have 16-25% higher LTV.
- Referred customers have 18-37% lower churn.
- Referred customers refer others at 2-3x the rate.

## Launch checklist and email sequences

**Before launch:**
- Define goals and success metrics.
- Design the incentive structure.
- Build or configure the referral tool.
- Create a referral landing page.
- Set up tracking and attribution.
- Define fraud prevention rules.
- Write terms and conditions.
- Test the full referral flow.

**At launch:**
- Announce to existing customers.
- Add in-app referral prompts.
- Update the website with program details.
- Brief the support team.

**First 30 days:**
- Review the conversion funnel.
- Identify top referrers.
- Gather feedback.
- Fix friction points.
- Email reminders to non-referrers.

**Launch email.** Subject: "You can now earn [reward] for sharing [Product]". The body announces the program and states the reward for the referrer and for the friend. It then gives the unique link and three steps: share your link, friend signs up, you both get the reward.

**Nurture sequence:**
- Day 7: remind about the program.
- Day 30: "Know anyone who'd benefit?"
- Day 60: a success story plus a referral prompt.
- After a milestone: "You achieved [X]. Know others who'd want this?"

## Affiliate program mechanics, review programs and tools

**Affiliate mechanics.** Corey keeps detailed material on these in separate references:
- Buyout clauses at about 12× the monthly commission.
- A 20/80 super-promoter rule.
- Launch-affiliate tactics.
- Partner recruitment, cohort economics, executable terms, reconciliation, and measuring incremental growth.

**Review programs.** He treats reviews as the other half of customer advocacy. This covers:
- Review-request programs.
- Responding to reviews.
- Reputation triage.
- Reusing testimonials with permission.
- Platforms including G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Google and app stores.

He says to check each platform's rules before proposing incentives or automation.

**Tools he lists:**
- Rewardful: Stripe-native affiliate programs.
- Tolt: SaaS affiliate programs.
- Mention Me: enterprise referral programs.
- Dub.co: link tracking and attribution.
- Stripe: payment processing for commission tracking.
- Introw: channel partner programs with tiers, deal registration and QBRs.
- PartnerStack: enterprise partner and affiliate programs.
