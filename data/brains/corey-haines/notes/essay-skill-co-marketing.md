## Partner fit: audience overlap without budget competition

Corey's co-marketing skill starts with finding partners who share an audience but not a budget.

- Ideal partner traits:
  - same buyer persona, different problem solved
  - adjacent in the workflow (before, after, or alongside the tool)
  - similar company stage and customer size
  - complementary, not competitive
- Questions he uses to surface partners:
  - What tools do customers already use?
  - What do they use before and after the product?
  - Who else sells to the same ICP?
  - Which integrations do customers request most?
- Where to look:
  - **Integration ecosystem:** existing integration partners, same app-marketplace category, platforms the product plugs into.
  - **Community signals:** who sponsors the same podcasts and newsletters, exhibits at the same conferences, is active in the same communities, and whose content the audience shares.
  - **Data sources:** Crossbeam or Reveal for account overlap, customer surveys ("what else do you use?"), G2/Capterra category neighbors, and job postings that mention the tool alongside others.

## Scoring candidate partners

Corey rates each potential partner 1-5 on six criteria:

- **Audience fit:** how closely their audience matches the ICP.
- **Audience size:** reach worth partnering for.
- **Brand alignment:** would you be proud to be associated?
- **Engagement quality:** an active, engaged audience.
- **Reciprocity potential:** can you offer them equal value?
- **Ease of execution:** a partnerships team or a history of co-marketing.

He suggests scoring the top 5 candidates and researching their past co-marketing before reaching out.

## The five partnership types and what they pay off

Corey treats co-marketing as one of five partnership types, so a request gets routed to the right play instead of defaulting to joint content.

| Type | What it is | Primary payoff |
|---|---|---|
| Integrations | Product connects to another's (native, Zapier, API-first, embedded) | Retention, expansion, marketplace discovery |
| Reseller | Partners sell the product plus services | Distribution and services revenue |
| Affiliate | Promoters earn commission on referrals | Low-risk, pay-for-performance reach |
| Co-marketing | Joint content or campaigns with a peer | Borrowed audience, brand halo |
| App Store / Marketplace | Listing inside a platform's ecosystem | Built-in distribution, effective CAC |

Proof points he cites:

- HubSpot's partner program: $100M ARR, about 40% of revenue, 3,400+ partners. Mature programs average about 28% of revenue and 2x growth.
- Integrations are a decision factor for 83% of enterprise buyers. Calendly staged its ladder: calendar, then sales, then marketing.
- Affiliate power law: 20% of affiliates drive 80% of revenue. Buyout clauses run about 12x monthly commission.
- Permissionless co-marketing: Notion built templates for Airbnb, Amazon and Tesla to ride their brand, with no contract needed.
- App Store distribution: Grammarly went from 0 to 10M.

Affiliate program mechanics belong to his referrals skill; here he keeps affiliate work at the partnership-strategy level.

## Campaign formats by effort and lead sharing

**Content**

| Format | Effort | Lead handling / best for |
|---|---|---|
| Co-authored blog post | Low | Shared byline, link exchange; thought leadership, SEO |
| Joint ebook/guide | Medium | Gated, split leads; lead gen |
| Research report | High | Gated, split leads; authority, PR |
| Guest newsletter swap | Low | Each keeps own leads |
| Podcast guest exchange | Low | Each keeps own leads; relationship building |

**Webinars and events**

- Joint webinar (medium): lead gen, product education.
- Virtual summit panel (medium): multi-partner exposure.
- Co-hosted workshop (high): hands-on education.
- Conference booth sharing (medium): cost splitting.
- Joint happy hour or dinner (low): relationship building.

**Product and integration marketing**

- Integration launch (medium).
- Joint case study (medium), for shared customers.
- "Better together" landing page (low), for integration discovery.
- Bundle or discount (medium), for conversion and cross-sell.
- In-app cross-promotion (medium), for activation.

**Community and social**

- Social takeover, joint giveaway or contest, Slack/Discord collab, joint AMA or Twitter Space. All low effort.

## Brainstorming a campaign with a specific partner

Corey frames brainstorming around four lenses:

- **Shared audience moments:** trigger events, seasonal moments and industry trends that matter to both customer bases.
- **Combined value propositions:** what customers can do with both tools that they can't with one, and which workflow or pain point the combination solves.
- **Unique assets:** list each side's audience size and engagement, content expertise, product capabilities, brand credibility and customer stories.
- **Idea prompts:**
  - "What would we create if we had to launch something in 2 weeks?"
  - "What content do both our audiences desperately need?"
  - "What would make customers say 'finally, someone did this'?"
  - "What exclusive thing could we offer together?"
  - "What data do we both have that would make a compelling story?"

## Outreach and call prep

His cold outreach template:

- Subject: "[Your Company] + [Their Company] co-marketing idea".
- Body: your role and a one-line description, a specific observation about audience overlap, a one-sentence pitch for a specific campaign type, and an ask for a quick call.

For the call, prepare:

1. Account overlap data (Crossbeam/Reveal if available).
2. 2-3 specific campaign ideas, "not just 'let's do something'".
3. Your audience metrics: list size, traffic, engagement.
4. Examples of past partnerships.
5. A clear ask, covering what you want and what you'll provide.

## Structuring the agreement and measuring results

Alignment questions before launch:

- Lead ownership: split or shared?
- Promotion commitments from each side.
- Who creates and who approves assets.
- Timeline, success metrics, and whether to do more if it works.

His simple agreement outline covers:

- campaign description
- responsibilities
- timeline
- lead handling
- minimum promotion commitments
- branding and logo approval
- costs
- post-campaign metrics sharing

Metrics:

- **Quantitative:** leads (total and per partner), MQL/SQL conversion, attributed revenue, audience growth, content engagement.
- **Qualitative:** ease of collaboration, partner responsiveness, audience reception, brand lift, relationship strength.

After the campaign, he has you share metrics with the partner, debrief on what worked, and discuss future collaboration.
