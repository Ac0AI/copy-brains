## Mental models as an operating system for marketing decisions

Corey Haines frames marketing psychology as a set of "thinking tools" that help marketers understand why people buy, influence behavior ethically, and make better decisions. His method has four steps:

1. Identify which mental models apply to the situation.
2. Explain the psychology behind each.
3. Give specific marketing applications.
4. Suggest how to implement ethically.

Before applying any model, he reads the product's marketing context file (`.agents/product-marketing.md`, `.claude/product-marketing.md`, or the legacy `product-marketing-context.md`) so recommendations fit the specific product and audience.

His quick-reference mapping from challenge to models:

- **Low conversions:** Hick's Law, Activation Energy, BJ Fogg, Friction.
- **Price objections:** Anchoring, Framing, Mental Accounting, Loss Aversion.
- **Building trust:** Authority, Social Proof, Reciprocity, Pratfall Effect.
- **Increasing urgency:** Scarcity, Loss Aversion, Zeigarnik Effect.
- **Retention/churn:** Endowment Effect, Switching Costs, Status-Quo Bias.
- **Growth stalling:** Theory of Constraints, Local vs Global Optima, Compounding.
- **Decision paralysis:** Paradox of Choice, Default Effect, Nudge Theory.
- **Onboarding:** Goal-Gradient, IKEA Effect, Commitment & Consistency.

Five questions he asks before any task:

- What specific behavior are you trying to influence?
- What does your customer believe before encountering your marketing?
- Where in the journey (awareness, consideration, decision) is this?
- What is currently preventing the desired action?
- Have you tested this with real customers?

## Foundational thinking models for strategy

Corey uses these models to pick the right problem before optimizing anything.

- **First Principles:** Break problems to basic truths and use the 5 Whys. Don't assume you need content marketing because competitors do. Ask what problem it solves and whether a better solution exists.
- **Jobs to Be Done:** People "hire" products to get a job done. A drill buyer wants a hole. Frame the product around the job, not the specs.
- **Circle of Competence:** Don't chase every channel. Double down where you have real expertise and advantage.
- **Inversion:** List what would guarantee failure (confusing messaging, wrong audience, slow landing page) and prevent each.
- **Occam's Razor:** If conversions dropped, check the obvious first (broken form, page speed) before assuming complex attribution issues.
- **Pareto (80/20):** Find the 20% of channels, customers, or content driving 80% of results. Cut or reduce the rest.
- **Local vs. Global Optima:** Optimizing email subject lines (local) doesn't help if email is the wrong channel (global). Zoom out before zooming in.
- **Theory of Constraints:** Find the one bottleneck first. If the funnel converts well but traffic is low, more conversion work won't help.
- **Opportunity Cost:** Time on a low-ROI channel is time not spent on high-ROI work.
- **Diminishing Returns:** "The 10th blog post won't have the same impact as the first." Know when to diversify.
- **Second-Order Thinking:** A flash sale boosts revenue but may train customers to wait for discounts.
- **Map ≠ Territory:** Your persona and dashboard are models, not reality. Stay in touch with actual users.
- **Probabilistic Thinking:** Don't bet everything on one campaign; plan for scenarios where the primary strategy underperforms.
- **Barbell Strategy:** Put 80% of budget in proven channels and 20% in experimental bets. Avoid the moderate-risk, moderate-reward middle.

## Understanding buyers: cognitive biases and how Corey applies them

- **Fundamental Attribution Error:** When customers don't convert, examine your process before blaming them. The problem is usually situational (e.g. a confusing checkout).
- **Mere Exposure Effect:** Consistent brand presence across channels builds preference through familiarity.
- **Availability Heuristic:** Case studies and testimonials make success easy to imagine.
- **Confirmation Bias:** Align messaging with what the audience already believes. Fighting beliefs head-on rarely works.
- **Lindy Effect:** Proven principles (clear value props, social proof) outlast trendy tactics. Don't abandon fundamentals for fads.
- **Mimetic Desire:** People want what others want. Waitlists, exclusivity, and showing desirable people using the product trigger it.
- **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Kill underperforming campaigns; past spend shouldn't justify future spend.
- **Endowment Effect:** Free trials, samples, and freemium let customers "own" the product and resist giving it up.
- **IKEA Effect:** Let customers customize, configure, or build something; their effort raises perceived value.
- **Zero-Price Effect:** Free is psychologically different from cheap. "The jump from $1 to $0 is bigger than $2 to $1."
- **Hyperbolic Discounting:** Emphasize immediate benefits ("Start saving time today") over future ones ("ROI in 6 months").
- **Status-Quo Bias:** Make switching feel safe and easy, e.g. "Import your data in one click."
- **Default Effect:** Pre-select the plan you want chosen. Opt-out beats opt-in for subscriptions, ethically applied.
- **Paradox of Choice:** Three pricing tiers beat seven. Recommend a single "best for most" option.
- **Goal-Gradient:** Progress bars, completion percentages, and "almost there" messaging drive completion.
- **Peak-End Rule:** Design memorable peaks (surprise upgrades) and strong endings (thank-you pages, follow-up emails).
- **Zeigarnik Effect:** "You're 80% done," incomplete profiles, and abandoned carts create pull to finish.
- **Pratfall Effect:** Admitting a weakness ("We're not the cheapest, but...") can raise trust and differentiation.
- **Curse of Knowledge:** Your product seems obvious to you but confusing to newcomers. Test copy with people unfamiliar with your space.
- **Mental Accounting:** "$3/day" feels different from "$90/month" though it's the same.
- **Regret Aversion:** Money-back guarantees, free trials, and "no commitment" messaging reduce fear of regret.
- **Bandwagon / Social Proof:** Customer counts, testimonials, logos, reviews, and "trending" indicators create confidence.

## Persuasion and influence models

Corey's influence toolkit, with the caveat to apply it ethically (scarcity "only when genuine").

- **Reciprocity:** Free content, free tools, and generous free tiers create obligation. Give value before asking.
- **Commitment & Consistency:** Get small commitments first (email signup, free trial); people who took one step are more likely to take the next.
- **Authority Bias:** Expert endorsements, certifications, "featured in" logos, and thought leadership.
- **Liking / Similarity:** Relatable spokespeople, founder stories, community language. "Built by marketers for marketers" signals similarity.
- **Unity Principle:** Position the brand as part of the customer's tribe with insider language and shared values.
- **Scarcity / Urgency:** Limited-time offers, low-stock warnings, and exclusive access, only when genuine.
- **Foot-in-the-Door:** Escalate in steps: free trial → paid plan → annual plan → enterprise.
- **Door-in-the-Face:** Show enterprise pricing first, then reveal the affordable starter plan so it feels like a deal.
- **Loss Aversion / Prospect Theory:** Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. "Don't miss out" beats "You could gain."
- **Anchoring:** Show the higher price first (original price, competitor price, enterprise tier).
- **Decoy Effect:** A clearly worse-value third tier makes the preferred tier look obvious.
- **Framing:** "90% success rate" and "10% failure rate" are identical but feel different. Frame positively.
- **Contrast Effect:** Show the "before" state clearly so the "after" makes improvements vivid.

## Pricing psychology

- **Charm pricing / left-digit effect:** Prices ending in 9 seem much lower than the next round number; $99 feels much cheaper than $100. Use .99 or .95 endings for value-focused products.
- **Rounded-price (fluency) effect:** Round numbers feel premium. "$100 signals quality; $99 signals value." Use round prices for premium products ($500/month) and charm prices for value products ($497/month).
- **Rule of 100:** Under $100, percentage discounts seem larger; over $100, absolute discounts seem larger.
  - $80 product: "20% off" beats "$16 off."
  - $500 product: "$100 off" beats "20% off."
- **Good-Better-Best:** Three tiers with the middle as the target. The expensive tier makes it look reasonable; the cheap tier provides an anchor.
- **Mental accounting reframes:** "$1/day" feels cheaper than "$30/month." "Less than your morning coffee" reframes the expense.

## Design and delivery models for behavior change

- **Hick's Law:** More options mean slower decisions and more abandonment. One clear CTA beats three; fewer form fields beat more.
- **AIDA:** Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Capture attention before building desire.
- **Rule of 7:** Prospects need roughly 7 touchpoints. Build multi-touch campaigns with retargeting, email sequences, and consistent presence.
- **Nudge / Choice Architecture:** Default selections, strategic ordering, and friction reduction guide behavior without restricting choice.
- **BJ Fogg Model:** Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt. High motivation but hard to do won't happen; easy but no prompt won't happen.
- **EAST:** Make behaviors Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely.
- **COM-B:** Capability, Opportunity, Motivation. Can they do it, is the path clear, do they want to?
- **Activation Energy:** Reduce starting friction with pre-filled forms, templates, and quick wins. Make the first step trivially easy.
- **North Star Metric:** One metric that best captures delivered value (active users, completed projects, revenue per customer). Align all efforts to it.
- **Cobra Effect:** Incentives can backfire. A referral bonus might attract low-quality referrals gaming the system, so test incentive structures.

## Growth and scaling models

- **Feedback loops:** Build virtuous cycles, e.g. "more users → more content → better SEO → more users."
- **Compounding:** Content, SEO, and brand building compound. Start early because benefits accumulate.
- **Network effects:** Design features that improve with more users: shared workspaces, integrations, marketplaces, communities.
- **Flywheel:** Content → traffic → leads → customers → case studies → more content.
- **Switching costs:** Raise them ethically through integrations, data accumulation, workflow customization, and team adoption.
- **Exploration vs. exploitation:** Don't abandon working channels for shiny new ones, but allocate some budget to experiments.
- **Critical mass / tipping point:** Reach critical mass in one segment before expanding. "Depth before breadth."
- **Survivorship bias:** Study failed campaigns, not just successes. The viral hit you're copying had "99 failures you didn't see."
