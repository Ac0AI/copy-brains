## Offers: why the offer comes before the copy

- Corey's core claim: "The offer is the thing, not the page." Better copy on a weak offer compounds slowly. A stronger offer with average copy converts immediately.
- He treats most "we need better copy" requests as "we need a better offer" requests in disguise.
- He wrote the skill because other marketing work (copywriting, CRO, pricing, launch, paywalls) handles how an offer is expressed. None asks whether the offer underneath is good.

**Where it applies**
- Offer construction matters most for services, agencies, courses, coaching, info products, high-ticket B2B (about $5K+ ACV with a sales conversation), and direct-response.
- For self-serve SaaS with tiered subscriptions, he says tier structure, value metric, and packaging do more of the work. Bonuses and guarantees are secondary there.
- For marketplaces, he says the offer is structural rather than constructed.
- In those cases he suggests skimming the value equation, then going to pricing.

## The value equation

Corey uses Alex Hormozi's frame from *$100M Offers*: Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice).

- **Dream outcome (raise it):** connect to the bigger goal behind the surface ask. Specify and name it.
- **Perceived likelihood (raise it):** proof such as case studies, named customers, and data; guarantees; methodology specificity.
- **Time delay (lower it):** faster onboarding, faster first win, faster end-to-end timeline.
- **Effort and sacrifice (lower it):** done-for-you delivery, a simpler process, fewer decisions, a lower learning curve.
- His implication: most "lower the price" requests are really "raise the numerator or lower the denominator" requests. "Price is the comparison, not the value."

## The six components of a complete offer

Corey says skipping any one hurts conversion.

1. **Core deliverable:** what they get.
2. **Bonus stack:** what else makes the core feel undervalued.
3. **Guarantee:** what happens if it doesn't work.
4. **Scarcity/urgency:** why now, not later.
5. **Name:** what the thing is called.
6. **Price and payment structure:** what they pay and how.

- Weak offers usually fail on bonuses (none), guarantees (none or the wrong type), or scarcity (none, or fake).
- Offers that are aggressive to the point of cringe usually fail on guarantee (over-promising) or scarcity (fake countdown timers).

## The diagnostic loop for a stuck offer

1. Identify the business type (service, course, coaching, info product, SaaS, agency, B2B), since the right playbook is type-specific.
2. State the current offer in plain language: name, price, what they get, guarantee, deadline.
3. Score each of the four value levers 1-10. The lowest is the binding constraint.
4. Audit which of the six components is missing or weak.
5. Fix one lever per iteration. Don't rebuild everything.
6. Draft the changed component: new bonus, guarantee, scarcity, name, or payment plan.
7. Project the lift honestly.

**Lift expectations**
- Plan on 10-40% relative lift from one component change.
- Multiply lifts, don't add them. Example: +20% then +30% = 1.20 × 1.30 = 1.56, so a 2% conversion rate becomes 3.12%, a gain of 1.12 percentage points.
- Two iterations rarely reach 2-3x. He says "anyone promising 5x is selling something."
- Confirm the combined offer with a test.

## What not to do when designing offers

- **Manipulative scarcity:** fake countdown timers and "only 3 spots left" lies. Short-term lift, long-term trust collapse.
- **Over-promising guarantees:** for example "double your revenue or refund + $1,000." Refund risk eats margin, and the few failures hurt your reputation publicly.
- **Bonus inflation:** stacking $50K of "bonuses" on a $497 product. Sophisticated buyers see through it. Bonuses should be additive, not exaggerated.
- **Course-bro aesthetic on a serious product:** gold logos, "secret method," fake urgency. It pattern-matches to scam.
- **Discounting to acquire:** discount-askers churn at about 2× the rate of full-price customers, and a coupon anchors the product as cheap. Discount only for upgrades and cross-sells to existing customers, or real seasonal windows. To win a new customer, raise value with an offer instead.
- His stance: "opinionated, but honest." Building offers well doesn't mean building offers loud.

## Language to avoid in offer copy

- Banned: "game-changing," "revolutionary," "disruptive," "next-level," "10x," which he says pattern-match to AI slop and course-bro.
- Banned: "secret," "hidden," "what they don't want you to know," which he calls clickbait.
- Don't say "limited time" without an actual time limit.
- Don't claim "worth $X" or "$Y value" with no comparable.
- Don't say "100% guaranteed" without stating conditions, which is risky legally and for the brand.
- Instead use specific numbers, named customers, concrete outcomes, and real timelines. "Specificity beats superlatives."
