## Principles for writing cold emails that sound like a peer

- Corey wants cold emails to read like they came from "a sharp, thoughtful human," not a sales machine following a template. The target voice is "a smart colleague who noticed something relevant and is sharing it": conversational but not sloppy, confident but not pushy.
- Use contractions and read the email aloud. If it sounds like marketing copy, rewrite it.
- Every sentence must earn its place. Cold email is ruthlessly short, and the best ones "feel like they could have been shorter, not longer."
- Lead with the reader's world. "You/your" should dominate over "I/we." Never open with who you are or what your company does.
- Calibrate to the audience:
  - C-suite: ultra-brief, peer-level, understated.
  - Mid-level: more specific value, slightly more detail.
  - Technical: precise, no fluff, respects their intelligence.
- Don't block on missing inputs. If there's a strong signal and a clear value prop, write the email and note what would make it stronger.
- Inputs he asks for: who the recipient is and why them, the outcome wanted (meeting, reply, intro, demo), the specific problem solved, proof (a result, case study or credibility signal), and research signals (funding, hiring, LinkedIn posts, company news, tech stack changes).

## Personalization, structure and the one ask

- Personalization has to connect to the problem. His test: "If you remove the personalized opening and the email still makes sense, the personalization isn't working." The observation should lead naturally into why you're reaching out.
- There is no single right structure. He lists shapes that work:
  - Observation → Problem → Proof → Ask: you noticed X, which usually means Y challenge; we helped Z with that; interested?
  - Question → Value → Ask: struggling with X? We do Y. Company Z saw a result. Worth a look?
  - Trigger → Insight → Ask: congrats on X; that usually creates Y challenge; we've helped similar companies; curious?
  - Story → Bridge → Ask: a similar company had a problem, solved it this way; relevant to you?
- One ask, low friction. Interest-based CTAs ("Worth exploring?" / "Would this be useful?") beat meeting requests. One CTA per email, easy to answer with a one-line reply.
- Don't ask for 30-minute calls in the first touch.
- One proof point beats ten features. Avoid feature dumps.

## Subject lines and follow-up sequences

- Subject lines should be short, boring and internal-looking:
  - 2-4 words, lowercase, no punctuation tricks.
  - They should look like they came from a colleague ("reply rates," "hiring ops," "Q2 forecast").
  - No product pitches, no urgency, no emojis, no prospect first name.
  - Never use fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" subject lines.
- Follow-ups should each add something new: a different angle, fresh proof or a useful resource. "Just checking in" gives the reader no reason to respond.
- Sequences run 3-5 total emails with increasing gaps between them.
- Each email should stand alone, since the prospect may not have read the earlier ones.
- The breakup email is the last touch, and Corey says to honor it.

## Deliverability setup before the first send

Copy only matters if it reaches the inbox. His pre-send checklist:
- Send from secondary domains, never the primary one. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and warm mailboxes for 3-4 weeks.
- Verify every list right before upload and keep bounces under 2%. Tools he lists for verification: Truelist and Hunter.
- Turn off open and click tracking. Measure replies instead.
- Include a plain-text opt-out line and your postal address in every email.
- Avoid HTML, images and multiple links.
- Sending tools he lists: Instantly, lemlist and Outreach. Instantly's webhooks (reply received, lead interested, bounced, unsubscribed) are, in his words, "the simplest way" to drive cross-channel stop and reply triage from an agent.

## Multichannel outbound and plays

- Corey says most outbound that works in 2026 runs on more than one channel: email, LinkedIn, calls, video and ads, with cadences varying by account tier.
- Cross-channel rule: a reply on any channel stops every channel.
- LinkedIn steps follow an engage-then-connect sequence. Tools he lists are HeyReach and lemlist, both of which carry LinkedIn terms-of-service risk.
- Outbound plays he names:
  - Value-first teardowns for services.
  - Product-led outbound for self-serve SaaS.
  - Problem-led outbound with a free sample.
  - Founder-led outreach.
- For the list-building step upstream of cold email, he points to a separate prospecting skill. Call scripts and voicemails sit in sales-enablement.

## Handling replies

- Classify every reply into one of: positive, information request, objection, not now, referral, out of office, unsubscribe.
- Answer positive replies within minutes.
- Book with two proposed times plus a link. Calendar tools he lists: Calendly and SavvyCal.
- Log replies in a CRM (he lists HubSpot, Attio and Close).
- He draws a line between what an agent can handle alone and what needs a human.

## What to avoid: copy that signals "AI wrote this"

Corey says prospects spot these in the first line and delete the email:
- Openers like "I hope this email finds you well," "I came across your profile," or "My name is X and I work at Y."
- Jargon: "synergy," "leverage," "circle back," "best-in-class," "leading provider."
- Contrast reveals ("It's not about X, it's about Y") and "no X, no Y, no Z" lists.
- A claim followed by a comma and more restating clauses.
- Self-answered questions and colon reveals ("The result? 40% more meetings."). A real question you want answered is fine.
- Stock phrases: "Here's the thing," "I'll be honest," "Quick question" as an opener, "Say goodbye to," "Unlock," "Take it to the next level."
- Em dashes.
- Identical templates with only {{FirstName}} swapped.

## Quality check before presenting an email

Corey's gut-check questions:
- Does it sound like a human wrote it? Read it aloud and check for the AI tells.
- Would you reply to it if you received it?
- Does every sentence serve the reader, not the sender?
- Is the personalization connected to the problem?
- Is there one clear, low-friction ask?
