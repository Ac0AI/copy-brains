## Lead magnets: what Corey says to plan first

Corey's "lead-magnets" skill starts from context, not ideas. Before planning, he reads `.agents/product-marketing.md` (or `.claude/product-marketing.md`) if it exists and only asks for what's missing. He gathers four things:

- **Business:** what the company does, the ideal customer, the problems the product solves.
- **Current lead gen:** how leads are captured today, which magnets exist, the current email-capture conversion rate.
- **Content assets:** blog posts, guides and data to repurpose, expertise to package, internal templates or tools.
- **Goals:** list growth vs. lead quality vs. product education; awareness, consideration or decision stage; timeline and resources.

He also asks five task questions:

- What existing content or expertise could become a magnet?
- Where does the audience spend time online?
- What's the most common question prospects ask before buying?
- Is a nurture sequence already set up?
- What's the design and promotion budget?

## Five principles for a good lead magnet

- **Solve one specific problem.** "How to write cold emails that get replies" beats "Marketing guide".
- **Match the buyer stage.** Awareness needs education, consideration needs comparison and evaluation, decision needs implementation help.
- **High perceived value, low time investment.** It should look worth paying for and be consumable in under 30 minutes (ideally under 10), with an immediate, actionable takeaway.
- **Natural path to the product.** It solves a problem the product also solves, exposes a gap the product fills, and shows expertise.
- **Easy to consume.** One clear format (don't mix ebook, video and spreadsheet), works on mobile, needs no special software.

## Formats, effort and stage matching

| Type | Best for | Time to create |
|---|---|---|
| Checklist | Quick wins, process steps | 1-2 hours |
| Cheat sheet | Reference, shortcuts | 2-4 hours |
| Template (doc/spreadsheet/Notion) | Repeatable workflows | 2-8 hours |
| Swipe file | Inspiration, examples | 4-8 hours |
| Ebook/guide | Deep education, authority | 1-3 weeks |
| Mini-course (email) | Education + nurture | 1-2 weeks |
| Mini-course (video) | Education + personality | 2-4 weeks |
| Quiz/assessment | Segmentation, engagement | 1-2 weeks |
| Webinar | Authority, live engagement | 1 week prep |
| Resource library | Ongoing value, return visits | Ongoing |

Stage examples he gives:

- **Awareness:** "10-Point Website Audit Checklist", "SEO Cheat Sheet for Beginners", "The Complete Guide to Email Marketing", the quiz "What Type of Marketer Are You?".
- **Consideration:** "CRM Comparison Spreadsheet", "Marketing Maturity Assessment", "5 Companies That 3x'd Their Pipeline", the webinar "How to Choose the Right Analytics Tool".
- **Decision:** "Ready-to-Use Sales Email Templates", a 14-day free trial, "Migration Checklist: Switch in 30 Minutes", an ROI calculator (handled by his free-tools skill).

He also treats a course, webinar series, podcast or newsletter archive you own as packageable into an installable agent skill (rights checks, an extraction pilot, source-backed lessons, release).

## Gating strategy and form fields

| Approach | Use when | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Full gate | High-value, bottom-funnel content | Max capture, lower reach |
| Partial gate | Preview plus full version | Balances reach and capture |
| Ungated + optional | Top-funnel education | Max reach, lower capture |
| Content upgrade | Blog post plus bonus | Contextual, high intent |

What to ask for:

- **Email only:** highest conversion, lowest friction.
- **Email + name:** enables personalization, slight friction.
- **Email + company/role:** better qualification, more friction.
- **Multi-field:** only for high-value offers like webinars and demos.

His rule: ask for the minimum needed, because "every extra field reduces conversion by 5-10%".

Framing the exchange:

- Make the value obvious ("Get the full 25-page guide free").
- Show a preview (table of contents, first page, sample results).
- Add social proof ("Downloaded by 5,000+ marketers").
- Reduce risk ("No spam. Unsubscribe anytime.").

## Landing page, delivery and thank-you page

Landing page order:

1. Headline stating the benefit.
2. Preview or mockup (cover, screenshot, sample page).
3. "What's inside" with 3-5 takeaway bullets.
4. Social proof (download count, testimonials, logos).
5. Minimal form with a clear CTA.
6. FAQ addressing hesitations (is it really free, what format).

Delivery methods:

- **Instant download:** immediate gratification, but no email verification.
- **Email delivery:** verifies the email and starts the relationship, with a slight delay.
- **Thank-you page plus email:** best of both, a bit more complex.
- **Drip delivery:** builds habit, only for courses and series.

The thank-you page should not be wasted. After conversion:

- Confirm delivery ("Check your inbox").
- Offer a next step (book a demo, start a trial, join the community).
- Offer a pre-written social share.
- Recommend related content.

## Promotion and distribution

- **Blog CTAs and content upgrades:** inline and end-of-post CTAs, plus post-specific upgrades (a bonus checklist on a how-to post). He says content upgrades convert "2-5x better than generic sidebar CTAs".
- **Exit-intent and popups:** trigger on exit intent or scroll depth, and match the offer to the page content.
- **Social:** share snippets and teasers, build carousels from key points, put the magnet as the CTA in the bio.
- **Paid:**
  - Facebook/Instagram lead ads for top-funnel magnets.
  - Google Ads for high-intent magnets (templates, tools).
  - LinkedIn for B2B.
  - Retargeting blog visitors.
- **Partners:** cross-promotion, guest webinars on partner audiences, partner newsletters, bundles in resource collections.

## Measuring success and testing

Benchmarks he lists:

| Metric | Benchmark |
|---|---|
| Landing page conversion | 20-40% warm traffic, 5-15% cold |
| Lead-to-customer rate | 1-5% (B2B), varies widely |
| Email engagement | 30-50% open, 2-5% click |
| Cost per lead | Varies by channel and industry |
| Time to conversion | Track by lead magnet source |

A/B test ideas:

- Benefit-focused vs. curiosity-driven headlines.
- Checklist vs. guide on the same topic.
- Full gate vs. partial preview.
- Email-only vs. email plus name.
- CTA copy: "Download Free Guide" vs. "Get Your Copy".
- Instant download vs. email delivery.

Lead quality signals:

- Higher-than-average email engagement.
- Leads reaching trial or demo at expected rates.
- Low unsubscribe rate after delivery.
- Leads matching ICP demographics.

## The deliverable Corey's skill produces

When asked for a lead magnet strategy, the output has five parts:

1. **Recommendation:** format and topic, buyer stage, why this format for this audience, estimated creation effort.
2. **Content outline:** key sections, length and scope, what makes it unique.
3. **Gating and capture plan:** what to gate, form fields, landing page structure.
4. **Distribution plan:** promotion channels, content upgrade opportunities, paid amplification if relevant.
5. **Measurement plan:** KPIs and targets, what to A/B test first.

He keeps scope tight by handing adjacent work to other skills: interactive tools to free-tools, the content itself to copywriting, the post-capture sequence to emails, and form and landing-page optimization to cro and popups.
