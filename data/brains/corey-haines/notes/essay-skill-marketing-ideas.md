## Marketing ideas as a stage-matched shortlist, not a catalog

Corey's "marketing-ideas" skill is a playbook for an AI agent, built on a library of 139 marketing ideas. The agent's job is to match a few ideas to the user's stage, budget and resources instead of listing everything. He treats this skill as the starting point whenever someone is stuck ("I don't know how to market this", "what else can I try").

Workflow he prescribes:
- Read the product marketing context file first (`.agents/product-marketing.md`, `.claude/product-marketing.md`, or the legacy `product-marketing-context.md`) and only ask for what is missing.
- If unclear, ask about product, audience and current stage.
- Suggest 3-5 of the most relevant ideas, then detail implementation for the ones chosen.
- Weigh the user's time, budget and team size.
- Four questions to ask: current stage and main growth goal; budget and team size; what has or hasn't worked; which competitor tactics they admire.

## Output format for each recommended idea

For every idea Corey wants five things:
- **Idea name:** a one-line description.
- **Why it fits:** the connection to the user's situation.
- **How to start:** the first 2-3 implementation steps.
- **Expected outcome:** what success looks like.
- **Resources needed:** time, budget, skills.

For specific channel execution he points to other skills: marketing-plan (a full plan; its section 12 cross-references all 139 ideas against AARRR stages), programmatic-seo (#4), competitors (comparison pages, #11), emails, free-tools (engineering as marketing, #15) and referrals (viral growth, #93).

## Picking ideas by stage

- **Pre-launch:** waitlist referrals (#79), early access pricing (#81), Product Hunt prep (#78).
- **Early stage:** content and SEO (#1-10), community (#35), founder-led sales (#47).
- **Growth stage:** paid acquisition (#23-34), partnerships (#54-64), events (#65-72).
- **Scale:** brand campaigns, international (#131-132), media acquisitions (#73).

## Picking ideas by budget and timeline

Budget:
- **Free:** content and SEO, community building, social media, comment marketing.
- **Low:** targeted ads, sponsorships, free tools.
- **Medium:** events, partnerships, PR.
- **High:** acquisitions, conferences, brand campaigns.

Timeline:
- **Quick wins:** ads, email, social posts.
- **Medium-term:** content, SEO, community.
- **Long-term:** brand, thought leadership, platform effects.

## Shortlists by use case

- **Need leads fast:** Google Ads (#31) for high-intent search; LinkedIn Ads (#28) for B2B targeting; engineering as marketing (#15), a free tool for lead gen.
- **Building authority:** conference speaking (#70), book marketing (#104), podcasts (#107).
- **Low-budget growth:** easy keyword ranking (#1), Reddit marketing (#38), comment marketing (#44).
- **Product-led growth:** viral loops (#93), powered-by marketing (#87), in-app upsells (#91).
- **Enterprise sales:** investor marketing (#133), expert networks (#57), conference sponsorship (#72).

## Guerrilla marketing (#121) deep dive

Corey keeps a separate reference for guerrilla marketing, covering:
- A direct-mail three-rule framework: relevance, relationship-building and precision targeting.
- ROI discipline.
- "Think in stories, not campaigns."
- "Test small before going big."
- A named case library: WePay, Xero, Red Bull, Antimetal, Arrows, ProfitWell, Buzzsprout and Wistia.
