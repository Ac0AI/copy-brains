## Why Product Hunt should be a late-stage amplifier, not the first marketing effort

- Corey says Product Hunt "should not be your first marketing effort." You need an existing audience that can amplify you.
- Positions are largely won in the first hour, and that takes a supporter army from social media and an email list.
- He recommends using it to reach the masses rather than to get your first customers. He calls it "the crescendo of your launch," the final push into "escape velocity."
- Launching later means you can be more confident that traffic converts into customers. #1 of the day is wasted if the landing page is weak and signups are low.
- His track record: SavvyCal (#2 Product of the Month), Swipe Files (#2 Product of the Day), and other earlier launches. People who used his advice reached #1 of the day, including Reform and Potion.
- His core claim: "There is no secret. There is no magic growth hack." #1 comes from nailing the basics and executing, and most people execute a half-decent launch plan badly.

## Don'ts and timing: which days to launch and what to avoid

Corey's don't list:
- Buy upvotes.
- Ask for upvotes on social media (ask for "support" instead).
- Criticize other launches on your day.
- Spam or cold outreach.
- Have people create accounts, or second accounts, to upvote on the day.
- Try to game the system or algorithm.
- Launch on Product Hunt the day after a big announcement or official launch. You've already used up your social capital and the buzz is gone, and "the results are never good."
- Launch on a Saturday, Sunday, or Monday.

Day choice:
- Avoid Mondays, when everyone launches.
- Avoid weekends, when traffic is lower and attention is harder to get.
- Tuesday through Friday are better.

Corey says his method comes from reverse-engineering past launches. You can look through years of past launches, see where each ranked, and investigate what they did. It also draws on some trial and error of his own.

## Prepare the landing page and onboarding before launching

- Treat the landing page as part of the launch. Before the SavvyCal launch, the team changed the landing page copy and customized the above-the-fold section for Product Hunters. This tripled the visitor-to-signup conversion rate.
- They also confirmed signups were turning into customers. The SavvyCal trial conversion rate was above 50%.
- Test the onboarding flow so people can actually use the product. You don't want to be fixing bugs or broken flows during the launch.

## Hunters: treat them like influencer marketing

- A hunter posts on your behalf and is listed as "Hunter" on the post.
- Hunters used to matter more because Product Hunt notified all their followers. That still happens but is much less effective now.
- Corey's advice is to think of a hunter as influencer marketing. Pick someone with industry overlap and credibility.
- The person needn't be a known hunter. A prominent investor, friend, or influencer with an audience you want to leverage works.
- He lists a leaderboard of prominent hunters at upvote-bell.com/leaderboard.
- You don't need a hunter and can hunt it yourself, but a hunter is a bonus. Corey occasionally hunts products himself.

## Build the Product Hunt post like a landing page

Optimize the post for two outcomes: upvotes and visits to your site.

- **Thumbnail, name, tagline:** This is what people see while scrolling the front page, so it must make them click.
- **Categories:** Up to 5. They help Product Hunt group you with similar products and competitors, and they surface you in search.
- **Video:** Upload a walkthrough of 2 minutes at most. It should introduce the founder, show the major differentiators, and have clean transitions between pages. Corey uses and recommends Tella for product demos.
- **Images:** These are critical, because visitors spend a few seconds judging the post mainly by scrolling through them. Use high-fidelity, annotated screenshots, with at least two images. Order matters: the first image becomes the preview card on Twitter, Facebook and similar, so put extra effort into it.
- **Short description:** Keep it direct about what the product does, like an extended tagline. It sits high on the page, so it's a good place to offer a coupon code.
- **First comment:** Make it personable, detailed, and easy to read. It works as your elevator pitch: the origin story and a distilled reason to check it out. Think of it as the PR headline.
- **Tools and handoff:** Preview Hunt (previewhunt.com) lets you test the post and crop images. It's no longer an exact match after Product Hunt's UI update, but it's still helpful. If you have a hunter, package all assets in a Google Drive folder or Notion page.

## Announce the launch ahead of time

- In the old days people kept the launch quiet for fear someone would hunt them early and ruin the plan.
- Today the Product Hunt team is "exceptionally gracious" about launching when you want and delisting if that happens, so there's nothing to fear.
- Build hype in advance so your audience is anticipating the launch.
- A cunning variant: use the upcoming launch to get more users before a flood of signups, and to exploit a scarce resource like a username.

## Launch day: post at 12:01am PT and promote immediately

This is the single most important tip, per Corey.

- Schedule the post to go live at 12:01am Pacific, when the day resets. Stay up late or pull an all-nighter if needed.
- Promote it immediately. Aim for 30-100 upvotes in the first hour.
- Post on social media asking for "support" and send an email at midnight PT to your personal list, user list, friends, and family.
- Timing logic: Product Hunt has a global audience. Midnight PT is 8am in the UK (start of the workday) and 5pm in Australia (end of the workday).
- "The top 5 positions are won and lost between 12:01 – 1:00am."
- The virtuous cycle: build upvotes right after midnight, then visitors click the #1 spot, a percentage upvote, and #1 stays #1 from random visitors' upvotes.
- The front page is like Google's: the top 3 positions take the lion's share of traffic. Unlike Google, no opaque algorithm stands in your way.
- If your post isn't on the front page, DM the Product Hunt team to ask to be featured. Corey DMs them regardless, to check he sees what everyone else sees. Product Hunt still curates, and they may tweet about you later in the day, as they usually do for a few top products.

## Playing defense for the other 23 hours

- Link the post from your personal and company profiles to catch traffic from people passing by.
- Keep posting on social and send 1-2 additional emails (3-4 extra posts/newsletters through the day).
- Other content ideas: host a Twitter Space, livestream a product demo, and write threads on what you've learned, the timeline, how you built the product, or how customers use it.
- Add Product Hunt badges to your site. This converts visitors who haven't upvoted, including those who didn't arrive from Product Hunt.
- Upvote and reply to every comment. Corey has no data, but he's "99% sure" comments influence ranking by creating activity.
- Ask friends by DM or email to upvote, leave a comment or review, and like, comment on or retweet. Post in Facebook, Slack and Circle groups, Indie Hackers, and entrepreneur groups.
- Spend the whole day on it: "Hustle. Scrape and claw for every single upvote." The gap between #1 and #2 can be a few upvotes.
- Cautionary example: SavvyCal held #1 all day until about 11pm PT, when another post overtook it. He calls it a lesson learned the hard way.
- Upside: a Product Hunt newsletter feature the next day sent SavvyCal 3x more traffic than the actual site posting.
- After launch: thank supporters, put the Product Hunt badge in your footer for social proof, and link the post in automated emails for continued upvotes.
- His recap: nail the fundamentals of the post, and build early momentum as soon as you launch. Product Hunt can be an inflection point for MRR growth.
