## Getting hired starts with proof, not a degree

- Corey volunteers on a marketing advisory board for a local university and answers the same question every year at senior portfolio reviews: "If you were me, how would you go about getting a job in marketing?"
- His blunt answer: it's not enough to have a degree in marketing. This holds even though he thinks that program is a better marketing education than most other universities offer.
- People hiring marketers want to see that you can actually market something. "They want to see the proof. The receipts."
- Marketing, programming and design are unusual in that you can build real experience without having a job, so there is no excuse to wait for permission.

## Start your own projects to build a track record

Corey's list of ways to demonstrate marketing ability on your own:

- Start a Shopify store and dropship tee shirts.
- Start a niche blog and try to rank on the first page of Google for your target keywords.
- Start a podcast and interview industry thought leaders.
- Fork an open source code base and get users.
- Make a YouTube video about learning a new skill.
- Flip products you find on OfferUp or eBay.
- Start a community or messaging group for people with a similar interest.
- Put on a fundraiser for a local nonprofit or church.
- Make a cheap commercial (or even better, an infomercial) for a product you love.

## Do real work for other people's businesses

Corey also suggests offering your skills to real businesses, which gives you results and references:

- Run some ads to a friend's Etsy store.
- Make posters for a local business and hang them in high-traffic areas.
- Offer to implement a referral program for your favorite coffee shop.
- Offer to follow a solopreneur around for the day, film what they do, and chop it up into social content.
- Offer to improve the Google Business and Yelp profiles of a mom and pop store.
- Offer to set up basic web analytics for a consultant you know.

## Practice with spec work on brands you love

When you can't get a client, create the work anyway:

- Rewrite the copy on your favorite product's website.
- Make a proposal for a new product line of a brand you love.
- Imagine a product collab between a brand and a celebrity and mock up a marketing plan for it.
- Create launch assets for a feature a software tool you love just released.

## Mindset: cost, time, relevance and inexperience don't matter

Corey dismisses the usual objections:

- **Money:** It doesn't matter if it costs you money. "In fact, it's better if it does — it shows your commitment and resourcefulness."
- **Time:** It doesn't matter if it costs you time. "Stop worrying about what your time is worth and start doing things that make your time worth more."
- **Relevance:** It doesn't matter if the project is unrelated to what you want to get into. "Everything is marketing. It's all related. It's all interconnected."
- **Experience:** It doesn't matter if you've never done it before. "That's the whole point."

## Show the whole story, failures included

- Corey's loop: "Try. Fail spectacularly. Learn. Iterate. Try again. Fail again. Then try again."
- Show your failures, successes and everything in between, because "there's a lot of gray area in marketing."
- The goal is to be able to say to a hiring manager: "Show them that you've done it."
- A traditional marketing degree, or a program like Praxis or Alt Marketing School, is a bonus on top of this proof, not a substitute for it.
