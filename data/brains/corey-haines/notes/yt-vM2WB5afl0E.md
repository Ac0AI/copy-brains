## AI removes grunt work and makes every marketer a "creative director"

- Corey says AI is "really, really good at a lot of things that humans aren't, but that we had to do anyways." For him that means weekly reports, digging into why traffic dropped on a given day, and other repetitive minutiae. He was never a spreadsheet or data person and hated that work.
- He uses an analogy from rewatching Mad Men. 60-70 years ago account men, creative directors, copywriters and the art team all relied on typists and secretaries for their grunt work.
- AI plays the typist role now. His line: "we all get to just be creative directors essentially."
- He thinks the level of creativity this unlocks is "mind-blowing." He expects marketing to be more fun and more productive, and expects people to do "a lot more with a lot less."
- The tradeoff is that the bar goes up. Everyone will expect you to use as much AI as you can, because it makes you more productive, and people who don't will get scrutiny on whether they are keeping up and are a good fit.
- He doesn't want people to stop thinking for themselves. AI "should enhance and unlock your creativity, not replace it."

## How marketing hiring and team structure change

- Corey sees hiring bars rising. Entry-level and junior marketers are in a "super tough spot," because nobody wants to hire someone who doesn't know anything, hand them AI, and say "let her rip." He calls that scary and not very productive.
- His view is to give AI tools to senior-level subject matter experts. A team that needed 10 people may need four to accomplish the same thing, because each person is far more efficient.
- On leadership, he says "leading from the front" has always been his view of real leadership. A marketing leader, even at CMO level, should open the terminal, look into a skill, and work alongside the team rather than telling them "you guys adopt AI."
- He acknowledges everyone is being forced to adopt AI and learn uncomfortable things at a fast pace. He says you have to be intentional about keeping up, because a year from now things will look very different and there will be more to catch up on.
- In his rapid-fire answers he picks an AI agent over a junior marketer, a creator over a strategist, and founder-led content over brand content.

## Why marketers should become technical, and work in the terminal

- Corey started marketing in 2016 at Cordial, a San Diego startup, then was first marketing hire at several startups. During COVID he moved into consulting and started the Swipe Files newsletter.
- In 2023, within about two months, he started his agency Conversion Factory and did a coding boot camp. The boot camp meant flying to someone's house for two weeks to learn to code. It made him "technical enough," and he began vibe coding and building real products.
- He discovered Cursor at that boot camp, very early. That gave him close to three years of watching agentic tools evolve: before skills there were Cursor rules, and before that he had a library of prompts. So when skills arrived he recognized what they were.
- He "100%" recommends marketers become technical and made a site on it, codingformarketers.com. His mission is to get marketers beyond just using Claude Code or Codex, because they need to know what is happening under the hood.
- Knowing the basics means he can watch a terminal, see what Claude Code is doing, and tell whether it is doing what he wants. Without that, people hit a failure, say "that's frustrating," and give up.
- He recommends not fearing the terminal, which he says every marketer is "deathly afraid of." He has used it for three years and calls it his most recommended way to vibe code and "vibe market," since "there's no other parallel to that level of access."
- He works in a raw terminal (via a fancy terminal client) and has moved on from IDEs.

## How Corey keeps his creativity alive while using AI

- His method is to "try a lot of stuff all the time." His terminal has around 30 tabs open, each a Claude Code instance in a different project.
- He constantly opens new tabs to test new models, capabilities, skills, CLIs and APIs. That keeps him fresh and prompts questions like "what if I could do this?"
- He says that as long as you follow that trail your creativity stays alive and isn't "smothered by your own laziness using AI."
- The failure mode is becoming "a prompt monkey" who keeps clicking yes and continue.
- He pushes back on people who say "the stupid AI is not good at this." With any tool you need to prompt well, have a vision, and bring some insight or "creative nugget." AI lets you iterate faster, but the idea still comes from you.

## Magister: a collaborative, browser-based marketing copilot built on marketing skills

- Corey open sourced "marketing skills" (skills for Claude Code). A new startup, Magister, grew out of it.
- His reasoning: not everyone will get comfortable in the terminal, and the terminal is not the best long-term tool because it isn't collaborative. You can't share a terminal with co-workers or your boss, and work lives locally on your computer.
- Magister self-hosts an OpenClaw instance with all the marketing skills pre-built. It adds one-click integrations and connections so it works as a marketing copilot, in a browser, shareable with a team, supporting multiple models.
- He avoids the "AI CMO" label, calling it overblown and not a replacement for a team or a person. His description is an "exoskeleton for a human being" that enhances what you can do.
- A key differentiator is AI working inside your tools rather than just telling you what to do in them. It can take over the browser or call APIs, so you may not need to go into a tool's UI at all.
- Magister uses the marketing skills to build, grow and market itself, and Corey calls this a virtuous cycle of dogfooding ("drink our own champagne"). His team is its own biggest power user.
- He notes that three years ago he was a very non-technical marketer, and now he pushes production code on an AI marketing startup.

## Moats and the future of marketing software

- Corey says software moats are interesting because there is no true moat beyond things like the "Seven Laws of Power" style items: network effects and access to a cheap resource.
- For software he says it comes down to price, user experience, and positioning.
- He expects the big shift over the next couple of years to be agent friendliness. Marketing tools will need first-class developer experience: APIs, CLIs and MCPs, so agents can connect to them and use them from a terminal or Claude Code.
- He predicts two kinds of tools: ones that embrace this and ones that dismiss it as a fad and get left behind.
- He also expects a split between vertical and horizontal software. Tools in the middle that do "a little bit of both" will struggle, because buyers will pick best-in-class vertical software for their niche or best-in-class horizontal software that does one thing better than anything else.
- He pictures marketers assembling an "Avengers" team of tools and agents from those best-in-class pieces.

## Advice for using AI skills well: look under the hood

- Since releasing marketing skills, Corey gets many questions about using skills, preventing context decay, and avoiding AI slop and production issues. His main advice is "just look under the hood" and think from first principles.
- A skill is just a markdown file, so you can read the text. When people ask how a skill works, he asks whether they read it, and tells them to look, try it, and report back.
- Skills behave differently depending on what you feed them, such as how your knowledge base is set up, and different models behave differently too. Marketers should understand how an LLM works and what different models are good and bad at.
- A basic habit when downloading a new skill: ask the agent "how should I use this skill?" and have it walk you through step by step. He says agents can tell you how to inform them, so you don't have to figure it out alone.
- He wants marketers comfortable with the technical side, including the terminal, and not afraid to get their hands dirty.

## Rapid-fire opinions on channels and tactics

- Brand over pipeline: brand.
- Build community or buy ads: buy ads.
- Outbound or content: content.
- AEO vs SEO in 2027: he thinks next year will be AEO but has a "controversial take" that they are the same. Anything done for SEO is good for AEO, so it is "a two for one." He says people are getting too deep into the semantics.
- MQLs: he doesn't think it's time to kill them off, and says we can still chase them.
- Run the playbook or burn it: run it. "There's still a lot of good playbooks out there. The fundamentals."
- Ship fast and ugly or slow but polished: fast and ugly.
- Claude or ChatGPT: Claude.
