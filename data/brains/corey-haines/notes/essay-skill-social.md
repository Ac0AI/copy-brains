## Storytelling comes first on every platform

- Corey's central rule for social: "storytelling and connecting with your audience come first." The better someone shares their story, the better the posts perform.
- Platform tactics and algorithm mechanics amplify a good story. They never replace one.
- He puts this at the center of every recommendation in his social skill.

## Setting up before creating content

Corey has the agent gather context before writing anything:
- **Goals:** the primary objective (brand awareness, leads, traffic, community), the action people should take, and whether the brand is personal, company, or both.
- **Audience:** who to reach, which platforms they use, what content they engage with.
- **Brand voice:** tone (professional, casual, witty, authoritative), topics to avoid, terminology rules.
- **Resources:** time available, existing content to repurpose, ability to make video.
- He checks for an existing product marketing context file first and asks only for what is missing.

## Platform quick reference and content pillars

| Platform | Best for | Frequency | Key format |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | B2B, thought leadership | 3-5x/week | Carousels, stories |
| Twitter/X | Tech, real-time, community | 3-10x/day | Threads, hot takes |
| Instagram | Visual brands, lifestyle | 1-2 posts + Stories daily | Reels, carousels |
| TikTok | Awareness, younger audiences | 1-4x/day | Short-form video |
| Facebook | Communities, local business | 1-2x/day | Groups, native video |

Corey builds content around 3-5 pillars. His example split for a SaaS founder:
- Industry insights 30% (trends, data, predictions)
- Behind-the-scenes 25% (building the company, lessons learned)
- Educational 25% (how-tos, frameworks, tips)
- Personal 15% (stories, values, hot takes)
- Promotional 5% (product updates, offers)

Questions he asks per pillar: what unique perspective do you have, what does your audience ask, what has performed before, what can you create consistently, and what aligns with business goals.

## Hook formulas and avoiding AI tells

"The first line determines whether anyone reads the rest." His hook shapes:
- **Curiosity:** "I was wrong about [common belief]." / "[Impressive result] in [surprisingly short time]."
- **Story:** "Last week, [unexpected thing] happened." / "3 years ago, I [past state]. Today, [current state]."
- **Value:** "How to [outcome] (without [common pain]):" / "Stop [common mistake]. Do this instead:"
- **Contrarian:** "Unpopular opinion: [bold statement]" / "I stopped [common practice] and [positive result]."

A formula is only a starting shape. Fill it with a specific fact, number, or story. Readers scroll past posts that read as generated, and on LinkedIn they say so in the comments.

Corey's blacklist:
- **Contrast reveals:** "It's not X, it's Y." State Y directly, with the reason.
- **Negation lists:** "No X, no Y, no Z." One plain absence like "No card required" is fine.
- **Trailing pile-ons:** end the sentence at the claim.
- **Self-answered questions and colon reveals:** "The result? 3x faster."
- **Stock phrases:** "Say goodbye to," "Unlock the power of," "Here's the thing."
- **Em dashes** in posts. Use a period or line break.
- **Broetry:** one short sentence per line for the whole post. Write paragraphs.
- **Engagement bait:** "Agree?" "Thoughts?" "Let that sink in." End on the point or a real question.
- **Manufactured vulnerability** with no real story behind it.
- **Emoji as bullets.**
- Limit fragments and lists of three to one per post.

For carousels, he picks one of five slide-by-slide frameworks first (Value-Stack, Problem-Proof, Hack List, Rant Callout, Demo Walkthrough) before writing slides.

## Repurposing: extract content atoms from pillar content

Corey's view: "The best social content isn't created from scratch." It is extracted from longer-form content and adapted per platform.

**Blog post:** LinkedIn key insight with link in comments, LinkedIn carousel, X thread, Instagram carousel, Reel summary.

**Content atoms from podcasts and video:**
- Quotable moment (15-60 sec): X, LinkedIn, TikTok
- Story arc (60-90 sec): Reels, TikTok, Shorts
- Tactical tip (30-60 sec): LinkedIn, Shorts
- Controversial take: X, LinkedIn
- Data/stat callout: LinkedIn carousel, X
- Behind-the-scenes: Stories, TikTok

**Podcast workflow:** get a transcript (Whisper, Descript), mark the 5-10 best moments, extract clips (Descript, Opus Clip), write standalone captions, add subtitles, and schedule across 1-2 weeks.

**Per episode target:** 3-5 short clips, 1-2 LinkedIn text posts, 1 X thread, 1 carousel, 1 newsletter or blog section.

**General workflow:** create pillar content, extract 5-10 atoms, adapt format/length/tone per platform, make each post work without context, spread across the week, and reshare evergreen content every 3-6 months.

## Calendar, batching, and scheduling

- Weekly template: Mon industry insight / thread / carousel; Tue behind-the-scenes / engagement / story; Wed educational / tips / Reel; Thu story post / thread / educational; Fri hot take / engagement / story.
- Batch in 2-3 hours weekly: review pillars, write 5 LinkedIn posts, 3 X threads plus daily tweets, plan carousel and Reel ideas, schedule everything, and leave room for real-time engagement.
- Schedule core posts, threads, carousels, and evergreen content. Post live for real-time commentary, news responses, and engaging with others.
- Keep 1-2 weeks queued, review weekly, and leave gaps for spontaneous posts.
- Publishing from an agent: ask what scheduling tool the user has (Typefully, Buffer, Marky). Create posts as drafts first and "never auto-publish without an explicit go-ahead." Pull post stats next session to adjust timing from real data.

## Engagement routine and analytics

**Daily 30-minute routine:** reply to all comments (5 min), comment on 5-10 posts from target accounts (15 min), repost with added insight (5 min), send 2-3 DMs (5 min).

**Quality comments:** add new insight rather than "Great post!", share a related experience, ask a thoughtful follow-up, or disagree respectfully with nuance.

**Relationships:** identify 20-50 accounts in your space, engage consistently, share their content with credit, and eventually collaborate.

**Metrics:** impressions, reach, follower growth (awareness); engagement rate, comments (valued above likes), shares, saves (engagement); link clicks, profile visits, DMs, attributed leads (conversion).

**Weekly review:** top 3 and bottom 3 posts and why, follower and engagement trends, best posting times.

**Fixes:**
- Low engagement: test new hooks, times, and formats, and engage more with others.
- Declining reach: avoid external links in the post body, post more often, engage in comments, test video or visuals.

**Starting out:** document your journey, share what you're learning, curate with commentary, and engage heavily with established accounts. **Stuck:** repurpose top posts, ask the audience, comment on news, or share a failure or lesson.

## Reverse engineering viral content and short-form video

**Reverse engineering:** instead of guessing, find 10-20 high-engagement creators in your niche, collect 500+ posts, analyze hooks, formats, and CTAs, codify a playbook, layer your own voice, and bridge attention to business results.

**Short-form video specs:**
- TikTok: 15-60 sec, trending sounds, raw feel
- Reels: 15-30 sec, polished, rewards saves/shares
- Shorts: 30-60 sec, YouTube SEO applies

**The 3-second rule:** every video needs a visual hook, a verbal hook, and a text overlay, all landing in the first second.

**Structures:**
- Problem-Solution (15-30 sec): hook with the problem (0-3s), agitate (3-10s), solution (10-25s), CTA (25-30s).
- List (30-60 sec): "X things that [outcome]", one item every 5-8 seconds, then CTA.
- Tutorial (30-60 sec): show the end result first, then overview, quick steps, result plus CTA.

**Captions:** they raise watch time by 25-40%. Max 2 lines on screen, 3-5 words per line, bold sans-serif with black outline, highlight key words, match speech timing. Tools: CapCut, Descript, Captions.ai.

**Ideas by business:** SaaS shows feature demos with the outcome first and "Watch me do X in Y seconds"; e-commerce does unboxings and comparisons; services show process reveals and myth-busting; personal brands share lessons and day-in-the-life.

**Common mistakes:** slow hooks, no text overlay, poor audio, too long, no CTA, ignoring comments (first-hour engagement matters).
