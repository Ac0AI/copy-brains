## Positioning: PR is a multiplier, not a substitute for distribution

- Corey says PR is "a multiplier" for distribution, not a replacement for it.
- Earned media doesn't drive direct conversions. A TechCrunch hit won't give you 1,000 paying customers.
- What it does give: backlinks, brand legitimacy, AI-citation surface area, and ammo for sales conversations.
- He says to chase press for the compound effect, not the traffic bump. The bump fades in a day. Authority, journalist relationships and AI-citation surface compound.
- Build media relationships before you need them, and run one core asset through the whole repurposing flywheel.
- Speed beats polish on reactive PR: a B+ pitch in the first hour of a story beats an A+ pitch on day three.

## When PR is worth it, and when to skip it

Worth it when you have:
- A real story: proprietary data, a strong opinion, a milestone, a customer with a sharp before/after, or a fresh angle on a trending topic.
- Founder or exec time. Journalists want quotes from people with skin in the game, not a PR rep.
- A destination, such as a press page, blog post or product launch, that converts attention into something useful.

Skip it for now when:
- You're pre-launch with no story beyond "we exist."
- No one can sustain pitching for 4-6 weeks. PR is a momentum game.
- You have no clear ICP. Journalists ask "who reads my piece because of this?" and if you can't answer, neither can they.

## What makes a story pitchable

- The story is not your product. It's the trend, the data, the conflict or the human. The product is the evidence.
- Pitch journalists the way you'd pitch a customer: specific, useful, fast, and never about you.
- Every pitchable story bends toward one of three angles:
  - Founding Story
  - David vs Goliath
  - Have an Enemy (a broken system, never a competitor)
- Proprietary data can be turned into a data story.
- A milestone alone isn't a story. A milestone with narrative is.

## The four-mode PR mix

Corey says most teams over-index on one mode and should run at least three.

| Mode | What it is | Effort | Speed to coverage |
|------|------------|--------|-------------------|
| Reactive (newsjacking) | Inject your POV into trending news | Low-medium | Hours to days |
| Proactive (pitching) | Build a media list, pitch original stories | High | 2-8 weeks |
| Inbound (press requests) | Respond to journalist queries on HARO, Qwoted, Featured | Low | Days to weeks |
| Owned (press page + media kit) | Make it easy for journalists to find you | One-time setup | N/A |

- His workflows: newsjacking means scoring the story with a rubric, drafting 2-3 angles, picking the best, then drafting the pitch.
- For inbound requests like HARO, he keeps responses under 200 words.
- For proactive pitching, he researches each journalist's recent articles and builds a scored list.

## Pitch quality bar: the checklist before sending

Corey says all of these should be yes before any pitch goes out. If any is no, don't send.

- Does the journalist cover this beat? Check their last 5 articles.
- Is there a clear news hook, something that just happened or is about to?
- Could the journalist write a complete story from the email alone (data, quotes, customer name, contact)?
- Is the subject line specific enough to predict the article's headline?
- Is the pitch under 150 words?
- Did you avoid "revolutionary," "game-changing," "disruptive" and "synergy"?
- Is the ask clear: interview, embargo, exclusive or quote?

## Owned media: press page and media kit

Corey calls this the cheapest PR investment with the highest ROI on every future story. Set it up once at `/press` or `/newsroom`.

The page should include:
- A one-paragraph, copy-paste-ready company description.
- Founder bios with downloadable high-res headshots.
- A logo pack (SVG and PNG, light and dark, with usage guidelines).
- High-res product screenshots.
- A list of recent coverage, as social proof for the next journalist.
- Founding date, employee count and funding, if disclosed.
- A press contact email, not a form: "journalists hate forms."
- Recent press releases and announcements.

Put one sentence at the top: "For interview requests or assets, email press@yourcompany.com — we respond within 24 hours." Then actually respond within 24 hours. He says most companies build this in an afternoon and forget it for a year, which is fine.

## Podcast appearances as an AI-visibility play

- Corey says episodes get transcribed and cited by AI assistants, so a good appearance compounds in AI answers for years.
- He treats podcast prep as an AI-visibility play, not just interview polish.
- His prep process:
  - Research the show via RSS feed, then its site, Apple Podcasts, and the web.
  - Extract the recurring threads and host profiles.
  - Map the guest's stories onto those threads.
  - Deliver a brief.

## Crisis communications

- He says PR also has a reactive side: breaches, outages, viral complaints and executive controversies. The goal shifts from earning coverage to responding fast and accurately.
- First, assign a severity tier. Then run a first-60-minutes checklist:
  - Confirm the facts.
  - Designate one spokesperson.
  - Publish a holding statement.
  - Set the next update time.
- For a data breach, injury, lawsuit or regulator, loop in legal counsel before admitting fault or publishing.
- He keeps templates for holding, breach, outage, apology, billing-error and postmortem statements.

## How Corey measures PR

| Metric | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Coverage count (placements per month) | Activity baseline |
| Domain rating of placements | Backlink value |
| Referral traffic from coverage | Did anyone actually click? |
| Brand search lift | Did people search you after reading? |
| AI citation rate (ChatGPT, Perplexity quoting your brand) | "The new measurement that matters" |
| Sales conversations citing the article | "The only one that matters for revenue" |

- He says not to obsess over AVE (advertising value equivalency), calling it "a vanity metric PR firms invented."
