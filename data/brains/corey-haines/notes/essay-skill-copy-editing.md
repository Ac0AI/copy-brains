## Never edit by diagnosing the mode first

- Corey's copy-editing skill starts with a mode check: A is blank or slop (rebuild from the facts), B is a real voice (restore it, keep the author's argument and words), C is a point edit (one line, word, or section).
- A point edit skips the Seven Sweeps. Corey changes only what was asked plus the minimum around it, returns just the changed lines with a reason, and leaves everything else as written. The full sweeps are for modes A and B.
- Corey checks for `.agents/product-marketing.md` (or `.claude/product-marketing.md`, or the legacy `product-marketing-context.md`) before editing, and uses its brand voice and customer language to guide edits.
- Core principles: don't change the core message, multiple focused passes beat one unfocused review, every edit needs a clear reason, and the author's voice is preserved while clarity improves.

## The Seven Sweeps, in order

Corey edits in seven sequential passes, each on one dimension. After each sweep he loops back through the earlier ones to make sure the new edits didn't break them.

1. **Clarity.** Look for confusing structures, unclear pronouns, jargon, ambiguity, missing context, sentences that say too much, and the point buried in qualifications. Mark problems first, then recommend edits. Afterward, confirm the "Rule of One" (one main idea per section) and the "You Rule" (copy speaks to the reader).
2. **Voice and tone.** Read aloud. Catch formal/casual shifts, "we" mixed with "the company," unintended humor swings, and technical language appearing randomly. Then return to Clarity.
3. **So What.** Ask "Okay, so what?" of every claim. Example: "Our platform uses AI-powered analytics" becomes "Our AI-powered analytics surface insights you'd miss manually—so you can make better decisions in half the time." Add "which means..." bridges where features lack benefits.
4. **Prove It.** Flag unsupported claims such as "Trusted by thousands" (which thousands?), "Industry-leading" (according to whom?), and "Customers love us" (show them saying it). Proof types: named testimonials, case studies, statistics, third-party validation, guarantees, customer logos, review scores. Add proof or soften the claim.
5. **Specificity.** Replace vague language with numbers, timeframes, and examples. Remove content that can't be made specific, since it's probably filler. Upgrades Corey lists:
   - Save time → Save 4 hours every week
   - Many customers → 2,847 teams
   - Fast results → Results in 14 days
   - Improve your workflow → Cut your reporting time in half
   - Great support → Response within 2 hours
6. **Heightened emotion.** Make the reader feel something: paint the "before" state, use sensory language, tell micro-stories, reference shared experiences, ask reflective questions. The emotion must stay authentic and serve the message.
7. **Zero risk.** Remove barriers near CTAs: unanswered objections, missing trust signals, unclear next steps, hidden costs, and a vague "Contact us." Risk reducers include money-back guarantees, free trials, "No credit card required," "Cancel anytime," social proof near the CTA, and clear expectations of what happens next.

## The AI-tell check

Corey runs this on every edit after the sweeps, because "a reader who spots an AI tell tends to doubt the claims around it." The shape of a sentence gives it away more than any word does.

| Pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast reveal | "It's not X, it's Y." "Not because X. Because Y." | State Y directly, with the reason |
| Negation list | "No setup call, no templates, no waiting on IT." | Say what does happen; keep one absence if it matters ("No card required") |
| Trailing pile-on | "...the data you already have, no exports, no spreadsheets." / "..., ensuring nothing slips." | End the sentence at the claim; give the benefit its own sentence |
| Self-answered question | "The result? 3x faster." | Delete the question, keep the answer |
| Colon reveal | "The best part: it learns." | Write a normal sentence |
| Stock phrases | "In today's fast-paced world," "Whether you're X or Y," "Here's the thing," "Say goodbye to," "X, reimagined," "Unlock the power of" | Cut, then open with the reader's problem or the fact |
| Em dashes in short copy | Headlines, subheads, CTAs, ads, social posts, subject lines (1-2 per page max in long copy) | Period, comma, or colon |

- **Caps:** one fragment and one list of three per section (a hero, a page section, or one short post).
- **Placeholder words:** seamless, robust, powerful, streamline, and empower. Replace each with the fact it stands for. If two appear in one paragraph, rewrite the paragraph.
- **How to fix:** go back to the facts (mechanism, number, customer, step) and rewrite from those. "Swapping in synonyms creates new tells." If the fact isn't available, mark `[NEED: ...]` instead of inventing one. Keep the brand's register; a formal sentence that makes a specific claim is fine.
- **Leave alone:** FAQ questions, a single "No card required" by the CTA, a list of three when there really are three features, and a clause after a comma that adds a new fact ("Imports run nightly, keeping your existing IDs").

## Quick-pass word, sentence and paragraph checks

For faster reviews when the full process isn't needed.

- **Cut:** very, really, extremely, incredibly (weak intensifiers); just, actually, basically (filler); "in order to" (use "to"); often "that"; things, stuff.
- **Replace:** utilize → use, implement → set up, leverage → use, facilitate → help.
- **Replace with the fact, not a synonym:**
  - Innovative / cutting-edge → what it does that alternatives don't
  - Robust → the uptime, scale, or failure case it handles
  - Seamless → the real setup step count or time
- **Watch for:** adverbs, passive voice, and nominalizations ("make a decision" → "decide").
- **Sentences:** one idea each, vary length, front-load the important information, max 3 conjunctions, usually no more than 25 words.
- **Paragraphs:** one topic each, 2-4 sentences for web, strong openers, logical flow, white space for scannability.

## Expert panel scoring for high-stakes copy

After the Seven Sweeps, Corey adds a multi-persona review because it "catches issues that a single perspective misses."

1. Assemble 3-5 expert personas relevant to the copy type.
2. Each scores the copy 1-10 on their area and gives specific critiques, not only scores.
3. Revise the lowest-scoring areas first, then re-score. Iterate until every persona scores 7+ and the panel averages 8+.

Recommended panels:
- **Landing page:** conversion copywriter (clarity, CTA strength, benefit hierarchy), UX writer (scannability, cognitive load, flow), target customer persona (does this speak to me, do I trust it), brand strategist (voice consistency, positioning accuracy).
- **Email sequence:** email marketing specialist (subject lines, open/click), copywriter (hooks, storytelling, persuasion), spam filter analyst (deliverability red flags, trigger words), target customer persona (relevance, value, unsubscribe risk).
- **Sales page / long-form:** direct response copywriter (offer structure, objection handling, urgency), skeptical buyer persona (proof gaps, trust issues, red flags), editor (flow, readability, conciseness), SEO specialist (keyword coverage, search intent).

Rubric: 9-10 publish-ready; 7-8 strong, minor tweaks; 5-6 functional with clear gaps; 3-4 significant issues, major revision; 1-2 fundamentally broken, rethink the approach.

Usage: always for launch copy, pricing pages, and high-traffic landing pages. Recommended for email sequences, sales pages, and ad copy. Optional for blog posts, social content, and internal docs. Skip for quick updates, minor edits, and low-stakes content.

## Common copy problems and Corey's fixes

- **Wall of features** (what the product does without why it matters): add "which means..." after each feature.
- **Corporate speak** ("Leverage synergies to optimize outcomes"): ask "How would a human say this?"
- **Weak opening** (company history or vague statements): lead with the reader's problem or desired outcome.
- **Buried CTA:** make it obvious, early, and repeated.
- **No proof** ("Customers love us"): add specific testimonials, numbers, or case references.
- **Generic claims** ("We help businesses grow"): specify who, how, and by how much.
- **Mixed audiences:** pick one audience and write directly to them.
- **Reads as AI-written:** run the AI-tell check and rewrite those sentences from the underlying facts.
- **Feature overload:** focus on 3-5 key benefits that matter most to the audience.

## Collaborative workflow and questions to ask first

When editing with an author, Corey:
1. Runs a sweep and presents findings with the reason each is an issue.
2. Recommends specific edits rather than only naming problems.
3. Requests the updated copy so the author makes the final decisions.
4. Re-checks earlier sweeps after each round.
5. Repeats until a full sweep finds no new issues.

Questions he asks before starting:
- What's the goal of the copy (awareness, conversion, retention)?
- What action should readers take?
- Are there specific concerns or known issues?
- What proof or evidence is available?
- Is this new copy or a refresh of existing content?

## Refreshing existing content and choosing the right skill

- Existing pages decay: outdated stats, stale examples, drifted brand voice. Corey applies a content refresh framework when traffic is declining, data is stale, or the product has changed. It covers SERP gap analysis, a refresh checklist, a refresh-vs-rewrite decision matrix, and a cadence guide.
- Skill routing:
  - New page copy from scratch: copywriting.
  - Reviewing, improving, or editing copy you just wrote: copy-editing.
  - Structural or strategic page changes: cro.
- Related skills he points to: marketing-psychology (why certain edits improve conversion) and ab-testing (testing copy variations).
