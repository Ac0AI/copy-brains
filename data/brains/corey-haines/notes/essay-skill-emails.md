## Email sequence principles and structure

Corey builds email sequences around one rule: each email has one job and one main CTA. He leads with usefulness, earns the right to sell, and favors fewer, better emails over volume. He segments for relevance and says "quality > frequency." Every email should move the reader somewhere, with links that do something useful and obvious next steps.

His assessment before drafting a sequence:
- Sequence type: welcome/onboarding, lead nurture, re-engagement, post-purchase, event-based, educational, or sales.
- Audience: who they are, what triggered entry, what they already know or believe, their current relationship with the sender.
- Goals: primary conversion, relationship-building, segmentation, and what defines success.
- Follow-up questions: what triggers entry, the conversion action, what they know about you, what other emails they receive, and current email performance.

## Sequence length, timing and subject lines

Corey's lengths depend on sales cycle length, product complexity, and relationship stage:
- Welcome: 3-7 emails
- Lead nurture: 5-10 emails
- Onboarding: 5-10 emails
- Re-engagement: 3-5 emails

Timing:
- Welcome email: immediately.
- Early sequence: 1-2 days apart.
- Nurture: 2-4 days apart.
- Long-term: weekly or bi-weekly.
- B2B: avoid weekends. B2C: test weekends. Send at local time zone.

Subject lines and preview text:
- Clear beats clever; specific beats vague; benefit or curiosity-driven; 40-60 characters ideal. Emoji are polarizing, so test them.
- Patterns that work: question ("Still struggling with X?"), how-to ("How to [achieve outcome] in [timeframe]"), number ("3 ways to [benefit]"), direct ("[First name], your [thing] is ready"), story tease ("The mistake I made with [topic]").
- Preview text extends the subject line at about 90-140 characters. It should not repeat the subject; it completes the thought or adds intrigue.

## Welcome and lead nurture sequence blueprints

**Welcome sequence (post-signup):** 5-7 emails over 12-14 days. Goal: activate, build trust, convert.
1. Welcome plus the promised value (immediate)
2. Quick win (day 1-2)
3. Story/why (day 3-4)
4. Social proof (day 5-6)
5. Overcome objection (day 7-8)
6. Core feature highlight (day 9-11)
7. Conversion (day 12-14)

**Lead nurture sequence (pre-sale):** 6-8 emails over 2-3 weeks. Goal: build trust, demonstrate expertise, convert.
1. Deliver lead magnet plus intro (immediate)
2. Expand on topic (day 2-3)
3. Problem deep-dive (day 4-5)
4. Solution framework (day 6-8)
5. Case study (day 9-11)
6. Differentiation (day 12-14)
7. Objection handler (day 15-18)
8. Direct offer (day 19-21)

## Re-engagement and product onboarding sequences

**Re-engagement:** 3-4 emails over 2 weeks. Trigger: 30-60 days of inactivity. Goal: win back or clean the list.
1. Check-in (genuine concern)
2. Value reminder (what's new)
3. Incentive (special offer)
4. Last chance (stay or unsubscribe)

**Product onboarding:** 5-7 emails over 14 days. Goal: activate, drive to the aha moment, upgrade. Email supports in-app onboarding and does not duplicate it.
1. Welcome plus first step (immediate)
2. Getting started help (day 1)
3. Feature highlight (day 2-3)
4. Success story (day 4-5)
5. Check-in (day 7)
6. Advanced tip (day 10-12)
7. Upgrade/expand (day 14+)

## Lifecycle email types to cover

Corey audits a lifecycle program by category:
- **Onboarding:** new-user series, new-customer series, reminders for key onboarding steps, new-user invites.
- **Retention:** upgrade to paid, upgrade to a higher plan, ask for review, proactive support offers, product usage reports, NPS survey, referral program.
- **Billing:** switch to annual, failed payment recovery, cancellation survey, upcoming renewal reminders.
- **Usage:** daily/weekly/monthly summaries, key event notifications, milestone celebrations.
- **Win-back:** expired trials, cancelled customers.
- **Campaign:** monthly roundup/newsletter, seasonal promotions, product updates, industry news roundup, pricing updates.

## Email copy: structure, formatting, tone, length and CTAs

Structure: hook (first line grabs attention), context (why it matters to them), value (the useful content), CTA (what to do next), and a human, warm sign-off.

Formatting and tone:
- Short paragraphs of 1-3 sentences, white space between sections, bullets for scanability, bold sparingly, mobile-first.
- Conversational, not formal. First-person (I/we) and second-person (you), active voice. "Read it out loud. Does it sound like a person?"

Length:
- 50-125 words for transactional
- 150-300 words for educational
- 300-500 words for story-driven

CTAs: buttons for primary actions, links for secondary ones, one clear primary CTA per email, and button text that pairs action with outcome.

## Avoiding AI tells in email copy

Corey's reasoning: "Subscribers learn to recognize generated email fast, and they stop reading." He writes from the specific thing the reader should know or do. Patterns he bans:
- **Contrast reveals:** "It's not X, it's Y." "Not because X. Because Y." State Y directly, with the reason.
- **Negation lists:** "No X, no Y, no Z." Say what does happen. One plain absence ("No card required") is fine.
- **Trailing pile-ons:** a full claim followed by a comma and more restating clauses. End the sentence at the claim.
- **Self-answered questions and colon reveals:** "The result? 3x faster." "The best part: it learns." FAQ questions and a question in the reader's own voice are fine.
- **Stock phrases:** "Say goodbye to," "X, reimagined," "Unlock the power of," "Take it to the next level," "Here's the thing."
- **Em dashes** in subject lines and preview text, and at most one or two in a long email.

Email-specific tells:
- **Subject-line bait:** fake "Re:" or "Fwd:", "You won't believe...", "🔥 Big news." The subject should hold up if it's the only line the reader sees.
- **Stock openers:** "I hope this email finds you well," "Just checking in," "Great news!" Open with the reason for writing.
- **Sign-off filler:** "Feel free to reach out if you have any questions." Ask for one specific action, or end.

## Sequence deliverable format and tooling

Corey's output format for a sequence:
- **Overview:** sequence name, trigger, goal, length, timing, and exit conditions.
- **Each email:** number and purpose, send timing, subject, preview text, full body copy, CTA (button text and link destination), and segment/conditions if applicable.
- **Metrics plan:** what to measure, with benchmarks.

Tools he lists for implementation: Customer.io (behavior-based automation), Mailchimp (SMB email marketing), Nitrosend (AI-native email, sequences via prompts), Resend (developer-friendly transactional), SendGrid (transactional at scale), and Kit (creator/newsletter focused). He checks existing product marketing context and its learnings before proposing angles, offers, or formats that were already tested. He pairs email with other skills: lead magnets feeding nurture sequences, churn prevention for cancel flows and dunning, in-app onboarding, copywriting for landing pages, A/B testing, and popups for capture.
