## Defining "multipreneur": one person building a company that creates companies

Greg defines a multipreneur as "someone who creates multiple products per year, with the aim of creating a company that creates companies." He frames this as a shift from the old tech rules ("it's all or nothing" and "location is king") toward multi-entrepreneurship, where diverse portfolios replace moonshots and single-mission startups. He wrote the manifesto on Jun 22, 2023.

- The old model was the venture studio or product studio, which for 20 years pumped out venture-backed startups like an assembly line.
- Greg argues you no longer need VCs or "a Scrooge McDuck-sized vault of cash" to get a product off the ground.
- He rejects "go big or go home" and the need to raise venture capital in the "it" places: "You don't need to go big or go home anymore, you can go big from home."

## Venture community: validate with your community, not with VCs or geography

Greg has long championed community-based products and calls his alternative to traditional VC "venture community."

- Validation comes from the community instead of from geography or the deep pockets of venture capitalists: "Who needs geographical validation when you can get community validation? That's where the real juice is."
- His advice: listen to your community, because they are the ones who matter and everything else is "static on the airwaves."
- This freedom lets you make multiple bets per year, stay creator-led, operate with low overhead, and integrate your products together.

## A multipreneur is not a solopreneur with more projects

Greg draws a hard line between the two.

- Juggling 5+ businesses solo is "like playing dodgeball with a dozen balls coming at you simultaneously," a failure waiting to happen. His example: the first time the customer service inbox lights up, "you're toast."
- Being a multipreneur means building a small, capable team for each venture and refining as you go: "It's not about going it alone, it's about leading a pack."
- AI, no-code and global talent let you do this from anywhere, working wherever, whenever and with whoever you want.

## Step 1 and 2: write your product thesis and operating thesis

Greg's four-step process for building a company that creates companies starts with two written theses.

**Product thesis (what do you stand for, why do you exist?)**
- Late Checkout's thesis: "We believe community-based products outperform non-community based products. Therefore, we start every business by creating a community and monetize by building products to serve the community."
- Let the belief flow through every aspect of the business.

**Operating thesis (what are the constraints of your business?)**
- This is your philosophy for building products in your multipreneur empire.
- Example constraints: launch a new product every month, use only no-code tools, or make each product an upsell to a specific product.
- Greg suggests writing 5-10 constraints for exactly how the company will run.

## Step 3: write your growth thesis, starting with an audience for every product

The growth thesis answers how each product under the umbrella gets marketed, and it gets baked into the incubation of each business.

Questions Greg says to answer:
- Which social platforms do you start on, and how do you grow those accounts?
- Are you using paid advertising?
- What is your unfair advantage on social media?

Late Checkout's version:
- The first step is to create an audience for the product, as done for Boring Marketing, Dispatch and You Probably Need A Robot.
- Then they "tweet like a human" and interact with other popular accounts in the space.
- This positions the productized services as creator-led alt brand accounts (covered in his "guide to alt brand accounts" post).

## Step 4: build your teams, and use global talent plus AI

The final step is deciding who actually builds and operates each business.

- Decide the breakdown between agencies, full-time employees and contractors.
- Greg says that with global talent and AI, finding the right person to lead each product "is the easiest it's ever been."

## Productized services Greg points to as examples of the model

Greg's Late Checkout portfolio illustrates the approach, with each offering sold as a productized service.

- **Boring Marketing:** helped Danny Postma rank high on Google for "headshots" with his AI startup HeadshotPro. Greg mentions a secret SEO audit priced at $3000.
- **Dispatch:** a design partner offering a design audit, landing page design or deck design for a flat fee, or monthly work. Greg notes it is moving to waitlist only due to demand.
