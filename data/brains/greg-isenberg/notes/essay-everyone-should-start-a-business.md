## Why Greg thinks everyone should start a business now

- **AI is both the threat and the tool.** Greg cites Dario Amodei's warning that AI could wipe out half of entry-level white-collar jobs and push unemployment to 10-20% within one to five years. His response: "maybe it's time to think beyond your 401k," and use the same AI to become irreplaceable.
- **AI agents let one person move like a team of ten.** The old barrier was finding someone to build the idea. Now AI can code, design, write copy, handle customer service and run marketing campaigns.
- **Tools and distribution are nearly free.** He argues "$100 and an audience gets you further today than $1M did in 2010." What used to need venture capital "now requires a weekend."
- **Builders stand on existing infrastructure.** He names OpenAI, Shopify, Cloudflare and Stripe as the rails that used to require teams.
- **Automation tools like Lindy and n8n** handle the boring parts, which makes building more creative.

## Start while you still have a job

- Greg calls "start when you have nothing to lose" backwards. Start when you are comfortable, well-paid and have good benefits.
- Desperation makes you lead with fear. When bills are covered you can be patient, and "patient people win."
- He reframes the salary as funding for experiments rather than a thing holding you back.
- Quit only once you are "bringing in serious revenue and have true PMF."

## Test demand before building

- Greg puts up a tweet or spends $50 on TikTok ads before writing any code.
- The goal is to learn whether demand exists before sinking years into the idea.
- His advice to idea-holders: if an idea keeps you up at night, "trust your gut" and give it more than a Google Doc.
- He suggests spotting boring problems through personal passions. Examples: someone obsessed with spreadsheets sees the inefficiency others accept; a snowboarder notices ski shops managing rental inventory with 1997-style clipboards.
- He promotes his Ideabrowser as a source of daily, trend-backed startup ideas.

## The math of a small profitable business

- Example: a business making $2,000/month profit is $24,000/year.
- Run it for 10 years and sell at 3x revenue, and Greg says you would have made $336,000.
- His framing: a job gives "linear returns for linear time," while a business gives "exponential returns for front-loaded work."
- He also says that once you realize you can make money while you sleep, your relationship with time and work changes.

## Two businesses in one: product and brand

- Greg says every business is really a product business and a brand business.
- A product can be copied, but "your reputation, your network, your voice" cannot.
- Starting a business forces you to get good at storytelling, which spills into every part of life.

## What starting a business does to you

- It forces you to learn sales, marketing, product, finance and operations at once, so you "stop thinking in job descriptions and start thinking in systems."
- You spot inefficiencies and revenue opportunities in ordinary conversations, and see how money flows beyond paychecks.
- It changes your peer group toward "people who make things happen instead of people who make excuses."
- When the next layoffs hit, you are in the group hiring rather than the group looking.
- It is an emotional rollercoaster that surfaces your biases, fears, strengths and blind spots. "Most people sleepwalk through life. Entrepreneurs often stay awake."
- He adds that helping people with something valuable is a major reward.

## The closing push

- "The riskiest thing you can do is not start." He argues starting is now insurance, a way to learn and meet people, and fun.
- His instruction: "Start tonight. Start small. Start scared. Just start."
