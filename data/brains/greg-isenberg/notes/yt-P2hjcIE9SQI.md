## A safety-review site for solo female travelers (startup idea Greg says someone should steal)

- Source: ideabrowser.com idea. Greg calls it a "10K MRR plus" startup idea.
- Concept: a map-based review platform where verified female travelers rate neighborhoods, hotels and restaurants on safety metrics. Example metric: green zones for "walked alone at midnight, felt safe".
- Problem: he cites that 84% of solo female travelers feel unsafe on trips, yet have no reliable way to get basic safety answers. They scroll old Reddit threads, often answered by male backpackers. Platforms like TripAdvisor don't capture harassment frequency.
- Inspiration: Pieter Levels' Nomad List and HoodMaps (Greg lives in Miami and points to HoodMaps' viral neighborhood reviews). He says the product can be built "simple like that".
- Trend check: he likes that solo female travel is growing over time, because he wants to see the trend rising when building a startup idea.
- Revenue path he sketches:
  - Launch with 5,000 paying members across 20 destinations, about $500K ARR.
  - Scale to 100,000 members.
  - Hotels pay $500 a month for safety certification.
  - Such a business could sell at 5-8x ARR; at minimum, with some product-market fit, $10K a month.
- Pricing caveat: a $30/month verified safety guide may need adjusting ("is it too cheap, is it too expensive?").

## Acquisition plan and value ladder for the safety-review idea

- Facebook groups: the "Solo Female Travel" group has 400,000 members. Post side-by-side safety comparisons there.
- Influencers: partner with female travel influencers. Greg says many will take a few hundred dollars ($100, $500) to promote.
- Paid and short form: Meta ads, Instagram short-form video aimed at women searching travel safety, and videos built on real traveler experiences. He sees a huge short-form opportunity.
- Value ladder (a framework for structuring offers in ascending levels of value and price, starting irresistible and low-barrier, building trust at each step):
  1. Free lead magnet: an interactive travel safety self-assessment quiz (easy to vibe code). It captures emails to nurture and sell to later.
  2. Premium travel community access.
  3. Verified safety guide subscription, maybe $30/month.
  4. Safety tips and alert service.
  5. Safety certification sold to travel businesses.

## Trend: gamification, and an agency idea around it

- Gamification means points, badges, leaderboards and challenges to drive participation and loyalty.
- Evidence: Duolingo "crushes it" with it, and he argues social media is itself a game.
- Example: a friend's "brain rot" app shows your brain rotting in real time the more you scroll X and Instagram. Greg sees the pattern as brand name, mascot, gamification, and a killer use case (here, focus mode).
- He expects the gamified UI trend to get more and more popular.
- Startup idea: an agency that focuses only on gamification for apps and websites, helping companies make existing products more gamified. He calls it at least a 10K MRR idea and says whoever becomes "the gamification person" has a huge opportunity.
- His design firm LCA (design firm "for the AI age") already does some of this work for very large companies, and he says it is very popular.

## Pushback on "AI app startups will be crushed by foundation models"

- Greg responded to a viral post (20M views) by Reddit's ex-CEO arguing every AI application startup will be obsoleted by foundation model providers, leaving only 12-18 months of cash flow or an acquisition. The ex-CEO's advice was to find niches with unique data barriers tied to atoms and hardware.
- Greg disagrees. His points:
  - Even OpenAI doesn't know exactly how things will play out. He relays that a well-known AI founder said an OpenAI executive told him this.
  - Some startups will be hit within 12-18 months, but the "great AI wrappers" will build valuable companies.
  - The winners are those who own the workflow, own the customer, accumulate data, and build network effects.
- Analogy: people said Apple would clone the most popular App Store apps and end them. That didn't happen; Apple has successful apps, but many other apps created billions if not trillions of dollars of value.

## Growth framework: how to grow a (SaaS) product through creators

Greg says he uses this three-step framework pretty often.

1. Attach the right creator.
   - List 100 ideal creators for your product. The top 10 may have 500K to 1.2M+ followers; shoot your shot, but the most success usually comes from smaller creators (around 120,000 followers and fewer across platforms).
   - Outreach: agents (he has shown Lindy AI for this) or old-fashioned DMs. He likes a real selfie video, e.g. "Hey, my name is Greg, I'm the co-founder of Idea Browser, I think you'd love this product for these reasons."
   - Don't pitch from day one. Build the friendship first, then involve them. Let them know you have money and upside.
2. Attach a generous affiliate percentage.
   - Not 10-20%; 30-50%, especially early, and lifetime. Equity upside is possible, and sometimes some cash up front gets creators excited. Start with the affiliate percentage.
3. Gamify the experience (the step he says most people miss).
   - Example: the best affiliate gets a trip to Miami.
   - He recounts hearing that supplement company Goji (apple cider vinegar gummies) grew to what he recalls as hundreds of millions in enterprise value via one of the best affiliate programs on TikTok Shop, offering things like a trip to Miami and literally buying a BMW for top affiliates. The numbers made sense for them; you need yours to make sense too.
