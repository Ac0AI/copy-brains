## Greg's picks: action apps, loneliness, elder tech, AI employees, pet health and AI-native media

**Action apps: agent-first mobile apps**
- Greg's favorite category, which he says holds "100 ideas or more". Today's apps (Instagram, TikTok, Uber, DoorDash, Salesforce) either feed you content or ask the human to do the work.
- The opportunity is apps where agents act on the user's behalf: clearing the inbox, booking the calendar, filing expenses.
- His example is email. An AI-first Superhuman or Gmail would learn how you answer, handle about 80% of emails with agents, and show you only the one or two per day that need you. He calls that "the holy grail of email".
- Analogy to mobile: Facebook struggled to move to mobile and bought Instagram, the mobile-first version of itself. He expects the same with agent-first apps, and thinks incumbents will find the transition hard.
- You don't build the AI from scratch. You can build on the Claude Agent SDK and other SDKs, the way GPT wrappers worked, now as "agent wrappers".
- His formula: mobile-first, plus agent, plus a niche, plus willingness to spend, plus a boring workflow, plus a way to reverse engineer TikTok or Instagram reels to get people into the app.
- He thinks a studio focused only on action apps could work, and that the apps get acquired.
- On the worry that fully automated apps hurt retention, he says people want convenience to the max and take the path of least resistance. Cooking your own steak isn't what customers want.

**Loneliness, third spaces and community**
- Greg cites a chart showing almost 22% of Americans have fewer than one close friend. He sees it as an impact problem and a business opportunity.
- Two examples he points to:
  - 222: personality test, pick an experience (dinners, cocktail bars, salsa, basketball), then meet 5 to 7 matches in your city. He is a small investor and says they are "absolutely crushing it".
  - Fabric: 75+ gatherings a month, New York and Chicago, 500 members, applicants join a wait list, 5,000 to 10,000 sq ft spaces. He frames it as retooling struggling commercial real estate into community infrastructure.
- Membership gives recurring revenue.
- Niche down: he suggests "dads play XYZ" across games and hobbies, a "dad company" solving loneliness for men.
- Few people want to run event businesses, so someone who likes organizing events has an advantage. He says he doesn't want to do it himself, but it is "a killer business" for the right person.

**Elder tech and picking the right audience**
- 70+ million boomers in the US and few companies build for them. He lists hearing, mobility, social, memory and vision as pain points AI can help with.
- Target 65+. Don't use "old people" imagery in positioning. These users just want products that make them happier and healthier. Formats: mobile apps, desktop apps, agents, AI plus hardware.
- Greg also says "fish where the fish are". Everyone targets 13 to 25-year-old gamers, so older gamers are underserved.

**AI employees for a niche**
- Greg has done an episode on selling $5,000/month AI employees. The play is to set up and manage digital workers for businesses, either hosted by you or in the customer's cloud, or teach them to run it.
- Verticalize. Pick a niche (for example accountants or law firms in Germany) and a job title. Ask ChatGPT or Claude to list the 50 jobs to be done for that role.
- Launch with two or three jobs at a tenth of the cost, then add jobs over time until you have a real digital employee to sell.

**Pet health**
- The pet industry is about $140B, but under 2% of pets have smart monitoring. Products he names: PetPace and Whistle.
- Playbook: look at what people buy for themselves and apply it to pets. Athletic Greens for pets, Eight Sleep for pets. Look at who sponsors Huberman and ask what the pet version is.
- He says someone took his "Athletic Greens for pets" idea from the My First Million podcast and reached hundreds of thousands of dollars a month, for no cut.
- Another angle is injecting a "$30 AI brain" into existing pet devices.

**AI-native media companies**
- Greg's last idea, and the opposite of live and anti-AI content. Use AI avatars and AI-produced content, disclosed as AI, to build an audience, then sell products to it.
- Example: Rowan Cheung grew an account from zero to almost 400,000 followers in roughly 18 to 24 months with an AI avatar covering news. Tools mentioned: HeyGen and ElevenLabs.
- Quality bar: he doesn't believe AI slop works in 2026 and beyond. The goal is the top 1% AI-native media company in a specific niche, ideally with a human in the loop.
- Study how the best operators make their videos good, then replicate it for your niche.

**Choosing the niche: "date the product, marry the niche"**
- Greg's rule: many people pick the wrong niche, with no big pain point and little disposable income, and then struggle even with a great product.
- Pick a niche you love, where you have an unfair advantage, and that has disposable income. Also look for an underserved audience with a big pain point.
- The product can change as you learn. The niche is the commitment.
- Pet owners aren't always the richest group, but they spend their disposable income on pets because they care.
