## The shift: AI agents become the customer of the internet

- Greg argues the internet's primary user is changing from humans to AI agents. He frames the opportunity as "the machine to machine economy" and says "almost nobody is building for it yet."
- His thesis, from a tweet: over the next 10 years there will be "a market of billions of customers, aka agents, with millions of wallets that want to use your services."
- Agents will be personal (in your pocket, on the web) and business agents. He cites Codex, Claude Code, Open Claw and Hermes as early signs, and predicts agents will outnumber humans and agent traffic will exceed human traffic.
- The core difference: the human customer wants persuasion, while the agent customer wants "structured capability, permission, and trust."
- He expects the internet to split into a human internet and an agent internet.
- His exercise for founders: look at every SaaS tool you use (Notion, Slack, Stripe) and ask what the version built purely for agents looks like. Examples: agent-native payments, communication and memory. "Every single category gets rebuilt for agents."

## The agent buying journey

Greg maps what agents do, since each step has missing infrastructure (the old internet assumed a person did the work):

- **Finding:** e.g. "find a payroll tool for 40 contractors."
- **Evaluating:** reading docs, pricing, APIs and reviews.
- **Trust:** checking policy and limits, and being asked for identity.
- **Transacting:** paying, booking, signing, subscribing.
- **Using tools:** filing tickets, changing settings.
- **Recommending:** telling other agents what worked, which he calls the weirdest part.
- He sees Moltbook, a social network for agents (acquired by Facebook), as "just the tip of the iceberg" for agent-specific social products.

## What agents need that humans don't

Greg's list of agent primitives, each a product category:

- **Identity:** who the agent is acting for.
- **Tools:** which actions it can safely invoke.
- **Inbox:** where replies, OTPs, docs and threads land.
- **Memory:** its knowledge of preferences and rules.
- **Wallet:** what it can spend and who approves it.
- **Receipts:** what it saw, decided, changed and bought.
- He compares the wallet to an employee: as trust grows you give a credit card, then raise the limit.

## Concrete examples he points to

- **AgentMail** (YC-backed): an email inbox API for AI agents, giving them their own inbox the way Gmail does for humans. He calls it a huge opportunity and says he hears it's doing really well.
- **Stripe's agent wallet:** a way to give an agent a wallet. He expects many more fintech examples, such as a purchasing agent buying software with spend caps, approval rules, shared payment tokens and an audit trail.
- **Support:** a user's agent files the ticket with logs, asks for the refund, follows up and escalates when ignored.
- **Procurement:** a CFO agent compares 12 vendors, reads SOC 2 docs, negotiates terms and recommends the one fitting policy.
- **MCP server for a SaaS app:** tools to search customers, create invoices, refund orders, update tickets and pull reports. The agent doesn't scrape the UI.
- **Local business:** a travel agent books dinner, changes the reservation, calls the hotel, pays the deposit and updates the calendar.
- He says he needs concrete examples to understand a shift, and that they make it obvious why software must serve agent traffic.

## Making your own site and product agent-readable

- A human homepage has brand, video, copy, social proof, demo and pricing. An agent-readable one has structured docs, schemas, policies, examples and endpoints.
- Agent-readable means MCP tools, SDKs, OAuth, checkout, sandbox and receipts.
- His warning: if an agent can't understand what you do and safely act with you, "you're basically invisible to them."
- Tactic: structure your site around agents, including a `/agents` page and dedicated entry points.

## Old-playbook to agent-playbook swaps

Greg's list of what changes for builders:

- SEO becomes AEO: optimize for agents (and ChatGPT, Perplexity) citing, trusting and recommending you. He offered a deeper AEO ranking episode if viewers want it.
- Forms become tool calls: the call to action becomes an action endpoint.
- Support docs become executable support: agents do the refund, return, reschedule, troubleshooting and escalation.
- Landing pages become capability manifests: agents care less about slogans and more about what they can do with the product.
- Sales calls become agent procurement: buyers send agents to a shortlist before a human appears.
- Analytics become agent analytics: which agents visited, what they asked, where they failed, what made them bounce.
- He draws on his earlier career in analytics and voice-of-customer work (surveys plus Google Analytics) to lift conversion "from 1% to 4%," and asks what conversion-rate optimization looks like when the visitor is an agent. It's a question he "can't stop thinking about."

## Startup ideas he lists

- Agent SEO agency
- Agent identity and permissions technology
- Agent receipts and audit trails
- Agent-ready docs generators
- Agent inbox security
- Agent-readable pricing pages as a service
- MCP servers for franchises
- Agent support desk
- Sandbox for agents to test SaaS
- General method: take popular tools and apps and ask "what's the agent version of that?" His big prediction is "a lot of opportunity over the next 10 years" in startups for agents.
