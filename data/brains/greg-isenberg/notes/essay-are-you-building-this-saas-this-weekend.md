## Picking and positioning the niche

Greg's starting point for a zero-to-$100k/month AI SaaS is to pick a niche he deeply understands, such as ghostwriters, short-form video editors, or newsletter operators. These are people with "recurring, painful, time-consuming tasks that AI can automate."

- He suggests finding ideas on Ideabrowser.com, but only choosing one he understands.
- He would talk to 5 people in the niche and learn where they spend time and money.
- He would write one sentence of positioning: "I help [this person] do [this painful thing] in 10 minutes instead of 10 hours."

## Building the prototype

Greg would build a rough prototype with Cursor, Bolt or Lovable, optimizing around one killer feature.

- The brand should feel "warm and fuzzy". He would scan Dribbble for a great designer or do the design himself.
- The first use should "feel like magic."
- He would use Upwork to take the prototype further, mainly for security and iterating on a few features (he also hears Replit bounties are good). He notes he is a computer science college dropout.

## Trading margin for distribution with revenue share

Greg sets the affiliate payout from what a customer is worth. His example is a customer worth about $300 a year.

- He would give up 30 to 50 percent in revenue share to build momentum. He notes "Most people think that's too high. But they're thinking short-term."
- The framing is "You're trading margin for distribution."
- He would lower the share for future affiliates in a year or two.

## Building the affiliate engine with B- and C-list creators

Greg wouldn't pitch only 10 A-listers. He would build a list of 100 or more B- and C-list creators with 5k to 70k followers. They already have trust, aren't over-messaged, and know their audience well.

- Tools: ChatGPT writes custom DMs, Lindy sends and follows up, and Gumloop manages replies, tracks interest and automates next steps.
- The pitch is short and specific: "You talk to [audience]. I built something that solves [pain]. You get 50 percent of every dollar it makes. Want a walkthrough?"
- He would record a 90-second Loom for each creator showing how it works, and set them up with a white-labeled affiliate dashboard.

## Gamifying affiliates and users

Greg turns the affiliate side into a competition, with leaderboards and milestones.

- Examples: "Earned your first $1,000. Badge unlocked." and "Top partner this week gets a custom landing page and a shoutout." He also jokes about "Top affiliate gets a used BMW."
- He would automate weekly earnings updates and spotlight top performers on social.
- He calls this a vending machine: "Paying out lots but making more."
- The product side gets the same treatment: streaks, progress bars, unlockables, and referrals that feel like sharing a cheat code.
- He gets inspiration from playing Fortnite or CS2. The goal is that the user feels like they're winning every time they log in.
- He mentions the Ideabrowser.com Pro plan's AI agents for checking whether product ideas are any good as they evolve.

## Co-creating the roadmap with AI and building in public

Once users are inside, Greg filters product ideas through Claude or GPT with questions like: "Which use cases are users getting stuck on? What are the top 3 complaints this week? What's the lowest-effort feature we could add that unlocks the most value?" He says it's "like having a full product team in my inbox," and he builds what's already happening organically, which keeps churn low and word of mouth high.

- He builds in public with updates, teardown Looms, before-and-after workflows, and even mistakes.
- He wants to be "the main character of this movie" while giving value to the people consuming the content.
- Each artifact builds trust, so new releases feel like the next chapter in a story people already follow. He calls this "a serious moat."

## Optionality over exit, and the lean outcome

Greg isn't building for a big exit but for optionality. A buyer might offer something like 7x revenue, but with zero full-time employees he doesn't need to sell.

- With a lean setup he can cashflow, license the playbook, or spin up a second tool in the same niche with the same engine.
- His line: "The exit isn't the win. The leverage is. The freedom is."
- His trajectory is $30k per month, then $50k, then $80k, with just himself, maybe some contractors, and many AI agents. He uses no paid ads and no outbound sales team, and says those can be layered on later.
- He says it's doable alongside a full-time job or school, and acknowledges "STARTUPS AREN'T easy."
