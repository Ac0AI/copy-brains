## Distribution is the new moat

- Greg argues the hierarchy has flipped. When he moved to Silicon Valley in 2014, engineers ranked first, then product, and marketing was "the laughing stock." He now ranks people who understand distribution first, then product people, then developers.
- He believes "the wealthiest people will be marketers over the next 10 years."
- "Code used to be the moat... distribution is the new moat." Code is fully commoditized, product is commoditized in many ways, and AI can't build distribution.
- He cites 200,000 new vibe coding projects created every day on Lovable and asks how many get seen.
- Peter Levels is his example. He runs a one-person business with zero employees, 750,000+ followers, strong SEO, and trust built over years. Greg says he has tweeted about 125,000 times in 8 years.
- Founders often build, market, hear silence, add features, launch again and hear silence again. Greg says "if you build it, they will come" doesn't work.
- Greg's close: "pick two of them, start this week, don't just vibe code."

## Distribution first, product second

- Smart builders grow an audience to about 1,000 people first.
- They ask that audience what it needs.
- They build it in a weekend, 24 hours or 72 hours.
- The audience is surprised that the thing they wanted exists.
- They launch to a warm audience, iterate with real users and start making money.
- Greg's rule: "distribution first, product second, always."

## MCP server as your sales team

- A user asks Claude or ChatGPT a question. The AI discovers your MCP server and returns your product, and the user gets value. Greg says this means zero CAC, because the AI assistant becomes your sales team.
- Greg compares building an MCP server in 2026 to building for mobile in 2010. He sees a lot of opportunity to show up inside LLM connections, plugins and MCP servers.
- He gives a fintech friend's example: 150+ installations in 30 days, $0 ad spend, on something vibe coded quickly.
- Greg says it isn't for everyone. Boring businesses and some software businesses may not fit. It suits some SaaS.
- Steps for this week:
  1. Identify the question your product answers.
  2. Build an MCP server that returns that data. He says you can vibe code it in 24 hours or less.
  3. Publish it to MCP registries such as Smithery, MCPT and Open Tools.
  4. Every AI assistant that connects then sells for you 24/7.

## Programmatic SEO

- Greg says it is applicable to anyone (service, SaaS, app, agency or boring business) and is still under-tapped.
- Pick a keyword pattern such as "best X for Y" (e.g., best CRMs for dentists) or "roofing in Miami."
- Build a dataset by scraping with Firecrawl or using existing databases.
- Build a page template in Next.js, Webflow or WordPress, using whatever you already use. Claude Code can build it.
- Use AI to write unique content per page: "not just variable swaps, actual paragraphs." Add a human in the loop to edit.
- The content must not feel like AI, which takes optimization.
- Publish 100 pages as an MVP, monitor indexation, then scale.
- His directional math:
  - 10,000 pages at 30 visits a month each gives 300,000 monthly visitors.
  - At a 2% conversion rate that is 6,000 conversions a month.
  - At $10 each that is $60,000 a month from pages built once.
- He cautions that 30 visits per page won't happen overnight, because SEO takes time to compound.

## Free tool as top of funnel

- Greg's line: "the tool is your marketing." He says he is doing this in all his businesses as much as possible.
- His example is Ahrefs' free backlink checker. You enter your domain and get a taste of the full product, and getting more means spending hundreds or thousands of dollars a month.
- The loop:
  1. Build a free grader, analyzer or calculator.
  2. The user gets instant value (e.g., "your site scores 43 out of 100"). Capture their email or phone number to give access.
  3. The user shares their score, which drives virality, social proof and backlinks.
  4. More users discover it, creating an organic viral loop.
  5. Upsell the issues it finds to a paid product.
- The strategy is old, but Claude Code changes it. A free tool used to take months. Now he says you can "vibe code a free tool in a day, ship it by lunch," and build one a week.
- To start, tell an LLM what you're working on and ask for 10 free tool ideas that could act as top of funnel. Prioritize them, and ask your audience which they'd prefer.
- Greg suggests keeping a "free tool calendar" like a content calendar.

## Answer engine optimization (AEO)

- Greg says SEO is declining and zero-click searches are growing, though SEO still has opportunity. He thinks AEO in 2026 is where SEO was in 2010, so first movers can own niches for years.
- The goal is to be cited by ChatGPT and Perplexity, using structured direct answers, FAQ format, schema markup and comparison tables AI can parse.
- He recalls Peter Levels saying his AI referrals jumped from 4% to 20% in one month. He is not fully certain it was Levels.
- Steps for this week:
  1. Google the top 20 questions your customers ask.
  2. Write the definitive, structured, citation-worthy answer to each. He does not mean 3,000 words of fluff.
  3. Add schema markup and FAQ blocks.
  4. Publish on a domain with authority, or build authority through the other strategies.
  5. Monitor your Perplexity and ChatGPT citations with tools like Otterly or Profound, or by manual testing.
- Domain authority built over time is why Levels can win at SEO and AEO faster than most people.

## Shareable product outputs (viral artifacts)

- Greg frames it as asking "what does my user want to brag about?" Then make that thing beautiful and shareable.
- Examples:
  - Spotify Wrapped, which he says gets 100 million shares every December.
  - GitHub's contribution graph.
  - Stripe Atlas's incorporation milestone and anniversary posts.
  - Duolingo's daily streak count.
- He suggests studying what social products do and borrowing that psychology.
- Steps:
  1. Identify the output or milestone users would screenshot and share.
  2. Make it beautiful and branded. The logo should be "subtle, but present," because "no one wants to share someone else's logo."
  3. Add a share button that pre-fills the post with the artifact.
  4. Every share is then free impressions to your exact target audience.
- It also works for B2B, because "B2B is people, too." Users might share in Slack or Microsoft Teams. Ask what they'd share with the group you want more of.

## Buy a niche newsletter instead of building an audience

- Greg says building an audience can take years, needs daily content, has no guarantee and starts at zero subscribers. Acquiring a newsletter means you inherit trust from day one.
- He says you can buy a 10,000-subscriber newsletter for $5,000 to $20,000. Many small newsletters monetize poorly, so owners may welcome an offer of around $10,000.
- Steps:
  1. Look on Duuce.com (a listing of newsletters for sale) or search your niche on Twitter or Substack.
  2. Target newsletters with 5,000 to 50,000 subscribers in your niche.
  3. DM the owner and ask if they've thought about selling. He says many would take the call.
  4. Offer a fair price. Many owners make $0 to $500 a month, and he gives $5,000 to $20,000 as the range.
- This makes sense if you have a product you believe in and understand its LTV.
- It gives a direct channel you own. Unlike social media, it can't be taken away and reach isn't suppressed.

## AI content repurposing engine

- Create one pillar piece, such as a podcast, YouTube video, long blog post or essay, and use AI to turn it into many.
- Greg's example output: 5 to 10 tweets, 3 to 5 LinkedIn posts, 2 to 3 short-form videos, a newsletter, a blog post, 5 to 10 quote graphics and email sequences. He says it can be 20, 50 or 75 pieces.
- Tools he mentions: a Remotion skill in Claude Code for short videos, OpenClaw, Claude Dispatch, Claude Cowork and Perplexity Computer for automation.
- To start, record one 30-minute podcast, video or voice memo. It can be internal and never published. Transcribe it, drop the transcript into Claude, and ask for five tweet threads, three LinkedIn posts and one newsletter. Then schedule across platforms.
- He says a voice memo or podcast is much easier than writing a 3,000-word essay.
- "You're going to get slop by default," so you need a strong brand, reference images, a routine, research on new topics and the right skills.
- If you do this weekly, he expects more content than your competitors in 3 months.
- He calls it a "shots on net" strategy. With For You pages, you can't predict when you'll go viral in your niche, and you don't need many followers to start.
