## Greg's framework: the five-box test for a $50K MRR mobile app

Greg calls it the "Greg's 50K MRR app framework". Find a group that checks all five boxes:
- Spends money (e.g. a 65-year-old has more money than a 19-year-old).
- Has a repeating problem.
- Uses photos and videos as inputs.
- Cares about accuracy.
- Has bad existing tools.

His worked example is Vinyl Snap: older collectors who spend, who want to know what records are worth, who can just photograph the vinyl, who care because the gap can be thousands of dollars, and whose current option is driving to a record store and asking someone to buy it. If all five boxes are checked, he says you "might be sitting on a 50K MRR mobile app idea."

He admits this is an oversimplification. You still need the right niche, a well-built app, strong UI and an easy experience, and he stresses he's making it simple, not easy.

## Six frameworks for coming up with mobile app ideas

Greg derived these after reviewing the apps.

1. **Start with a nerve, not a market.** A niche works when it has identity (collectors, professionals, churchgoers), urgency ("I need an answer now"), stakes (money, reputation, time) and repetition (same job weekly or daily). With all four, "AI app can eat it." The question to ask: "who has a repetitive pain that they'd pay to make disappear?"
2. **Solve one job that always must be done.** Each app does one job "brutally well" (tell me what this is worth, tell me what to buy next, restore this image, scan and identify this object). The formula: one job × one obsessed group × one recurring need.
3. **Build around a single high-intent input.** Photo, address, collectible, logo idea, prompt, plant, room. High-signal inputs lead to higher conversion, and AI creates instant value when the input is hard for a person to interpret.
4. **Use AI to unlock a premium insight.** AI is the engine; the product is the insight. Examples: price into worth, photo into diagnosis, listing into summary and forecast, object into identity, room into design plan, artifact into appraisal. The question: what premium insight can AI unlock instantly that used to take time and expertise?
5. **Wrap it in a simple, desirable interface.** Many of these apps are "brutally stupid simple": one screen, one button, one transformation.
6. **Complete the chain.** High-intent input (photo, video, address) → AI premium insight → simple interface → recurring behavior loop → target audience → 50K MRR.

## Apps in visual and creative transformation (and how to clone them)

- **Flash Loop (AI video generator):** powered by VO3 and Sora 2, about $50K/month and 50K downloads in 30 days. Greg's reasoning: vanity, since people want to see themselves as characters, and the results get shared on TikTok. Clone it for pets, cosplay, weight loss, aging, outfits.
- **AI Home Decor (AI interior design):** about 100K downloads and $100K revenue in a month. You upload a photo, pick a style, and get a makeover for rooms, gardens and exteriors. Plans are weekly, monthly and yearly. Greg's reasoning: people hate guessing what a space could look like, and the problem exists in every home forever. Clone it for landscaping, RV and camper interiors, commercial office layouts, retail displays, Airbnb staging, patios, gym and garage transformations. He notes it's a horizontal app with many niches to go after.
- **Logo Maker:** about 100K downloads and $200K revenue in a month, priced at $6.99 per week or $40 per year. Greg argues the plain name works because people search "logo maker" or "AI logo" in the App Store (ASO). Adjacent niches: YouTube thumbnails, packaging, posters and flyers, podcast cover art, brand kits, intro graphics. He says to search the App Store for a creative-asset category you can own.
- **MojiLab (stickers and emoji):** about 100K downloads and $100K revenue in a month. Messaging is high-frequency, and fresh packs keep retention up while AI keeps creation cost down. Ideas: sports-fan packs, city and neighborhood packs (he suggests Montreal neighborhoods), culture and holiday packs, religion-specific packs. It's not his favorite, because it solves less of a pain point than the interior design app.

## Apps built on rituals, communities and collectors

- **Bible note-taker and recording app:** about $60K revenue and 60K downloads in 30 days. One tap records a sermon or prayer, then gives transcriptions and smart summaries, plus daily devotions and a lock screen widget with daily verses. It offers yearly and weekly subscriptions. Greg's reasoning: a built-in weekly or daily cadence, a strong desire to capture and reflect, high-intent content people don't want to lose, and identity, ritual and repetition. He notes lock screen widgets have been a trend over the last 6 to 9 months. Clone it for therapy sessions, AA and recovery notes, mosque sermons, Torah study groups, meditation journals, marriage counseling notes.
- **Vinyl Snap:** about 70K downloads and $70K revenue in a month. You scan a record, state its condition (mint, sleeve condition), and get a market valuation: "Know if you found a $10 album or $1,000 treasure." It mixes nostalgia with financial stakes, and collectors buy, sell and trade constantly with some owning hundreds or thousands of records. Greg points to Pokémon outperforming the S&P over 15 years as evidence that people want to collect things. Clone it for used books, comics, sports cards, toys, vintage clothing, signed merchandise (scan, identify, manage a collection).

## Apps built on bundling, daily habits and body tracking

- **Genora AI:** about 300K downloads and $300K revenue in 30 days. It bundles GPT, DeepSeek, Claude and Gemini in one app, with real-time search. Greg dislikes that it uses older models and sells to people who probably don't know, but he takes the lesson: most users don't want five AI apps, and bundling creates daily usage and stickiness. Bundle niche workflows instead: student research, sales and CRM, investing and trading, teacher lesson planning, legal and medical documents. The pitch would be "the best app for XYZ niche."
- **Lang Lang Learn (AI English tutor):** about 200K downloads and $300K revenue in 30 days. You practice real-life conversations with instant AI feedback and no judgment. Greg's reasoning: huge global demand, but learners are shy, busy or can't afford a live tutor, and short daily sessions drive habit and subscription. He says that if Duolingo started today it might look like this. Ideas: Spanish, Chinese, business English (own that ASO term), interview prep, customer support scripts, accent coaching, medical and legal English. He adds that building cash flow plus enterprise value could lead to an acquisition by a Duolingo.
- **MenuFit (healthy eating out):** about 30K downloads and $60K revenue in a month. You scan any restaurant, ask an AI about menu items, and get what to order and what to avoid based on your goals. It turns a stressful decision into a quick choice every time someone sits down to eat. Clone it for bar menus, airport food courts, hotel room service, school cafeterias, stadiums, cruise ships, meal delivery and meal kits.
- **ZozoFit (3D body scanner):** about 40K downloads and $40K revenue in a month, with over 2 million body scans and Apple Watch integration. A 360° scan shows measurements and changes over time, and Greg argues this makes progress tangible. He thinks the name is among the worst on the list and would have leaned into "3D scan" or "3D nutrition scan" for ASO. Ideas: posture and rehab tracking, remote coaching check-ins, clothing tailoring and sizing, cosplay fitting, pre- and post-surgery tracking.

## Naming and App Store optimization

- Greg repeatedly says the name matters: pick one that can rank in ASO.
- Logo Maker (plain, searchable) works partly because users type that exact phrase into the App Store.
- ZozoFit is his example of a name that leaves search value on the table.
- When picking a niche, he suggests searching the App Store for what you can own.

- AI golf swing coach: phone video with biomechanical corrections.
- AI auction strategist: photo in, expected bid range and strategy out.
- AI closet stylist: photo of clothes, outfits, resale pricing and purge guidance.
- Pet health scanner: photo or video, early issue detection and care guidance.

- Used car analyzer: VIN and photos, repair risk, price comps and negotiation scripts.
