## Greg's case that ideas now matter more than execution

- Greg disagrees with the line "ideas are worthless". He says it ignores the world of 2024: "In a world where AI makes turning ideas into software businesses borderline effortless, execution isn't the bottleneck anymore. It's the idea itself."
- Everyone has access to tools and platforms that streamline building products. Greg concludes: "In 2024, execution is worthless. Ideas are everything." He calls it "the golden era of ideas".
- He says the future belongs to people who can dream big, think creatively, and use AI, software and automation to turn those dreams into reality.
- Good ideas often look obvious in hindsight. Greg describes them as the result of years of compounded thoughts and experiences.
- Some ideas arrive as a sudden lightbulb moment. Others grow slowly under the right conditions. Greg is more interested in how to get into the creative zone on purpose: "No fun in waiting for lightbulbs moments. They might never happen."

## Distribution ideas

- Greg's first category of idea is the distribution idea. The right channel can turn an obscure product into "a money printer overnight".
- There are infinite channels on the internet, so many products can win a market, not just one.
- His principle: "the best product doesn't always win, the best distribution often does."
- Example: Logan Paul and KSI's Prime drink. Greg doubts it is a better product than brands you've never heard of. He says they won because they "cracked the distribution ideas".
- Tactic: find a fast-growing startup and copy its distribution strategy in an unknown space.
  - Example: Athletic Greens is "everywhere". It scaled by getting tech and health influencers to talk about it on podcasts.
  - The question he poses: "what other products are ripe for this?"

## Niche ideas and how Greg finds them

- Greg mines YouTube, TikTok and Reddit, and uses tools like GummySearch, to uncover hyper-specific niches that were invisible before.
- He sees niches as "untapped reservoirs of demand", not just smaller slices of a market.
- Venture-backed startups and large platforms often can't be bothered with "small markets". That leaves room for someone who serves a niche others don't see.
- His niche recipe: "Find a $10M+/year business, pick off a smaller niche and get to building."
- Tip: keep a list of data tools for finding niche ideas. His examples are EtsyHunt, GummySearch and PublisherRocket.

## Startup ideas in the AI era

- Greg says AI doesn't just automate tasks. It "redefines what's possible".
- He imagines startups offering services so personalized that they feel bespoke. He says this was unthinkable a few years ago and is now "the new baseline".
- Example: on his podcast he discussed an AI app generating $190k/month that used shared infrastructure with this kind of customization strategy.

## Creative ideas

- Greg says creativity is about connection, not just aesthetics. A well-crafted story, a unique perspective, or a striking visual can turn a passive observer into an active participant.
- The right creative idea doesn't just attract attention. It "compels action" and "turns engagement into evangelism".
- He uses DesignScientist.com to produce creative and turn ideas into reality.
- Tip: curate your feed with visual inspiration, which he calls an unfair advantage.
  - He uses Pinterest.
  - He bookmarks on X into a folder called "inspo".

## Keeping an ideas swipe file

- Greg keeps a note file on his phone called "ideas".
- It has sections for distribution ideas, niche ideas, startup ideas and creative ideas.
- He treats it as a swipe file: whenever he sees something cool, he writes it down.
- This is his answer to not waiting for lightbulb moments. He collects inputs steadily so ideas can compound over time.
