## Finding trending subreddits with Gummy Search

- Greg uses Reddit as his first stop for startup ideas, looking for validated ideas. He says he has "come up with startup ideas from Reddit that have built me multi-million dollar businesses."
- Step 1 is the Trending section of Gummy Search (free to sign up, he uses the paid plan). He sorts by largest, most active and growing, and watches daily, weekly, monthly and yearly growth.
- His main rule: don't focus on the giant subreddits. He filters to subreddits with 10,000 to 100,000 members. Those communities answer the "why now" question because they are usually newer, trending and in the Zeitgeist.
- He scrolls the list and asks whether he has an unfair advantage in each niche, for example if he or someone he knows lives with the condition (his example was a subreddit on autistic people with ADHD). He takes notes in a Google Doc as he goes.
- Switching the time frame to daily growth surfaces communities he wouldn't see in the monthly view.
- He rechecks Gummy Search every few weeks to see what's new.
- Gummy Search also has curated audiences (for example 26 subreddits for software developers), but he cares more about what is growing and why, because that is where the opportunities are.

## Turning a subreddit into problems and solutions

- Greg picks a subreddit, adds it to an audience in Gummy Search (the audience groups similar subreddits, for example "people into dog food" with 8 communities), then works through the pain and anger, advice requests and solution requests views.
- The business type is open: agency, SaaS, marketplace, software, brick and mortar or CPG.
- **Pain and anger:** the tool uses AI to summarize about 54,000 words of submissions into common patterns, taking about 20 seconds. In the dog food example he saw potty training struggles with new or rehomed dogs, dogs reluctant to walk outside, loss of a pet, and appetite problems. He writes down an idea for each pattern, such as a potty training product or content about stubborn dogs.
- **Advice requests:** he treats people asking "what product should I buy" as "people screaming" for products to exist. His job is to figure out how to build and market it efficiently. He checks upvotes and comments because the AI summaries aren't always accurate, so he reads them as directional.
- Examples he sketched: a survey site that recommends a dog breed from your lifestyle or Spotify login, and softer dog food for senior dogs after a post about a poodle mix losing teeth.
- **Solution requests:** he checks this last. It shows what products people recommend and how they cope, which gives him the competitive landscape. He adds it to his Google Doc, which becomes a one-pager for the business idea.
- On existing competitors, he says to research the landscape, but "more often than not" the incumbents' marketing is bad, they do nothing on social, and there is room to partner with creators.
- He says a simple MVP is cheap enough that someone with a full-time job can build a small cash-flowing business on the side and go all in later if it works.

## Researching creators and reading their comments

- After the one-pager, Greg asks who the creators in the niche are. He prompts Perplexity (free to sign up), for example "give me the 25 most popular dog influencers on YouTube," and puts the results in a Google Sheet.
- He refines the prompt when results miss. When it returned funny pet channels, he asked for educational dog channels and got trainers and tutorials.
- He picks a creator (for example a puppy training channel with about 43,000 subscribers), sorts videos by most popular, and reads the comments. One top video had 741 comments.
- He reads the comments to learn what people want and puts himself in their shoes. He does this across roughly 20 to 25 videos and writes what he learns into the one-pager.

## Wireframing and naming the idea

- Once he has the problems, solutions and creator insights, he wireframes the website.
- His example: in a startup-founder niche he found through Gummy Search, people were upset with designers whose sites "might look beautiful but it doesn't convert." He wireframed a design agency that cares about copy and conversion, a mix of copywriter, marketer and designer.
- He built it in Framer in a few hours, put it up and started posting about it.
- He looks for a catchy, validated name, and sometimes the company can share the subreddit's name.
- The insight behind designscientist.com came from people saying they wished a designer was "more like a scientist, less art more science." He calls it a new category and says it is now a seven-figure business from this methodology.

## Using Reddit to come up with content ideas

- Greg says building a product isn't enough anymore: you need to attract an audience and build a community, and content is key to that.
- In Gummy Search he opens the audience's Top Content view to see the best-performing posts and the common patterns.
- He looks at which formats work (long stories, memes, rescue stories) and what the community cares about, then iterates from there. In the dog example, posts like "today I saved a dog's life" and loss-of-a-pet stories did well.
- He applies the same process he uses for startup ideas: read, note patterns, write down content ideas.
- He asks the AI direct questions about the audience, such as whether it loves memes. The comments showed it is a meme-like community even though nobody says so.
- He describes Gummy Search as "kind of like an entrepreneurial assistant", a co-founder you don't pay a salary or give equity to.

## Greg's four-step process in short

1. Find a trending subreddit in the 10,000 to 100,000 member range and check you have an unfair advantage in it.
2. Mine pain and anger, advice requests and solution requests to see the problems, demand and competitive landscape.
3. Find the niche's creators (Perplexity), read comments on their top videos, and add the insights to a one-pager.
4. Wireframe the site, pick a catchy name and publish quickly, then post about it. Reuse the top-content view to plan content.

- His framing: using Reddit, YouTube and TikTok is "like you're teleporting to different communities", and his job is to observe, write and come up with ideas that resonate.
