## What a personal holding company (PHD) is

Greg defines a personal holding company as productizing yourself around a thesis. You hold multiple bets in one thesis area rather than one venture-backed company you spend 10 years on.
- Stakes can be minority, majority or 100%.
- The businesses are not necessarily built to be sold. They can simply cash flow.
- It "always comes back to productizing yourself and based on the thesis".
- Greg says he started his own after being at WeWork, which he calls the greatest example of venture capital going wrong ("growth at all costs").
- Greg says it is far easier today to get products off the ground, so you can do a lot before needing venture capital and can own your own destiny.
- Core recipe: become an expert in a niche, write and podcast about it, build products or operate in the space, and invest.

## Fund the PHD with a cash-flowing business, ideally bought

- Greg started his with a product design agency, since he was a partner in a design agency as a teenager. It grew to about 40 people.
- Agency margins are not great and it is not SaaS. But it brings smart people together who gain insights daily, and it produces cash to reinvest.
- He says about 90% of the agency's revenue is recurring, from clients coming back.
- It grew in 2022, a hard year, and he expected growth in 2023.
- He prefers buying your first cash-flowing business to starting one. People who start agencies often spend 1-4 years kick-starting the cash flow.
- Andrew Wilkinson (Tiny) told him "it's so much harder to incubate than to buy businesses".
- Buy small. Spending eight figures on a first business is too much to manage.
- Service businesses and media companies trade at low multiples compared with Silicon Valley tech.
- Pick the target from your "common denominator". For a media person, that means a newsletter, a podcast, or an agency serving creators.

## Structuring acquisitions and partner deals

- Greg says people are writing the rules right now, and founders are more open than you'd expect, especially in a rocky economy.
- Deal shapes he has seen:
  - Buy 25% as a more passive investment.
  - Buy 50% now and reach 70-100% over five years.
- A founder gets some liquidity on day one, more on day 365 and more in year two. You bring tools they lack, such as your audience posting about them.
- For an agency owner making $200-300K a year, Greg would offer equity in the new holdco. The owner takes 50-80% of their usual pay in exchange for upside, with no hunt for clients.
- Write out your commitments, such as tweeting about the business, making intros, or finding financing. He says founders are very receptive because the path is lonely.
- Sell the upside: studio projects could become $100M-$500M exits, "like a Morning Brew".
- For a young writer with 10,000 subscribers and no revenue, the pitch is mentorship. Do it alone, or "two to five x faster" with coaching and your network.
  - He'd take roughly 30-70% equity depending on the deal.
  - He suggests adding a scout fund as a value-add.
  - The model is YC-like: you accelerate them, so the equity is worth it.

## Building an audience from zero in 2023

- The internet "feels like television" now. In 2021-22 every major platform added short-form video.
- Step 1: pick a niche, then go one level narrower. "Luxury watches" is too broad; "Rolexes from the 1980s" might work.
- Step 2: choose the platform where those people live and that is not saturated for your content.
- His best platforms for going viral are TikTok, LinkedIn and Twitter.
  - They are built around "15 minutes of fame": TikTok's For You page, LinkedIn's reach, and Twitter's shift to algorithmic recommendations.
  - He notes a 2016 tweet with 15 likes against a recent one with 6,000 likes.
  - Whenever a big platform makes a fundamental change, creators should look for the opportunity.
- On LinkedIn, he says even small accounts can get 4K-50K impressions.
- Instagram is great for monetizing but hard to grow from nothing.
  - Route TikTok traffic to Instagram through the profile button.
  - Dozens of people reach 1M TikTok followers every month.
- The end goal is email addresses. Platforms can change the algorithm and zero out your work.

## From audience to products: map, validate, prioritize

Greg gave this process to a creator with 1.5M followers who wanted to start a PHD.
1. Ask what motivates your audience.
2. Map the audience into niches and sub-niches, and write down each group's motivations (for example, wanting to go viral, or luxury goods).
3. Validate on Reddit and Discord, looking for shared pain points.
4. Prioritize products that are most valuable and easiest to launch.

Young creators can follow the funnel: a TikTok show builds the audience, then a community with emails and phone numbers, then products.

## Unbundling Reddit and Community-First startups

- Reddit is hundreds of thousands of like-minded communities where you can see pain points and build for them. Greg says hundreds, maybe 1,000+, of people have told him they built businesses from his post.
- Discord (valued at $10B+) began as a tool for the League of Legends subreddit, expanded to Esports and then to "a place for everyone". Start niche and go horizontal.
- He uses redditlist.com to find fast-growing subreddits. Example: a non-alcoholic beer subreddit growing from 1,000 to 100,000 in 24 hours. Ask why.
- Become a moderator of a passionate subreddit, or create one. It teaches the lingo, systems, loves and hates, and he calls it better than being an "entrepreneur in residence".
- His agency did a brand design sprint for After Hour, a pre-launch startup from the Wall Street Bets moderator.
- Community, done right, lifts product retention and engagement through rituals.
  - Example: Lenny's meetups make people more likely to join his Slack, read the newsletter, and refer others.
  - The Hustle's Trends community was paid, added retention and engagement to a content business, and became a big business.

## Services, agencies and where Greg is taking the holdco

- Greg thinks people undervalue services businesses and romanticize social and tech businesses.
- He sees "new blood" competing with old-guard Madison Avenue shops.
- He believes "the McKinsey for Community" is a nine-figure-a-year business.
- Growth path for the agency:
  - Ask clients what else they need. Some said they'd pay for growth marketing instead of using other agencies.
  - Take a $500K-a-year client to $1.5M by adding services.
  - Fortune 100 clients prefer one agency over many small ones.
  - Acquire agencies to build a "family of agencies". Example: an ad agency sold to a client like Procter & Gamble, who already uses his team for digital products.
- Incubate with creators, since every creator has a community.
- Reinvest cash flow into startup investments for compounding.
- Funding creators: Slow Ventures backs big YouTubers with about $1M at a $10-20M valuation, and also backs multi-time entrepreneurs via studio structures. Night Media (Mr Beast's Feastables) is a manager that becomes an incubation partner.

## Greg's own content stack in 2023

- Newsletter: moving from a post every 6-9 weeks to one every week on Substack.
- Podcast "Where It Happens": it builds affinity at the top of the funnel because listeners spend 60 minutes with you and build trust.
- Short-form video: chop the podcast into clips and post to YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Twitter and LinkedIn. He says it has "never been a more valuable time to start a podcast".
