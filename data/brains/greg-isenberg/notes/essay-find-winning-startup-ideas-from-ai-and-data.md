## Core framing: startup ideas sit on the surface, not underground

- Greg argues that most people treat idea-finding like mining for gold, digging through their imagination and hoping to strike something valuable.
- He says the best ideas "aren't buried underground waiting to be discovered." They are "sitting on the surface, scattered everywhere, waiting for someone to pick them up."
- His claim is that these ideas are already validated, already profitable, and hiding in plain sight.
- In his June 2025 post he lists 20 ways to spot them using AI and data. The sections below group those methods by where he looks.

## Use AI and your own behavior as idea sources

- **Ask AI directly.** Prompt ChatGPT: "Give me 10 tedious workflows a [job title] does that AI could automate." Then "rinse and niche down." He also calls Manus helpful here.
- **Mine your ChatGPT history.** Scroll your own conversations. If you repeat the same type of prompt weekly, that is "a product hiding in plain sight."
- **Audit your browser behavior.** Look for places where you copy and paste between tools. That friction is a startup idea.
- **Study your calendar.** Recurring meetings, prep time and repetitive agendas are software opportunities. This is especially true if you copy templates or documents each time.

## Find unmet demand in online communities and search

- **Reddit search.** Run this Google search: `site:reddit.com "is there a tool that"` plus your niche. Greg says you will find users begging for tools that don't exist yet.
- **YouTube comments on AI tool reviews.** People write what they wish the tool did. Greg calls that "your roadmap."
- **High-view AI tool videos.** Track YouTube AI tool videos with 60k+ views. Read the comments to find where users hit friction or ask how to apply the tool to their niche.
- **Industry Discords and Slack groups.** Look for "does anyone have a tool for..." or people manually stitching workflows together.
- **Niche Facebook groups.** Search terms like "Notion for teachers" or "real estate automation." Look for posts where people hack workflows together in the comments, then build the tool.
- **Multi-step tutorials.** If someone posts "How to automate your podcast workflow using 8 tools," build the one-click version.

## Rebuild products that have traction but weak experience

- **Product Hunt and Gumroad.** Search for AI tools getting traction but with clunky UX. Rebuild them with better UX and positioning. Greg says "people will switch."
- **2-star Shopify and WordPress plugins.** Read the reviews. Low stars combined with high usage is usually a signal that people care but the experience is poor. That is the time to build something for them.
- **Legacy software on G2 or Capterra.** Look at top-rated tools in old-school categories such as inventory, fleet and scheduling. If there is no AI built in, build the AI layer.

## Turn manual services and gigs into software

- **Fiverr and Upwork services.** Look for offers like "I'll use ChatGPT to write your product descriptions." These are manual, AI-powered workflows waiting to become apps.
- **Upwork job boards, weekly.** Look for repeated gigs that combine AI and grunt work, such as summarizing Zoom calls or writing cold emails. If multiple clients want it done manually, "it's ripe for productization."
- **Solo and small agency owners.** Ask what clients beg them for repeatedly. If the request is repetitive, annoying and not worth their time, turn it into software. Greg says you can even pay them for their time if they aren't responding.

## Talk to small businesses and watch their spreadsheets

- Greg tells builders to watch what small businesses manage in spreadsheets. Examples are a local dentist, a boutique agency or a gym owner.
- If they update something daily in Excel, he says there is an AI agent to be built.

## Use job titles to find a B2B wedge

- **Growing industries.** Scan LinkedIn job titles and ask: "What would make this person 10x faster at their job using AI?" Greg calls that your B2B wedge.
- **Emerging roles.** Use LinkedIn's job title search to spot new or exploding titles, such as "AI operations lead." Then ask what workflows those people are likely stuck doing manually.

## Use Ideabrowser to find validated ideas

- Greg says he built Ideabrowser.com for himself "because it works."
- It scans Facebook groups, subreddits and Google Trends to surface ideas with the highest likelihood of success. It also gives ideas on positioning and marketing.
- He frames this as a way to "steal validated startup ideas" rather than invent them.
