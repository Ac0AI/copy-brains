## Finding AI agent startup ideas: boring, Excel-and-email industries

- Greg argues the best B2B AI agent opportunities sit in "boring" industries where expensive human labor is trapped in spreadsheets, document review and email chains. Examples he names: specialty insurance, equipment leasing, commodities trading, commercial real estate operations, logistics management, medical claims processing.
- Signals to look for:
  - Middle managers drowning in spreadsheets
  - Six-figure mistakes happening monthly
  - Documents needing manual review by highly paid professionals
  - Data siloed across systems
  - Each deal taking 20+ emails and 5+ meetings to close
- These industries resisted earlier software waves because workflows are too specialized, regulations too complex, or stakeholders too traditional. He says that makes them "perfect for AI agents."

## Why AI agents win where traditional SaaS failed

- Greg's example: a friend runs a commercial real estate company managing 42 properties. His team spent 30+ hours a week extracting data from leases, building reports and flagging issues. They evaluated a dozen software products over five years and none stuck.
- Reason: traditional SaaS forces humans to adapt to rigid workflows (precise categorization, fixed data formats, exact processes).
- AI agents flip this. They adapt to how people already work: leases in any format, conversations staying in email, spreadsheets staying in Excel. Agents "meet users where they are."
- He calls this the key insight most AI founders miss.

## Build small, specialized agents, not one big one

- Greg warns against a single massive agent that automates everything. His reasons: it needs too much context to be reliable, makes opaque decisions, is harder to train and maintain, and is hard to sell to risk-averse industries.
- Instead, build small agents that each own one discrete step, then chain them:
  - Extraction agent: pulls data points from PDFs, emails, documents
  - Comparison agent: checks current data against historical patterns
  - Anomaly detection agent: flags unusual terms, conditions or numbers
  - Drafting agent: writes initial responses, summaries, reports
  - Coordination agent: routes information between systems and people
- Three advantages he lists: easier to sell (start with one agent on an immediate pain point), more reliable (narrow focus), and better visibility (customers see each step).

## Worked example: commercial lease operations agent chain

- The manual process: extract key terms from 50+ page leases (dates, rates, escalations, renewal options), compare to market and portfolio averages, find discrepancies, write abstracts and summaries, set critical-date reminders, communicate to stakeholders.
- His five-agent chain:
  1. Extractor: takes lease PDFs, extracts 50+ data points in a consistent format, with confidence scores per extraction
  2. Analyzer: compares to portfolio benchmarks, flags terms outside standard deviations, validates against business rules
  3. Abstractor: produces human-readable abstracts, executive summaries, visualizations, formatted per stakeholder
  4. Scheduler: critical-date notifications, calendar entries, workflow triggers, renewal processes
  5. Communicator: drafts stakeholder and tenant emails, board reporting, answers common questions
- Result he cites for one client: a 6-hour process cut to 15 minutes and $230,000 in annual errors eliminated.

## Distribution playbook phases 1-2: content, then paid ($0-$3M ARR)

- Greg says the biggest mistake AI startups make is expecting the product to sell itself.
- **Phase 1 (0-$300K ARR): authority through content.** Post 60-second videos on LinkedIn, X and YouTube that show the exact pain (ideally screen recorded), demo the solution with real results, and end with a call to action.
  - Cadence: 3 platform-native posts daily, 2 video demos weekly, 1 in-depth case study monthly.
  - End every piece with a free lead magnet (templates, calculators, a free AI agent).
  - Sample script: "Commercial property managers spend 12 hours weekly updating tenant spreadsheets. Here's how our Extraction Agent pulls this data automatically in 45 seconds."
  - Metrics: a video hitting 2%+ CTR signals a resonant message; aim for 3-5% conversion on lead magnets and $50-100 CAC from organic.
- **Phase 2 ($300K-$3M ARR): scale winners with paid.** Pour ad spend into the best organic content, target $3 per lead and 100 leads/day, ship a new free lead magnet every 4 weeks ("lead magnet factory"), and retarget through increasingly valuable offers.
  - Lead magnet ladder: free template, free basic agent, industry benchmark report, ROI calculator, demo, pilot.
  - Funnel benchmarks: lead magnet to email list 40-60%; email to demo request 5-10%; demo to paid pilot 30-40%; pilot to customer 70-80%.

## Distribution phase 3: webinar funnel for high-ticket ($3M-$10M ARR)

- Greg adds a webinar funnel at this stage: weekly industry-specific workshops, thought leadership, behind-the-scenes demos of full agent chains, and limited-time implementation offers.
- Economics he gives:
  - $100-300 per registrant
  - 40-60% attendance
  - 10-15% convert to a sales call
  - 30% close rate
  - $30K-$50K average contract value

## Expansion paths and exit options

- **Vertical expansion:** go deeper in the first industry with more specialized agents for adjacent workflows, integrations with industry platforms, and industry-specific data models and benchmarks. Example: moving from lease abstraction to full lease lifecycle management (negotiations, renewals, facilities).
- **Horizontal expansion:** apply the same agent architecture to similar workflows elsewhere, partner with industry experts, acquire small players with expertise but weak tech, or license the agent framework. Example: taking extraction, comparison and anomaly detection from commercial real estate to specialty insurance underwriting.
- **Exits after 3-5 years:**
  - Keep scaling: he says 70%+ gross margins are common and $10M ARR is "just the beginning" for a capital-efficient business
  - Strategic acquisition: 5-8x ARR exits are common for specialized B2B SaaS with strong metrics
  - Private equity rollup: PE firms are building industry-specific automation platforms and clean unit economics make financing straightforward

## Why vertical agents work, what it takes, and a 7-day start

- Why it works: clear ROI (replacing expensive labor), defensible deep industry knowledge, enhancing existing workflows rather than replacing them, immediate value from even one agent, compounding value as agents are added, and a data moat from every document processed.
- What it takes: industry expertise (or a way to learn fast), AI skill in designing and connecting specialized agents, sales acumen in the industry's language, consistent content creation, and systems thinking. He says you don't need to be an AI research scientist or have raised millions.
- 7-day plan:
  - Days 1-2: pick 3 industries running on Excel and email; research workflows, pain points and economics; start posting daily to build an audience.
  - Days 3-4: for each, list the top 5 repetitive, document-centric tasks highly paid professionals hate; estimate time spent and error cost.
  - Days 5-7: design one agent for one task, defining exact inputs, precise outputs, integration points with existing workflows, and the value proposition in time and error reduction.
- His bottom line: build for one industry's pain point and you end up with a playbook for automating any business process still run by humans and Excel.
