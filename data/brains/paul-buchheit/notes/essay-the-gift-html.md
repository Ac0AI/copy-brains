## Paul's framing: death as clarity, and the question he asks himself

- After his brother Steve died (Steve was 33, Paul was 27), Paul says he understood death "at a visceral level" for the first time, not just intellectually.
- He found "live every day as though it's your last" impractical, so he reframed it as a question: "Is this how I want to spend the rest of my life?"
- He first asked what he would do with six years left. At first he thought the answer was an audacious goal like curing cancer. Over time he concluded it simply meant being mindful of how he lives.
- The answers can be surprising. Cleaning the kitchen is relaxing and makes a pleasant, healthy space for family and friends. Sitting in a windowless conference room while people he doesn't love debate things he doesn't care about is not how he wants to spend his life.
- He stresses this is personal: others may find real meaning in what he doesn't.
- His summary: "Death strips away everything that doesn't matter... People matter. Everything else is noise."

## Using mortality as a filter for how to spend time

- Paul treats the question as a recurring check on daily activities, not a one-time life plan.
- It filters out low-meaning work: meetings and debates with people and topics he doesn't care about.
- It favors small acts that serve the people around him (a clean kitchen as a gathering place).
- He admits he struggles to retain this: "Life gets busy, and I forget what matters." The reminders are all around us "if only we can open our eyes."

## Presence and unconditional love, learned from his premature daughter

- His daughter was born 100 days early, weighing 1 pound 10 ounces, on a ventilator, eyelids still fused, during a trip in northern New Mexico. His wife April was airlifted to Albuquerque; he drove several hours alone overnight.
- Doctors couldn't say if she would survive. Each visit might be the last, so he lived "every day as though it might be her last."
- Complications followed: brain bleeding, hydrocephalus requiring brain surgery, a flight to San Francisco (no pediatric neurosurgeons in New Mexico), more surgeries, meningitis, and a stop in breathing.
- His coping method: the future was too terrifying and the past a reminder of loss, but "most moments, on their own, were ok." He focused on holding his baby, appreciating each moment, and loving her unconditionally.
- Practical takeaway he draws: when everything is uncertain, all you have is now. You can love and expect nothing in return.
- Outcome: she is now a healthy seven-year-old (as of 2012).

## Finding the gift in tragedy

- His thesis: "In every tragedy, there is a gift, if we are able to see and accept it."
- From his brother: a personal understanding of death and a constant reminder to live as though life may end at any moment.
- From his daughter: what unconditional love means, loving without expecting anything in return.
- He acknowledges the gifts "were delivered at great cost."
- When life is "smashed to bits," people look for a North Star. His is unconditional love, which he wants to feel at a visceral level, not just understand intellectually.

## The fable: God of Unconditional Love vs. the Devil's false gods

- Paul wrote a simple story because stories engage the imagination and make abstract concepts "come alive inside of us." He says you don't need to believe in anything supernatural to get it.
- The setup: the Devil boasted he could gather more followers than God. God's method was simple: give everyone Unconditional Love and Forgiveness, "nothing more and nothing less."
- The Devil's plan: lie that he is the One True God, promise a great afterlife reward, and threaten hell for non-followers. He bet that Greed and Fear are stronger than Love and could trick even good people.
- His test for a false God (or false image of God):
  - Promises a reward (such as 72 virgins) for violence, such as flying a plane into a building.
  - Threatens hell for loving the wrong person.
  - Tells you to coerce others into worshiping.
  - Promises protection in exchange for doing his bidding.
- Principle: the God of Unconditional Love "brings union through love"; false gods "bring division through fear and greed."
- Genuine love "must be freely given and freely accepted, with nothing expected in return." It can't be delivered "at gun point, or with the threat of eternal damnation."
- His closing call: give unconditional love and forgiveness to others and, most urgently, to ourselves.
