## Why Dagobert says 100K followers didn't make his startup succeed

- Dagobert has 100K followers on X, yet the startup he built for 5 years never made more than $4K/month.
- He went viral often and got 8-10K visitors per month, but the product "just wasn't solving a big enough problem."
- Six years after starting his startup journey, he was making barely $1K per month despite the audience.
- His initial product died, and his Twitter course gradually lost momentum despite updates and good reviews, as X kept getting worse.

## How his audience helped early, then hit diminishing returns

- Building an audience helped grow revenue from $300 to $3,000 per month at the beginning, which he calls "a game changer" at the time.
- After reaching about 10K followers, he only got diminishing returns on the time he spent.
- Sales stopped growing, and he had to spend 2+ hours per day on Twitter just to maintain revenue.
- He says that at that point he should have stepped back, fixed the product, and built other distribution channels.

## The mistake: chasing followers instead of sales

- Instead of chasing sales, he started chasing followers and tried to become an "influencer."
- This led to spending around 4 hours a day creating content and engaging, which was never his dream. It made him miserable and led to a massive burnout.
- It caused him to neglect the hard work of fixing his product and building other distribution channels.
- He doesn't regret everything: he became a good writer, made founder friends who support him, and got his first taste of success from a Twitter course built for his audience.

## Why followers are a consequence, not the cause, of success

- He warns against assuming that a successful person with lots of followers owes their success to the followers (unless they only sell info products or boilerplates).
- His examples are @dannypostmaa and @tdinh_me, both with 100K+ followers. He says they succeed because:
  - their products solve real problems
  - they build long-term acquisition channels like affiliation or SEO
- Their followers are a consequence of sharing their journey toward success, not the reason for it.

## His formula for making it big

- To make it big you need to solve a real problem, build a great product, and figure out at least one distribution channel. "There is no way around it."
- Going viral and getting followers won't be "the magic bullet" for a startup.

## How much audience-building he recommends

- Hustling to a few thousand followers at the beginning is great, and he recommends every founder do it.
- Benefits he lists: making friends in the community, opening doors for partnerships and media coverage like podcasts, and getting initial traffic for everything you build.
- Once you are somewhat established, he says time is better spent "in the trenches" doing the hard work of growing a real business.
- He's now doing that again after years of getting carried away, enjoys starting from scratch, and says he has never felt better.
