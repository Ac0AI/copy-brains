## Starting Logology from a shared design and dev partnership

Dagobert Renouf started Logology with his wife in 2018 as a passion project. They wanted a business that used both their talents so they would be happy to come to work every day. As a team of one designer and one developer, they settled on an online tool in the design space.

- His wife was a freelance designer specializing in logo and brand identity design. She worked with startups and noticed early-stage companies often couldn't afford her services.
- They looked for a way to make it cheaper without losing quality. They landed on automating the brand research and design work, which cut the price by 10x.
- His rule of thumb: the best ideas come from problems you've noticed first-hand.
- They spent 2 years building the product and 2 more years learning how to market it.
- Logology is now an established online tool in the indie startup community. It helps dozens of founders every month figure out their brand identity and logo design.

## Twitter as the marketing channel that finally worked

Dagobert spent the first 2.5 years struggling to find customers. Paid ads and sponsorships produced no growth. In summer 2021 he started tweeting about his startup journey.

- He went from 150 to 40k+ followers in a bit over a year, simply by talking about the lessons he learns on the startup journey.
- He noticed more people visiting his website, talking about the product and recommending it to other founders.
- He made it part of his daily routine and now spends about 2 hours a day on it.
- Results he credits to it: 10x more traffic per day, invitations to 10+ podcasts, and new partners.
- He calls Twitter "an under-rated marketing strategy for new founders" and "the game changer."

## Share your journey publicly to open doors

Dagobert recommends every founder start talking about and sharing their journey with the public.

- It feels weird at first but builds up over time and opens new doors: finding customers, getting new partnerships, even investors.
- His framing: "You are the public face of your company and have a lot to win by connecting with the world directly."
- He sees this as the thing he does over and over and recommends everyone else do.

## Surviving a marketing failure by trying new ideas

His main failure was marketing. He couldn't figure it out and felt like a huge failure because of it.

- He almost quit after 2 years, believing he wasn't the right person for the job.
- He didn't want to quit, so he kept trying different marketing ideas.
- One day he found the right one: growing a Twitter audience.
- His lesson: even if you fail at something for a long time, keep trying and you could finally succeed.

## Slow, small growth beats rocketship pressure

Dagobert's contrarian belief is "You don't need to go fast to succeed. All you need is to never quit."

- Every year he wastes months not working on the right things, or stressing and procrastinating about the future, yet Logology grows every year.
- He argues it's time to loosen up on the need to grow like a unicorn or rocketship.
- Building a small business that moves slowly and allows mistakes gives him more happiness.
- He hopes to see more people quit traditional careers and build their own thing, dreaming of a future where 30% of the population works for themselves.
- He wishes he had started sooner. At 20 he wanted to build things but chickened out; he took the plunge at 28 and found it "was all I ever wanted and not as scary as I thought."

## Daily routine, timeboxing and time tracking

Dagobert splits his day into two parts.

- First part: communicating and promoting the product to other startup founders, mainly by building an audience on Twitter.
- Second part: either writing marketing content or building and improving the product.
- He has tracked his time precisely for 10 years and timeboxes everything. He started while freelancing, to know how much time to bill each client.
- Over the years he saw it helped his own productivity: timeboxing stops him getting sucked into a mindless task for hours.
- He uses llamalife.co, a simple productivity app focused on timeboxing, to plan and accomplish the right things each day.

## Generating ideas and working style

Dagobert has his best ideas while meditating and relaxing, which is hard to balance with the need to be productive.

- His strategy is to alternate between periods of thinking up new ideas and periods of intense work to make them happen.
- His favorite quote, from a French painter: "I like the rule that corrects emotion." To him creativity is the source of effort but needs boundaries to come to life.
- He recommends Peter Thiel's *Zero to One*, noting he only read the first third because the rest didn't apply to him, which shows how much value is in the first chapters.
- His best recent $100 spend was LED lights to improve webcam and video quality: "the result is so much better now that I wonder why I waited for so long."
- An idea he'd give away: a social book notes app where readers take notes, share a page of books and notes, and earn affiliate revenue from recommended books.
