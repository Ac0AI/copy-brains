## The job honeymoon ends after three months and the trade-off becomes clear

- Dagobert took a job at Abisol and says that after about three months the honeymoon is over. He likes the team, has autonomy, feels he brings value, and is glad to be paid.
- He still frames it plainly: it is a job, and he doesn't want to spend 40 hours a week of his life on one.
- He has made peace with it. He sees the job as stability and income, not as something that has to fulfil him.
- Early on he put all his eggs in the job. After about two months he took a week off and felt deeply low, "what's the point of living" low, which he traces to expecting the job to fulfil him.
- Once he lowered his expectations, he got more productive at work. His approach became to complete the mission, help others and do the job well, without hoping it would give him deeper meaning.

## Why building his own things feels different from job work

- Dagobert wants to build things that come from inside him. He describes it as less like being an artist and more like giving something of himself.
- Example: he rebuilt Abisol's homepage, which is now in an A/B test against the old version. He spent weeks and months understanding the product and the value to convert visitors.
- Yet it isn't the same as the homepage for his own course, which carries his style, his voice, and a design that is on brand with his personality.
- On his own work he feels people see him. On the job he is spreading the company's message.
- His earlier freelancing, before Logology, felt like constantly running after money. He rarely had a stretch where he could switch work off and on.

## Burnout came from imitating the lifestyle indie hackers

- He identifies a cause of his burnout: comparing himself to and imitating Pieter Levels, Daniel Vassallo, Jon Yongfook and Danny Postma. They make a lot of money and promote the lifestyle side of indie hacking.
- Daniel Vassallo often talks about working only a couple of hours a day. Dagobert says if he did that he would not be happy.
- His real driver is to contribute, help and support others, and that is how he connects with the world and feels happy. He did this for a long time on Twitter by supporting and connecting with people.
- He doesn't want to build a product only because it is trendy. He wants to create things that help people, specifically indies, freelancers and solopreneurs.
- This clarity helps him accept the job: it gives him financial stability, so he doesn't need a side project to make money.

## What he learned from Logology

- Logology sold almost 900 logos, and he is proud of that.
- He thinks it failed partly because the team focused mostly on how to help people without money get a good logo, instead of on how to build a good business.
- He says he has a hard time losing and still believes Logology can succeed.

## Side project ideas he is exploring

- **Logology pivot:** a design AI assistant with a ChatGPT-style chat interface, built on Logology's accumulated design knowledge. A user says they are building a product but are bad at design. The tool asks a few questions about the product and guides them toward a better look.
- **Marketing version:** the same assistant idea applied to marketing, which he says is more his thing, to help people market their product.
- **Course update:** his Twitter course has basically stopped selling. Now that Twitter is called X, it is obvious the course hasn't been updated in a year, which he thinks scares buyers. He wants to ship a free "version 2" update of roughly 1 to 1.5 hours on adapting to the new algorithm.
- None of this is committed yet. He says he has "nothing clear for now" and will work on it in evenings and weekends.

## Stability, savings and the risk of overcommitting

- Going full-time on Logology worked because he had savings and knew he had to make it work. He calls that both very helpful and incredibly stressful.
- He sees no clean solution: side projects are hard to stay motivated on week after week, while full-time indie work is stressful on money.
- He says he tends to get excited about something, pour everything into it, get things done for a while, then retreat. A steady paycheck gives him stability against that pattern.
- He acknowledges he needs the job's income and can't simply stop the job to chase side projects. He plans to see how he manages a 40-hour job alongside side projects.
