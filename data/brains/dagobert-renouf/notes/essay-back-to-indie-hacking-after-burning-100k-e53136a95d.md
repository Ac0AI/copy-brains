## Rebuilding after a $100k failure: financial freedom before the next startup

Dagobert's previous bootstrapped startup failed. He lost his life savings ($100k+), his marriage ended in divorce, and he burned out badly enough to need months of rest, meditation and therapy. He took an 8-month job, moved, and made real-life friends to get stability back. He describes his health as back at about 80% and says he is ready to build again, but he is also scared.

## How Dagobert sees the risk of startups

- He says running a startup is "10x harder" than the media success stories suggest.
- Success is never fully in the founder's control, even with validated ideas and a polished product.
- Building a startup is a bet: you win rarely and lose often, and the game continues until you sell or quit.
- Even with product-market fit and revenue growth, things can collapse at any time:
  - Social or SEO algorithm changes can cut traffic by 90% and kill growth.
  - Platforms you depend on (payments, APIs) can change pricing or ban you for no valid reason.
  - OpenAI can launch a feature that makes your product obsolete.
- His metaphor: a founder is "the captain of a ship in a never-ending storm" who must constantly avoid the rocks.
- His reason for entrepreneurship was "more freedom and joy out of life, not less", so he won't repeat the same ordeal.

## Safety net before taking risk

- He is leaving his job and will receive French unemployment benefits, which cover living expenses for 7 months.
- He also keeps a $20k emergency fund in case anything goes wrong.
- He will not put all his hopes for financial freedom into one "crazy startup idea" again. Instead he wants "the most efficient way to get revenue without selling my time."

## Monetizing assets he already built

From his last startup he kept three assets:

- A social following of 85K people.
- A podcast with about 750 listeners per episode.
- A Twitter course that still sells $500/month.

He admits that because he was obsessed with growing the startup rather than with financial freedom, he missed obvious revenue opportunities from these assets. Now he sees them clearly.

## The four small bets

1. Write a short ebook on his failures and lessons learned, and sell it to founders who follow him.
2. Accept paid sponsors on the podcast.
3. Update the Twitter course to bring it back to $2-3k per month, the level before the X rebrand.
4. Offer $1000/hour consulting calls on social media strategy.

He thinks these four bets can reach about $6k per month, mostly on autopilot. That is enough to cover taxes, living expenses and emergencies and give him time and financial freedom.

## Content creation as the stepping stone

- He admits being a content creator "isn't as sexy as a startup founder", but sees it as the right next step.
- The logic: get stable, sustainable income first, then be "in a better position to take the risk of building startups again."
- He plans to document the journey in a weekly newsletter with a deep lesson about entrepreneurship, his best meme of the week, and journey updates.
