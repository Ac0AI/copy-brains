## Why Dagobert is going all-in on content, not another startup

Dagobert quit his job and now has about 8 months of French unemployment benefits to build a sustainable solo income. His only revenue is a Twitter course that brings about $500 a month. He needs $4K a month to live.

- He rejects starting another startup: "I'm tired of it already." He spent 5 years on his own startup with his ex-wife, then 8 months at another startup, both stuck in the uphill grind of finding product-market fit and chasing customers.
- His stated focus for the next months is survival, not scale: get back to indie hacking full-time and avoid going back to a job.
- He picks content because it is the quickest path to money and is "only one thing": writing, thinking and connecting with people. It avoids the code, product and marketing split of a startup.

## The 4-part income plan to reach $4K a month

Dagobert lists the levers he is pulling at once:

- **Podcast:** one episode a week, with sponsors.
- **Book:** finish the book he started on all the mistakes he made with his first startup, then start selling it.
- **Twitter course:** update it into something like "how to dominate X", which he admits "sounds pretty porny" but thinks is a good title.
- **Newsletter:** a weekly deep thought about entrepreneurship, his best meme of the week, and a short journey update. Linked from his Twitter bio.
- He also diversifies onto other social platforms.

## Why he drops the paycheck-style startup math: $4K vs $10K

Dagobert explains why his burnout risk is lower now.

- With his ex-partner, the two of them had to run a formal company. In France that meant heavier taxes, so they needed about $10K a month in revenue to make a basic living.
- Alone, he can use the auto-entrepreneur (freelancer) status, which has lower taxes on revenue. He lives well on $4K a month, the same standard as $10K a month before.
- He was the one responsible for bringing in revenue and customers, restarting from zero every month, on top of social media and product work.
- A second cause of burnout was being pulled in two opposite directions: viral social media and community engagement on one side, deep focused product building on the other. He says context switching between them was impossible for him.
- Now he focuses on one kind of work, so he is still wary of burning out again but sees the main causes as removed.

## Why he now takes sponsors

- Earlier he refused sponsors because it "felt dirty". The podcast was meant as free promotion for his startup Logology, and he wanted to look like a "pure founder" so people would buy his products.
- After his burnout he calls that "the stupidest thing". He treats the podcast as a business and needs to make money.
- Rules for sponsors: only apps and products he loves and thinks his audience could love, with the same transparent, authentic tone: "I got to make money."
- Audience size he cites: about 750 listeners per episode, counting the full episode posted on Twitter, YouTube and the audio platforms.

## Content strategy: honest struggle instead of success stories

Dagobert's differentiator is sharing the dark side of indie work.

- He argues social media is full of success stories, and that seeing only the positives makes him "feel like [ __ ]". He wants a community of founders rather than people sharing wins for likes.
- Each weekly episode is either an interview with an indie maker about the difficult parts of entrepreneurship, or a solo deep reflection on the same theme. He doesn't enjoy guest-only podcasts where the host never shares his own story.
- Recurring topics: stress, anxiety, fear, and the impact on personal life (divorce, relationships, health, lost sleep, quitting workouts).
- Examples from founders he has spoken to: one has an app with thousands of paying users but risks burnout from hiring and spending time on accounting; another loses sleep when he goes 2-3 days without new sales.
- He has five big indie makers lined up as guests.
- Audience interaction: an "Indie Hotline" page on his site where listeners send audio, video or text (10 MB upload limit on the free form tool, with an optional anonymous checkbox). He plays and reacts to the messages in solo episodes. He plans to upgrade the form once he makes more than $500 a month.

## What he learned from the burnout recovery year

- After his February 2023 burnout he returned to a job in June 2023 as a product manager at a Paris startup, working fully remote. It was a deliberate recovery job and let him build unemployment entitlement (about one month of benefit per month worked, 6 months minimum to unlock).
- He rebuilt health, social circle and finances during this period, including joining a local co-working space with entrepreneurs and creators. He says real-life friends help his health more than online ones.
- His boss reacted to his resignation notice by saying he could leave in two weeks, rather than staying 3-6 months as he had offered. Dagobert says it hurt his ego and wrecked his plan for more months of salary, but he saw it as a "cold shower" he was ready for.
- He says he got addicted to the "reality" of being indie, being exposed with a tiger looming, and calls it the only real life.
