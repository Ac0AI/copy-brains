## Product Hunt's mission and why monetization came late

- Ryan says people have asked "How will you make money?" since Product Hunt's beginning.
- He argues there's "no right way to startup," but every team needs a focused goal early on, because everything on a startup's to-do list takes the place of something else.
- Product Hunt's mission: "surface awesome products and help makers find an audience."
- The team stayed focused on growing the community and on new ways for makers and consumers to share and discover products, rather than charging on day one.
- Some experiments failed completely; others had big community impact.
- By the end of 2017 Product Hunt hit new milestones in user growth and products discovered, and earned its first real dollar.
- Footnote: in 2014 the team made nominal revenue from job postings and t-shirt sales, only to pay its Heroku bill for a short period.
- After the first dollar, Product Hunt booked and collected over $250K in revenue, and half of that came in the final three weeks before the post.
- The 2018 aim was profitability, "a milestone few startups achieve."

## Ship: a SaaS toolkit as the first revenue line

- Ship launched September 14, 2017. It's a toolkit for makers, inspired by community feedback and by what the team learned supporting thousands of product launches each year.
- It uses a SaaS model with free and paid versions.
- It's integrated into the core Product Hunt experience but is essentially its own product with a small, dedicated team.
- Early stats:
  - 600,000+ email subscribers
  - 2,800+ upcoming product pages created
  - hundreds of paying startups
- The goal for a February update: make Ship a core part of product development and "the best tool to talk to users, build a community, and develop a real connection between the makers and users of a product."
- Product Hunt also used Ship internally to announce new projects and collect community feedback.

## Product Hunt Deals: monetizing behavior that already existed

- Makers were already posting exclusive discounts for the Product Hunt community in the comments of their launches.
- The community welcomed these, and the discounts often drew more interest and adoption.
- Ryan's team wanted to encourage that behavior and give the community access to discounted and exclusive deals.
- The paid offering lets makers host and promote deals directly on Product Hunt.
- The pattern: find something users already do for free, then build a paid, better-supported version of it.

## Promoted Products: building what makers kept asking for

- For years, makers asked how to get more exposure after they'd already launched.
- That demand inspired the third revenue line, Promoted Products.
- How it works:
  - It resurfaces popular products on the Product Hunt homepage and in the newsletter.
  - Each is marked with a "PROMOTED" badge.
  - It's only for makers already part of the community, and not for brand-new launches.
  - It's limited to popular past products, measured by upvotes from the community.
  - New launches remain free: "All makers are welcome to post their new product launches as they normally would without charge."
- Ryan describes beta testing it quietly for a few weeks "amidst overwhelming demand from makers."
- January and February were sold out, and reservations for March were open.
- He says the team monitored community feedback closely, and that it remained supportive.
- The beta partners included Intercom, InVision, HubSpot for Startups, Appcues, GitKraken, Front Chat, Shopify's Draggable and Lonely Planet's Trips, among about 30.

## Diversifying revenue while protecting the core product

- Ryan says that although Ship was growing nicely, the team understood "the importance of revenue diversification," so they added Deals and Promoted Products.
- Revenue wasn't the sole focus. The team kept working on:
  - increasing product discoveries
  - driving maker engagement
  - encouraging positive contributions from the community
- He calls all of these important to "a growing, sustainable, and healthy platform."
- Non-revenue projects in progress:
  - a rebuilt mobile app
  - Sip, an experimental mobile app delivering infrequent tech news updates
  - Maker Goals
  - Team Profiles
  - the Golden Kitty Awards
- Ryan's stance: it's important to be thoughtful about what you charge for and to ensure Product Hunt "continues to be valuable for makers of all kinds."
- Paid features were designed so the core experience, including free launches for new products, stayed intact.

## Why profitability matters and building in public

- Ryan calls Product Hunt "my baby," a community and platform he hopes will thrive for decades.
- His argument: "If done right, generating revenue and achieving profitability will ensure that we can continue building forever and ultimately lead to a better product as we reinvest capital into the platform."
- Product Hunt has a culture of building in public.
- He cites that culture as part of his motivation for sharing the revenue numbers and the 2018 profitability goal.
- He was also open that one reason for the post was to raise awareness of the paid initiatives.
