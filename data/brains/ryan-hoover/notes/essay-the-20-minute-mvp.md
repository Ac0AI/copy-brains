## Validating Product Hunt with a 20-minute, no-code MVP

Ryan's origin story for Product Hunt: he had the idea (a community for product people to share, discover and discuss new products), but building even a basic Ruby on Rails version would have taken him weeks of nights and weekends. He wasn't willing to spend that on something nobody might use.

- Instead he looked for the fastest way to test the hypothesis.
- He remembered Linkydink, a link-sharing tool by Makeshift. You create a group, invite people to share links with other contributors and subscribers, and each day's posts are emailed to the group.
- In about 20 minutes he created a group, invited a few startup friends to contribute, wrote a quick blog post, announced it on Quibb and tweeted.
- His summary: "Within 20 minutes, I had an MVP."

## What an MVP is for

Ryan argues an MVP is a learning tool, not a thing you must build.

- "The purpose of an MVP is to learn, to validate and invalidate assumptions."
- "Entrepreneurs often assume an MVP needs to be 'built.'" He says there are almost always faster ways to validate than building a product.
- The Linkydink MVP let him validate assumptions and watch real user behavior "without a single line of code."
- "An MVP is Not a Product." Once the idea had traction, the next step was a real product, but he still sought something fast to test his next set of assumptions.

## A three-step process before writing code

Ryan's checklist for any new product or startup idea:

1. **Identify your assumptions.** What must be true for the product to succeed? How do you identify your audience?
2. **Test your assumptions.** Take the riskiest assumption and brainstorm quick ways to validate or invalidate it. His examples: landing page tests, Wizard of Oz experiments, customer interviews, and other ways to get feedback from the target audience.
3. **Learn and iterate.** If the assumption is invalid, reevaluate the idea and the target audience. If it holds, test the next riskiest assumption.

He says this lean approach was used to birth Product Hunt and guide its ongoing development.

## Early results and what counted as traction

- Within two weeks, over 170 people had subscribed to product discoveries from 30 hand-picked contributors (startup founders, VCs and prominent bloggers).
- Early feedback was strongly positive. Ash Bhoopathy (Sequoia Capital) called it "the PM / growth dude's best replacement for 'Dribbble' for collecting inspiration." Andrew Weissman (Union Square Ventures) liked that "all kinds of VCs" were subscribed. Talton Figgins (Disqus) wrote the idea off at first, then got "hooked" after seeing the recommendations.
- He weighted unsolicited emails and in-person conversations expressing love and support as encouraging signals.
- This came despite the minimalism of the MVP and his "(intentional) lack of marketing."

## Honest caveat: the audience was earned

Ryan notes he didn't launch from a blank slate.

- Years of blogging, relationship building and projects like Startup Edition gave him an audience and network of supporters.
- "The term 'startup' is deceiving. Successful companies don't start up overnight; they are founded upon years of experience and help from others that must be earned."

## Choosing the "real" build: pick the fastest option

After the MVP gave him confidence, Ryan researched how to build a full product.

- Engineering friends suggested Sinatra or Ruby on Rails, but he had no experience with either and was still working with many hypotheses about what the product could be.
- He wanted something sooner to test his next set of assumptions, so he looked at Sacha Greif's Telescope, an open-source app for creating your own Hacker News or Reddit-style community.
- He asked designer/developer friend Nathan Bashaw, who built the first version over Thanksgiving break.

## Launch sequence for Product Hunt

- Telescope decision to launch took eight days.
- November 26 (2013): began seeding the community with a few select founding contributors.
- One week later: formal launch in the press (a Pando article), which brought another healthy spike of adoption and feedback.
- Ryan framed it as the first post in a series on the strategies, tactics and surprises of building Product Hunt.
