## Audience-first startup building

Ryan Hoover's core advice for founders who lack a team or traction: build an audience before building the company. He gave this feedback to someone asking how to find a technical co-founder for a startup idea: "Build an audience yourself first."

## How to build the audience

Ryan lists the channels he considers viable:

- Blogging
- Podcasting
- Starting a newsletter
- Hosting meetups

He cites Casey Neistat as an example: Neistat, co-founder of beme (acquired by CNN) and a prominent YouTube star, started his vlog to build an audience for his company. Ryan quotes him: "I originally started the vlog two years ago so I could share with an audience, my company beme. What it meant to start a company, to maybe build an audience that might be excited about what I was doing[…] And that worked and it was awesome."

## Three benefits of audience-first

Ryan names three things an audience helps with:

1. **Recruiting.** Pre-product and pre-traction, it is difficult to convince anyone, technical or not, to join. An audience gives a founder credibility and a pool of people to draw from.
2. **User acquisition.** Acquisition is hard both before and after launching a product. The sooner a founder de-risks it, the better.
3. **Research.** Audience-building feeds customer development and a better understanding of who the founder is building for.

## How it played out with Product Hunt

Ryan says he took this approach unintentionally when building Product Hunt. In the years before Product Hunt started, he wrote a lot:

- He blogged about product design, marketing and startups in general, inspired by observations and conversations while working in tech.
- He wrote 150 essays in 2013.
- He guest authored on Fast Company, Pando, The Next Web and other tech publications.
- He hosted small brunches with founders.
- He started an email newsletter called Startup Edition with Nathan Bashaw.

## The audience as a seed community

These projects built relationships and an audience that became the seed of the Product Hunt community. Ryan notes it was relatively small but established a platform, and argues that "without this, Product Hunt would have been DOA."

## How big the audience needs to be

Ryan's takeaway on scale: "Sometimes you just need 1,000 true fans to get started." The audience does not have to be large; it has to be engaged enough to supply early users, recruits and feedback.
