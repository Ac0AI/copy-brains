## Utility comes before community

Ryan argues "community" is a buzzword that audience-builders have misappropriated because it sounds more valuable than "users" or "followers". Founders who chase it before they have a clear, useful product fall into what he calls the community trap.

## What a real community is

- A true community is not a prescribed label. It is about belonging, shown through the actions of a group of people.
- He treats the difference between a label and actual behavior as the test of whether a community exists.

## Product Hunt's early community building

- Community building was foundational to Product Hunt from 2013.
- The team built a global audience of makers and founders excited about the future and technology.
- The audience produced hundreds of meetups, from 1,000+ person events in San Francisco to local gatherings in Amsterdam.
- Members wore Product Hunt swag, including forehead tattoos (temporary), and supported each other's launches daily.

## Community is secondary to utility

- Ryan states: "But community is secondary to utility." He says this was true for Product Hunt then and now, and for every other company.
- "No one wants to join a community. They want utility – something that solves a problem or provides clear value to their life."
- Product Hunt's utility for makers: spreading the word about a new product, acquiring first users, gathering feedback, and earning status by reaching the top of the daily leaderboard.
- Product Hunt's utility for consumers: following the latest tech trends, getting inspired, and connecting with likeminded tech optimists.

## Sequencing: utility first, then community

- "Utility brings people through the door, while community keeps them at the party, inviting their friends."
- Done well, community drives word of mouth, increases retention and creates a sustainable brand.
- This must come after utility.
- Ryan says many founders ask him how to build community before they have figured out utility, meaning the specific problem they are solving and for whom.
- Even though he advocates community building, he says focusing on it without clear utility is a trap.
