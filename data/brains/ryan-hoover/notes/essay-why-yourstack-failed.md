## Why YourStack existed and what it was meant to do

- In 2019 Ryan carved a small part of the Product Hunt team out to build YourStack. It had its own brand and domain, separate from producthunt.com, and was built from scratch using five years of Product Hunt lessons.
- Product Hunt is about what's new: a firehose of the latest tech launches. YourStack was about the products you use and love, regardless of age.
- The goal was to go beyond tech and help people discover and buy products recommended by people they know and trust. It used a follower model instead of a daily-launch leaderboard, to personalize the feed and widen the products surfaced.
- The thesis: "People will try and buy products from people they trust." Longer term, the aim was a new product graph, a data set of products people actually use and endorse.
- It launched in early 2020 and got 15K signups in the first week. Ryan calls that "not crazy, but a great start". It still failed.

## Product discovery is not a daily habit

- Ryan's first reason for failure: "Product discovery isn't a daily or even weekly activity." People rarely seek new products to buy.
- Most consumer social products need frequent engagement, or no habit forms. External triggers like push notifications can bring people back, but the internal trigger (a desire to discover or buy products) was infrequent.
- Designing for DAU then means fighting "the natural state of what people want".
- Contrast with Product Hunt, which is designed for the DAU. Its internal trigger is staying up to date on what's new in tech, following trends and getting inspired by what people built. It has a clear flywheel that drives adoption and re-engagement.
- A DAU experience can be forced by steering engagement toward tangential use cases, but that changes the product's underlying goal. His example: surface product recommendations while the core loop centers on financial management.

## Too broad: niche down first

- YourStack deliberately aimed beyond tech gadgets, SaaS and apps. Ryan now says: "sometimes niching down is the best way to start."
- A narrower focus makes it easier to build the ideal experience for that use case or interest.
- It also clarifies the target audience, which simplifies messaging and the GTM strategy for reaching those people.

## Mobile-first for consumer products

- Product Hunt started on web and desktop (2013), which fit its audience, team skills and era. YourStack carried that web focus forward, which Ryan calls a mistake.
- His rule: when building for a wide consumer audience, especially one that demands frequent engagement, mobile should probably be the priority.
- A mobile app started post-launch was confined by what had been designed for web. It also split the team's focus and made it harder to iterate on the core model across both platforms.

## Overbuilding before launch

- Ryan says they should have launched YourStack, or a version of it, much sooner to test the core interaction model and assumptions.
- Instead they "got too cute and fell into the midwit trap". Before launch they built:
  - a product Q&A experience
  - a scraper to ingest product recommendations from Twitter
  - user-created tips on how to use specific products
- The extras pushed back the launch date, split engagement and added more surface area to maintain.

## Founder burnout and the iterations needed for PMF

- By launch Ryan was nearing 7 years as CEO of Product Hunt and losing motivation. He says that in hindsight it is clear what he should have changed to avoid burnout, but once he "caught the bug" it was hard to sustain the stamina needed to iterate toward product-market fit.
- He argues founders underestimate how many iterations it takes to build something people want, especially in consumer social, where "the smallest changes can make a massive difference". "The first version is never it."
- After launch he knew what they had wouldn't work, and he knew the commitment required to reach PMF.

## What Ryan still believes and where the opportunity is

- He still holds the thesis: "The best way to drive product adoption is through an authentic recommendation from a trusted person." It looks even more obvious now, given influencer marketing and creator-led brands.
- YourStack aimed to let everyday consumers "influence" their micro networks, lengthening the tail of product discovery and elevating products people actually use. The key is that recommendations are authentic and relevant to the recommender's background.
- Social recommendations may be the best route to serendipitous discovery. When people know what they want, they go to Amazon, Google or YouTube. Social recommendations can pull them toward products they didn't know existed.
- As the cost of bringing products to market falls, more options will appear and the need for curation grows.
- The winner may not tackle product discovery head-on and may enter "through a backdoor". Example: TikTok (and Musical.ly before it) isn't designed for music discovery, yet has done more to break artists and shape culture than any music discovery platform in the last decade.
