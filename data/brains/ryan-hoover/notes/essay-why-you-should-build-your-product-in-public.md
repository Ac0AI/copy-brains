## Why Ryan thinks startups should build their product in public

Ryan argues that most startups, especially those building a community, have more to gain than lose by sharing unfinished work and involving users in design. He says there are legitimate reasons not to be this transparent, but "the majority of startups, especially those building a community" should do it. He wrote this in April 2014, after sharing incomplete mockups of an improved comment system for Product Hunt.

## What happened when Ryan shared Product Hunt mockups early

- He shared mockups of a new Product Hunt comment system even though the design "wasn't perfect" and "wasn't even complete."
- He used InVision for annotated feedback.
- Within 24 hours he received over 50 annotated pieces of feedback from the community.
- The feedback sorted into what users liked, what was unclear, what was concerning, what was broken, and new ideas to make Product Hunt better.
- One user's sorting suggestion (from Geoffrey) made it into the final design.
- The result made him wonder why more startups don't do this.

## Three benefits of building in public

1. **Increase buy-in.**
   - People want to be heard, and their investment in the product rises when their feedback is acknowledged.
   - When their ideas are incorporated, they feel a sense of ownership (he cites the ownership bias), which drives engagement and loyalty.
2. **Get feedback early.**
   - Designers close to a product miss usability issues, so fresh perspective helps.
   - Feedback is best gathered before design is committed to code, which gives a better product and less waste.
   - Caveat: people don't always know what they want, especially for products driven by emotional needs. The best product designers know what to listen to, what to ignore, and how to identify useful feedback.
3. **Excite people.**
   - People love sneak peeks. He compares it to fans paying a premium for a backstage pass, and Kickstarter backers paying top-tier prices for behind-the-scenes early access.
   - The excitement captures consumer attention and creates evangelists for the product.

## Examples of companies building in public

- **Monkey Inferno / Bebo:**
  - After reacquiring Bebo, the team skipped the traditional approach of building a product and revealing it months later.
  - They built Bebo Insiders, a mobile app giving their audience a preview of what they were building.
  - Users got a push notification soliciting feedback as the team iterated, and could chat in real time with CEO Shaan Puri.
- **Buffer:** Ryan calls it possibly "the most transparent startup around." It publishes employee salaries, employee equity options, monthly financials and culture principles.
- **Quibb:** Editor-in-Chief Sandi MacPherson, known for authenticity and personal touch, announced a friends and family round in January 2014 that let the community invest. His take: "Is there a better way to get users invested in the product than having them invest their own money?"
- **Assembly:**
  - A crowd-building platform where users submit product ideas.
  - Ideas with enough support are designed and built by the community.
  - Each project's feature ideas, roadmap, contributions and financials are public.
  - Builders receive equity as compensation.
  - Ryan calls it "crazy ambitious and potentially very disruptive."

## Tools that make building in public easier

- **Funding:** Kickstarter, Crowdtilt, Patreon and Alphaworks let businesses raise money directly from their audience.
- **Communication:** Intercom, Customer.io and Olark give direct access to users. Twitter is "arguably most impactful" for removing barriers to engage and broadcast personally.
- **Storytelling:** Medium makes it easier for founders to share their story.
- **Marketing:** Thunderclap assembles fans to spread news on Facebook and Twitter in coordinated "crowdmarketing" events.

## Ryan's own practice and view on limits

- He says not every startup can or should build in public.
- He has found tremendous value in Product Hunt community feedback, and says sharing Product Hunt's story in public has positively influenced community-building.
- He planned to keep sharing Product Hunt's story and experimenting with new ways of building in public.
