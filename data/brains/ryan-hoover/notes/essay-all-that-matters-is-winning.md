## Why sourcing and picking stop being an edge

Ryan argues that an investor's job is four things: source promising startups, pick the right ones, win an allocation, and help founders. Investors take pride in sourcing and picking, built on years of coffee meetings and pattern-matching. Ryan says that edge is "deteriorating and will increasingly lead to false confidence."

- Many investors are exceptional at both today.
- The risk is that confidence in these skills outlasts their actual advantage.
- His conclusion: "there's no edge in sourcing and picking in the near future. All that matters is winning."

## Everyone will see every deal

Deal flow already travels fast through investor catchups and deal-sharing networks. What is new is public signaling by founders, plus tooling that surfaces them.

- Signals that a founder is preparing to raise: launching on Product Hunt, getting loud on X (often with a hockey-stick chart), and following VCs on social.
- Tools that use public data to find newly minted founders and opportunities: Signa, Harmonic, Landscape, Tracxn, plus internal systems many VCs have built.
- Ryan expects these tools "will only get better," so sourcing advantage narrows as visibility becomes near universal.

## AI will pick better than humans

Ryan expects AI to out-select human investors, and not only on objective metrics.

- It can comb through data rooms more thoroughly than any person, instantly evaluating comps, the market, and the team's background.
- Examples he cites: En Verite is productizing this, and firms like ADIN have internal systems to help pick.
- It will also evaluate founders: automating reference checks and analyzing nuanced signals in a pitch captured via Zoom plus Granola.

## Winning the allocation is the game

With sourcing and picking commoditized, Ryan says earning access and winning allocations becomes the most important part of investing.

- He admits he is "being slightly provocative" but stands by the point.
- Exception: investors with truly proprietary, non-public data may still have alpha in sourcing and picking.
- Reputation, which is earned by helping founders, is what wins deals. Brand is shaped by perception, "for better or worse." He grants that brand-building and "thread boi maxxing" will still serve a purpose.

## Why this is good for founders

Ryan frames the shift as positive. Investors spend less time deal-hunting and arduously analyzing opportunities, and more time helping founders. Helping founders is the core input into their ability to win the next deal, so the incentive to be useful grows. "And that's great for founders."

- Implication for investors: invest in being genuinely helpful and in reputation, since that is what wins allocations.
- Implication for founders: signaling publicly gets you seen by everyone, so choose investors on how much they help, not on access to them.
