## Where Ryan looks for side project ideas

Ryan (writing in November 2016) is a big fan of side projects. He calls them "a great vehicle for expression," similar to how an artist communicates through their art. He says they can make you a better engineer, designer, marketer or salesperson, and they can turn into a business: Product Hunt started as a side project.

His view is that it's "difficult to manufacture a truly great idea in a whiteboarding session." Ideas are often serendipitous, triggered by stories and changes in the world, so he lists places to look for inspiration rather than a brainstorming formula.

## Mine your own life and your friends' lives for pain points

- **Reflect on your day-to-day.** Ryan says the best ideas often come from your own experiences. Write a detailed diary of everything you do in a typical day, then identify things that could be improved.
- The problem can be important (for example, what tasks to prioritize in your day) or small (for example, deciding whether to bike or take an Uber to work).
- **Ask your friends.** Run the same exercise with them and look for frustrations or pain points that could be eliminated.

## Build on emerging platforms

- Ryan worked in video games when Facebook's games platform was on the rise. He combed through Facebook's API and developer docs to find new, creative things to build on the platform.
- His principle: "As platforms emerge and evolve, new opportunities arise."
- As of 2016, he said he might explore Google Home, Alexa or Facebook Messenger.

## Browse what other makers are building

- **Product Hunt.** He admits he's biased, but argues "great ideas are often built off each other." Products created today will inspire the next Snapchat, directly and indirectly.
- **Kickstarter and Show HN** are two more places he suggests for browsing new products.
- **GitHub.** Even non-programmers can use it for inspiration. Browse trending repos for new ideas or projects to join.
- **Hackathons.** Hackathons are designed for side projects and give you a motivating place to share ideas with other enthusiastic makers.

## Turn a feature into a standalone product

- Look at favorite apps, sites or products and ask whether you can repurpose and improve one of their features as a standalone product.
- His example: Medium created an elegant way to share and present comments inline. Other people might want that functionality on their own sites. He notes this side project already exists.

## Read the internet

- Reading the news and blog posts increases the chances of discovering new ideas.
- Ryan treats this as an ongoing habit. Stories and changes in the world are what trigger serendipitous ideas.
