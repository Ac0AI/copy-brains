## Generative AI disrupts media incumbents through change and the innovator's dilemma

Ryan opens from a core startup principle: "Successful startups are born from change." Regulatory updates, cultural movements and new technologies create vulnerabilities for incumbents that fail to adapt, and generative AI is one of those big changes.

- Ryan notes that wildly impressive demos of AI-generated videos, songs and memes go viral on Twitter daily, and results keep getting better fast.
- He says it is hard to predict how things will play out, but incumbents that spent years building content libraries "will be challenged."
- He frames the dynamic as "a classic case of innovator's dilemma." Incumbents can use the technology too, but if it interferes with their business model or existing stakeholders, they may struggle to adapt.

## Why on-demand generative media can beat static content libraries

Ryan argues that for many use cases, on-demand generative AI media will deliver better results than the incumbent's solution.

- Unlike traditional static media, generative AI media adapts in real time to the user's requests, preferences and context.
- It can appeal to the smallest niche.
- It works without licensing restrictions or costly human labor, and can do so infinitely.
- Ryan's implication for defensibility: a content library's moat is at risk when synthetic content is just as good (or better) and available at a fraction of the cost.

## Examples of reimagined media products

Ryan sketches how new products could replace incumbents by generating instead of curating:

- **Music (Spotify competitor):** generates entirely new music matching the user's query, not limited to existing catalogs, producing personalized and novel sounds. Suno is an early example.
- **Image inspiration (next Pinterest):** generates house renovation inspiration on the fly, adapted to the user's home, budget and style. Catbird is exploring this.
- **Fiction (future Wattpad):** writes fan fiction instantly, possibly including the reader's friends or relatable experiences for a more engaging story. Storybooks does this for children's books.

## How incumbents get trapped by their stakeholders

Ryan explains why incumbents struggle even though they can adopt the same tech: the new model undermines the people who currently supply and pay them.

- **Spotify:** artists and major labels won't be pleased if a large portion of the audience shifts toward royalty-free music.
- **Ecommerce on Pinterest:** merchants will be upset if discovery of their products collapses as attention moves to synthetically generated images.
- **Wattpad:** writers won't be happy if sales of their werewolf romance stories drop as readers seek AI-generated novels.

Ryan's footnoted ways incumbents could adapt:
- Spotify could let artists and rights holders opt in to use of their likeness and sound for generating new music, earning a cut of revenues.
- Pinterest could present merchants' products inside synthetically generated images, driving more attention and higher conversion rates.
- Wattpad could help storytellers and IP holders create more content for their fans.

He concedes Spotify, Pinterest and Wattpad "may thrive in this new world if they can adapt," but until then there is an opening for startups "to reimagine (literally) media consumption."

## Which content libraries are more or less defensible

Ryan distinguishes libraries by timeless, cultural relevance rather than treating all as equally threatened.

- Spotify's vast music network "will always be relevant and desired by listeners." His personal example: he will never stop listening to Porter Robinson's *Worlds* album.
- Pinterest's image database has less timeless, cultural relevance, so it is more exposed to synthetic alternatives.
- The rule of thumb: libraries tied to emotional attachment and culture hold up better than libraries that are functional inspiration or reference.

## Moats built on real humans

In a future where media is abundant and cheap (and "humans are synthetic"), Ryan says he is "increasingly bullish on companies that establish a moat with real humans."

- People will keep desiring human connection, both professionally (LinkedIn) and socially (Instagram).
- AI companions like Replika and Character.ai will succeed at satisfying the primal desire for entertainment and play, but he sees them as "not a complete replacement" for the personal connection and opportunities gained from other people.
- Status matters: "people are status-seeking monkeys" and need other humans to see them flex to feel acknowledged (referencing Eugene Wei's "Status as a Service").
- Takeaway for founders: as content becomes a commodity, defensibility shifts toward networks, identity and status among real people.
