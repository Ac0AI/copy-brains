## Rejection can seed a better relationship than acceptance

- In mid-2013, after 3 years at PlayHaven, Ryan wanted a new startup challenge. Incubating new consumer products was his "dream job."
- Shaan Puri, CEO of Monkey Inferno, invited him to lunch, then back a second time for product feedback, then offered a "Trial by Fire" test for an Entrepreneur-in-Residence role.
- Ryan's work: over a weekend he researched, built wireframes and a deck as if he were product leader of their Q&A platform, Boya. He pitched it with no access to data.
- Shaan rejected him, but with a long, transparent, constructive email explaining the reasoning. He ended by hoping to "stay in touch."
- Ryan treats this as a model for how a rejection should be handled. Most employers don't give that much detail.

## Never burn a bridge: the rejection became a partnership

- Ryan stayed in touch with Shaan. When he started Product Hunt, he asked Shaan (Product Hunt user #15) for feedback, and Shaan stayed supportive.
- Shaan emailed days before Product Hunt LIVE launched, offering help. Monkey Inferno was building Blab, a platform for live video conversations.
- After the public launch, they met to use Blab's tech to make Product Hunt LIVE more intimate. The first Blab-powered LIVE Chat was with Matt Mazzeo, on the day of the post (October 19, 2015).
- Ryan's lesson: "it's important to never burn a bridge in Silicon Valley (or anywhere, really)." The partnership happened "because we stayed in touch and genuinely liked each other."
- Takeaway: relationships built through a failed hiring process can pay off years later as distribution or product partnerships.

## Product Hunt LIVE: text Q&A first, then video

- Product Hunt LIVE launched in August 2015 as "a place to chat with the world's most interesting makers," starting with text-based discussions.
- Within about two months it had hosted Q&As with over 150 guests, including Amanda Palmer, Aileen Lee, Ashton Kutcher, Ben Horowitz and Tony Robbins. Ryan did one too.
- The plan from the start was to eventually expand into livestreaming video to make it "even more intimate and fun."
- Ryan frames the mission as connecting the community directly with their favorite makers in an authentic conversation. Video is "the closest thing to meeting someone face-to-face" and lets people "sit in the room" with founders, investors, artists, authors, game creators and entertainers.
- Approach: validate the format in a simple medium (text), then partner for the richer one (video) rather than building it from scratch.

## Reframing "no": rejection as a sign of ambition and a path to better outcomes

- Ryan quotes Chris Dixon: "If you aren't getting rejected on a daily basis, your goals aren't ambitious enough."
- He argues "sometimes 'no' is good." He was disappointed by the rejection, but "Product Hunt wouldn't exist if I got the job."
- Practical advice for people facing rejection: respond graciously, keep the relationship warm, and stay open to where it leads.
