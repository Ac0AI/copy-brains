## Why second-time founders feel more anxiety than first-timers

Ryan argues that starting a company the second time can be harder than the first, even though second-time founders are better equipped. He wrote this on October 17, 2023, and says he has felt all of these anxieties himself.

- Entrepreneurs self-report dramatically higher rates of depression, ADHD, substance abuse, and bipolar disorder (he cites a study). He notes this doesn't prove causation, and it's unknown whether the mental health challenges or the company came first.
- The "really bad stuff" founders face is rarely shared in public.
- Founders can develop debilitating anxieties. "The body doesn't want to go through it again," so many become hesitant to swing again.
- He sees a growing, silent wave of company shutdowns amid macro difficulties, and invites second-time (or third, fourth) founders working on something new to reach out.

## The founder's hidden struggle: the smile versus the internal monologue

Ryan shares challenges he faced building Product Hunt, most of which he couldn't share at the time:

- A large company threatened to sue before the company had raised a dime.
- An internet mob attacked the company and his character.
- The company almost ran out of money after a "sea of investors told us 'no'."

He calls the hardest part maintaining a smile and instilling confidence with his team, investors, and the community while his internal monologue was filled with doubt and dread. "The dissonance between the internal emotions and external mask is agonizing." He adds that other founders have had it much harder than he did.

As an investor, he says he has admiration for founders who bring something new into the world and put in the work, even when it doesn't work out, because he knows the sacrifice required. When a founder the firm backed texted him that they were shutting down the company, he took the call and treated the founder with that respect.

## Anxiety 1: "UGH! That really hurt" (scars and lost naiveté)

- First-time founders are gifted with naiveté. They may have heard how hard it is, but they haven't felt it. Like a child unafraid of daring stunts on the playground, they lack painful memories that introduce hesitation. That lack of fear of failure empowers them to try bold ideas.
- Second-time founders have scars. They know how hard it is, so they make excuses not to pursue ideas or bow out of entrepreneurship entirely.
- Ryan's advice: ask yourself how you would feel 10 years from now knowing you didn't try to bring your idea to life.

## Anxiety 2: "Everyone's watching" (reputation pressure)

- First-time founders have less to lose reputationally. Expectations are lower, so fewer people in their community are watching and judging (or so it's perceived).
- Second-time founders have a reputation to uphold. The more successful they were before, the more pressure they put on themselves to achieve even more.
- Ryan's advice: most people don't care about you, and the people who truly do care about you for you, not for your startup success or lack thereof.

## Anxiety 3: "My next thing has to be huge" (paralysis by ambition)

- First-time founders are OK solving "small" problems. Many simply want to build something people want without overthinking the grand vision.
- Second-time founders can over-intellectualize into paralysis. They focus on systems design and potential TAM instead of solving a specific problem for a specific set of people, "i.e. the basics."
- Ryan's advice: there's no right way to start up, and it's fine to dream big, but not at the cost of starting. "It's OK to start small because even the smallest nudges can lead to an avalanche."

## Why second-time founders still have the edge

- Data point he cites (Ali Maseb, author of Superfounders): nearly 60% of CEOs and CxOs who founded a startup valued at $1B+ have past founder experience.
- Second-time founders have more experience building, recruiting, raising, selling, and scaling. They've met challenges that were invisible and unknowable to people without the experience.
- Their network is larger, opening doors to potential customers and future hires.
- They have more credibility in the market, even if their previous venture failed. "There's a reason VCs pay a premium to back second-time founders."
- His message to them: recognize your edge "with humility."

## Ryan's own return to building

- He admits he hesitated to get back to building, often over-thinking or using prior commitments as an excuse not to act.
- A few months before writing, he started a couple of projects that solve his own problems, and says he's "having so much fun." The projects relate to Weekend Fund and investing.
- Unlike with Product Hunt, he is not building in public this time, despite acknowledging the benefits of doing so.
