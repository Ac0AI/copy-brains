## Why Ryan invests in venture funds as an LP

Ryan has quietly backed 30 funds over 8 years. He ranks his reasons in this order:

1. **Learn.** He loves reading quarterly LP updates, where investors highlight new investments, explain their view of the market and share the results of their experiments.
2. **Get deal flow.** He is often introduced to new companies that spark his interest. Fund managers are often happy to make an introduction when there is allocation available, and sometimes between rounds.
3. **Support fellow investors.** "Fundraising, especially today, is hard!" Besides his "tiny check," he often introduces other LPs to the fund.
4. **Make money.** This is the lowest priority because his checks are so small, not because he doubts the funds will do well. He says this will change once he has more liquidity to tie up for 10+ years.

## Which funds Ryan backs

Most of his fund investments are in managers he has known for many years. He names these LP positions: Earl Grey, Factorial, HF0, Founders Inc, Hustle Fund, Julian Capital, Prototype Capital, Spice Capital and Thales, plus several others.

He focuses on three criteria:
- Small funds, ideally sub-$30M, investing in pre-seed and seed.
- Highly connected generalists with unique access, or deep specialists in one vertical.
- Investors who take the job seriously.

## Why Ryan prefers small funds (sub-$30M, pre-seed and seed)

- Small funds have historically delivered better returns on average. He points to Carta's 2024 VC fund size performance data and SVB's H2 2024 State of the Markets report.
- Fund-size math: a $300K check into an early-stage opportunity can be a multiple-fund returner for a small fund.
- Larger funds ($250M+) must invest far more capital to capture meaningful ownership and outcomes, which is "almost always far more difficult."
- Those large funds often need to lead rounds. They must beat a growing number of competing investors, including multi-stage behemoths with massive teams and scout networks.

## Generalists vs specialists in his LP portfolio

- **Generalist funds** help with deal flow because they align with what he typically invests in at Weekend Fund.
- **Specialist funds** help with diversification. They give him more exposure to industries he doesn't invest in, such as biotech, deep tech and climate tech.
- He wants generalists with unique access, or specialists with real depth in one vertical.

## Backing investors who take the craft seriously

- He sees "managing other people's money" as a responsibility.
- He wants to back investors who are "thoughtful, disciplined, and committed to the craft."
- Because most of his LP checks go to managers he has known for years, he can judge this firsthand.

## How he helps funds he backs

- Beyond his own small check, he introduces other LPs to the fund, because fundraising is hard.
- He invites others who want to invest alongside him to reach out via a form.
- For people trying to break into venture, he points to an article he and his team wrote on the topic at Signature Block.
