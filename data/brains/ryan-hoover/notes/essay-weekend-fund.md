## Weekend fund: a $3M angel fund for nights-and-weekends founders

Ryan Hoover announced Weekend Fund on September 21, 2017. It is a $3M fund to support founders at the earliest stages. He frames it as backing the people who are building the next big thing in their spare time, and he keeps Product Hunt as his number one focus. He describes the fund as his own nights and weekend hustle, like the startups he plans to invest in.

- The fund runs on AngelList's new Angel Fund platform.
- AngelList handles back office, legal, and many of the logistical headaches of setting up a venture fund, at a fraction of the cost.
- Ryan calls Angel Funds "a potentially great option for operator angels", meaning people like him who work full-time at their startup. He admits he is biased.
- He offers to connect anyone interested in setting up an Angel Fund with the right people.
- His LPs range from seasoned entrepreneurs to experienced investors to leaders in entertainment.
- He invites people to follow the portfolio through his infrequent email newsletter.

## Product Hunt as the origin story for side projects

Ryan uses Product Hunt as his own example of a side project that became a company.

- It started nearly four years before 2017 as a side project while he was transitioning from another startup.
- The goal was simple: share and discover interesting new apps and products with friends.
- He worked nights and weekends, and friends joined to help build the community. He says that without them Product Hunt wouldn't exist.
- It began as a simple email list among friends. Momentum then built quickly and unexpectedly, and the opportunity became clear.
- By 2017, more than 30,000 entrepreneurs had launched a product on Product Hunt, leading to more than 100M product discoveries.
- It grew into a platform to discover products and help makers reach an audience.

## Thesis: the next big thing is built on nights and weekends

Ryan argues that Product Hunt's upbringing isn't unique, and that many of the most transformational companies began as side projects driven by curiosity and an eagerness to build something new. He says the next Facebook is being built on nights and weekends right now, and that these curious, creative founders are who he wants to support.

He cites Chris Dixon: "What the smartest people do on the weekend is what everyone else will do during the week in ten years."

## Examples of companies that started as side projects

Ryan lists these as evidence for the thesis:

- **Facebook:** "Thefacebook" launched in 2004 on Harvard's campus.
- **BuzzFeed:** Jonah Peretti started it while working at Huffington Post, curious about why and how people share content online.
- **eBay:** Originally AuctionWeb, it was created over Labor Day weekend in 1995 alongside other sites by its French-born founder.
- **Imgur:** Alan started it as a personal project in college. Asked if he expected it to get so popular, he said "Never... I just stuck with it."
- **Tattly:** Tina Roth Eisenberg built the temporary tattoo company after working on various other projects.
- **Craigslist:** Craig started it in the mid-90s as an email newsletter to keep friends up to date on events around San Francisco, and incorporated two years later.
- **Gmail:** Paul Buchheit was given autonomy to build something new after finishing the first version of Google Groups. He was unhappy with existing email services and created Gmail.

He notes that side projects can also come from inside existing companies, as Gmail did. He also points readers to a separate post with tips for finding inspiration for a next side project.
