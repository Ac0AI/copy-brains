## Strategy: sell a finished demo video, not a service

- Priyam argues the fastest way to make about $10k with Claude Code's Remotion skill is to generate a finished product demo video and pitch it directly to the company.
- As a founder, he says he has "paid thousands of dollars to people who used to create videos for my products."
- The skill lets you make animated videos from prompts alone, with no coding.
- The core move: you are "not asking if they want a video. You're showing them one."
- He skips the funnel, call booking and positioning deck.

## Picking the niche

- Best targets are boring SaaS and iOS apps.
- Examples: dev tools, internal dashboards, and B2B products with ugly websites and no explainer video.
- These companies need a video and rarely have one.

## Making the demo

- Generate a 30 to 40 second demo video using Remotion + Claude Code.
- No watermark and no hard sell.
- Show just their product, animated cleanly, so it looks "ten times more legit than before."
- Attach whatever assets the company has (videos, logos) and prompt for what you want.
- Setup needs only a laptop and any paid Claude Code plan ($20/mo). Install the skill from Terminal, then run Claude Code and prompt it.
- He points to a Remotion example prompt gist from Jonny Burger: https://gist.github.com/JonnyBurger/5b801182176f1b76447901fbeb5a84ac

## The outreach and offer

- Send a cold email or Twitter DM with the preview attached.
- His template: "Hey, made a short demo video for your product. Attaching a preview. If you want the full version or custom changes, happy to deliver it for $1k."
- Price: $1k for the full version or custom changes.

## Expected income math

- "One decent client pays more than most people's monthly salary."
- Two clients validates the idea.
- Five clients "bought yourself time."
- You don't need to be the best, "You just need to be early."

## Timing and saturation

- Priyam doesn't expect this to be a long-term business: "This stuff always gets saturated."
- His reasoning: the tool gets better, prices race to zero, agencies rebrand and freelancers move on.
- He frames it as "a speedrun" to capture value while it's new.
- He says that if he weren't already building and shipping his own products, he'd be doing this "without thinking twice."
