## Opportunity: cash in on a new trend with productized demo videos

Priyam's lens: "everything is based on a trend." He says he has made more than a million dollars online and, as a SaaS/app founder, has spent thousands of dollars paying for product demos.

- The trend (Jan 2026): Claude Code plus a Remotion skill. Remotion makes videos programmatically from code, and Vidi has used it for over a year.
- Product demos used to need professional designers who knew After Effects and Premiere Pro. Now you can prompt Claude, feed it assets such as a SaaS logo and product details, and get an animated demo video.
- His view: if he were starting out and wanted his first $5k to $10k, this is the easy route. He has tried it himself and says it works.
- Setup: install Claude Code, then install the Remotion skill (he links a guide in the description, written as a Twitter article).

## The $1,000-per-video outreach play

Priyam's step-by-step:

1. Find SaaS or app founders on Twitter, ideally "a boring kind of product."
2. Make a simple product video with Claude Code and Remotion.
3. Cut it down to a teaser (roughly the first 30 seconds, or a minute-long video).
4. Email or DM them: "Hey, I've made this for you. Do you want the full version?"
5. If yes, send a Stripe link: "pay me $1,000, and it's yours."
6. Repeat for about 50 SaaS companies or apps.

- Why it should sell: founders, corporate founders and product managers "just care about the results and nothing else."
- He says that if the video is good enough he would have paid for it himself.
- Channels: cold email and Twitter outreach.

## Quality: iterate before you send

- Priyam stresses that you have to test and retry multiple times to get something "perfect."
- He compares it to prompting Gemini and ChatGPT for good images: people who have hacked around to get strong outputs there can do it here.
- He calls the video itself low effort and gives no full tutorial. His aim was to share the idea as fast as possible, since the opportunity is time-sensitive.

## Mindset: short-lived trends are still worth taking

- He admits this probably isn't long-term, but says "so is nothing on the internet."
- About his first AI SaaS, built roughly three years earlier: someone told him it wouldn't work, and he knew it wouldn't last long-term either, but he treats the whole thing as "gamified": make the money, then start something else.
- His reasoning: "If there's a lot of money you can make out of something, why not do it?"
- He rejects judging everything by stable monthly cash flow: $20k in 3 months (about $60k in total, by his math) beats worrying that it isn't steady monthly income.
- "That's the kind of mindset you must carry when you're making money on the internet."
