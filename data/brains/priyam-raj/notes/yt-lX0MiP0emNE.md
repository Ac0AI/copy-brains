## Targeting a small niche company beats mass-applying to jobs

Priyam advises students in their final or pre-final year of college to stop following the traditional route of studying DSA and applying to companies. His reasoning comes from experience: one of his first jobs ever paid him more than 2 lakhs a month, and he says he is confident he could replicate how he got it to this day. He also says he had done the same thing with a Canadian company earlier, and that those earlier attempts didn't add up to helping him in that specific case.

- The alternative: target a very small, specific company instead of applying broadly.
- He cannot promise it will work, but says it is what he would do and has already done.
- He frames it as an answer to the question students keep asking him: how to get a job in this market.

## How to pick which companies to approach

Priyam says to look around yourself and go smaller than famous giants.

- He uses Discord as an example of "too big" and suggests starting with something like Steam if you play games, then looking at small companies in that orbit.
- The target profile is companies that make roughly "10k, 20k a month" in dollars.
- He also calls Notion too big for the point he is making, and uses it only as an easy illustration.
- The logic is to look at products you actually use or know, so you can speak about them with real familiarity.

## How to reach out: organic, not templated

Priyam says to offer help directly to the person who can decide, and to do it in a human way.

- Reach out to the founders, the product manager, or whoever is the best fit in that specific case.
- Be "very organic", not "in a very ChatGPT or AI kind of way" with generic emails offering to be "your tech help".
- Be enthusiastic about the product.
- He stresses this is aimed at real, individual outreach, not mass applications.

## Do the product homework and pitch a specific improvement

Priyam says the pitch should come from having actually studied the product, and should sound like you are going to help the person.

- First look into the whole product properly and see everything it has to offer.
- Find something concrete you can improve, change or bug-fix.
- Name that specific feature you could add or thing you could fix, and say how you would do it.
- Show enthusiasm for the product itself.
- Using Notion as a hypothetical (if it were a smaller company), he would study everything it offers, then pitch a specific improvement or fix.
- He frames this mainly from a software engineer's perspective, but says it can work for other roles too.

## What a good pitch looks like (the founder's view)

Priyam is the founder of a software company, ViddAI, and says he receives many applications himself. His complaint: the usual pitch is "help me out, I need a job", which does not help him.

His example of a strong pitch, as if sent to him on Instagram:
- Point out that ViddAI has a specific issue that could be improved.
- Explain how you would improve it.
- Make a small, low-risk ask, for example "just pay me $700 a month", framed as being an intern who wants to help and is enthusiastic about the product.

He says that as a founder he would "any day" want to hire someone with more enthusiasm for his product rather than someone who just needs help. He first says such a pitch would be seven times better than a generic one, then corrects himself: "Seven is too low. I would say it's like 50 times better."
