## Casey's approach to testing multiplatform podcast campaigns (publisher-side view)

Casey is the founder of listener.com, a multi-platform campaign reporting platform for podcast publishers. He has also hosted his own podcast for eight and a half years, mainly interviewing founders, and has had scientists on the show.

- Listener works with top publishers such as Johnboy Media. He says some of them start brands with audio only, then show the brand what it missed. His example: "you missed out on 20 million impressions of the short form," so the next campaign includes it.
- He frames the audio-to-multiplatform progression (audio, YouTube, short form, newsletter) as a testing phase brands often go through before a full multiplatform campaign.
- He notes the brand often controls campaign length, not the creator or publisher, which is why the choice between a one-year and a three-month sponsorship is hard for sellers.
- His question to the panel: how do you know when to keep testing versus "let it ride" over a longer partnership? He observes that some creators skip testing because they know results take three to six months or longer.

## Why brand-team interviews belong in podcast sponsorships

Casey endorses having a brand's own experts appear on shows rather than relying only on host-read ads.

- He argues there is "such a value in that not only long format of, you know, brands spending money on shows." He likes when brands say "you should have our CEO on the show or a head scientist."
- He calls this a trend that "people don't talk about enough or hear about enough."
- Example from his own show: he worked with Merrick Health, a specialized blood work company with 150 biomarkers. He interviewed about three of their scientists because he was not the best person to explain the product in depth.
- His takeaway: complex products need customer education, and the brand's team supplying guests is how that education happens on the show.

## Reporting and attribution as a publisher's renewal tool

Casey's company sits on the reporting side, so he frames the whole conversation around what publishers need in order to win renewals.

- Publishers hear what brands care about, then have to represent the campaign back to them well "for the goal of getting renewals" and "hitting those KPIs or the earned media."
- He asked the brand-side panelists what specific signals and internal tools they hold in their back pocket for judging campaign success, beyond just knowing what works.
- Signals the panelists named, which he used to frame publisher reporting needs: last touch, attribution, earned media value from social posts, post-purchase survey data, and blended code, survey and pixel attribution.
- His framing: multiplatform reporting (audio, YouTube, short form, newsletter) is what lets a publisher show the total footprint of a campaign instead of just downloads.

## Framing for the session

- He sets the topic as going "beyond the download or beyond the midroll" in podcast advertising, and treats it as "a full scale partnership with creators."
- He positions the panel from a technology lens: working with publishers while brands speak to what multiplatform campaigns look like from their side.
- He asks both panelists for dos and don'ts and for case studies of best and worst campaigns, looking toward 2026 into 2027, aimed at both brands and publishers bringing ideas to the table.
