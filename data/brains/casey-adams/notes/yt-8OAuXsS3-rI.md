## Why Casey started the podcast and how it compounds

- Casey started "The Casey Adams Show" at 17 (late 2017), after reading Gary Vaynerchuk's "Crushing It", whose last chapter pushed podcasting as a conversation-based medium.
- He had no podcast experience and began by walking around his room on Apple headphones.
- He gives three core reasons, all still true: meet quality entrepreneurs and build a network, learn from them, and create business opportunities.
- He believes "business is a people game". The people on the team are the critical element, even in the AI era (he cites Sam Altman at OpenAI).
- He sees the show as the foundation of his brand, career, fundraising, relationships and later business ventures.
- He says you can never predict what an intentional conversation with someone you want to learn from will do for your life.

## Cold outreach and the 60-in-90 interview sprint

- His early approach to getting guests was mainly cold outreach on Instagram, plus having friends on the show first.
- He set a goal of 60 interviews in 90 days, because he had a 5-10 minute meeting lined up with Gary Vaynerchuk and wanted to arrive with reps and momentum.
- That momentum built "such a fire" for high-quality conversations and relationship building.
- He admits he had to "get my reps in": his early interviews look very different from his current ones.
- On pitching brands or guests, he says never expect anyone to reach out to you, even while gaining momentum.
- In the early days with no audience, guests are "literally zero" unless you do the work: pitch, follow up, then schedule once you get a response.

## Raising the media kits round from podcast guests

- Media Kits (launched 2021) was a tech product that let creators, podcasters, musicians and athletes build a digital resume with real-time, verified analytics, shareable by link.
- It solved two problems: creators had no way to aggregate social stats in one place, and brands had no way to view trustworthy, verified creator data (numbers on static kits are easy to fake).
- Origin: in 2018 a YouTuber friend (J.R. Garage) kept asking Casey and his partner Kieran for Canva media kits for Pennzoil and Ferrari. Each took about an hour, and the numbers went stale within weeks.
- The idea was shelved in 2018 because the timing was wrong. They revisited it in 2020 in Scottsdale, Arizona, when TikTok and influencer marketing were booming.
- They validated by surveying qualified creators and people in the space, then bootstrapped with a design agency in mid-2020.
- In early 2021 they committed and raised just over $1 million (he also says "1.3, 1.5").
- The product launched in August 2021, with a Wiz Khalifa performance at a launch party attended by 700 creators.
- Roughly 90-95% of the investors (about 37 people) were previous podcast guests.
- He credits years of trust and people watching his journey. He could sit down with them and present the opportunity directly.
- Neither founder was technical and they had no founding CTO. He says they "had to think so differently about business".
- About a year later, in 2022, Media Kits was acquired by Viral Nation, an influencer marketing tech company in Toronto that he says raised a $200 million private equity round. He worked there about six months.
- Fit with the buyer mattered to him: Viral Nation represents 1,500+ creators, and its vision matched how the founders saw the product's future.

## How to build a personal brand

- He defines personal branding on social media as "representing the most authentic version of yourself in a digital format".
- Step 1: pick your medium. Short-form clips, or long-form like a podcast that you then cut into short clips for distribution.
- Step 2: clarify your foundational story. He suggests recording yourself telling it to get clear on "the story that I tell myself" versus the story you want to put into the world.
- Step 3: choose your niche or what you want to be known for, even if you're, say, a realtor.
- Step 4: model the top 5-10 people in your space in your own way.
- Don't overthink it. Posting one Instagram story with 200 followers counts as the first step: documentation and output over input.
- Consistency is the hard part. He frames it as being a producer, not a scroller, and ties it back to discipline.
- When he started at 16 he had no businesses or successes, so he built the brand on book reviews, his personality and his story.

## Pitching brands when you don't have a big following

- He spoke to a UCLA class of creators and marketers about this.
- Brands "appreciate and respect" creators who proactively reach out, get on a call, build a relationship with someone on the team, and express genuine enthusiasm for the product.
- He says to put about 99% of your effort into creating those opportunities, rather than waiting for a payout or expecting a brand to come to you.
- Pitching brands works like podcast guest outreach: do the work to pitch, follow up and schedule.
- Brands want to work with truly aligned creators. Big creators get paid through managers and marketing budgets, so small creators win on relationship and fit.

## Fear of failure, the voice in your head, and "keep going"

- Casey cites a quote he may be butchering: "the fear of failure kills more dreams than failure ever will".
- He says you don't have to silence the voice of doubt (imposter syndrome, "why would anyone listen to me"), but you can't listen to it or believe it.
- He counters it with small wins and small daily actions. Stacking daily momentum is how you prove to yourself you're capable.
- His mantra is "keep going, never quit". He writes it at the end of nearly every journal entry and put it on a gold-painted neck brace trophy.
- He tells the story of the Honolulu Marathon CEO, who regretted stopping during his first marathon: "you'll never have the opportunity to run your first marathon ever again".
- He says there are moments for reflection and pivoting, but the default is to not stop.
- He adds that regret lasts a lifetime while failure is temporary.

## Discipline, 75 Hard and the neck brace origin

- Casey's drive traces to a football injury at 16 that put him in a neck brace for six months and ended his football career.
- He spent about three months depressed, then dove into Gary Vaynerchuk, Andy Frisella and Tony Robbins content.
- That led to his first business at 16: managing Facebook ads for local businesses. He had no entrepreneurial family background.
- He did 75 Hard in 2020, did it again this year, and kept going past day 75 (about day 95 at recording).
- The daily list: two 45-minute workouts (one outdoors), a diet, a progress photo, a gallon of water, no alcohol or cheat meals, and 10 pages of a physical book.
- He values the "no deviation" rule: 10 pages seven days a week, not nine pages across six days.
- His rule for continuing was asking why he wouldn't do each task that day. He couldn't answer, so he kept going.
- He says it becomes a lifestyle and second nature, and compares discipline to a muscle.

## Listening, and what Casey says about advice

- The best advice he has received is from Larry King: "I've never learned anything while I was talking".
- He applies it to teams, customers and investors, saying the best leaders and entrepreneurs "are all great at listening".
- He ranks the Larry King interview as his most impactful, recorded when Casey was 19 and King was 87.
- On the unknown future he echoes the Steve Jobs line that you can only connect the dots looking backwards.
- He says that if you don't know the dream, you can start with what you don't want, then mimic the lifestyle you want today.

## Listener.fm and where he's building now

- He recently launched Listener.fm, an AI tool that automates podcasters' post-production.
- It generates topics, summaries, timestamps and suggested titles for segments.
- He says he uses it himself and believes the podcasting medium will keep changing and growing, citing the growth of ad revenue and investment since 2017.
- He also mentions a new Instagram page called "manifestation", built with his team around short video content.
