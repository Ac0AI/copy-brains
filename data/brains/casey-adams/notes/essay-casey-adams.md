## Starting from small things that scale later

- Casey started his first company at 16 running Facebook ads for local businesses (a chiropractor, a dentist, a local sports shop).
- The trigger was a neck injury on day one of sophomore-year football that ended his playing career and put him in a neck brace for six months. He went down social media rabbit holes on business, which got him into content creation and digital advertising.
- Earlier he shoveled driveways in snow and sold bracelets at the local pool for movie money, with no entrepreneurial family background.
- He started his podcast at 17 and calls himself someone who "got the bug."
- By the time he raised capital he had done 200+ podcast interviews with founders, VCs and creators, and treated the show as a way to build trusted relationships he could run ideas by.

## Spotting the MediaKits idea and validating it

- The idea came from a friend, an automotive YouTuber with roughly 600-700K subscribers, who was asked by Pennzoil for a media kit and didn't know what one was.
- Casey and his co-founder googled it, made one in Canva/Photoshop in about an hour and a half, and he landed the deal. Three weeks later Ferrari asked for one and the data was already outdated after 100K new subscribers.
- That exposed two gaps: creators had no media kit that never goes stale and shares via a link, and brands had no verified, trustworthy creator data, since "it's so easy to say you have X engagement."
- Timing was wrong in 2018 (he was finishing high school and ramping his podcast), so they shelved it with no capital and no tech experience.
- They revisited it in late 2019/early 2020 when TikTok creators were being "minted overnight," bootstrapped with their own resources, served dozens of creators from their networks, and validated before fundraising.
- Early 2021 they went to raise, got $1.5 million in about six months, and launched in August 2021.

## Fundraising through the podcast network

- Of 37 angel investors plus a couple of funds, Casey says roughly 90% were podcast guests or people he had nurtured relationships with.
- Fundraising meant stacking up 10 to 20 nos in a row, which he compares to getting kicked in the face.
- One breakthrough: a short, authentic DM on Twitter to Balaji Srinivasan. The reply was "send me your deck, and send me your wire instructions. If I like it, you'll see money in your account." Money arrived the next day.
- His takeaway on the highs and lows: a single influential believer can flip a bad stretch, and outside belief from people beyond your core team is strong feedback.

## Launch stumbles, traction and the acquisition

- The launch party in LA (with 700 creators attending) happened while the website and application weren't fully working. His fix in hindsight: put hard dates on the calendar and fill the time you have.
- The first ~60 days after launch "fell flat." They were non-technical co-founders shipping a scrappy product and things kept breaking.
- Traction came 60-90 days in after shipping TikTok and Twitch integrations: from roughly 500-1,000 creators to over 10,000, then over 25,000 by early 2022.
- They were preparing a formal seed round when the CEO of Viral Nation messaged him on LinkedIn. Open conversations about partnering turned into acquisition talks, and the company was acquired in September 2022, about a year after launch.
- Casey was 20 when he started the company and 22 at the time of the interview.

## Do things that don't scale, and be hands on

- His main lesson from MediaKits: build the product through boots-on-the-ground conversations with creators, and "do things that aren't scalable" in the earliest days (he references the Airbnb co-founder's advice).
- He warns against living in "the clouds of projections" and focusing on brand and communication before anyone has tried the product.
- Practical version: get as much feedback as possible on how the product looks and feels, prioritize DMs and direct interactions with potential users, and aim for a base of 1,000 true fans. Getting people to know, like and trust you through effective communication matters more early on than polish.

## Tell your story as the founder

- He argues founders must be "the biggest evangelists for what you're building," whether through organic social content or telling friends and family, so people are aware of it at all.
- He cites a Gary Vaynerchuk clip where a nonprofit founder who wanted to change the world was posting one video every couple of weeks; the point was that volume of output has to match ambition.
- His reasoning: people fall in love with stories, not single moments, and brands, products and communities are built by sharing the story authentically so people can attach themselves to it.

## Listener.fm: building for your own problem and finding users

- Listener.fm is an AI post-production tool for podcasters. It takes an audio file and generates SEO-optimized titles, show summaries, timestamps, blog posts and social content.
- He frames podcasting in three buckets: recording, post-production, and publishing/sharing. Post-production is the bucket where podcasters fail, give up, or hire a team, and it is what he built for.
- It came from his own problem, from running a show for almost six years. It launched roughly 90 days before the interview.
- Early distribution was not marketing but direct conversations: he tapped his network of podcasters and used Twitter, searching "podcast" to find shows that had just gone live and reaching out genuinely, sometimes with an audio message or a crafted message tied to their show.
- The goal was to get the first 10, 20, 50 people through the process and collect feedback to iterate on, with a roadmap toward an all-in-one podcast tool and a better listener experience.

## Principles on relationships and curiosity

- Casey says his superpower is curiosity about people's stories, which is why he stayed consistent with podcasting even when people asked why he spent time on a show with no sponsors or brands.
- His view: "you are in the people business," from executives to customers to team, even as AI shifts things.
- He treats kindness, quality relationships and doing the right thing as the foundation, and calls being a true listener and staying curious (rather than always wanting to share an opinion) the "North Star" for how he runs his business.
