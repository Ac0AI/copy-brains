## Starting from a negative: the injury that created the brand

- At 15, Casey was diagnosed with an injury (he describes it as inner spine ligament damage from football) and wore a neck brace for about six months.
- He had planned to play football or lacrosse in college. The injury ended that and he says he lost his identity.
- He used the setback to rebuild: personal development, social media marketing, and networking.
- He went all in at 15 with no plan B and no plan for college. A month after graduating high school he moved across the country to live with friends he met on social media.
- He is from Richmond, Virginia, from a non-entrepreneurial family, and did not fly in a plane until he could pay for it himself.
- At 19 he says he has interviewed Larry King, Jordan Belfort, Gary Vaynerchuk and hundreds of other successful people, while providing services to their teams.
- His framing: daily and monthly wins compound, and "the journey is long".

## How he got world-class guests on his podcast

- Casey started the podcast in late 2017 with no long-term vision, only to network with people he had already talked to on social media.
- He did about 40 interviews in four to five months while preparing for his Gary Vaynerchuk interview.
- His core premise: a podcast is a platform you give to someone to talk about themselves, their product or their business. That is the value you bring.
- He worked to get the show into the top 200 business podcasts and invested in the marketing behind it, so the platform had real value.
- Tactic: nearly every guest came from a simple Instagram DM. He asked directly ("loved the podcast, are you free next week?").
- Brand, content and consistency are what make the DM get a reply. Example: a sports broadcaster he had pinged repeatedly said he was free that same day at 3:00.
- Keep outreach short and simple, and be consistent.
- Creative effort: Grant Cardone said yes to an in-person interview next Thursday at 3:00. Casey had never been to Miami and booked a flight that Thursday, then showed up at Cardone's office. He says he learned that tactic from Cardone's book.
- Strategic value: for Jordan Belfort, Casey leveraged an in at TikTok (he had worked with their corporate team for about eight months) to help get Belfort verified on the platform, which led to the podcast.
- Compounding: after 5 to 10 quality guests, he could reach out to their friends and collaborators ("hey Grant, I've had X on the show"). He calls this "association marketing": building a brand on top of qualified people instead of saying "look at me".

## Building a personal brand in 2020

- Definition: "building the most authentic perception of you online". The foundations stay stable while the tactics keep changing.
- Follow trends: in 2020 he says TikTok (valued around $75 billion at the time) was the place to build an audience quickly. Create an account and go all in on content.
- Drive followers to Instagram, which he still calls the main place to build community and network. Keep a quality presence there.
- Start simple. Pick three brand pillars. His were podcasting, networking and traveling.
- Ask yourself what you want to be known for and whether you have credibility in it. If not, build it and document the process publicly so the audience grows with you.
- Collaboration is his "number one secret of branding". Reach out to people in your industry (chef, trainer, entrepreneur, athlete) and build through them.
- Podcasting is a good vehicle. He says there were under 2 million podcasts, about half inactive, and recommends starting on Anchor FM (acquired by Spotify about a year earlier), where he moved his show's network.
- His two 2020 foundations: start a podcast and learn to talk, plus consistent content and networking.
- He keeps one authentic Instagram account, the same one he started at 14, rather than separating a "brand" account from a personal one.

## Handling fear, imposter syndrome and judgment

- Casey's own start: he posted Snapchat and Instagram story videos about books from his room, and his brothers and parents questioned it.
- He says he was not good on camera or at public speaking and still redoes podcast intros. If you are not good on camera, do a video every day.
- Questions to ask: what am I putting out content for, and who am I trying to reach?
- He cites "fear is false evidence appearing real".
- Focus on the action and the outcome you want, not on the fear. People stall because they worry what friends will think.
- Get clear on intention first: write down what you are grateful for, your goals, and why you want them.
- Intention and passion make imposter syndrome fade.

## Manifestation, preparation and give-to-receive

- He writes down who he wants to interview weekly. His journal had a 2017 note: interview Larry King.
- He calls himself a realist and a "delusional optimist": "execution creates the reality". Manifestation needs the steps, like an airplane trip needs the airport, TSA, check-in and boarding.
- Sculptor versus lottery approach: chip away at the rock daily instead of staring at it. Even a lottery winner had to buy the ticket.
- Larry King told him "preparation creates luck".
- Case study: friend Charlie Rocket posted a fan-made Nike commercial. Casey reached out and spent about $200 on Facebook Ads. It reached 1.2 million views in a week, a Nike executive saw it, and Nike signed him as a sponsored athlete.
- He believes in give and receive: whatever you want, give it first. In one quarantine week he reached out to about 20 people and recorded about five podcasts in a day.
- Do not attach to your current version of yourself. Define who you need to become for the goals to become real.

## Daily habits for mindset

- He has not owned a TV in four years and avoids watching the news, because its business model is negativity.
- During quarantine he advises one of three things: build a new habit, do what you were putting off, or learn a new skill (a book, a podcast, starting a podcast).
- He keeps a roughly 600-page journal and each day writes a brain-dump of gratitude, sometimes a list of about 50 items. He says repetition is key to happiness.
- He controls his environment: positive posters and affirmations, and people who do not shut down positivity. "You are a product of your environment."
- Core values he wrote down daily early on: never change, stay humble, stick to your morals.

## Starting a business and which skills to build

- His own work: affiliate marketing, social media advertising, podcasting, personal brand consulting, brand strategy and social media management for executives and brands.
- Recently (past 6 to 8 months) he has worked on TikTok at the corporate level, helping brands and laying out franchise-style TikTok campaigns.
- Example: a brand named Popsockets-style tag product (he says "poeple") whose fourth TikTok video went to a million views in a week and later reached 70 million. He uses it as evidence of the platform's opportunity.
- He is building podcast software to connect podcasters, guests and advertisers. He expected to focus on it heavily over the next three to six months.
- Advice for starting: build a skill set first. Know what you are good at (sales, design, contracts, hiring).
- Reach out online to run ideas by people, because ideas should keep evolving (he notes Facebook in 2010 is not Facebook in 2020).
- "Don't force ideas, let ideas come to you." His podcast software idea came naturally over about six months of events and conversations.
- In a downturn, you cannot control the economy but can control your knowledge, skills and response. Pick a concrete skill such as graphic design, audio engineering, Facebook advertising or social media management, and learn from free YouTube and Google content.
- For services, ask how you can help a business on a new platform where they already have a presence.

## Communication as the core business skill

- Casey calls communication "the most important skill set" for raising money, building a team, serving clients and building an audience.
- He recommends "How to Win Friends and Influence People" (use someone's name, provide value).
- He used to fear speaking in front of the class and says confidence came from passion and practice. A podcast improved how he asks questions and adapts to a room of two or 20,000 people.
- Communication includes body language and how your Instagram brand communicates.
- Everything is people-oriented: "every business is run by people".
- While confined at home, practice by starting a podcast with friends as guests, or by sending 10 to 15 second video or audio messages to contacts.
- His daily volume: he sends about 10 to 25 DMs a day and responds to about 50, and engages with his community.
- Vulnerability, as he defines it, is saying what you truly mean even when you hesitate. He recommends the "We're Not Really Strangers" card game for building connection.
