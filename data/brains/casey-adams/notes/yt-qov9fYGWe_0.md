## From injury to business: how Casey Adams got started

- At 16, a football neck injury (neck brace for six months, no more football) left him with free time and led him to YouTube and business content.
- He followed Tony Robbins, Gary Vaynerchuk and Tai Lopez. He and a friend each put in $30 for Tai Lopez's 67-day course.
- He bought more books than ever (Think and Grow Rich, How to Win Friends and Influence People, Crushing It) and describes the stretch as creating "this new identity."
- By sophomore year he cut his course load to the bare minimum, committed to business, and skipped the college path.
- He moved to Arizona a month after graduating high school, with a media company and a podcast already running for two to three years.
- He calls curiosity his biggest innate advantage, and says it is "very easy to shut off an opportunity."

## Stacking early wins to build momentum

- Casey says small early wins let him keep going, and that they matter for earning trust from parents as much as for himself.
- At 16-17 his first company ran Facebook ads and Instagram for local businesses (dentist, chiropractor, sports shop) on monthly retainers.
- Earning a couple thousand dollars a month at 16 won his parents' belief and gave him freedom to travel to LA, New York and DC for events.
- Around his 30th-35th podcast interview he booked Chris "Drama" Pfaff, which his mom had watched on TV with him as a kid. He calls it reinforcement of his commitment, not a money win.
- His advice: actually publish episodes instead of thinking about starting a podcast. "Micro wins do build momentum," and momentum helps you stick with something longer.

## The 90-day podcast sprint

- He read the podcasting chapter of Gary Vee's Crushing It and it "instantly spoke" to him.
- He started listening to many shows, followed favorites, then launched his own.
- A simple Instagram DM set up a meeting with Gary Vee. Within 90 days he had done 60 interviews and was a full-time podcaster.
- He says he did not realize in the moment that reading the chapter would become his whole identity and brand.
- He sees every career chapter starting the same way: something captures attention, consumes your mind, and you read and watch everything about it.

## The origin of MediaKits and why timing mattered

- In 2018, friend Jeff (JR Garage, an automotive YouTuber then at roughly 500-600K subscribers) was asked for a media kit by Pennzoil.
- Casey and co-founder Kieran O'Brien built it by hand in Canva or Photoshop (headshot, bio, analytics screenshots) in a couple of hours, and Jeff got the deal.
- Weeks later Ferrari asked for one. The kit was outdated, so they manually updated numbers and re-exported a PDF.
- That manual work sparked the idea of a link-based media kit with real-time data that never goes stale.
- They shelved it because they lacked the experience and network to launch a tech company in 2018.
- In 2020 (COVID, TikTok creators like Charli D'Amelio and Josh Richards exploding) they restarted it as a side project: 20-30% of their time, bootstrapped, using contractors.
- In early 2021 they raised a family-and-friends round, closing about $1.25M in four to five months, and launched in August 2021.
- His takeaway: "the time will never just be right," and an idea can still be worth pursuing years later.

## What MediaKits is and how it was built

- It is software that lets creators connect social platforms in about 30 seconds and share a real-time-data media kit with brands.
- Integrations at launch: Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube. Later TikTok and Twitch. Spotify for podcasters and musicians is next.
- Casey is a non-technical founder; the team relies on an engineer who built the product.
- He turns the non-technical status into an advantage by making team-building the core focus.
- He says "there's no part-time founder": once you commit, a company becomes your life.
- He frames the fundamentals as being excited about the idea and excited about the people you build with.
- As of the interview there were just over 20,000 creators on the platform, and the team regularly surveys them.

## Knowing when to move to the next chapter

- Casey says transitions happen gradually and you often do not notice them while you are in one.
- He journals daily or at least weekly to document what he is doing, what matters to him and his goals.
- His career steps: Facebook ads, podcasting, media company, live events in Arizona, a book at 17, viral TikTok marketing help, then MediaKits.
- He argues "saying yes to something is saying no to another thing," so follow what is pulling you.

## Building confidence

- Put yourself in uncomfortable rooms. At 16-17, around 30-50 year old speakers and founders, his confidence deflated, and the fix was to keep introducing himself.
- Story: he met Larry King's son Chance, then saw Larry King in a wheelchair at the Century City mall. He took about 6-10 seconds, said hello, got a photo and sent it to Chance. A month later Larry was on his podcast (late 2019).
- Watch how you talk about yourself. He says he used to say "I'm not a runner" before training for a marathon (17 miles was his max when he hurt his knees).
- Use repetition through things like cold emailing, cold calling and sales. He sold fundraising cards for his football team in high school.
- He pairs confidence with gratitude: being grateful for past lessons makes you confident in the next move.

## Web3 and the creator economy

- MediaKits is Web2 today, built on social integrations, but Casey says the team has plans for some Web3 integrations (no details given).
- He has been deep in NFTs for about a year and made an NFT drop with Television (as referenced in the conversation).
- He says most creators know the word "NFT" but not the impact on their business over the next 5-10 years.
- His view is that launching an NFT project is not the right first step. Community and real utility come first.
- He cites Gary Vee's VeeFriends as the model: real-life utility, an annual event, podcast access, real IP, and a "10, 20, 50 year play."
- He mentions a travel influencer whose NFT project fit his brand and community goals.
- His advice for creators is to take even 1-10% of what Gary Vee does and learn from the community he built, whether or not you hold the NFTs.
