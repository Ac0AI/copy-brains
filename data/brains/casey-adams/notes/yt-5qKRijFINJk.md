## Branding is commitment, not credentials

- Casey argues personal branding "comes down to laying out your story over time" in an authentic way. Everyone has a story, whether just starting or having sold a company for $100 million.
- Titles, companies and past achievements are not the point. What counts is how your content makes people feel and what value you give them.
- His advice to beginners: "tell people that be authentic as possible because that's what people want, that's what they respect."
- He says people dislike big labels like "e-commerce expert" or "stock trader". They want real stories and useful information in your own voice.
- He warns against copying Gary Vee, Grant Cardone or Ty Lopez. Study what they do (content creation, publishing, connecting with people), then pick and choose, because a brand "is geared towards you".
- He frames a personal brand as the asset that survives company failure: "if a company fails... you still have your personal brand".

## Three brand pillars and a clear bio

- Casey's first tip is committing fully. At 50% commitment, thinking about the brand two days a week and scrolling and consuming the rest, "you're going to lose".
- His second tip is defining three brand pillars: why you want to brand yourself, how you want to brand yourself, and what that looks like.
- His own pillars, visible in his Instagram bio: (1) interviewing world-class entrepreneurs via his podcast, (2) CEO of Build Your Empire, (3) traveling and networking.
- The first line of the Instagram, YouTube or other bio should make clear exactly what you want people to know.
- Consistency of collaborations and willingness to be rejected separate him from other 18-year-olds. He has DM'd hundreds of people, and some said yes, some no, some never replied. The yeses (Grant Cardone, Ed Mylett, Andy Frisella) helped shape his brand.
- He treats it as a numbers game: more outreach gives more interviews, more calls give more business, and you create opportunities by being in rooms like networking events and Facebook groups.

## Document the journey, don't fake it

- Casey says there are no shortcuts to building a brand. Consistency and collaborations are the keys.
- Advice for 18-25 year olds: "document the journey, talk about what you're doing day to day, talk about the struggles, talk about things that are not sexy."
- He notes that Gary Vee has 32 people on his brand and Grant Cardone has 50 people making calls and a studio worth about $200,000. Beginners should not feel everything must be perfect.
- He started his podcast with plain Apple headphones: "it's not about being fancy, it's about making things happen."
- He sees faking clout or credentials as a losing move. He says people don't know your character, they know your reputation, and a reputation built through content, collaborations and consistency lasts.

## How he landed big-name podcast guests

- He did not start with big names. He began by calling friends and people in his network, such as Kieran O'Brien, Jaden Gross and Jeremy Haynes, to build his craft.
- His rule: go from zero to one, one to two, two to three, building stepping stones rather than jumping from zero interviews to Gary Vee.
- Once he had 5-10 interviews, he could pitch bigger guests by naming the people he had already interviewed.
- He leads with value, not a request. For Grant Cardone, he said the books had changed his life (he had read five) before mentioning the podcast.
- He interviewed associates and right-hand men first, because "you don't want to know Drake personally, you want to know Drake's right-hand man."
- Practical pitch tip: reference a mutual connection. A DM like "I interviewed [mutual person], he said great things about you" gives you "10 times more likelihood" of a yes.
- He says to still DM the big names directly, even 10 times, but not to rely on that route.
- His interview style changed. His first 15-20 interviews were fully scripted, and now he goes in with no questions: a conversation driven by curiosity, open-mindedness and a willingness to listen. Andy Frisella called him one of his best podcast interviews.

## The DM and event that changed his trajectory

- At 15 he suffered a neck injury (ligament damage) playing football, spent 25 weeks in a neck brace, and was told he could never play again. He turned to self-development: Ty Lopez's 67-step program (split about $33 each with a friend), Gary Vee's "Crush It", and "Think and Grow Rich".
- Early money came from affiliate marketing. He sold "Online Sales Pro", an email automation tool at $37/month with $20/month residual, and sold about 50 memberships through a Snapchat group of about 500 people that he built by posting in forums and doing book reviews.
- He sent 50 reporters a day to get press, and a local news station interviewed him. He shouted out Caleb Maddox, who then invited him to speak at an event in San Diego on January 13, 2017, with about 100 people in the room.
- He bought a plane ticket impulsively, with his dad accompanying him as a condition, and it was his first flight. He met Caleb Maddox, Dan Fleyshman, Jeremy Haynes and Gerard Adams there.
- Afterward he sent Ty Lopez a gratitude DM of roughly 5-7 sentences, after earlier unseen attempts. Ty replied within about 20 minutes and flew him to LA.
- At Ty's house he met Jaden Gross, who flew him out the next month. He took this as proof of a core belief: "you're only a DM away, a conversation away, a call away from that next pivot point."
- In 2017 he flew to LA about once a month, funded by a social media marketing agency running ads for personal branding clients. His advice: spend money on experiences and relationships rather than things.

## Build Your Empire and what he was building (late 2018)

- Casey and two partners acquired the Build Your Empire brand about eight months before the interview. It came with 1.5 million Instagram followers, the email list and the name rights.
- He said they were restructuring the brand to be more than a motivational page, with new colors, scene structures, videos and content that has "real value and real core information".
- The plan was an entrepreneur incubator (the Build Empire house) with quarterly events, the first on January 19-20, 2019, and niche events such as real estate, e-commerce, social media and music.
- It also included an interactive learning platform, described as "the Netflix for entrepreneurship content", with a membership site and a Lightspeed VT backend, plus a personal branding agency and consulting for personal brands.
- He was also CMO of a peer-to-peer consulting app that handles on-screen invoicing and conversation records, and said they were raising money for it within three months.
- His podcast, "Rise of the Young", had interviewed over 80 entrepreneurs, with a book of the same name. He said it ranked as a top 25 business podcast.

## Mindset notes

- He says "things don't happen to you unless you make them happen", and that most beginners set goals and then nothing happens within a week or a month because they don't commit.
- He quotes Gary Vee that "you're only as good as your last at bat" and says to fall in love with the journey rather than treating a milestone as "I made it".
- On clarity, he says today's abundance of information (e-commerce, Forex, Amazon, SMMA, stocks) makes it essential to pick one thing and commit.
- His default question for planning is: where are you now, where do you want to be, and what's next.
