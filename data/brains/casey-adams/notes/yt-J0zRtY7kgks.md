## Starting the podcast: low-friction launch and relationships as the payoff

- Casey started his podcast in 2017, at 17, from his bedroom in a small Virginia town. He had no entrepreneurial family background and had never flown until he bought his first ticket.
- The trigger was Gary Vaynerchuk's "Crushing It", whose last chapter pitched podcasting in 2017 as a way to connect with great people, have great conversations and learn something along the way.
- He recorded the first episode of "Rise of the Young" with Apple earbuds in his bedroom, with no plan for what to say. He presses this point: "a lot of people overthink just getting started."
- After 5 years and 400+ interviews, he says the main return is relationships, not downloads. He describes it as a natural path: DM someone on Instagram, build a relationship, host them on the show, and eventually have them as an investor in the company.
- He says "99%" of the people he knows today came through social media and the podcast.

## Creating luck: how the Larry King interview happened

Casey uses this as a case study in engineering opportunity. He interviewed Larry King at 19, at the end of 2019, when King was 87.

1. A close friend and business partner worked with Tilman Fertitta. Casey interviewed Fertitta on his show for Fertitta's book.
2. Fertitta hosted a dinner of 20-25 people who had helped promote the book. There Casey met Chance King, Larry King's son, and they became close friends.
3. Two to three weeks later, Larry King rolled past Casey in a Century City mall. Casey chose to walk up, introduce himself, mention that he'd just met Chance, and take a selfie, which he sent to Chance.
4. He told Chance he'd love to host his father someday. King was recovering from a stroke, so it looked unlikely.
5. Around Thanksgiving 2019, Chance replied that his father wanted to do it. About two weeks later Casey was in King's studio.

His lessons:
- Put yourself around great people, at dinners, networking events or through a podcast, and unexpected opportunities show up if you keep doing it.
- Taking action matters as much as the opportunity. Many people have chances land in front of them and never act.
- Mall moment: "you really have to decide in that moment" whether to say hello.

## Interview prep: from heavy research to pure curiosity

- Early on he researched heavily and wrote out all his questions, which gave him confidence.
- He noticed he spent too much time thinking about the next question and too little listening. He cites Larry King's line, "I never learned anything while I was talking."
- After roughly his 20th-30th interview he stopped writing questions entirely.
- His current method:
  - Watch other interviews with the guest to understand their story and background.
  - Walk in curious, with no preset end goal, and treat it like lunch with a friend.
  - Keep a loose plan tied to the show's theme (business and entrepreneurship through a young person's lens), but avoid a formal Q&A.
- He says this takes pressure off and makes him more present, and that it took many reps before he felt comfortable sitting with anyone.

## Media Kits: origin, validation and fundraising

**The problem he saw**
- Casey and co-founder Kieran (who then ran a marketing agency) helped a friend, a YouTube car creator with about half a million subscribers, build a media kit in Canva or Photoshop when a brand asked for one.
- Two weeks later Ferrari asked for his media kit. Every number was stale after a few viral videos, so they edited the numbers by hand again.
- That prompted two questions:
  - Why can't creators have a media kit that never goes stale?
  - Why can't brands see verified data instead of creators simply claiming an engagement rate or demographic?

**Timeline**
- They shelved the idea because Casey was still in high school and neither had experience launching a tech company.
- In 2020 they moved to Arizona. With TikTok exploding and creators being discovered overnight, they revisited it.
- Mid to late 2020 was spent ideating, prototyping and designing.
- They went full-time in early 2021 and raised $1.25 million in 4-6 months.
- They launched on August 11, 2021.
- A large share of investors came directly from relationships built through the podcast.

**The product and where it goes**
- Media Kits is a tool for creators, musicians, podcasters and athletes to build a media kit with live, verified analytics. The site is mediakits.com and takes under 60 seconds to set up.
- He cites 25,000+ creators using it.
- The goal is to become the most trusted cross-platform data source, because no single place shows a creator's full social presence with verified, continuously updated numbers.
- The media kit is the "Trojan horse" into a broader ecosystem of secondary tools. He says the kit is the one thing every creator needs.
- He frames the market as roughly 50 million full-time creators and growing every year.
- He says building and shipping the software, and hiring the team, was the most educational process of his last five years, even though he is not an engineer or developer.

## Using social media as a producer, not a consumer

- His rule: spend time on social media producing, not consuming. Producing includes:
  - Posting content and building a brand.
  - Replying to comments in your community.
  - Messaging friends and answering DMs.
- When he watches Instagram stories, he turns consumption into output. If he views 50 stories in a day, he sends a real message to a friend or someone who inspires him, reacting to their content or asking how he can help.
- He enters the apps with an intention, to respond, help someone or post something that might inspire others, so the apps don't consume him.
- His analogy: a hammer can build a project or break a window, so how you use the tool and where you put your energy is what counts.

## Operating principles: daily improvement and optimizing dead time

- Core principle: "If I'm not getting better every day, what am I doing?" He rejects complacency at any level of success and aims to improve about 1% a day in the gym, relationships, products and work.
- He ties this to David Meltzer's idea of "the relentless pursuit of your potential." After 400 interviews he sees great people as constantly evolving, and he feels like a different person every 6-12 months.
- Favorite quote: "Get comfortable being in uncomfortable situations." He says real growth happens in uncomfortable moments such as approaching Larry King, giving a keynote, or pitching investors.
- His push to act is "If not now, when?" Opportunities come and go, so don't bet on a second chance.
- He doesn't own a car, a choice he's held for 4-5 years (a car is shared with his girlfriend). He uses rideshare so traffic time becomes time for calls, email, DMs and audiobooks. He says 30 minutes each way, four days a week, adds up to about four hours a week over 52 weeks.
- He stacks tasks. He listens to podcasts while folding laundry, and takes calls or answers messages on a 10-minute walk to the gym. He credits Ty Lopez on optimizing what you do and Dan Fleyshman, who has never owned a car.
- Long-term, he plans to keep building Media Kits, keep doing the podcast indefinitely (a goal of 10,000+ interviews by age 50), and eventually move into real estate with his brother.
