## How Casey built credibility as a teenager

- Casey's core principle for credibility: "build credibility by associating with credible people," and to do it genuinely.
- His vehicle was a podcast, started December 2017 by interviewing friends his own age, including his business partner Kieran and Caleb Maddox. It grew from there to guests like Larry King, Rick Ross and Gary Vaynerchuk.
- He set a goal of 60 interviews in 90 days. He did two to three interviews a day after high school, being "obsessive in the DMs."
- He started small and stayed consistent: he spent all of 2016 putting out content before the podcast launched.
- He says the podcast became a huge part of his identity and "opened up 99 doors," including MediaKits.

## Showing up in person: the Grant Cardone and Miami story

- From 2017 to 2019, while still in high school, Casey flew to LA almost once a month and spent every dollar he made on trips to events. Mentor Dan Fleischman invited him to his "elevator nights."
- He calls the hardest sale of his life asking his parents to let him travel to California alone at 17.
- Tactic from Grant Cardone's book *Sell or Be Sold*: he DM'd Cardone for a podcast spot, got routed to Cardone's assistant, and emailed her that he would "be in Miami next week from Wednesday to Friday."
- He had no plans to be in Miami and had never been there. She booked him for Thursday at 3, so he booked flights with friends and missed two or three days of school.
- He used the same move to land an interview with the mayor of Miami.
- His takeaway: it isn't the most scalable strategy, but "just get in the room with people." He says to commit first and figure out logistics after.

## Showing up digitally and documenting the journey

- Casey says to show up in the DMs, in content and in physical rooms. Some investors in MediaKits' pre-seed round came from a cold DM.
- He says to document your experiences and tell the stories: "you learn through stories."
- He tells people to tell their story authentically, and stack credibility over time.
- His personal brand began as book reviews and a record of his learning on Snapchat while he was in a neck brace for six months after an injury ended his football career.
- During that period he followed Tai Lopez, Gary Vaynerchuk and Tony Robbins, and journaled a personal brand game plan.
- He says the content then stacked into a brand that has shifted a lot over almost five years.

## Pivoting and how he handles it

- Casey's pivots: affiliate marketing on Snapchat, then a book, then a podcast, then Build Your Empire (a media company running events in Scottsdale, about two years), then MediaKits.
- He moved across the country after high school to join Build Your Empire. The dozens of events there helped him meet early guests such as Drama, Rob Jurdeck and Robert Green.
- He also renamed his show from "Rise of the Young" to "The Casey Adams Show" when he felt he'd outgrown it.
- Before MediaKits he was working on a podcasting startup called PodSwipe. It never launched and raised no money.
- Rule he uses: "find where pivots make sense and go all in on it." Build it, see whether it works, stick to it if it does, and pivot again if it doesn't.
- He admits pivots are scary in your head, and says the moment you do it "it feels so good."
- He has journaled "be comfortable getting uncomfortable" since age 16. When he gets comfortable, he adds a challenge. He stepped up from 2 to 3 mile runs to 5 miles, then began training for a marathon, because physical challenges transfer to business.

## The MediaKits origin story

- A media kit is a document creators use to pitch themselves to brands, showing bio, past work and analytics.
- Around 2018, friend and automotive YouTuber J.R. Garage got a Pennzoil brand-deal inquiry and asked Casey and Kieran to build him a media kit.
- They built it by hand in Canva or Photoshop with screenshots of his analytics. It took a couple of hours, and he got the deal.
- About two months later Ferrari reached out and wanted to fly him to Daytona Beach for a brand deal. His stats had changed, so they had to update everything manually, "changing twos to threes and fives to sixes."
- That was the light-bulb moment: why isn't there a way for creators to build a media kit that never goes out of date and can be shared through a link?
- They shelved the idea because they lived in Virginia and lacked time and resources.

## Timing the launch and building MediaKits

- In 2020, Casey and Kieran were roommates during the pandemic. TikTok was exploding and creators like Charli D'Amelio and Addison Rae became celebrities overnight, so they judged the creator economy to be growing year over year.
- Casey had relationships from the podcast to help raise money. Kieran told him the timing was right, and Casey dropped PodSwipe to build MediaKits.
- They hired a developer and a designer, then held a launch party in LA with around 600 creators (the Callee performance mentioned).
- Launch: the first two months were "a disaster," with bugs and international developers to fix. After about a month and a half of fixes they relaunched in mid-October.
- Afterwards they kept shipping integrations, most recently Twitch. He says hundreds of streamers are using it and hundreds of creators message them saying it helped them land a brand deal.
- Their stated vision: let creators of every type (musicians, podcasters, athletes, streamers) organize cross-platform data in one place.
- Casey says being a creator himself and getting pitched with media kits all the time gives him conviction about the product.
