## Path from a high school injury to podcasting and MediaKits

- At 16, Casey suffered a severe football injury that left him in a neck brace with a threat of paralysis. He says the experience sparked his passion for entrepreneurship, business, social media, technology and interviewing.
- He started his podcast, The Casey Adams Show, from his bedroom as a high school junior.
- Over five years he conducted over 300 interviews with influential and successful people.
- The podcast led him to co-found MediaKits, which uses real-time data and analytics to help creators design professional media kits.
- Timeline: raised $1.5 million in early 2021, officially launched in August 2021, and was acquired by Viral Nation in September 2022. Viral Nation is described as a leading force in the creator economy and the world's first influencer talent agency.

## Listening as a leadership skill (the Larry King lesson)

- Casey names his interview with Larry King as the most memorable of his 300+ interviews.
- The lesson that stuck: King's belief in the power of listening, summed up in his line "I've never learned anything while I was talking."
- Casey applies this by spending more time listening to employees and team members.
- Result he reports: by actively listening he gains insights and perspectives he would not have considered otherwise, which helps him make better decisions and be a more effective leader.

## Weathering economic headwinds as a small business (2023 outlook)

Casey's framework for a small business facing increased competition and economic headwinds has three parts:

1. **Stay agile and adaptable.** Keep up with industry trends, stay open to new ideas and technologies, and be willing to try new things and pivot to new challenges.
2. **Stay competitive.** Offer high-quality products and services at a competitive price. That may mean streamlining operations, reducing costs, and being mindful about pricing and promotions.
3. **Build strong customer relationships.** In a crowded market a loyal customer base is a major advantage. He ties this to excellent customer service, responsiveness to customer needs, and building trust and credibility through consistent, high-quality interactions.

His summary: the future of work for small businesses needs "a combination of adaptability, competitiveness and customer focus."

## Using Slack huddles for focused, fast collaboration

- Casey calls himself a productivity expert and Slack power user.
- Huddles are the feature he singles out: quick, ad hoc small-group discussions for specific projects or tasks.
- Why it works for him:
  - Conversations stay focused and free of interruptions from the main channels.
  - A team can be gathered quickly for time-sensitive projects.
  - Discussions are synchronous and get input from everyone.

## Building team relationships virtually

- Casey uses direct messages and huddles for personal, focused conversations with team members.
- He gets to know the team on a personal level by sharing updates about life outside work, such as pictures of his family and pets.
- He says this creates a more human connection that strengthens professional relationships and fosters a sense of community.

## Tool stack for a small business: Slack integrations

Casey's advice is to take full advantage of Slack's features and integrations, using the tools the team already depends on inside Slack to streamline workflows and improve productivity. His favorites:

- **Google Drive:** share and collaborate on documents, spreadsheets and presentations inside Slack; saves time and improves asynchronous work across teams.
- **Zoom:** schedule and join meetings from Slack, making team collaboration and communication easier.
- **ClickUp:** project management inside Slack. Users can create and assign tasks, set reminders and due dates, receive notifications, and use Slack commands to search tasks and projects.
