## Background and origin story

- Casey Adams grew up in Chesterfield County, Virginia, 30 minutes south of Richmond, in a non-entrepreneurial family. His mom was a special ed teacher and babysitter, and his dad worked at Philip Morris for about 30 years.
- As a kid he always looked for ways to make money: knocking on doors, shoveling snow, selling bracelets, charging his brothers $5 to cut the grass.
- He was a lifelong athlete (hockey from age 3, then lacrosse, then football). At 15 he landed on his head in the first hitting practice and was diagnosed with spinal ligament damage. He could never play football again and wore a neck brace for six months.
- He spent the first two to three months depressed and angry, having lost his identity as an athlete. In the back half he immersed himself in personal development and entrepreneurship content (Gary Vaynerchuk, David Meltzer).
- That led him to start a business doing Facebook advertising and social media for local businesses. He calls the injury "the one moment in particular that completely changed the course and direction of my future."
- He didn't go to college. He moved to Arizona a month after high school and sees himself as a lifelong learner.

## Why he started a podcast and how he launched it

- He launched The Casey Adams Show on December 21, 2017, at 17, recording the first episode on wired Apple headphones while walking around his room.
- The stated purpose has not changed in five years: meet people, learn something, and create business opportunity.
- His first 10 to 15 guests were friends and young entrepreneurs he'd met on social media.
- He set a goal of 60 interviews in the first 90 days, using cold DM outreach and publishing three to four a week, sometimes two a day. It built confidence in reaching out and taking a "no" or a "yes."
- The podcast also grew his personal brand, through clips and guests sharing their episodes.
- By his account he has interviewed about 400 founders, entrepreneurs, musicians and athletes.

## Podcasting as a relationship-building and fundraising engine

- He argues a one-on-one recorded conversation builds a much deeper relationship than meeting at an event or over a drink, and it expedites the early stages of a relationship.
- It also forces both people to prepare, which makes it a better medium than a loud networking event.
- He deliberately used the podcast to build rapport with people he might one day ask for tactical advice, such as on fundraising.
- It paid off with Media Kits: he says 90 to 95% of the investors in the round were past podcast guests.

## How he landed the Larry King interview (and what it taught him)

- The chain: a friend introduced him to restaurateur Tilman Fertitta, who was promoting his book "Shut Up and Listen." Casey interviewed him, which led to an invite to a dinner in Beverly Hills.
- At the dinner he sat across from Larry King's son, Chance. They were about the same age and became friends without Casey asking for anything.
- He says his approach is not to meet someone and ask for an intro. He believes in "letting things happen" and building real relationships, and he texts people on holidays to put good energy out there.
- Months later he asked what it would take to have Chance's dad on the show. The first answer was "not the best time" (Larry was coming off a stroke). Around Thanksgiving, Chance came back with a yes.
- The interview happened on December 10, 2019. Larry was 87 and Casey was 19, a 76-year age gap.
- His takeaway on opportunity: say yes to things even when you don't know what they'll bring. Opportunities arrive disguised as events you don't feel like attending, and most won't be a "grand slam," but some become real friendships.
- The lesson from Larry: "I've never learned anything while I was talking." Casey admits that in his first 10 to 20 episodes he was anxious about the next question, and he now treats being present and listening as the core skill.

## The origin and build of Media Kits

- **The idea (2018-2019):** a YouTuber friend (JR Garage, 1M+ subscribers) asked Casey and his partner Kieran to make him a media kit. They built it in Canva/Photoshop in about an hour and he got the deal. Weeks later Ferrari asked for his kit, and they had to manually update numbers after he gained 55,000 subscribers.
- **The insight:** why can't a creator have a media kit that never goes out of date, and why can't brands see verified, trustworthy data directly from the creator?
- **Timing:** they shelved it in 2018-2019. In mid-2020, with TikTok exploding and "creators being minted overnight," they came back to it. They bootstrapped designs for about four months and validated the idea with creator friends.
- **Funding:** they raised just under $1.5M in a pre-seed, with a rolling close over about six months. It was Casey's first raise.
- **Product:** a platform where creators, podcasters, musicians and others get a digital resume with real-time data via the APIs of Instagram, Facebook, Twitter, Twitch, YouTube and more.
- **Timeline:** users could sign up on August 11, 2021, and the company was sold to Viral Nation in late August the following year, about 12 months after launch.
- **Team and focus:** a small team of 8 to 10 the whole time. They wanted to be the best at one thing, the go-to source for real-time data in a shareable link, and used the phrase "inch wide, mile deep." The domain was mediakits.com.
- **Launch:** a big launch party in Los Angeles with Dan, Wiz Khalifa performing and about 700 creators and friends, to make a branding splash.
- **Early struggle:** in the first 45 days they didn't get more than 1,000 users on a free tool with a paid upgrade, which forced them to dig into why people weren't signing up or using it.

## Non-negotiables and operating principles

- He says "how you do anything is how you do everything" and ties business and life together.
- Three core values:
  - **Stay curious:** about the world, about your own ability, about how you can help people and about new technology. He notes small-town people often cap themselves by assuming some opportunities are "not for me."
  - **Never stop learning:** he treats learning as lifelong and as continuing well after school.
  - **Extreme gratitude:** he looks for moments daily to be grateful, and he cites Robert Greene's chapter on confronting your mortality as a reminder that life is fragile.
- He also draws inspiration from guests who have overcome adversity, such as Nick Santonastaso, and says they help him look at his own life.

## Discipline and doing hard things

- He is on day 58 of his second 75 Hard (the first was in 2020, which he failed once before completing). The daily rules are:
  - Two 45-minute workouts, one outdoors.
  - A gallon of water.
  - 10 pages of reading.
  - A progress picture.
  - No alcohol or cheat meals.
  - Following a diet.
- He calls it a mental toughness challenge, not a fitness one. He says there's never a perfect time to start, and the discipline transfers to every area of life.
- He says his first marathon in December 2023 came when he was not "naturally a runner" and had never run more than about two miles.
- He does a cold plunge five days a week and deliberately puts something daunting on the calendar.
- His belief is that doing hard things prepares you for life, and that staying "emotionally at Center through the ups through the downs" is what allows you to be great.
- He says the initial excitement for any commitment (75 Hard, a business, a podcast) fades fast and "the only answer is truly discipline."

## Current direction: Listener.fm

- After leaving Viral Nation he is building in the podcasting tech space, focused on AI automating post-production.
- His tool Listener.fm takes an audio file and outputs titles, descriptions, show notes and similar assets. He says it's being tested with many podcasts and podcast networks.
- His stated aim is to take what he learned from Media Kits and apply it to a new venture, while keeping the podcast at the core.
