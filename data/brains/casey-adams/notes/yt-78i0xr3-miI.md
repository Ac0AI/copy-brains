## How Casey raised his first round from his podcast network

- Casey's first fundraise was a pre-seed "family and friends" round for Media Kits, just over $1.5 million, closed in mid-2021. It was his first time raising capital for a startup.
- He says the round came almost entirely from relationships he already had. His line: "your network is your net worth."
- The majority of the money came from previous guests of his podcast, which he has hosted since he was 17.
- He did not pitch broadly. He picked a handful of people in his network who could add value, understand the product, and give quality feedback.
- He set up "dozens and dozens" of meetings in a short period.
- He leaned on a mentor, Aristotle Loomis, who coached him through the process and "picks up the phone whenever you call." Casey says a mentor who stays with you through the hard times and teaches you the process matters more than just "find mentors."

## Casey's three pieces of advice for first-time fundraisers

1. **Nail your story.** Every product and company is a story: why you, why now, and why this market. Know why you're the best operator for the role, especially when building the deck.
2. **Pitch people who give real feedback and want you to win.** Cold email pitches are fine, but get your first 10, 15 or 25 pitch meetings with people you already know. Their feedback sharpens the story and builds the confidence to pitch well.
3. **Be consistent.** His raise took about six months of "pure consistency": hearing no, getting rejections, and staying consistent with outreach, pitching and follow-ups.

He adds that there is never a perfect time to raise, and that fundraising conditions were drying up in places. His advice is to use confidence in the story and get feedback as early as possible.

## Building a network from nothing

- Casey grew up in a small town in Virginia (Chesterfield) and knew nobody in business, marketing or startups.
- At 16 he went to his first networking event, a real estate conference in downtown Richmond. He was the youngest person in the room among roughly 500 people.
- At the start of 2017 he was invited to speak at an event in San Diego through a friend, Caleb Maddox. It was his first trip to California, and his parents did not want him to go.
- That led to more events in LA, New York and Miami. He says he skipped about 60 days of school in junior and senior year to attend.
- He then recorded podcasts with many of the people he met. He describes the payoff as compounding over four to five years, showing up in his ability to raise capital and get into certain rooms.
- His core point: "finding a vehicle to connect you with people in a deeper way is highly underrated."

## Why an interview podcast is a growth hack

- Casey calls starting an interview show "such a growth hack to building a network."
- He lists three benefits:
  1. You learn from the people you interview.
  2. You build a network.
  3. You build content and a personal brand you can use your own way.
- He sees podcasting continuing to get better as more people recognize this.

## How he attracted high-quality guests

- It didn't start with big names. He started in his bedroom with Apple headphones and a quick two-minute intro, posted right away to build momentum.
- He set a goal of 60 interviews in 90 days, which forced him to learn his interview style and sharpen his skills.
- Step one: his first 20 to 30 guests were people he had already met at events or had talked to briefly in Instagram DMs. Saying yes was easier for them.
- Step two: use that base of interviews to reach out to others: "I have a show, I've done XYZ type of interviews, would love to have you on."
- Most of his guests came from social DMs, mainly Instagram and Twitter, sometimes LinkedIn.
- He advises never being afraid to reach out and follow up, and asking in a polite, unique way. He talks about finding "that unique third door" that gets a person to say yes.
- He believes genuine passion and curiosity naturally pull bigger guests over time.
- His 400+ interviews include the founder of Twitch (Justin Kan), the founder of Netflix (Marc Randolph), Elon Musk's mom and sister, and Larry King.

## His interview preparation process

- He now goes in without written questions and focuses on listening and curiosity. He researches the guest and watches past interviews, then jots down ideas.
- He wants it to feel like "a coffee shop with someone getting to know them for the first time": background, current work, struggles, challenges, what excites them.
- He tells new podcasters to test both extremes, an interview with 50 questions written down and one with none, and then pick whatever feels most comfortable.
- He credits Larry King's quote: "I've never learned anything while I was talking."

## Building lasting relationships with guests and influencers

- Relationships are built, which takes continual effort: showing up to events they invite you to, answering texts and calls, and small gestures like sending a green juice to someone who posted they were sick.
- His view: "the little things in any relationship are the big things."
- Example: he met mentor Aristotle after interviewing the CEO of Cameo (Steven Galanis). He had Aristotle on the podcast, and they have rarely gone more than a few days without talking since 2020.
- He says relationships also depend on timing. People who aren't close now may be closer in a couple of years.
- His summary: put in the effort and time, and show up when people present an opportunity.

## Advice for people in their 20s

- Casey's main advice is "just get started." He started at 16 with no background in business and no relationships, relying on work ethic and drive.
- He says not to let opportunities pass without taking action, and to learn by trial and error what to say yes and no to.
- He sees ages 18 to 25 as a special window to learn, meet people and have experiences with youthful energy.
- He writes "keep going" in his journal every day, because reaching a milestone just reveals the next one, and he says the value is in the journey, not the destination.

## Media Kits context

- Media Kits is a platform for creators to build a media kit with real-time data and analytics. It launched at the tail end of 2021.
- It was acquired by Viral Nation, announced about four weeks before this interview. Casey continues to build the product inside Viral Nation.
