## Origin of the Listener name and the podcast-first career

- Casey started a marketing agency at 16 after diving into business biographies, marketing and entrepreneurship during a six-month recovery from a sports injury.
- He launched The Casey Adams Show at 17, in December 2017. It is a business interview show with 450+ episodes and guests including Tyler Winklevoss, Rick Ross, Larry King, Netflix co-founder Marc Randolph and Whoop CEO Will Ahmed.
- His interview with Larry King in 2019 (Casey was 19, King was 87) shaped his core principle. King said he'd never learned anything while he was talking.
- Casey's takeaway: being a better listener is how you make "not only a great interview, but a great life and great relationships."
- That idea became the brand: "everything in the world of podcasting goes back to the listener." Every piece of content, episode and sponsoring advertiser is listener-focused, so "listener" is a powerful word in podcasting.

## Using a premium domain to launch MediaKits

- Casey co-founded MediaKits, a startup that helps influencers design media kits from real-time social data. It was his first tech company.
- It got traction after YouTubers such as JR Garage used and recommended it.
- Owning MediaKits.com gave him "so much conviction, not only around the idea, but as the industry leading media kit tool for creators." His reasoning: with that domain, "there's no other place to go."
- He says the domain was "such an edge to first impressions, brand building awareness, and that initial growth and trust."
- Result: 25,000 creators signed up within 6 months, and he and his co-founder sold the company to social media marketing agency Viral Nation in mid-2022.

## How Listener is structured

- Casey built Listener as a vertically integrated podcast network aimed at the pain points of podcasting, with three arms:
  - A tech platform for AI-driven podcast post-production (Listener.fm).
  - A network to elevate and distribute podcasts (Listener.com).
  - A premier podcast booking service that matches guests with hosts.
- He began working on Listener.fm right after selling MediaKits (the first domain inquiry was December 2022).
- The Casey Adams Show and other shows, including "Questions," are distributed by the Listener.com network, which is how he seeds the network with his own audience and assets.
- He launched the network about a month after acquiring Listener.com and got significant attention across social media.

## How he bought Listener.com (slow, low-pressure negotiation)

- He searched for his ideal domain, found Listener.com for sale via a GoDaddy search, and inquired in December 2022.
- He treated it as exploration: "I wanted to learn more. I wanted to understand the cost and just go from there. It wasn't like this urgent need."
- He kept the conversation going "slowly but surely" with an Afternic broker for roughly a year before closing. He says the actual negotiation at the end was swift.
- The trigger to buy was brand growth. After building the tech product on Listener.fm, he concluded he needed the .com: "there was no other option but to go do the same thing again" as with MediaKits.com.
- It was his first time using a broker, and he valued having one for the process and for answering questions.
- His expected payoff: "long tail effect of brand building." Owning the category word means anyone in podcasting who thinks or says "listener" thinks of Listener.com.
- He places heavy emphasis on valuable domains, trust and brand value, especially for first impressions on social media and when his podcast is presented under Listener.com.

## Digital assets and brand names as a startup priority

- Casey interviewed the founder of Plunge about buying Plunge.com for $250,000, and Acquire.com founder Andrew Gazdecki about securing Acquire.com through a broker, so he has studied domain acquisition closely.
- He holds premium social handles for his brands: @Questions, @Listener and @Casey on Instagram.
- His advice to founders before starting a business:
  - Check whether you can get the social handles on all platforms.
  - Check whether the preferred domain is available for sale.
- On intellectual property: "If you don't value the intellectual property of whatever you're building today, you're going to have to value it later if you're ever going to have to go through an acquisition or take a company public."
- He says a premium domain won't make or break the business, but you can prioritize getting it now or later, and the key is to "just start a conversation, like I did twelve months ago with Listener.com."
