## Launching 8 products in 3 months: what actually happened

Alex returned after finishing his books and ran a rapid series of launches. His own verdict on the first three months: "Not good. I'll be honest with you." The pattern repeated what he lived through in his first run of building products, and he treats the repetition as a relearning of old lessons.

## Content lessons: human posts, originality, and the format he can sustain

- Week 1: posts about trying out Codex and his laptop did ok. A comeback video filmed on his sofa also did ok. Lesson: "Human posts do really well."
- Week 2: a second sofa video, with a novel idea, a funny intro and hook, shot on a camcorder in retro style, went viral with 100k views and created a trend.
- Lesson: videos that are "a tiny bit different" do really well. He calls it "The minimum effective dose of originality." Not a crazy degree, just a little.
- Week 3: a visit from his sister, then a week in Greece, derailed him from a roll.
- Realization: he needs a video format he can always do, even when not feeling like performing and when traveling. "I am not a natural YouTuber." He wants a podcast with other people, on a fixed day every week.
- Best hook found for his Twitter: "Building a $1M business for the second time." He says it is basic storytelling and more exciting than simply launching.

## Launch 1: Agency Siblings (community idea, priced too high)

- A community-style idea, "very hard to pull off." It had traffic and some traction but quickly died off.
- He thinks the premium tier at $1,500/month was too expensive.
- He was working backwards from getting back to $500k/year. His correction: "scaling when you are at $0 is getting to $1. Not $500k." He has written about this before and is relearning it.
- Communities are not easy, and starting with community-style ideas mirrors his first run.

## Launches 2 and 3: the podcast and AI Employees

- Podcast: recorded with his friend Dmytro, roughly 1,000 views. He doesn't care about the numbers; he enjoys it, and believes that if he does it in the format he envisions and stays consistent, it will do amazingly well.
- AI Employees: very high hopes, months of building, zero customers. It reminds him of his first book-one era.
- Lesson: "stop wasting time, launch just landing pages and figure things out if you get paid."

## Launches 4 to 6: trend-jacking, a fast build that paid, and a polemical tool

- Launch 4 (Jev trend): he built a small product meant to ride a trend that took over the internet for a couple of days. It produced nothing. It keeps momentum but feels like the throwaway products he built before.
- Launch 5 (Business Wiki Page): his first dollars, actually $80, from a project built in 24 hours. It was also his 5th launch, like GitGardener, which also made its first dollar on launch five. Contrast he draws: months on AI Employees went nowhere, while 24 hours on businesswikipage made money. "$75 from a new project means so much more than $150/month from a zombie subscription on CyberLeads."
- Lesson: "Don't overthink it."
- Launch 6 (Growth Projections): a tool where you connect Stripe and get a projected MRR plateau. He set very low expectations and wanted to clear it from his TODO list. He calls it a 2016-style SaaS, not 2026.
- He tried a polemical angle ("stop measuring useless metrics like churn"), got snarky comments, and concluded being polemical is not his style.
- Two very successful people he looks up to gave constructive but brutal criticism, "like cold water thrown to my face": "I can't just launch shit and expect to find 'the one'."

## Launch 7: CyberLeads 2.0 and moving on

- He returned to his "first love", CyberLeads, launching a new AI version. His thesis: companies make millions automating outbound with AI agents, and he could do the same while focusing on agencies, in a blue ocean.
- He prepared everything and launched. Result: "Nothing. Crickets."
- His reaction is mixed: scary, but also relief. He no longer wants to work on CyberLeads.
- Launch 8 was due the next day (Oct 16, 2026).

## Overarching lessons on launching

- Move fast.
- Don't overthink it.
- But also don't throw completely random things at the wall.
- Find that balance.
- Put in more effort with your launches.
