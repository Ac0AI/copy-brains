## Alex's growth pattern: shotgun, then sniper

Alex describes his five-year path as a repeating loop:

- Shotgun many experiments until a successful product appears.
- Sniper on that product until it plateaus.
- Shotgun again to find the next lever of growth.
- Sniper on that lever until it plateaus, and repeat.

He assumes a hit rate of around 5%:

- It took 20 product launches to find CyberLeads.
- It took 20 experiments within CyberLeads to find Twitter as a channel.
- It took 20 experiments within CyberLeads to find the productized service.

When looking for the CyberLeads idea, he went channel and market first, and picked an idea that already existed and was validated. He adds: "there are no recipes", this is what worked for him and others must find their own way.

## Alex's five-year trajectory: alternating stuck years and breakthrough years

Alex recaps his path as success and failure in alternating years:

- Year 1 (university): spent a year building City Vibes and learned to code.
- Year 2: spent 12 months perfecting City Vibes before getting a first user. It made $0.
- Year 3: after discovering bootstrapping, built and launched many products fast. Made his first dollar and reached $100/month across 3 profitable products.
- Year 4: spent a full year trying to grow his favorite of those 3 products with every tactic, then gave up, took a full-time job and started from scratch.
- Year 5: launched products fast again and found CyberLeads. Launched with a landing page built over a weekend, hit $300/month, then $2k/month, and quit his job.
- Year 6: tried to grow CyberLeads with SEO and failed. Just before year end he launched a new offer with a single email to his email list and instantly reached $100k/year.
- Next: run the productized service and maybe reach $250k/year with CyberLeads.

He notices progress comes in roughly 2-year blocks, stuck one year and fast growth the next.

## Speed and price compounded together

Alex shows that both his launch speed and his prices rose in 2-year steps:

- Speed: a product built over 2 years made $0. Two years later a product built and launched in 1 week made his first dollar. Two years after that, a landing page got to $300/month. Two years after that, an email launched a new offer that hit $100k/year.
- Price: with a free product, 4 new customers meant an extra $0/yr. With a $5/mo product, 4 customers meant an extra $240/year. With a $100/mo product, 4 customers meant roughly $5k/year. With a $2k/mo product, 4 customers means an extra $100k/year.

Takeaway: higher price points make each customer worth far more, so he needs far fewer sales to move revenue.

## Commit to the home run instead of chasing a second one

After CyberLeads worked, Alex first imagined running multiple similar products at once, assuming a "Midas touch". A few failed launches ended that idea, and he dedicated his full attention to CyberLeads.

His baseball analogy: 19 swings missed, the 20th (CyberLeads) sent the ball high in the air. Instead of running, he got cocky and sat waiting to hit another perfect one. He snapped out of it and started running.

His views:

- He does not care whether success was luck or skill, or whether he could repeat it.
- He is fine being a "one trick pony" and running the same business for decades, with nothing to prove to others or himself.
- A home run is not the best possible outcome. CyberLeads is, in his words, the most boring product he has built, but it works, is growing, and should keep growing for years.
- His goals are simple: make a lot of money, save a lot, have time, and live well.

## Choosing where to base the company: simple beats clever

Alex left Italy despite a 90% tax exemption:

- The exemption covered only one tax among many.
- Language barrier and bureaucracy were costly, for example Italian documents to sign and thousands of euros due by Monday.
- He would still pay almost half his money in taxes.
- He was legally required to stay 6 months a year.

How he compared setups:

- He read books, spoke to accountants and paid consultants.
- Most recommended multi-country structures, holding companies and flying in and out of countries.
- He ignored individual tax rates and benefits. His only metric was how much money ends up in his pocket at the end of the year at $100k, $250k and $500k profit, across options like self-employed, company, and taking everything out as dividends.
- He noticed experts pushed complicated setups that happened to be part of their expensive services. Setups promising zero percent tax turned out far more expensive and riskier.
- He had already been tricked once by the Italy exemption, so he chose caution.

His conclusion: all he needed was a simple business in a low-tax country.

## Why Alex chose Cyprus, and his final setup

Cyprus did not sound clever or sophisticated, but it won in every category:

- Language: English and Greek are spoken, and he speaks Greek fluently, so he can tell an accountant, lawyer or landlord "No, this is what I want. I know how things are done here."
- EU membership means no visa hassle, and it is close to family in the UK and Greece.
- Tax: on $100,000 profit he keeps $85,000, versus about $50,000 in Italy. It is low enough that he does not need to chase zero.
- Lifestyle: safe, sunny, friendly people.
- Mobility: he only has to stay on the island 2 months a year.

Investing is equally simple: one year of cash in the bank, the rest in index funds (US market, S&P 500), and a little in crypto.

His final setup:

- Business and tax residence in Cyprus.
- Customers mostly in the US, EU and UK.
- Banks in Italy, Greece, Lithuania and Belgium.
- Broker in the Netherlands.
- Lives all over the world.

His next goal is building the new version of CyberLeads to move from thousands to tens of thousands of dollars per month.
