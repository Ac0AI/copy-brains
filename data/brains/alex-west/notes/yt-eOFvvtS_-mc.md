## Rebuilding after Cyberleads: start from what he already owns

Alex's stated plan is directional, not precise. He says he does not know exactly what he will build, and he treats that as acceptable. He frames his game plan as a short list of constraints that keep an open mind from turning into a vacuum.

- Keep an open mind, be playful and creative, and run many experiments.
- Do not fall in love with any idea or business. He says doing so "wasted years of my life" and never worked out for him.
- Set limits anyway. He asks "am I going to build something for pet stores? Or like the newsletter industry?" and concludes there must be a line.
- He prefers working from concrete ingredients: "I don't like thinking in a vacuum. I cannot even write in a vacuum. You need something to play with."

## Don't assume you have the Midas touch

Alex describes a mistake from after he first made money with Cyberleads. He thought he suddenly had "Midas touch" and tried two or three other products back to back. The open market "slapped" him and humbled him.

- His correction was to refocus on Cyberleads, show it "the love it deserves," and grow it, which he did.
- He now says he is not confident in his ability to predict what will work. He does not want to "pretend that I am smarter than I am."
- Because of this, he plans to experiment broadly rather than bet everything on one prediction.

## Reuse the audience and channel instead of starting from zero

After eight years, Alex says building a marketing channel was the hardest thing to do, measured by output rather than input.

- He tried SEO, cold email, Reddit, LinkedIn and YouTube. He wanted ten diversified channels.
- SEO and cold email did work for him, "but not like this." He never built a second channel like his X account.
- He sees room to grow on X, since he is "still a small account."
- His focus is therefore the audience he already has. He calls starting from absolute zero (no customers, credibility, users or revenue) "the dark ages" and "the hardest part by far." He would not switch places with someone there.

## Mix existing assets into new product ideas

Alex does not plan to abandon Cyberleads. It stays alive "in zombie mode," and he will use it to spin off new products. He walked through what "new" could mean:

- **New feature for Cyberleads:** tried, found nothing, though he admits he may have missed an opportunity.
- **New offer:** he tried several for Cyberleads and found nothing. His earlier hit came from discovering a product-as-a-service offer that took off.
- **New product for the same audience:** the one he finds interesting.

His ingredients for mixing and matching:

- A thousand-plus past paying customers who hopefully trust him.
- A free newsletter with about 5,000 agencies on the free plan.
- Cold email skills, which he used for many companies as his job.
- A database of 50,000 funded startups he can reach out to.
- His X account.

He says constraints plus ingredients beat brainstorming in a vacuum.

## Catch a wave, and don't ignore them

Alex describes a belief he held for about five years: that he was not like people "chasing all these waves left and right," and that he was building something lasting instead. On reflection he calls that wrong.

- He was riding waves all along: building a business on the internet, building in public, solopreneurship, hiring remotely and automating with code. Back then these were taboo or extreme.
- He cites "The future is already here. It is just unevenly distributed."
- He recalls telling his parents around 2015 to 2017 that he would build a remote business, hire people he had never met and live in hotels with only Wi-Fi, since his customers were in America. They did not understand it. Now it is mainstream and he says he has "lost my edge completely."
- While running Cyberleads he ignored the NFT, crypto and AI waves, and they did not hurt him. He drew the wrong lesson: ignore everything and stay fixed on one product. Other waves later "crushed" him (he says this is covered in book 13).
- He adds that not every wave is worth catching.

## The wave he picked: AI employees and AI sales

- The target is AI, specifically AI employees and AI sales. Sales fits because it sits near his Cyberleads universe.
- He says it is an assumption and could be wrong, but it seems "pretty obvious" the market is heading toward AI employees. He admits he does not know what that means in detail: "I know as much as you."
- His plan is to build "10 20 products within that category" rather than one.
- He frames the whole strategy as possibly wrong, but wants to get ahead of the curve again.
