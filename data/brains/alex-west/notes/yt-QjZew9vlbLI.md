## Burnout and a 5x income drop: what happened to Cyber Leads in the year after Alex's first million

- Alex's business is Cyber Leads, his performance-marketing agency. His normal good month used to be around $40k, with months fluctuating between $25k and $50k. That is roughly $500k a year in revenue at about 80% margin.
- Over the last year revenue fell hard. He says his income dropped 5x, from about $500k a year to about $100k a year.
- He names two causes. The first was personal: he had burned out and was not in a good place to run the business. The second was the industry: his market changed a lot and he could not innovate or come up with new offers while traveling.
- He quotes a friend's line that he likes: "there are no business problems, they're only personal problems." He agrees with it "to an extent".
- The cycle fed itself. The business declining made his mental state worse, and his worse mental state made the business decline faster.
- He says he kept running the business day to day while it dropped, instead of stepping back to fix the underlying problem.

## Why constant travel killed his creativity and made the work boring

- Alex had always seen himself as a chill person who handled stress by working out. He thought he was not susceptible to burnout.
- The trigger was constant travel with no organization. He recently changed 13 houses in 12 weeks, which he calls the peak of the whirlwind. He was moving through places like New York, London and Bangkok, with visas and airports along the way.
- He argues that too much stimulation makes the actual work boring. New people, cultures, languages and women every day made sitting down to write a new Cyber offer feel uninteresting. "New chapter every two weeks, new country. And then you have to sit down and come up with a new offer for cyber, like it's not going to happen."
- His fix was the opposite environment. He rented an apartment by the sea in the Mediterranean and stopped using buses and cars. After about one month in this low-stimulation setup, he says he became creative again almost immediately.
- Lesson he draws: a "controlled boring", simple, pleasant environment is what restores the drive to build. He compares it to the early days when he woke up at 7am excited to open his laptop and wrote ideas down at night so he would not forget them.

## Hitting the first million and losing his drive

- Last summer Alex made his first million from his own projects, a week before his 30th birthday. He had chased it for many years.
- The goal "make a million from my own projects before 30" was directionally correct and moved him into productive ways of acting and thinking.
- Achieving it backfired. He compares it to "a letter bomb": he opened it and "exploded into nihilism" and lost his drive. His takeaway is that you are still the same person after the milestone.
- He says this is why he stopped posting at the same time, last summer.
- He says feeling lost is permanent for builders. You feel lost now, after a plateau, or after hitting your big milestone and wondering why you are not happy. "You have to be okay with that because this is the life you have chosen." He compares it to athletes and fighters: "the highest highs and the lowest lows."

## Taking a sabbatical that is the opposite of travel

- Alex's sabbatical is not a trip. He deliberately stopped traveling and moved to one quiet place by the sea to regroup and reflect on what is next.
- He has stopped all client work. He is only running the newsletter part of the business, which does not make much money, and keeps one or two clients.
- He is fine taking a big pay cut. He says he could actually stop working for many years, but that is not what he wants.
- Why he left client work: running performance marketing campaigns for clients carries a heavy weight of responsibility. He tries to make every client their money back, and he absorbs the stress of clients who are stressing. He says martial arts and the gym could not clear that accumulated stress, and it left no room for creativity.
- He says he still loves Cyber, which he has tattooed on his arm, and that it changed his life. But he is no longer in love with it because of the stress of running campaigns for clients.
- He is considering moving into consulting and has some new ideas, but says he is "starting from scratch." He accepts that Cyber Leads could drop to almost zero and he would have to start over.

## Finding ideas and building products: luck is real, so launch many

- Asked how to get started finding ideas and building products, Alex says luck is a real factor. You need to try many times.
- Product-market fit shows up as effort. Working on something without product-market fit "will feel like a drag... like you're fighting against nature." When it is the right idea, at the right time, with distribution channels you have, "it will feel effortless. It would just go by itself."
- He is prepared to fail 20 times. He says he is ready to build 20 products if needed to find another Cyber that changes his life.
- Past success does not guarantee more. He argues that having an audience and a track record does not make it easier, and says "not everything I touch turns to gold." He plans to publicly iterate through products to show that luck applies wherever you are in your trajectory.
- His advice: embrace the role of luck and launch many ideas.

## What Alex plans next

- He wants to get back to creating: writing again, recording videos, and posting more. He says he enjoys making videos because it lets him meet many people. He picks people from the comments to have conversations with and invites DMs on Twitter.
- Goal: get back to the excitement of waking up eager to open his laptop and build something new, rather than dragging his feet.
- He plans to move into consulting and test new product ideas in public, with followers able to follow along as he iterates.
