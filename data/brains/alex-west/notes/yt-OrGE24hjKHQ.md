## Alex's channel framework: inbound, outbound, and the extras

- Alex argues distribution matters more than product: "you can have the best product in the world if no one knows about it it doesn't matter." A mediocre product with strong distribution can get rich and improve over time.
- No major new channel has appeared since TikTok (2016), and competition keeps rising, so distribution gets more important every year.
- He simplifies channels into inbound, outbound and ads, plus a few extras (launches, marketplaces, affiliates, going viral).
- His basis: close to 10 years of experience, posting daily online since 2020, and tweeting since 2018.
- He urges people to test channels themselves because "anything can work and everything has worked for other people."
- He recommends the book *Traction* (co-written by the DuckDuckGo co-founder), which covers 19 channels and how to test them. There are two books called Traction, so check the author.

## Inbound channels: social, SEO, email list, word of mouth

- **Social is underrated, and the platform depends on your customer.** B2C leans Instagram and TikTok (easy to grow, convert badly). B2B leans Twitter and LinkedIn (harder to grow, convert far better).
  - His brother grew a TikTok account to 60k followers in 3 months and could not make a single dollar from it.
  - Alex's business is built on a roughly 30,000 follower Twitter account, with many millionaires and even billionaires following him. It took two years of consistent tweeting to reach 1,000 followers.
- **Audience first, product later.** He says the channel is the most important part of the business and that many people should start with a channel: "if you build up a Twitter audience trust me you can find a product." The same goes for YouTube and podcasts, which are harder to grow but convert even better.
- **Podcast mentions can be huge.** *My First Million* mentioned his business Cyber Leads twice, once in a 5-minute segment and once in an episode on million-dollar solo businesses. He estimates they brought in around a quarter of a million dollars in revenue, counting churn, and says that shows the distribution a big show has.
- **SEO** is, in his experience, very hard to grow, slow, and low converting. He has never made it work despite many attempts, and says it should not be your first channel. Blog content lasts 2-3 years versus 2-3 days for a tweet, but the slowness balances that out.
- **Email list** is hard to grow but converts very well.
  - His free Cyber Leads newsletter has about 3,500 subscribers (at recording) against roughly 35,000 Twitter followers. He says that is 10x fewer people but roughly equal power, maybe more, especially for B2B.
  - When he needs clients, he emails the list a short offer, for example 20% off for 3 days because he wants to take on a new client, and can get two or three clients whenever he wants.
- **Word of mouth** is his number one channel now, but he doesn't take credit for it. It is outside your control beyond providing the best service and building the best product you can.

## Outbound channels: warm and cold

- **Warm outbound** means messaging friends, family, past clients, past prospects, newsletter subscribers, Twitter followers, and anyone who already knows you. Conversion is high but reach is small (about 100 people), which makes it a great way to start.
- **Cold outbound** covers cold email, cold calling, cold DMs, and sending letters and gifts.
  - Email is the 80/20 of cold outbound: the easiest and it works pretty well.
  - He has never gotten results from cold calling.
  - Cold outbound takes more time than warm outbound but less than building social or other inbound channels.
- **Recommended sequence:** start with warm outbound, add cold outbound, and build social, SEO and the rest in parallel. Start outbound and build inbound at the same time.
- **Cold email works best when selling something expensive.** At a $50/month SaaS price he doesn't think it fits. It scales linearly rather than fast.
  - Counterexample: ConvertKit grew from about $2k MRR to $200k MRR with cold email, but its founder was already well known on Twitter, so the brand amplified the emails.
  - Without a brand it is hard to stand out in cold email.

## Launches, marketplaces, affiliates and going viral

- **Launches** are an underused strategy. Alex got his first 10 Cyber Leads customers by launching on Product Hunt, plus Twitter and Reddit.
  - You can realistically launch only every 6-12 months, but it gives momentum and validation.
  - His point is that the scariest thing is growing something you aren't sure can work. Early customers give you the confidence to commit to building a channel.
- **Marketplaces** (GitHub, Figma, Slack, the OpenAI GPT marketplace) are a hack for acquisition when you have no audience. "It's like casting your little net on a river full of fish." The risks are competition and long-term platform dependence, so he wouldn't rely on a single platform after four years in business.
- **Affiliates:** he never made them worth it. Paying affiliates was a pain, and he killed the program twice. The one useful result was learning from ads that an affiliate ran.
- **Going viral** is very hard to pull off. His examples:
  - Harry Dry's Kanye West dating site where Taylor Swift fans were banned.
  - Peter Levels' billboard saying you don't need to go back to the office, promoting remoteok.io.
- **Ads:** he skips them because they are capital intensive. He prefers high-profit businesses with organic acquisition, and says he hasn't run ads successfully (or at all).

## Channels compound rather than compete

- Alex stresses that channels are not mutually exclusive and feed each other.
- The loop: warm outbound gets your first customers, who give you testimonials and social proof, which build your brand and grow social, which brings more inbound.
- Cross-effects he sees:
  - People reply to his cold emails saying they know him from Twitter or *My First Million*.
  - Cold email brings people into his email list.
  - The email list becomes warm outbound.
  - Word of mouth keeps growing on top of all of it.
- Different channels peak at different times.

## Alex's tier list

- **A tier:** YouTube and podcast (deep trust, parasocial relationship, influence at scale, the best way to build a brand), email list, and word of mouth.
- **B tier:** Twitter/LinkedIn (very short half-life, a treadmill of content), SEO (slow but long-lived), and launches.
- **C tier:** cold outbound (works quickly but doesn't scale fast, and only fits expensive offers) and marketplaces (amazing but carry platform risk).
- **D tier:** Instagram, TikTok and ads. They can work but are capital intensive or convert poorly. He sees Instagram and TikTok mainly as brand amplifiers for other channels ("I know you from TikTok").
- **F tier:** cold calling, sending presents, trying to go viral, and affiliates. He has never made these matter.

## Alex's own growth path and channel timeline

- **First customers:** he reached about $400 MRR by launching on Product Hunt, Twitter and Reddit. That was about 10 customers at $29/month (about $290 MRR) plus roughly $100 from other products.
- **Hundreds to thousands per month:** Twitter growth took him from hundreds to thousands of dollars per month, which he calls the biggest gap, going from nothing to being able to live doing what he loves.
- **Stuck at about $4k/month,** and bored, he created a service offering priced at $2,000 per month rather than $200 (the transcript is ambiguous on the exact figure, so treat this as only a higher-priced offer). That made cold email efficient.
- **Reaching $40k/month:** cold email combined with Twitter, the email list, and word of mouth took him from $4,000 to $40,000 per month. His goal now is $1 million, and he thinks YouTube, podcasting and maybe SEO could help.
- **Timeline summary:**
  - Short term: launches, marketplaces, and warm outbound.
  - Mid term (around 3 months for cold outbound to work): cold outbound and social.
  - Long term: email list, word of mouth, SEO, YouTube and podcast, which scale highest.
