## Ruben's LinkedIn thesis: win by solving one problem, consistently

- Ruben reached 650,000 followers on LinkedIn. His blog on getting the first 10,000 is framed as how he would start over.
- 10,000 followers is not "being successful". Doing it the right way, and continuing to do it, is what makes you successful.
- Wrong ways he rejects:
  - Cold email automations.
  - Joining pods to cheat the system.
  - Spreading AI comments "like the plague".
  - Copy-pasting what another successful person did.
  - LinkedIn AI agents pretending to connect with leads.
  - Posting at an exact "right time" because a blog said so.
- The right way is writing good posts. Good means targeted, helpful, consistent, and getting better over time.

## Find one mission: one problem only

- Ruben says most people's LinkedIn topics are too broad. Half the posts resonate with the community and the other half don't, so focus is needed.
- Rule: solve one problem, and one only. His own: "How to AI with English, not code." He does not post about robots, coding or politics.
- Test: if you can't explain your mission in one sentence, you need help.
- Examples of one-clear-mission creators he likes:
  - Justin Welsh: run a solopreneur business without ruining your life.
  - Codie Sanchez: own a small, profitable business.
  - Rory Flynn: AI for images and videos.
  - Dan Go: why to get and stay fit.
  - Reno Perry: land better jobs.
- He built a free "Mission-GPT" in ChatGPT to help people define their mission.

## Profile setup, element by element

- Colors and fonts: he made a free "Color Theory" GPT to pick them.
- Profile picture: HD 1080 x 1080, super-zoomed, smiling, clear background. AI image tool Flux (not affiliated) is an option.
- Banner: HD 1584 x 396 px that says one thing. A slideshow banner is available with paid LinkedIn and surprises people.
- Headline: the mission with a twist, cheeky and clear.
  - Mission: "How to AI with English, not code."
  - Headline: "Master AI before it masters you."
- Featured section: one link, big text, no description.
  - No description means one click goes straight to the website. "Less clicks = More conversions."
  - Don't feature your best posts; he finds it weird.
- About section: he deliberately has none, so visitors reach the featured section faster.
- Experience section: put "who you are" here and write a lot, extensively. When people ask ChatGPT or Perplexity "Who is Ruben Hassid?", the answer mostly comes from his LinkedIn experience tab.
- LinkedIn Premium: gives the special link under your name when posting. "More links visible = More clicks = More conversions."

## Post formula: Mission + Solution + Angle + Format

- Mission is the one problem. A post is one of many solutions to it.
- A post needs the right solution, the right angle on that solution, and the right format for that angle.
- Worked example for a finance creator:
  - Mission: make investing understandable for rookies.
  - Solution: what is an ETF?
  - Angle: explain ETFs like grocery shopping.
  - Format: infographic.
- Workflow:
  1. Research with Perplexity (select "Research"). Prompt: "Act like an experienced financial educator; explain ETFs through grocery-shopping analogies, contrast them with stocks & mutual funds."
  2. Design: search Pinterest for "ETF infographic" or "Finance infographic", copy and improve it, use Canva, or pay a cheap designer.
  3. Caption: paste the research as "Topic" into EasyGen.io.
- EasyGen bonus for repurposing viral posts: Trending tab, find the highest "Outlier Index" (most viral), click "Repurpose", add a new topic, get a new viral post. He notes few people will do it, and those who do win more.
