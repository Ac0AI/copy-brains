## Simplified English as the fix for AI "slop" writing

- Ruben says people hate AI's default writing style: "It's not [X], it's [Y]", meta commentary, exaggeration, "It's the expression of something", and "that is important" filler.
- He claims a 2-word prompt fixes this for about 80% of chats: "Use ASD-STE100."
- Tested on Claude, he prefers the ASD-STE100 output in every side-by-side comparison. He asked for a calendar, then for more information, and in each case found the STE answer "clearer" and more "direct", describing it as "an organized, desloped Claude".
- He says the trick works on any AI: ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and coding platforms like Cursor, OpenClaw and Hermes.

## What ASD-STE100 is and why it works on AI

- ASD-STE100 is simplified English for instructions. It is an international standard for writing clear, simple, safe technical manuals, and is very well known in aerospace.
- Ruben's reasoning: models already know the standard well enough to reply in simplified English when asked to follow it, so the name alone acts as a compact style instruction.
- He treats it as a shortcut: one standard's name replaces a long list of "don't write like AI" rules.

## The /ste Claude skill

- Ruben built a more rigorous version: a Claude skill that encodes the actual ASD-STE100 rules so Claude "truly" talks like STE instructions.
- He built it with Claude's top model plus Research mode (he names it "Fable-5-High").
- It is a free download as a zip file. Install steps: open Claude, go to "Customize", then "Skills", then "Upload a skill", and drop the zip. It also works on ChatGPT.
- Invoke it with the command "/ste".

## When not to use ASD-STE100

- Ruben says do not use the prompt or skill all the time. It "falls short on some tasks".
- Creative writing: it is "really bad", for example a poem that is not fun to read.
- LinkedIn posts: "too cold. Almost German."
- Best fit: instructions, explanations, plans and informational answers where clarity and directness matter more than voice.

## Ruben's pre-writing research workflow (partly paywalled)

- He pairs a copy-and-paste research prompt, run on Claude or ChatGPT before any piece of text, with screen recordings of himself writing.
- The recordings show him writing a newsletter and the matching LinkedIn post live.
- His stated goal: never sounding like an AI, "even when you didn't intend to".
- He cites his own reach as context: newsletter of 885,000 readers and 900,000 LinkedIn followers. (Elsewhere in the same essay he mentions 800,000 people; the figures are not reconciled in the free text.)

## Distribution mechanics visible in the essay

- He gives away the core trick free in the opening ("You can stop the newsletter here") and asks for 10 more minutes for the skill and workflow, using a free value-first hook.
- He puts a free download (the skill) in the middle and gates the deeper workflow for paid subscribers.
- He asks readers to share: "This newsletter is free because people like you share it to people they love."
- He ends a skill section with a hiring line: "And yes, I am hiring."
