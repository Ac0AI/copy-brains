## Ruben's path: from music labels to AI content

- Ruben started writing video game guides at age 9 (Guild Wars), obsessively testing combinations and logging damage numbers on sticky notes. He calls this "theory crafting": the urge to find the optimal way to do something.
- Writing for English-speaking forums taught him English, helped by Arctic Monkeys lyrics and Louis CK stand-up.
- At 19 he ran a techno label (with Axel) in business school, finding artists on SoundCloud. Their second album reached 1 million views on YouTube and SoundCloud with no ads.
- His diagnosis of those artists: they had zero marketing and believed the music alone would win. He compares this to founders who think the best product sells itself.
- Around 2022 he lost his drive for the music scene: he found Berlin techno culture afraid of success ("succeed, but not too much"). He quit.
- He had earlier lost about €10,000, all he had, on a clothing brand called Matin (Covid, unsold t-shirts). He says he would not change it, because it led him to where he is.
- He took a social media job at Trade Republic, was told he was good by his bosses, then ghostwrote for Nicolas Cheron for 6 months before his own LinkedIn took off.

## How a single post launched the business (December 2022)

- When ChatGPT launched (30 Nov 2022), he went down the prompt engineering rabbit hole of academic papers and distilled them into plain terms: chain of thought (break a task into steps), zero/one/few shot (give examples of what you want).
- He posted his findings on LinkedIn on 7 December 2022: about 2 million views, roughly 10,000 new followers at once, hundreds of messages.
- Everyone told him to launch a course or academy. He refused, fearing the business would stop him creating good content.
- Instead he put every comment and DM request into a spreadsheet and published free content daily on whatever was asked most.
- He describes his role as writing "IKEA instructions" for AI tools: build the piece of furniture first, then write steps anyone can follow.

## Monetization: brand partnerships and distribution

- Revenue is "between 3 and 4 million" a year, with a media team of three (Ruben, Anicha, Axel).
- Payers are AI companies (Gamma, Notion, Anthropic). Partnerships are public and disclosed.
- Deal structure he prefers: a guaranteed minimum number of views; if he misses it, he commits to publish more posts. Rates are by post, month, year or views.
- Control: he decides how to cover the tool and sponsors do not review his scripts.
- Integrity rule: he won't promote a tool he thinks is worse than an alternative. He turns down 5,000 to 20,000 euro offers when the product is poor, because he plans to write for decades.
- Gamma example: he reaches about 2 to 3 million people a month for them and says he is their biggest creator and, he believes, their main acquisition channel. He has worked with them for over 2 years.
- Pitch to sponsors: he'd first ask whether LinkedIn is a major channel for them and whether LinkedIn users have high lifetime value. If both are true, he is the right person.
- Rough pricing: a post reaching 500k views at 10,000 dollars is about a 20-dollar CPM. Tens of thousands of dollars per deal; for small startups he'd consider equity.
- Side benefit: viral sponsored posts help the company when raising VC money.

## Anthropic case: pitching a better angle

- Anthropic wanted a Claude Code push. He tested it, judged it fit engineers rather than his non-technical audience, and proposed instead Claude's custom writing style feature (upload your posts so Claude learns your style, one per client).
- Sponsor's contact agreed on his condition: he'd make a PDF carousel, and if it flopped he'd make a video for free.
- The PDF got 500,000 views.
- Lesson he draws: the best partnerships happen when the sponsor trusts you to test and own the angle, and you commit to correcting course if it fails.

## Format and platform tactics

- He used a Synthesia avatar, an ElevenLabs voice and a Russian editing team (using B-roll) for LinkedIn vertical video. Axel wrote scripts and posted. About 60 million views in a few months.
- He stopped because of what he calls "fast marketing": when a platform pushes a new feature (LinkedIn vertical video, autumn 2024) it over-promotes it, then reach fades. His rule: spot the new feature early, exploit it, move on.
- He posts three times a day on LinkedIn, and not everything works. He also runs a newsletter read by about 130,000 people every Sunday.
- He doesn't do TikTok, Reels or Shorts: it needs a different style and he lacks the appetite for now.
- He does few conferences (down from three or four a year to about one) because travel and prep cut into writing time.

## Handling audience, haters and scale

- He can't reply to hundreds of daily messages and says so honestly; automated replies to people in distress would be wrong.
- Educational creators must accept that most followers won't apply the advice (fitness analogy).
- Haters: seeing negative comments on creators he admires convinced him it comes with success; people try to climb on an expert's back. He told his team that hate means you are doing it right.
- Content creation means going on stage "naked" daily, since everything is public.
- Public speaking: he worked with coach Noémie, including a poetry-slam cafe night in Paris, where he trembled on stage.

## His views on AI and work

- Career advice: don't chase "find your passion". Find what you're good at and get recognized for it; passion often follows. Keep hobbies as hobbies.
- AI is the average: it sits at the middle of the curve, so humans keep an edge through taste, direction and curation. He gives the DJ or orchestra-conductor analogy.
- Example: Nick St. Pierre generates huge numbers of Midjourney images (even auto-prompting from podcast transcripts every five words), then curates; his skill is selection, done for brands like Gucci.
- Most knowledge work is searching for information and rewriting it (slides, text, Slack, Gmail), which AI will heavily change even without AGI.
- Education: the thesis demo. His ex needed a 20-page thesis with 15+ academic sources. He used Consensus for literature gaps, downloaded the papers, had ChatGPT build the outline and citations. It took about 40 minutes and scored 92/100. His conclusion: schools should shift toward oral defence, with AI use allowed.
- Predictions: people will pay for human experiences (retreats, in-person), and he expects more deepfake-driven distrust. He doubts AI has consciousness and favours demystifying LLMs as matrix math and probabilities.
- Tools he rates: Nano Banana in Gemini (edits the existing image instead of regenerating it). He finds vibe coding promising but overvalued.
