## How Ruben started and why he gives content away

- Ruben found ChatGPT in early November 2022, when it had been out for a week or two. He was a ghostwriter for personal brands at a co-working space in Berlin.
- He began with jokes about a coworker's haircut, then poems in Spanish, Shakespeare style and rhymed forms. He kept adding detail to his prompts.
- He waited until early January 2023 to post a quick how-to guide. It got "more than a million impression[s]". He says the timing was right, and a November post would have gone unseen.
- He treats a personal brand like a startup and has "two hammers, one for monetization and one for growth". He hit growth first: "growth at all costs, everything is free."
- He sold a guide for $1, with pay-what-you-want tips; he says some people paid $50 or $100. It was a 1,000-plus prompt library and his carousels.
- Premium courses and conferences come later. He wants to "democratize AI" and be "the bridge between people and AI".

## Treat the AI as a team and prompt in layers

- He calls a prompt "how human talks to the machine". He says to think of generative AI as a team of creatives, not one genie. Talk to it like a manager.
- His "Good, Bad and Ugly" prompt course has three levels:
  - **Ugly:** "Write a podcast script between Jason and Ruben around AI." There is no context, length or voice, so the team cannot be creative.
  - **Bad:** he adds roles, such as Ruben being an obsessed content writer.
  - **Good:** he describes each speaker's age, tone and vocabulary, then asks for a table of contents first.
- His order is title, then table of contents, then each part. He mirrors how humans work: pick the topic, then the big parts, then the subparts, then write.
- He gives feedback like a boss: "the first part is good, the second part should lean more toward Ruben being French", then "write the first part", then "make it better".
- "You don't prompt once. You prompt with layers of prompts."
- He compares himself to a grumpy VP on the couch. He edits and picks the best ideas from his "team" and doesn't do all the work.

## Give it your data; don't ask it for opinions

- He says the biggest misconception is treating GPT like Google. GPT is best at generative work: "give it data and it gives back something really smart", like a summary, an outline or a strategy. It is not for asking opinions or judgments.
- "GPT is one hell of a liar." It is built to answer and not to say "I don't know". If you ask for sources, it invents plausible titles.
- His fix is to supply the sources, abstracts or full articles yourself and have it build from them. He does not use it as a fact-fetcher.
- He expects the future to be feeding it large private documents, such as a 100k-word PDF, and asking it to find better ways to work or to do the work. He has spoken to lawyers about a PDF summarizer for long documents.
- He was in talks with a Swiss healthcare company whose sales reps mostly do cold emails, cold outreach and articles. He knew nothing about healthcare. He sent sample outputs and the CEO said it was solid. Ruben's view: "one guy could make the job of 15 or 20."

## Content repurposing workflow

His most viral post, as he describes it, turned a YouTube video into many formats:

1. Pull the YouTube transcript with a tool (he used a Huberman Lab podcast, a neuroscience podcast that runs two to four hours).
2. Feed it to ChatGPT in chunks and say what the discussion is about.
3. Ask for several outputs: an SEO article, public-speaking notes, a cheat sheet, a thread, a LinkedIn post and a cold email.

- **Twitter thread hack:** he doesn't use Twitter much. He likes one big article summarized into 10 to 15 tweets of 280 characters. They are snappy, digestible chunks he can reuse as LinkedIn carousels.
- **Carousel pipeline:**
  - GPT writes about 10 slides of text and produces Midjourney prompts for the visuals.
  - Midjourney renders the images.
  - His example was an ad for natural aromatic oil for baby massage, from just the words "baby skin care".
  - GPT had proposed 10 ad ideas and he chose one.
  - He could not get text rendered inside images at the time.
  - He still builds the final million-impression carousels by hand.

## Using it for sales and outreach

- Build a personal brand first; "your life's going to be much easier."
- Ideate with the AI, giving it a real brief. His example was selling a Shure SM7B microphone:
  - He would not trust GPT to know the specs, so he feeds it the qualities and benefits.
  - Ask for article ideas, SEO keywords and email subjects.
  - Then work from the top: title, table of contents, then content.
- Then spend about 30 more minutes qualifying the output: "don't just copy paste." The goal is not to be any sales rep but "the best sales rep".
- He demoed this live for an insurance audience on GPT-4:
  - He prompted "act like an SEO copywriter expert" for 10 completely different article titles on auto and home insurance, with meta keywords.
  - He asked for fear-based angles, since fear drives opens in emails.
  - He picked one title, "The Ticking Time Bomb", then asked for an SEO table of contents.
  - He then asked for the first two parts, with an invented story as an example.
  - Finally he asked for the story to be more dramatic, detailed, with precise numbers and dates.
- Of 10 outputs there are usually only one or two "super good" ones, and the rest get discarded. He keeps those and keeps prompting.
- He made his LinkedIn headline "Master AI before it Masters you" this way. About 85% of the headline came from ChatGPT:
  - He ran 10 to 15 prompts, asked for 10 to 15 headlines each time, and gave detail about himself.
  - He kept asking it to be "more provocative".
  - He chose one from about 90 options and edited a single word, "rules" to "Masters".

## Productivity and jobs

- His historical frame is the steam engine and the tractor. In his telling, about 70% of people once worked on food and now it is about 1%. The freed people did other work.
- His view is that AI makes people "superhuman", not unemployed.
- He argues against laziness: "It's not the time to be lazier, it's the time to do 10 times more." The average gets higher, and the person who prompts deeper and edits still stands out.
- He says humans stay as editors who add the "touch" and "spice", and he doesn't expect to work less.

## Tooling and ecosystem views

- He pointed to the share of Y Combinator batches that are generative-AI startups as a sign of speed. He said it was about 10% in one batch and "more than 40%" in the latest.
- He expects a wave of new tools from GPT-4 and Midjourney, Stable Diffusion and Leonardo AI, plus Google's Bard.
- He warns that voice cloning needs only 3 to 5 minutes of audio, which makes impersonation scams likely.
- He says he had calls with Microsoft partners and OpenAI, and quoted one: "we are the good Jedi, how do we push the force to the positive?"
