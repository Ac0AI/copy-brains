## How Ruben thinks about AI, jobs and scaling a team

- Ruben argues AI creates more jobs than it replaces, citing Aaron Levie (CEO of Box): "We need **more** humans, so we can use AI **more**."
- He frames this with Jevons Paradox (1865): efficient steam engines led to more coal use, and more efficient cars led to more fuel consumed overall.
- Cheaper cognition means companies run more projects, experiments, personalization and long-tail ideas that were previously too expensive. Result: more white-collar work, innovation and growth.
- The mindset shift he demands: stop asking "How to replace 5 members of my team with AI?" and ask "How can 5 members of my team do the work of 50 with AI?"
- He says it starts with duplicating yourself.

## How Ruben duplicated himself, then hired

- He has been obsessed with AI since November 2022, with the goal of doing better and faster instead of hiring.
- Limit he hit: even if AI makes him 10x more efficient, he still works about 8 true hours per day, so he can't have 10x more hours. He hired people to use AI for him.
- His stack:
  - ChatGPT to analyze his content (upload best and worst LinkedIn posts, get a diagnosis, "Winner DNA" checklist, next-post concepts and repurpose angles).
  - Grok for search; he says it is slowly replacing all his LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude).
  - Gamma for carousels and decks.
  - Wispr Flow to write as he talks.
  - Prompt Maker to create prompts quickly.
  - Gemini for images, Opus Clip to edit videos, Claude to write.
- Team: Anisha, Axelle, Zane, Rica and three different agencies, running "How to AI" (newsletter, content, support). He partnered with Pete on consulting, helping Fortune 500 businesses in the US actually use AI.
- Without AI he says he'd still be solo, or two people at most. With AI the team handles 100x more: client workshops, curated content, case studies, research. "Efficiency created massive new demand."

## Start with one AI win today (research example)

- Wrong: wait for "perfect" AI. Right: 10x one real task now.
- His example is research, a common bottleneck:
  1. Pick research.
  2. Go to Grok (or Claude/Gemini); he prefers Grok.
  3. Paste a prompt: act as a world-class researcher in front of a [audience]; summarize the latest 2025-2026 trends on [topic] in bullets with key stats, 3 emerging tools, 2 risks, 1 opportunity for consultants; sources only from reputable sites (McKinsey, Gartner, a16z).
  4. Run it until the result matches your usual research.
  5. Teach someone to run it daily and decide what to do with it.

## How to teach someone to use AI for you

- Example workflow for his team: pull the 300 latest cs.AI papers from the arXiv API (sorted by submission date), paste the whole list into Grok, and run a prompt that picks exactly one paper most likely to go viral with non-technical audiences.
- The prompt ranks by: catchy or provocative title, surprising or myth-busting insight about LLMs, explainable by simple analogy, timely or emotionally resonant, little technical detail. Output: title and arXiv ID, a one-sentence viral hook, and 3 bullets on why.
- His briefing process:
  1. Record a Loom or Screen Studio video of himself doing the task, which the team can revisit anytime.
  2. Write a doc (Google Doc, Notion) with the standard procedure (SOP).
  3. Use a prompt to build the SOP faster: act as a senior SOP architect and AI enablement lead; phase 1 asks 15-20 questions in 5 sections (company and role, AI usage scope and forbidden tasks, tools and access, quality and workflow, safety and policy); phase 2 produces a 1-page summary, a detailed step-by-step SOP and a first-week training plan.
- He believes adoption must be top-down: C-levels first, which is how he consults businesses.

## Hire for psychology, not technical credentials

- Wrong: look for "AI experts" with degrees or certifications. Right: hire people who thrive in chaos.
- Traits he wants: humility to admit AI is sometimes better (or really bad), resilience to iterate 10x a day, fast learning and pivoting, zero tolerance for average output, and taste to steer AI toward excellence.
- His psychology framing: high "openness to experience" (Big Five) plus a Jungian "individuation" mindset, where people confront their shadow (ego, fear of obsolescence) and integrate AI as a tool.
- MBTI: Intuitive-Perceiving types (ENTP, INTP, ENFP, INFP) dominate his team, as they explore possibilities, adapt prompts on the fly and hate rigid structure. He avoids Judging/Sensing-dominant profiles as too slow to pivot.
- Hiring steps:
  1. Job post: "Must love experimenting, fail fast, learn faster. No ego. Taste required."
  2. Test task: give a bad prompt and mediocre output, ask "Make this 10x better in 3 iterations," and watch humility and resilience.
  3. Interview question: "Tell me about a time AI made you feel obsolete. How did you respond?"
  4. Red flag: brags about "knowing AI" instead of being "excited to discover daily."
  5. Green flag: personal projects, refined prompts, beautiful outputs.
- He says companies will keep needing more of these people, and that he trains exactly these profiles in his consulting.

## Jobs AI will still destroy

- Ruben says AI will create jobs but will also destroy many, including some well-paid ones, and that this stance is controversial.
