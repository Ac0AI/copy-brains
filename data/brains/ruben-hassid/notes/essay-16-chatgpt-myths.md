## Common ChatGPT myths Ruben debunks: knowledge, memory and accuracy

Ruben's essay "16 chatgpt myths." (July 20, 2025) corrects widespread misconceptions about how ChatGPT works. The first group concerns what it knows and how reliable it is.

- **"It knows the entire internet."** He says ChatGPT is not connected to the internet by default. The search feature must be toggled on, and even then it reads only the first few links, which are hard to control unless you share a specific link.
- Without search, it draws on training data (mostly books, Wikipedia, Reddit, YouTube transcripts, papers). That is "a lot of the internet, but not the entire internet", and it stops at June 2024.
- You have to "call" that data with the right prompt. His example: "Act like a data engineer" sends it to its "data engineer database", and the answer is instantly better.
- **"It remembers everything."** A chat forgets anything outside a ~100,000-token context. A token is part of a word. 100,000 tokens is about a 300-page book. Turning on the "Memory" feature in settings gives it much more memory.
- **"It never lies."** ChatGPT and nearly every other AI hallucinate, meaning they fabricate data. It predicts the next token, not the next fact, so when there is a data gap it fills the void with fiction.
- It can invent sources, quotes and entire studies. His line: "Confidence ≠ truth."
- His rule: treat every reply as a first draft. Verify, cross-check, then polish.
- **"It's unbiased."** Training data is the open internet, packed with cultural, political and historical bias. Reinforcement learning fine-tunes behavior, but humans with their own bias set the rules. He recommends using multiple prompts and perspectives to detect blind spots, and points to his post "How to make AI battle for you" on confirmation bias.
- **"It predicts the future."** It extrapolates trends and nothing more. Future facts are probabilistic guesses, "sometimes right by luck. Like the Simpsons." He advises asking for multiple scenarios, not certainties.
- **"It understands humor."** It understands patterns, not punchlines. Sarcasm relies on lived context it lacks, and sound-based wordplay ("knight/night") often falls flat. His advice: give it clear setups and tweak the timing, "or just write your own jokes".

## AI as co-pilot: the "replaces experts" myth

- Ruben says ChatGPT does not replace experts: "Think co-pilot, not captain."
- His claim: "On every possible benchmark, AI + human beats both AI or human alone."
- He believes "taste & experience is the ultimate MOAT no AI can reach. Yet."
- He frames ChatGPT as "a tool, a very smart intern (that can turn into a parrot)".
- He warns against "ChatGPT psychosis": people get validated in their own worldview until they reach total psychosis and paranoia. He says it is real and links a recent X thread as an example.
- He says he does not want to live in the movie *Her*, but "we're heading there". He says a terrible thing is when people fall in love with AI. He still prefers AI that can be flirty within limits: "I hate vanilla AI. The real world isn't."

## Pricing and plan advice: the "ChatGPT is free" myth

- **"It's free."** Ruben says nothing is free, "Not even Google."
- Free tiers reduce speed and context.
- Each token costs OpenAI energy and money. He cites "something like $15 million a day" and stresses "A DAY."
- His view: the real price is either your data or your subscription.
- He argues the $20 plan is "much, much better" than the free one. It gives access to:
  - gpt-4.5, which he calls "awesome at writing".
  - gpt-o3, "the smartest".
  - Deep research, "the best at searching".
- The $200 plan is "an overkill for 99.9% of you. I don't even pay for it."

## Privacy, confidentiality and data handling

- **"ChatGPT is private."** By default, chats are stored on ChatGPT servers and may train future models. OpenAI staff can review flagged content for safety. His fix: turn off "Improve the model" or use the enterprise tier if privacy matters.
- **"It's safe for my confidential files."** He says it is not, unless you have the Team plan.
  - Files can be stored up to 30 days for abuse checks.
  - They may be used to improve the model unless you opt out.
  - On the Team plan, "you're good. But most aren't."

## Image ownership and copyright

- **"ChatGPT images are yours."** Ruben says ownership comes with strings.
  - OpenAI assigns you the copyright, but you must respect trademarks and likeness rights, and "it's on YOU to respect it. Most don't."
  - Celebrity faces or branded logos can trigger takedowns.
  - He advises running a reverse-image check before commercial use. He admits there is no 100% safe way to protect yourself instantly and wishes such a tool existed.
- **"ChatGPT owns the image you upload."** You keep the original rights, but derivatives are tricky.
  - If your upload is someone else's IP, you still need permission.
  - Remixing a copyrighted photo can create an infringing twin.
