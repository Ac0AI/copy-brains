## Curse's scale and what it built (2010)

- Hubert was CEO of Curse, Inc., a platform helping gamers succeed in massively multiplayer online games (MMOs). In his words: "helping Gamers succeed in this very complex virtual worlds" by providing "the right tools and information they need when they play the game."
- Scale in December 2010: about 6 million unique users and about 100 million page views per month.
- The Curse Client was one of the two biggest projects. It organizes players' game files and add-ons, and about 1.2 million people were using it actively.
- Games covered: World of Warcraft, Warhammer Online, Age of Conan, Aion and Diablo 3.

## Distribution platform instead of building every tool

- Curse did not build the add-ons itself. The community of developers did.
- Curse acted as the distribution platform for small applications that players install and use while playing. Hubert compared it to the iPhone App Store.
- Reach: about 10,000 applications and more than 3,000 active developers.
- Curse's own part was the surrounding infrastructure: the website, forums, and databases of all the quests, weapons and monsters.
- Takeaway from his approach: let a community of creators supply the supply side, and own distribution and the information layer around it.

## Two revenue channels: advertising and premium subscriptions

- **Advertising** was the first channel. It is why Curse moved operations to the US: about 45% of its traffic was American, so the sales team had to be there.
- **Premium subscriptions** were the second. Users could subscribe monthly or yearly to get additional features on the site.
- Lesson in his reasoning: he placed the sales team where the traffic and advertisers were, not where the company started.

## Staying relevant in a fast-changing industry

- Hubert says gaming evolves constantly. What Curse was doing four years earlier "would be completely nearly useless" in 2010.
- His answer was to play all the games himself: "it's really important to play all the games to know what's going on."
- He hired a team that "really gets it." Every employee plays MMOs and has high industry experience.
- The principle: build for gamers with gamers. The team members are users of the product, so they know exactly what other players want and need.

## Roadmap and infrastructure

- Two big projects in the works: continuing development of the Curse Client, and a large social networking product.
- Infrastructure: Curse ran on a bit more than 25 servers, provided free through Microsoft's BizSpark startup program.
- Beyond free hosting, BizSpark gave easy access to Microsoft. Curse could talk directly to Microsoft developers to solve scalability problems.
