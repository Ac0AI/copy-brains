## Curse's origin as a player-built tool

- Hubert started Curse as an MMOG guild in early 2001, playing smaller Korean MMOGs and *Dark Age of Camelot*, where the guild gained some notoriety.
- *World of Warcraft* turned the guild into a service. He found that locating and downloading add-ons was "extremely difficult and disorganized", so he built Curse-Gaming to fix it.
- He built it "largely for myself and my fellow guild members". He never expected it to be "wildly successful".
- The add-on downloads database made it one of the most visited third-party WoW sites quickly.
- Lesson he draws: solving his own annoying problem as a player became a business, turning "my passion into a business".

## Growth and team scaling (2005 to 2007)

- Curse launched in 2005. By August 2007 it had over 3.5 million monthly unique visitors.
- Staff had quadrupled and traffic "grown rapidly" over those two years.
- He was young, so he deliberately surrounded the founding team with experienced professionals from the videogame industry.
- The company had been through several redesigns (V3, then V4) before the Curse.com relaunch.
- A Series-A round was raised to support every aspect of the business and the vision of innovating for the community. He hired technical and moderation staff ahead of the expanded scope.

## Expanding from one tool to a portal

- V4 moved Curse from an add-on download site to a social and encyclopedic portal: social networking, wikis (such as WoWWiki), in-game databases for *Vanguard* and *WoW*, blogs, forums, MyCurse and the Curse Client.
- He admits the wider scope is "a great challenge" but says it "translates into many opportunities".
- V4 had been in the works a long time: "We planted many of the seeds for the V4 changes in V3."
- He calls V4 "just a first step" and refuses to commit to a "V5" label. The team is always building new features "with our users in mind".

## Positioning against competitors

- Competitors named: Thottbot, Allakhazam, Wowhead (all owned by The Zam Network, an IGE spin-off).
- His view: they built good WoW item and quest databases but are not portals. They "only address one aspect of an MMOG gamer's needs", which he says brings no value to the community or to game developers.
- Curse's goal is "a single, centralized location" for MMOG gamers: add-ons, blogs, forums, databases, wikis or nearly anything else.
- Differentiation is breadth plus owning the whole player workflow, not winning a single feature race.

## Reducing dependence on one game

- WoW drove much of Curse's traffic. He acknowledges the whole MMOG industry benefited from Blizzard's success.
- Plan to broaden: keep leadership as the WoW portal while supporting all MMOGs.
- Tactics: build relationships with game developers to support existing and upcoming titles; expand into free-to-play and item-based games.
- Bet: Asian-market games would reach the U.S. the following year, and a mainstream console MMOG would eventually lift the whole industry. Curse intended to be there for it.

## Listening to the community

- User feedback is treated as a core input. Users share it actively, both positive and negative.
- Example: after V4 launched with a light color scheme, several users disliked it versus earlier dark versions. Within a day the team "cranked out" a dark color scheme, which many adopted.
- He frames this as small in design terms but proof of the commitment to user experience.
- The team are gamers themselves. They still play together and evaluate upcoming releases to decide how to serve each community. He says this understanding of the industry "has a very important impact on how we innovate".
- He used to play 14-hour days; running and growing Curse now takes the same time.
