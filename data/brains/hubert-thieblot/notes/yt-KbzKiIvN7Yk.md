## How Hubert decides on $150K checks: vibes, ambition and technical depth

- Hubert says he "mostly invest[s] on vibe" when writing early checks. The example is a roughly $150,000 check to two founders aged 17 and 18, decided right after a same-day conversation.
- The pair had built robots, machine learning algorithms and many other projects. Hubert was impressed by what they had done for their age.
- He is not concerned with the idea: "I actually don't care too much about the idea." He asks founders for the idea mainly to learn how ambitious they are.
- He also checks whether they are good people and team players, and he prefers to find them by looking around the internet for people with ideas.

## What Hubert screens for in founders

- **Ambition.** He asks whether you are ambitious.
- **Speed and resourcefulness.** He asks whether you will "move really fast and find ways to unblock yourself."
- **Technical ability.** He asks whether you can build a really hard product or company.
- **Focus area.** The firm generally tends to focus on emerging technology such as AI and robotics.

## The hangar: a 42,000 sq ft space for founders

- The idea is "a hangar for founders": a physical place where people gather, start their journey and create things.
- Founders are surrounded by other very fast movers and ambitious people.
- Hubert confirms the 42,000 sq ft space as the home for the founders he backs.

## Why Europe held founders back, and why San Francisco felt different

- Hubert started the company in December 2004 in Heidelberg, Germany, and later moved it to the US.
- He says many VCs had a "virus" of giving reasons you can't do something: you're too young, you have no degrees. He did not finish high school.
- When he landed in San Francisco, the attitude was the opposite: "why can you not do that?"
- His view is that Europe needs to take more risk, go faster and "break some regulation."
- He says that in venture you have to go fast, because if you go slow your competition will kill you.
- He allows that some worries about how fast AI is moving, such as job displacement, "can be legitimate."

## Curse: what he built and what it taught him

- Curse was a modding platform. In games like World of Warcraft or Minecraft, users could install mods to change parts of the game or the user interface.
- It was a 12-plus year journey before the company was acquired. He calls it "life-changing."
- He says there were lots of failures and some successes, and he had to learn how to create a company along the way.
- He credits some luck too: "I think I got lucky also."

## Advice for young founders

1. **Don't hire a compliance officer too early.** He says: "Do not hire a compliance officer before you have something that needs a compliance officer." He suggests it can wait until roughly a month after you start.
2. **Dream the biggest you can and take on the hardest problem you actually care about.** It is obvious when people work on random things they don't care about.
3. **Care, or you will quit.** Founders who don't care about the problem will mostly give up the first time it gets really hard, so he values grit.
4. **Win with ethics.** He says winning should come "with some ethics and beliefs."
