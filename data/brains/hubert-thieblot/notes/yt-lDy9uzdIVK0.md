## Why Hubert says founders should move to San Francisco

- Hubert has spent the past year urging founders to move to SF and built Founders, Inc. to make that move easy.
- He started his first company, Curse, at 19. It grew from zero to 150+ employees and $50 million in revenue in its last year.
- He knew before starting that this would only be possible in San Francisco.
- Being there changed the company's trajectory. He became "way more ambitious" and "nothing felt impossible."
- Since selling Curse, his stated life mission is to help the next generation of founders find their path and their people, and to fund them.
- His 2026 goal: help 1,000 founders start their company in SF and give them what they need to hit the ground running, "exactly how I wish it happened for me."

## The campus model: three locations in San Francisco

- **Fort Mason:** the first place invited founders visit. It is an always-on hub for building without distractions.
  - He says 90% of the experience is a daily cycle of building, learning and iterating alongside other founders on campus.
  - The team runs regular office hours and sessions with experienced founders who are anywhere from one to 100 steps ahead.
  - It also hosts public events for the wider SF community, such as hackathons, fireside chats and LAN parties.
- **The Garage:** the highest concentration of hardware founders in the city. It has four floors of industrial equipment and dedicated spaces for 30 teams.
- **A third, expanding space:** new space for the portfolio, with room for 100 founders.
- Hubert says the team runs the campus like a startup and keeps iterating as it grows.

## Hardware and physical AI conviction

- 50% of Founders, Inc.'s 2025 investments were hardware.
- He envisions a future where physical AI and robotics are the biggest companies in the world.
- He says that for founders who believe the same, Founders, Inc. wants to be their "first and biggest supporter."
- Hardware teams are accepted in every program. In the Blueprint program, hardware and physical AI become the only focus for everyone on campus.

## How to get in and how investing works

- The process is: apply online, get invited to the space, work closely together on accelerating growth, and invest if both sides feel it is a good fit.
- The campus runs 24/7. If you apply today, the team can contact you that night and you can be there the next day.
- He frames the evaluation as two-way: founders also get to judge whether Founders, Inc. is the right partner.
- If a founder felt real acceleration, Founders, Inc. finances the company.

## Programs that scale the intake

- Four main programs run over the year, each bringing in hundreds of founders at once.
- Each is followed by a six-week cohort and ends with a demo day festival, where thousands of builders and investors come to see the work.
- In the off-season, 200 students fly out for the summer.
- A fourth program is coming that will bring together founders from all over the world.

## No pitch decks: judge founders by working together

- Hubert says "we absolutely hate pitch decks." He never liked making them as a founder.
- His reasoning: building a company is about doing, not making a polished presentation.
- He argues the team learns far more about a founder's potential by working with them in person.

## What investment unlocks

- After the first investment, the campus becomes fully unlocked for the founder.
- Portfolio benefits include:
  - Internal events exclusive to the portfolio.
  - Weekly accountability sessions.
  - More perks and credits.
  - A community platform for finding elite hires and co-founders, and for support with downstream investors.
- The Founders, Inc. team works hands-on with portfolio companies on product, media and go-to-market.
- His summary of the offer: a path to San Francisco, a community of other ambitious founders, and the intensity to win.
