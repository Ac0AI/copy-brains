## Why Curse moved its HQ from San Francisco to Huntsville, Alabama

In September 2014, as CEO of the online game portal Curse Inc., Hubert explained why Curse relocated its headquarters from San Francisco to Huntsville, Alabama. The company's biggest challenge was growing the team fast enough to keep up with the company. He decided Curse couldn't "slug it out" for talent against the very large, very well-funded companies in San Francisco, so the company would "make its own path" and break from the Silicon Valley stereotype.

## Five criteria for choosing where to build a company

Hubert's team looked at more than 10 US cities. The factors, in order:

1. Talent pool
2. Work-life balance
3. Business environment
4. Cost efficiency
5. Potential

- **Talent pool comes first.** Moving somewhere without finding talented employees makes no sense.
- **The tech scene must be strong but not saturated.** If competition is too heavy, hiring becomes prohibitively expensive or so slow that it slows down production.
- **Match the place to the team.** Curse's staff is younger, active and educated, so the location had to appeal to and reflect that demographic.
- **Optimize for a sustainable work experience.** He wants happy employees and a lifestyle the location supports.

## Cost efficiency means value, not just savings

Hubert says the key "isn't to simply save," because Curse could put a team in another country for that. The goal was to "get the most bang for our buck" and build an infrastructure in a location that would be efficient.

On Huntsville's economics, he said: "Huntsville is the perfect location from a cost standpoint. It feels a little bit like Austin, Texas, about 10 years ago, when it started on its way up. Corporate real estate is inexpensive, the cost of talent is reasonable and housing for our staff is incredibly affordable."

## Be a big fish in an emerging market, not a drop in the bucket

- Hubert did not want Curse to be only "a drop in the bucket in a larger market," even as a fast-growing, successful startup.
- He chose not to follow the herd to the trendy corporate location. Curse would "rather blaze a trail toward an up and coming community" and help shape its evolution from the start.
- The result in Huntsville: a "small fish-big pond" mentality no longer applied, and the company felt wanted.
- Curse's Huntsville presence was set to grow from about 20 to about 40 people as the HQ relocation completed.

## Talent outcome in Huntsville

- Huntsville's talent pool comes largely from the aerospace industry and NASA (Marshall Space Flight Center). Hubert rated the level of expertise and education as very good.
- Curse was filling significantly more positions locally than it expected.
- Social and entertainment options and a thriving business community kept staff comfortable and happy.

## Working with local government and building a "Silicon South"

- Hubert credits the Huntsville Chamber of Commerce, Downtown Huntsville, the City of Huntsville and the Alabama Commerce Department with making significant resources available and easing the transition.
- He wanted a location that "would work for other companies similar to ours," reasoning that if Curse met its own criteria there, others would follow. He saw companies trending away from the high cost and difficult recruiting of established markets.
- Curse's ambition was to be part of establishing a "Silicon South" in Alabama.
- Tactics to promote the hub: talk to other companies and the venture capital community in the gaming space and tell the Huntsville story, and bring teams down to see the nightlife, activities and environment firsthand. Hubert reports the response is consistently positive.

## Context at the time of the move

- Hubert described Curse as having several rapidly growing products, newly secured funding to reach "the next level," and technical infrastructure that would scale for some time.
- He framed hiring capacity, not product or funding, as the main constraint on growth, and treated location as a hiring strategy.
