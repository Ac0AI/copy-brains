## Quitter: origin, idea selection and the product

- Alex started Quitter a year before this interview. It helps guys stop gooning (quit porn), and he says it has 1.5 million downloads and about $500K in revenue a month.
- His reasoning for the idea: self-improvement culture had made it normal to question junk habits (TikTok, junk food, porn). He "saw a gap in the market" for an app that made quitting feel cool rather than taboo.
- The mission already had a built-in audience: the NoFap community on Reddit has millions of members.
- Product mechanics: he gamified it with orbs unlocked for streak length ("battle passes in Fortnite" style), plus a community of people with the same problem. Because the topic is taboo, talking about it openly makes it easier to deal with.
- First month was about $30K in revenue. He met co-founder Connor through the YC Co-founder Matching platform, then in person in San Francisco, after both had the same app idea.
- Before Quitter he built "Rich AI" with his brother Chris and dropped it. His reason: "there wasn't really much marketing leverage I could pull." He treats knowing when you're wrong and being willing to "bite the bullet" and quit as an entrepreneurial skill.
- He says he doesn't want to be known just for this niche long term, but thinks Quitter can reach $3M, $4M or $5M a month "not with much difficulty."

## Reverse-engineering a proven app instead of inventing

- Core method: "the internet is beautiful because you can reverse engineer almost any business and any individual."
- For Quitter he used Cali (a calorie-tracking app) as the template. He broke down exactly how Cali makes money, how its paywall works and how it works with influencers, then replicated it, and says it worked.
- Same logic for a personal brand: he scrolled to the bottom of Drake's Instagram and moved up through the posts (30K likes, then 100K, then 200K likes) to see that "things start small then they blow up."
- If he went broke and had to restart (niche unknown), his plan:
  1. Study where money is flowing.
  2. Find the people making the most in a niche and break them down fully.
  3. Reverse engineer and launch his own version.
  4. Work very hard on on-the-ground marketing.
- Example of ground-level marketing from Quitter's early days: jumping into Reddit communities and posting "this app changed my life." He admits these were fake reviews, and says they generated thousands of dollars in the early days.

## How Quitter scaled from $30K to $500K a month

- **$30K in month one:** no one had heard of the product, so he had first-mover advantage. With no competition, "we can charge whatever we want," and getting people in the door and paying was easy.
- **30K to 100K:** scaled operations and worked with more influencers.
- **Around $70K a month, December:** launched Meta ads. Because Quitter was the first quit-porn app running ads, he had "complete penetration" and about a 7x ROAS. He says they put in 30K the first month and then 30K x7 after that.
- ROAS then fell as spend scaled.
- **January influencer video:** a 15-second skit that he thought was bad. It went from $5K to 10K, 20K, 30K, up to 80K in revenue in one day. The skit: an influencer lying on a bed with lotion thinks about it, pulls out Quitter and stops.
- Revenue chart pattern: a consistent climb with about three or four days in total where it spikes sharply.
- He has been stuck around $500K a month for four or five months. He is restructuring because profitability dropped, after he reviewed the work of the person he had given full trust in running marketing for 12 months and "lost complete faith" in it. His view: marketing is the most important function because without it you can't generate revenue.

## Influencer deals and the scaling problem of a taboo niche

- Payment model: view guarantees, about $2 CPM (about $2,000 per million views), tied to views so they are "almost always profitable."
- Check audience demographics before signing an influencer. This is "one of the prerequisites to signing" one (for example, a creator with 10M views a month where 8M are women is a poor fit for a male-targeted app).
- His framework: "Every business has a fundamental problem."
  - Quitter has high pain points, so people convert and pay well, but fewer influencers will tie porn to their name.
  - Cali is easy to scale because every gym influencer can promote it, but it faces accuracy backlash.
- Skit-style content worked well for Quitter because the creator doesn't need to claim personal use.

## Info products: App Mafia course and community

- Alex launched a course and paid community with App Mafia, his first time doing info products. He is pro info products: you're selling information people can use to make money themselves, so it makes sense even if it looks scammy.
- Launch numbers: $220,000 in the first 24 hours, $160K the next day, $100K the day after, so about $500K in three days. He notes the profit margin is close to 100%.
- Why it worked: "it took years of building reputation."
- He says he is helping people go from about $2K to $10K, $20K or $30K a month.
- App Mafia: "the Milk Boys for entrepreneurship." Four co-founders in a Miami mansion vlogging team-10 style. They post both lifestyle vlogs (to inspire) and education. They released a 13-hour free app-building course at plug-atm.com ("no benefit from my end").
- Spending after the launch: course money was spent fast. He says half a million went in 44 days, on Instagram handles (about $100K), club tables (about $50K), rent (about $250K over three months), a private chef (about $10K a month) and food (about $5K a month), leaving about $20K.

## Software leverage versus service businesses

- His argument for apps: "I'm making money sitting here doing nothing because people are downloading and paying." With an agency or sales-call business, "if I want to make 2,000 I have to go take an hour sales call," so you remain an employee of your own business.
- AI speeds app-building a lot: his roommate Blake built and launched an app on the App Store in 48 hours with AI, work that "usually takes several months."
- His view of the "$30K a month in info" plateau: it can keep you from the next level. If the goal is a very large outcome, "you're not going to get there if you play this mini game."

## Output, volume and mindset

- Doubling income: "Double volume. Try to figure out how to do two days of revenue in one day. That's how you double your monthly income." More shots at the dartboard means more micro-adjustments.
- Value principle: provide value to one person and you get rewarded; provide it to a million people and you get rewarded proportionally. He ties his higher revenue to helping 1.5M people download his app.
- On mentality: "Just think you're the best and become the best, and have the humility to learn from your mistakes."
- His own routine: 7 to 8 hours sleep, gym, meal prep planned in advance. He says "listen to your body" on rest.
- He says he started in 2024: first 18th-birthday money was £10K from his dad, then a roughly three-month San Francisco trip, then Miami, where he met Zach (18, running Cali, about $3.5M a month).
- Hiring principle on delegation: he's waiting until he hits about $75K a month before hiring closers and setters, and notes closers and setters don't want offers making under $100K a month.

## Environment and personal brand

- His advice for someone stuck: scrape together money to leave your environment. In a new place, "when you normalize it, it becomes more achievable."
- Surroundings matter: he declined to learn from someone driving a McLaren on finance who barely had liquid savings. He cites "surround yourself with the happy and lucky and you become just like them."
- He is building a personal brand (recent reels about 100K views each), is interested in music, and wants to move to LA (more cultured, "real wealth") over Miami, which he calls "a bubble."
