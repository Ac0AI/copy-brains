## Mistakes are information, not failure
- Alex says mistakes happen because "you don't understand what you're doing well enough, because if you did, you would just do it the right way."
- Since it's everyone's first time living, he treats messing up as inevitable.
- His rule: "the aim of the game really is just to not make the same mistake twice." Ask why you messed up, then be better next time.

## Be financially responsible: run the real numbers
- Quitter Aid (his app business) did $4.2 million in revenue in 2025, but Alex says the bank account "definitely isn't that number."
- Cause: "we just spent way too much money on dumb [stuff]." Generating hundreds of thousands of dollars a month made him feel invincible, especially with no prior business success.
- His worked example, at about $7,000/day of proceeds (revenue after Apple's cut):
  - minus about $3,500/day of ad spend
  - minus monthly team overheads divided by 30
  - minus Apple's cut and any random spending
  - minus fancy dinners and similar
  - Result: nothing left.
- Proceeds are now about $15,000/day, versus about $7,000/day back when the spending was reckless.
- Advice: "be smart, take it easy, take it slow, and be clinical." Run the math properly and don't run it in your favor. Don't tell yourself "I'm making X a month, I can do whatever I want"; know where you actually are.

## Don't be afraid to post
- Alex admits he held back posting unless it was perfect, "at the SL standard," to avoid critique.
- His realization: "The world just forgets you." He cites Liam Payne, once globally recognized, now rarely discussed.
- People follow you for a reason. If you stop posting they don't really notice or care, because they have other accounts to consume.
- His conclusion: "If you feel like you have something to bring to the world, bring it to the world. Don't hold back."

## Believe you're meant to be great
- Alex's claim: if you think you're going to be great, you're already more than halfway there.
- He backs his own confidence with specific skills: sales, branding, marketing, product and pricing, plus "killer instincts." He says people who think this way "are supposed to be great."
- Don't put money or success on a pedestal. Those who want it most and put in the most work get it.
- Framing: the law of cause and effect. "Everything you have in your life right now is a result of what you've put in." If you're unhappy, it's because you avoided the work, so "go hustle and get it."

## Accept what isn't meant to be
- Alex says "everything happens for a reason and what will be will be."
- In 2025 he put too much pressure on things that didn't work out and got frustrated over things that "wasn't supposed to be anyway."
- Two reactions are available: get upset and frustrated, or accept that this isn't in your story. If it isn't supposed to be, it isn't.

## Focus on what really matters: put the budget where it works
- Quitter Aid spent about $100K a month on Meta ads from February 2025 and did not increase the budget for the rest of the year.
- The team also paid influencers who weren't performing, because that was the game everyone else on Twitter was playing. Alex says there was no self-realization about whether it was right for the business, and it wasn't.
- On returning to the company, he pushed to raise the Meta budget and drop influencer pay into Meta.
- Result: proceeds of about $15,000/day, roughly $18,000-$20,000 gross revenue per day. He says it was "as simple as just doubling the budget on Meta" and that they should have done it ages ago.
- Next steps he names: keep raising ad budget, improve creatives, and diversify into SEO and Google search ads. The goal is reaching "2, 3, 4 million" months.
- Feeling at a standstill with Quitter Aid is part of why he started his other app, Reset: he wanted to move faster.

## Relationships: don't ignore red flags
- Alex met his ex in December 2024 and knew almost immediately it was "kind of pointless," with many red flags.
- He kept the relationship going out of comfort. Messages from February 2025 that he found later were clearly "toxic," yet it lasted until December.
- The reasons he didn't like her at the start were the same reasons he broke up with her at the end.
- His view: relationships "affect every aspect of your life" and become a cornerstone, so be intentional and careful. Act when you know instead of pushing it off.
