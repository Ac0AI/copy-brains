## Quitter revenue recovered after stepping back in: team cut from 14, revenue went up

- Alex stepped away from Quitter to test whether he was "the issue." His words: "spoiler, I wasn't the issue."
- 20 days after returning, the app was doing a consistent $9-10k in proceeds per day, about $15k gross revenue per day.
- His first week back produced roughly "like 140k in revenue" (the video title says $150k). The transcript wording is ambiguous, so treat the exact figure as roughly $140-150k.
- Per-user metrics were rising across the board: "ROAS, LTV, ARPU all these figures are going up and up."
- Team change: the team had 14 people, and "we fired everyone and the revenue went up. It didn't go down, it went up." He calls team roles a "big problem."
- His framing of the experiment: a founder pulling back to see whether the business runs without them is the "true test" of whether they are the bottleneck.

## What Alex does day to day when running Quitter

- He goes "behind the scenes" and looks at the paywalls, improving them "based on what I want."
- He works on the content itself, making "content that I want to watch." His taste is the filter for what ships.
- He reviews team roles and questions whether each one is needed.
- The three levers he names are paywalls, content and team structure, not new features.

## Standard for content: refuses to post mediocre content

- He filmed a "chapter 4" behind-the-scenes video, then didn't post it because footage of someone behind a laptop all day isn't entertaining. The edit exists; he held it back anyway.
- "I'm just not content with mediocre content." He cites this as the reason he posts rarely on Instagram and Twitter: "I want to be exceptional in every facet of life."
- He apologizes for taking 2 weeks to post, attributing it to a "very high standard."
- His target for better content is visually dynamic, high-stakes scenes rather than screen time:
  - travelling London to New York and filming behind the scenes
  - in-person meetings and whiteboard-style design sessions in a physical office
  - pulling up Quitter revenue on a Times Square billboard
  - packing presents for friends before flying to London
  - the build and launch of his new app, Reset
- He sums it up as wanting "constant exhilaration" in what he produces.
