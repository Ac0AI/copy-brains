## Quitter's app-building playbook: influencers, volume, and scaling past $500K/month

Alex describes the model behind Quitter (a quit-pornography app) and At Mafia as a simple loop, and says the hard part is scaling, not profiting.

- **Step 1:** build an app that is valuable, that you would pay for, and that actually works.
- **Step 2:** find influencers in your niche with a cult-like following who will push the app inside their videos.
- **Step 3:** some viewers of those videos download the app, and some downloads become sales.
- **Step 4:** reinvest the sales revenue into more distribution and more influencers, and you have a business.
- He says getting to the first $30K month "genuinely wasn't difficult".
- By his account the hard part is "systemizing things" and "scaling beyond your mindset".
- Example of the margin squeeze: nine team members, about $100K a month on ads, about $500K revenue, then Apple takes 30%, leaving "a bit of that".
- His fix is to become lean and keep scaling operations.

## How Quitter reached about $500K a month, and why he thinks it should be more

- He describes the growth as a few viral stunts plus a steady system, not one trick.
- The stunts included Quitter buying OnlyFans, which he says brought a couple hundred thousand to maybe $300K in revenue plus a lot of mind share.
- The rest was "a hell of a lot more influencers, a hell of a lot more ads" and keeping the system flowing.
- He says "it wasn't really that difficult".
- He quotes about $3.2 million in total revenue so far.
- He says the two companies he is affiliated with, At Mafia and Quitter, did $1 million combined in the last month.
- He is not satisfied: he had said he would hit higher numbers by April, May or June, but as of late September revenue was still stuck near $500K a month.
- He believes the business "should be doing" $1M to $3M a month.

## Inputs and outputs: the volume math for getting to $1M a month

- His plan is to stop chasing the big number and go back to fundamentals: look at the inputs (volume of content) and the outputs (views, downloads, revenue).
- Current run rate is roughly $12.5K to $15K a day.
- Tripling daily volume would give about $35K to $36K a day, which adds up to roughly $1M a month.
- The target is concrete: get three days' worth of content, views, downloads and revenue into one day. He calls this "pretty simple".
- He notes the mental barrier he hit at $500K a month came from staring at the headline number instead of the daily inputs.

## Volume and persistence as the core principle

- He cites a quote he credits to Hormozi: "do so much volume that it would be unreasonable to fail".
- His view is that if you put in real effort and don't stop until you "crack the code", you can't fail.
- His example is to treat any goal as research plus iteration. For a would-be top singer: study the rise of other artists, iterate on the beat, put out content constantly, make it catchy, post on TikTok, and obsess over it daily.
- On his own start: after his first video he did not just post it and move on. He acted on it.
- He says he was flown to San Francisco with about $5 in his bank account and "hustled".
- His advice is to set your standards much higher and think beyond money. "It's not 10k a month. It's not 100k a month. It's not driving a Lamborghini." Ask what gives you true purpose.
- He says he could rebuild his position again; it would only take time.

## Shifting culture instead of only selling an app

- He wants people to use Quitter because culture has changed, not just because they saw an ad.
- Goal: make it cool to quit watching pornography, and make people love the mission.
- The planned vehicle is a "winter arc" update with more gamification and stronger Quitter branding.
- He frames Twitter (social media) as the best resource for that cultural shift.

## Org lesson: promoting an operator to CEO

- He and his co-founder felt overwhelmed. They had a duty to lead a team but were distracted by lifestyle and At Mafia, and they were the bottleneck.
- They promoted an internal person who used to run ads (named Peter) to CEO.
- Peter is the one who "stepped up" and does the groundwork.
- The founders act as puppet masters: they protect the vision and quality bar, and make the key decisions.
- He says taking the founders away from daily operations lets them make better company decisions.

## Lesson: comfort and overpaying yourself kills output

- Over the last 3 to 4 months, after he stopped making YouTube videos, he got extremely comfortable.
- He paid himself too much each month from Quitter revenue while his work output did not rise with it.
- His read is that getting paid more for doing nothing "rewires" the brain to treat doing nothing as rewarding, which wrecks motivation.
- He lost sight of the mission and thought about his next five-figure payout instead of the team, cutting costs, bringing work in-house, or improving quality.
- His fix is to get realigned on the mission and tie effort back to output.

## Idea: a "Super Health" ecosystem

- Thesis: people increasingly care about health (he cites Whoop, cutting seed oils, Apple Watch). Quitter worked because there was a niche of men who wanted to quit pornography.
- He sees a billion-dollar opportunity in unifying health around one login, "the Apple ecosystem for health".
- The layers, from the bottom:
  - **App layer (dominate first):** own apps such as Quitter, Cali, Strava-type, MyFitnessPal-type, running and sleep apps.
  - **Physical products:** Whoop, Oura rings, Eight Sleep, Apple Watch.
  - **Supplements and food:** AG1, Blueprint.
  - **Top of the pyramid:** a Super Health profile that holds all the data in one place.
- Monetization idea: about "$39 a month" for a subscription across apps, supplements and sleep.
- He calls the idea a genius concept but "incredibly difficult to execute on".
