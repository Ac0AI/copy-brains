## Pricing and payments: moving US paywall traffic to Stripe to avoid Apple's fee

- Alex's app, Quitter, originally monetized through Apple's in-app payment paywall.
- He built a Stripe integration so the paywall can take payment through Stripe and skip Apple's cut. He describes it as "I can now get the Stripe pay and skip Apple's fee."
- The Stripe paywall is shown only to users in the USA region.
- Rollout was a 10% test: 10% of users see the Stripe paywall. As data comes back showing it is more profitable, he plans to "introduce it to everyone."
- He compared proceeds on the transaction-abandoned paywall (Stripe versus regular Apple). Stripe proceeds are "almost double... more like 30%" higher. The gap is the fee paid to Apple that Stripe avoids.
- His takeaway is that the integration lets the business "continue scaling proceeds" and that the proceeds number "should continue to go higher" as more people get the Stripe paywall.

## Revenue numbers and scaling paid ads

- Quitter does about $17-18k a day gross on Apple. After Apple's cut it is more like $12-13k a day in proceeds.
- Yesterday's figures: ad spend of $4.1k, about $17,000 gross and about $13,000 in proceeds.
- His scaling rule: raise ad spend 20% a day, on the condition that it remains profitable. If it stays profitable, spend steps up daily (roughly $4.1k to $4.5k to $5k to $5.5k and so on).
- Within about a week, if things go well, he expects to spend $10-20k a day. He says that would put Quitter over $1 million a month in revenue, a target he had wanted "for all of these months" and had just "figured out how to do it."
- He frames Stripe as extra upside on top of ad scaling: "with the Stripe integration, we could just go way higher."
- His ambition: if the business hit around $4 million a month while staying as profitable as it is, he thinks selling Quitter for $100 million is "not unrealistic at all."

## Cutting operational bloat and bringing in an experienced investor

- Alex was meeting an investor (Zach) at a Coconut Grove steakhouse in Miami, with a co-founder (Connor) present, hoping to "shake hands on the spot."
- His reasoning for taking on experienced help: "it's easier for someone with more experience to come in and tell you what to focus your time on rather than you guessing and trying to figure it out yourself."
- He has no doubt the founding team (himself, Connor, Chris) could figure it out. The point is that an experienced partner would save "several months" and add liquidity to his pocket.
- After going through the Slack workspace, the business and its operations with Zach, the team realized "how much [waste] there is." Alex says it felt "relieving" to cut it out and to shut things down as soon as they pop up.
- He sees the cleanup as the unlock: considering how far the business has come, "it's scary, exciting to see where we can go after clearing it all up."

## Location and environment as a performance lever

- Alex moved to London and, after about two months, felt restless and cut off. He concluded he wanted to stay in Miami instead.
- Reasons for Miami:
  - Creativity and opportunities "unlocked" within 24 hours of being there.
  - Fewer distractions: no school friends and no car to be pulled away by. He says he is "just focused."
  - The sun and energy make him "way more inspired" and "way more hungry."
  - Time zone: the team needs to be on East Coast time. Time zones are "a big one."
- His critique of London: everything "slows down to Porter speed" and staying would delay his "dream life by another 6 to 8 months." His line: "Why be the horse when you can be the Ferrari?"
- His plan: take meetings in New York, bring the team to Miami on an Airbnb for at least 20-30 days, go back to London for a month, then return to Miami for a fuller stint.

## Founder mindset: optimizing for stories and consistency

- Alex's life-optimization frame is experiences, memories and stories. He imagines telling his future kids about being a young founder in Miami "closing big deals, building massive companies," and says he is "busy writing the coolest story ever."
- On motivation, he and his co-founders discussed instant gratification: people with no purpose scroll TikTok, watch "lock in" content, tell a friend "this is the year we make money," and then "don't do [anything]." His point is that the dopamine loop replaces action.
- He ended a night by finishing the Stripe setup so he could "wake up tomorrow... knowing that I did something productive," which sets up a productive next day.
- A rent discussion for a Miami team house priced at $125k a month: he argues rent should be judged as a percentage of income. At $1 million a month in income, $100k rent is about 10%, "still a lot of money," but split five ways at about $20k each it is manageable.
