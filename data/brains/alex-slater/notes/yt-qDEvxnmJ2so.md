## Copying beats inventing, and onboarding is a cheap lever

- Alex's rule: "If your idea is any good, it's probably been done before." He assumes competitors have already A/B tested and are running the best version.
- QUITTR's onboarding was copied from the leading competitor, Brain Buddy. He advises anyone early or mid-stage to do the same and leave A/B testing until they are big enough.
- The same applies to ads. Coming up with new ideas is one of the worst things you can do. Change one small thing about something that already works instead of starting from scratch, "because when you start from scratch, who knows what works."
- Long onboarding works because of the sunk cost fallacy. Gamblers are not addicted to winning; they want something back for the time they spent. A user who has answered many questions is more likely to buy when the paywall appears.
- QUITTR's onboarding reached about an 85% paywall rate, and it has never been A/B tested, even now.
- Onboarding tweaks usually find only a 5-10% gain, and Alex says tinkering often hurts the app. At a thousand dollars a month, 10% is a hundred dollars, so bigger levers deserve the time.

## Creative is the main paid ads lever, and volume decides scale

- Alex says the most important factor in paid ads is creative, once everything is plumbed correctly.
- A high-quality creative pipeline at a steady cadence separates an account spending 20k a month from one spending 200k or 2 million.
- At 20k a month, a few lucky creatives are enough. Getting from 200k to 1-2 million needs a pipeline that brings in diverse creative from many sources.
- Typical sources at scale:
  - a creator program (QUITTR's UGC program)
  - several creative agencies at different levels
  - in-house production
- Meta shows each creative to the exact person it suits, using the same targeting as Reels. Feeds differ so much between people that no general creative strategist knows what works, so diversity is the lever.
- Alex's general summary: "the only thing that matters is volume." A $2-3k/month app that did a week's worth of volume in one day could add real revenue for the month.
- QUITTR at 100k versus 500k a month: "we spent five x more, we did five x more volume. That's the difference." His closing advice: don't overcomplicate it, stop reading Twitter, and lock yourself in a box and figure it out.
- Questions like which colors or what size are noise. A person asking them is focused on the wrong things.

## Expand your TAM with different creative styles

- Alex learned from people pushing around a hundred million a year in e-commerce that expanding your addressable audience is how you spend more.
- Do it by making different styles of creative for different audiences.
- Example he plans for QUITTR: a creative director tells UGC creators to make Muslim-niche Reels. They are shown only to that audience, so they don't affect the broader brand, and they open a new segment.
- Meta Ads and the ad library are most useful when creatives are as different as the audiences they serve.

## Creator programs: pay a cut of ad revenue, not retainers

- Alex sources creators through a platform called Tribe, using a set reward to attract applicants.
- Best incentive in his view: a share of ad revenue. QUITTR and Cali AI both introduced this recently.
- The deal: a freelance creator sends videos, the team runs them on Meta, and the creator earns 10% of the revenue the ad generates.
- Example: if 10k is spent on an ad because it performed, the creator gets 1k. That is low risk for the app and high reward for the creator, and the more a creator earns, the more they have made for the app.
- He doesn't think retainers are smart.

## Memes and cultural moments for cheap reach

- Alex hired a head of memes and calls memes an "untapped space." His view: "embed yourself in culture."
- Meta RevOps gives consistent revenue and perhaps 1.3x to 2x growth, with cash flow becoming an issue at scale, so he also chases big wins: 10-million-view influencer videos and memes.
- A meme needs high engagement and shareability, which comes from a simple message understandable in one read. His example: the "Quit Your Job buys OnlyFans and shuts it down" meme, which can be shared with friends.
- It also needs a good cause. A meme that goes viral but isn't serious (his example: renaming Epstein's island "Goonville") won't drive conversions.
- Things that announce news or sound like real news perform best.
- Memes get lower CPMs and fewer downloads than ads, but are far more likely to go viral.
- Alex's balance: serious enough to convert, shareable enough to spread.

## Meta account tactics: old ads, impressions filter, and install vs purchase

- **Copy cautiously.** Most people copy a competitor's oldest running ad. Alex suggests the opposite for your own account: put your worst ads in a campaign at $5 a day and let them run forever, because the oldest running ads are your worst ads, which sends competitors down the wrong path.
- **Most-impressions filter.** It applies to larger ad accounts, so a relatively new account can avoid it. Awareness campaigns have much lower CPMs than conversion campaigns. Alex describes running an awareness campaign, changing the CTA from "learn more" to "download", so a competitor sees an app ad with many impressions and assumes it is good.
- **Push through lower-event funnel pain.** For nearly every new app, an install-optimized campaign is the most efficient by ROAS for two to three weeks and gets paying customers more cheaply than a purchase-optimized campaign.
- Teams often stop the A/B test too early. Alex says the purchase campaign has eventually taken the lead every time in accounts he has seen, but it needs money burned to warm up the purchase event so Meta learns who your exact buyers are.
- The ideal is paying a lot for a small number of high-intent users. If target CAC is $15, you pay $15 for a customer who converts every time. Click-through rate may look poor.
- His conclusion: don't over-index on hook rate, click-through rate or CPM. Worry about CAC, and keep the account on the purchase event through the early dip.

## Mistakes: agencies, spreading budget, and staying on what works

- QUITTR burned over 100k on creative agencies while chasing organic reach. They promised around a thousand pieces a month and viral hits; it never happened and he regrets it.
- A bigger regret: after finding success on Meta, he expanded horizontally into influencers, UGC and organic before scaling volume on Meta. Budget was misallocated away from Meta even though influencers returned less. Had he reinvested Meta's returns into paid, he says he would be "in a far different situation."
- Advice: early on, scale Meta to about a million a month and focus on it. Then add TikTok, Google or DSPs, and Meta success makes those channels come online faster. Alex compares this to owning the S&P 500 first and diversifying later.
- Viral reach is not revenue. A video that reached over a million views and was retweeted by Elon led to no business, while boring Google Doc screenshots drove 30-40 new clients.
