## Running Quitter as a CEO: the day-to-day job is managing people, not doing the work

- Alex took back sole CEO status from a co-founder split. He says two leaders each owning "50% of responsibilities" left everyone confused and nobody overseeing the whole. As sole leader he talks to every employee daily, knows what is going on and what to hire for.
- He says the result was record months in January and February, with March on track to be bigger than both.
- His morning routine: catch up with the team on Slack, keep everyone on Rome (a virtual office tool), check Meta ad spend, and check the day's proceeds.
- Most of the CEO job is calling the full-time employees he pays to execute and checking they are doing a good job. He says this means a lot of waiting around, and he always finds something else to do, especially on weekends when others are off.
- He works at lunch too, reviewing a Loom-video application to join the team. His view: "the amount of care that you have for something reflects in the outcomes."

## Meta ads: creative is the lever, and more angles expand the addressable market

- Alex says Meta targets automatically from the creative, like the Reels algorithm. A motivation video gets shown to people with motivation content on their feeds.
- He believes most specific Meta ad settings "don't really matter" now. It is all about creative.
- Lesson he says he learned: the company needs more angles and different styles of creator, because that expands the audience. Quitter had sold well to young people who want to quit. Untapped groups he names: Muslims, Christians, gym bros and people who want high testosterone. To reach them, change the creative and Meta will show it to those people.
- He calls hiring a creative team Quitter's top focus.
- Revenue dips when Meta Ads has outages. On one such day he cites about 15.5k in proceeds, which he calls "all right" rather than great, blaming Meta showing ads to the wrong people.
- His three levers: more differently styled creatives, more money into the system, and fixing attribution. He says "we'll be taking over" if all three happen.

## Building the creative team at scale

- A Tribe campaign has roughly 300 creators who post for Quitter, producing about 1.3k creatives a month in different styles. Alex says he believes the figure is about 300 people.
- He is also trying to hire a creative director who can script and produce very high production videos at scale, with captions and editing, like Get Brick and other e-commerce brands. He thinks Quitter should try the same.

## Hiring a Chief Product Officer to squeeze more from the product

- Alex is hunting for a CPO through a head hunter named Ken, at about 25K a month. His reasoning: "What else am I going to do with the 25K a month? Throw it in the profit tank?"
- His ideal candidate has worked at big companies like Duolingo and knows high-level product changes that raise revenue, ARPU, LTV and customer satisfaction.
- Areas he wants them to obsess over: retention improvements within the community, and onboarding.
- SEO idea: rank the community for searches like "I'm struggling with porn, my wife hates me", the way Reddit does, so Quitter becomes a social platform.
- He argues that as spend and downloads grow, small percentage improvements matter a lot more.

## Why he reinvests: equity and exits beat cash

- He points to Cal AI being acquired by MyFitnessPal, with the announcement tweet getting 7 million views, as a blueprint for Quitter.
- His view: apps can claim half a million or 5 million a month, but take-home after marketing spend is modest. The real prize is the exit from "building a brand legacy and tens of millions of downloads", which he calls the magnum opus of the app space. "It isn't really cash, it's the big exits, the equity value."
- So he is ramping up: "We need to spend way more money and we need to go forward faster. Speed, speed, speed." He says he expects two, three, four million dollar months soon.
- Small dividends (he mentions 10K) "don't really matter much". The real money is in equity and long-term profit.

## First real profit and cash management

- On the day filmed, Apple's payout landed and he describes it as the first time the company had real gross profit it could retain without reinvesting. He says the company owes nearly nobody, and that for many months it did not turn a profit.
- He says about 300K more was still on the way and that profit was around 600K gross after debts were paid.
- He puts ad spend on a credit card and says he hit his card limit, with a balance of about 321k, so he paid it down as soon as the payout arrived.
- His point on money and time: a parking ticket only hurts because of the time it takes to earn the fine back. When touching a lot of money a month, you can absorb small costs like that without guilt.

## Focus versus lifestyle

- He says he does not vlog often because most days are repetitive desk work.
- His claim: "You can't have a super exciting life and also make an insane amount of progress. You need to pick one or the other."
- He says he balances this well but is "nowhere near the place I want to be", and that a co-founder worked much harder, partly because of clearer authority and a mentor's wisdom.
- He sees the next 3 to 6 months as his chance to turn the ship around and make Quitter ubiquitous, where "everyone knows what Quitter is".
