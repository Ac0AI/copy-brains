## Why getting people to care is the hardest part of marketing

- Alex says getting people to care is "pretty much the hardest thing you have to do as a person."
- Building something you love (art, an album, an app) guarantees nothing. He describes releasing a piece he was sure would "take over the world" and getting nothing.
- Everyone has their own life going on. "Why would they care about you and what you've released? Doesn't matter if it's good."
- Thought experiment: release Drake's or Kanye's exact discography, same albums and covers. Without an audience, "go find some listeners." Distribution and attention are the problem, not the quality of the work.
- He thinks this is what kills most people's ambitions. Friends who launched apps inspired by his and Chris's started with strong self-belief, got no traction, then doubted themselves ("am I delusional?") and gave up.
- His advice: expect to be "humbled very, very hard" when the world doesn't care on day one, then "stay persistent, stay consistent."

## Image and persona as a way to get people to care (music example)

- For music, Alex says caring starts with your image and who you are as a person, not just the songs.
- SD Kid: his covered face leaves people's minds to "fill in the blanks" and assume he's cool. Alex argues that unmasked, the same music might not hit the same way.
- Drake: "If Drake was a loser, no one would listen to Drake." The artist's lifestyle and persona make people sing the music.
- Joji: he was Filthy Frank, a big YouTube creator in a pink suit, but wanted to make sad music. Alex says that character couldn't credibly do it, so he dropped it and became someone new, and people can now sing his music "unironically."
- Alex applies this to himself: he decided not to tie his name too closely to software or data ("I can't be too nerdy"), because it would hurt his image and the audience he wants for music.
- His intended persona: a young guy in London making exceptional music, dresses well, drives a nice car, has on-point Instagram captions, and a way of life that is "amazing to follow."
- Takeaway: "Getting people to care sometimes is as simple as inspiring them."

## Put the user first in every piece of marketing (apps)

- Don't go on Reddit and say "I just released this app, download it." Alex says "that's not how you get people to care."
- In every piece of marketing, put the user first and show them their life could be incredible if they used the app. "People care mostly about themselves."
- Example with his app Reset, addressed to a viewer named Andrew: "You can become the best version of yourself. I know you're not in the place you want to be right now, but you can be if you try Reset."
- The reader then thinks about being healthy, happy and stronger, and tries it "because it's for me."
- The switch: "Instead of being like 'download my app,' you're like 'transform your life.' These are very different things."

## Don't copy content you don't believe in

- Alex criticises posting TikTok content you don't believe in and are only copying because it works. He addresses this to Lucas Plumbridge and Chris, who worked on the Discover app.
- He grants "there is some element of truth" in copying what works, but says you must "always put yourself first."
- Questions he suggests asking: What do you believe the world needs? What is it missing? How can you improve?
- His claim: if you get that right, "everything else should follow."

## Inspire to sell

- Core rule: always ask "how do I make the other person inspired?"
- "If you can inspire someone, you can sell to them," whether the product is an app, your music or your modelling.
- Marketing your own products yourself is, in his words, the hardest thing you have to do. Persistence matters because people "will figure out in due time."
