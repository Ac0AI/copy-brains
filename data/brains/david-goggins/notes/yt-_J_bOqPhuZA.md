## Embracing the suck: how David Goggins went from 297 pounds to Navy SEAL

David traces his turnaround to one night watching Navy SEAL training on TV. The source gives the full arc from a violent childhood to three Hell Weeks, plus the habits he built on the way.

## Starting from the bottom

- David describes a childhood of abuse. His father beat him and his mother, and the kids hardly went to school because they were bruised up and working in the family business.
- He had a learning disability. A third-grade teacher wanted to put him in a special school.
- He says that teacher "managed my expectations": she looked at a kid who was struggling socially and decided what he could be.
- A sophomore-year basketball game ended with the visiting crowd chanting racial slurs at him, the only black person in the stadium. His JV coach, Mr. Trout, came into the locker room and cried with him.
- At the time he saw only hate. At 42 he looks back with "clear eyes" and sees what the coach did for him.
- By 20 to 21 he had gone from 175 to 297 pounds. He says he was "everything everybody said I was going to be".
- He was spraying restaurants for cockroaches at night. He says it is not a bad job, but he didn't want to do it.

## The night that changed his life

- He came home, turned on the TV, and went to shower. Something pulled him back to sit down and watch a documentary on Navy SEAL training.
- He watched many candidates ring the bell, which means you quit, and put their helmets down.
- At the end, about 15 to 20 men sat in dress whites. The commanding officer said: "We live in a world where mediocrity is often rewarded. These men up here detest mediocrity."
- David says that line forced him to look at himself. He wasn't even mediocre; he was "at the bottom of the barrel of life".
- The show held him accountable for what he wasn't facing.

## The four-lane highway vs. the shovel

- David's metaphor for life choices: most people choose the four-lane highway, the easy route with gas stations and signs telling you the next service stop is 20 miles away.
- Off in the corner sits a shovel. Nobody wants it, because the shovel means you are going to hurt, suffer and hit rock.
- When you hit rock you have to go get more tools, but you keep digging.
- He had always chosen the highway. His decision was to pick up the shovel everyone else decides not to take.
- That decision led him to join the military and go to Navy SEAL training.

## Training the mind to remove every exit

- The normal mind, when something hits you that you're afraid of or not good at, asks "why am I here anyway? The water is too cold, the sun's too hot, I'm getting up too early."
- David trained himself to ask instead "how the hell can I get through this?"
- He refused to give himself a way out and built a "wall around all the ways out in my mind".
- He says he had to become obsessed, and that no matter what was in front of him he had to figure out a way to overcome it.
- SEAL training, as he describes it: 30 hours of continuous training with maybe two hours of sleep, and the first weeks prepare you for the hard week.

## Three Hell Weeks in one year

- He believes he is the only person in history to go through three Navy SEAL Hell Weeks in one year.
- First attempt: his legs were broken and he had double pneumonia, so he was rolled back to day one, week one.
- Second attempt: he broke his knee during Hell Week, limped for weeks, and couldn't continue. He was rolled back again.
- Captain Bowen, in charge of SEAL training, told him this third Hell Week would be his last chance. David was on crutches with a broken-down body and was given a couple of months, which wouldn't be enough to heal.
- David contrasts "managing expectations" with "exceeding expectations". He says Bowen's challenge, not a lowered bar, is why he's here today.
- His workaround for the third Hell Week: he duct-taped his ankles up to the calf every morning, put a black sock over the tape, and ran using his hip flexors instead of flexing his shins.
- He says it was a bad Hell Week and a man died that Thursday morning. David went on to become a Navy SEAL.

## Greatness and friction

- "Greatness is not something that you meet once. It's something that you meet thousands of times in your life", and you don't reach it without constant pursuit.
- Calling yourself great means you have stopped growing.
- He doesn't triple down on strengths. He works on weaknesses.
- "Without friction there's no growth." His answer to how he became who he is: "I put a bunch of friction in my life and I grew."

## Calloused-mind routine: do the thing that sucks every day

- Being mentally tough is a lifestyle, not a one-time event.
- He skips the snooze button and does the unwanted things:
  - Get up instead of hitting snooze.
  - Run when you don't want to run.
  - Swim when you don't want to swim.
  - Make your bed and clean your house.
  - Study when you don't want to study.
- "That's how you start to callus your mind."
- If you say you'll wake up at 4 a.m. to run, wake up at 4 a.m. It's going to suck and it's not going to be fun.
- His rule: "Do something that sucks every single day of your life. That's how you grow. Embrace the suck."
