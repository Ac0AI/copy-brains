## Overcoming a Rough Start: Background and Early Struggles

- ThePrimeagen describes a difficult start. His dad died when he was seven. His mom worked about 80 hours a week raising three kids, so "most of the time I was raising myself" and he never learned to take things seriously.
- He graduated high school with a 2.16 GPA, around the middle of his class, and one teacher called him the worst student she had ever had.
- He saw porn at age four and says it was a near-constant presence in his life until he finally quit it.
- After a breakup he put all his identity and worth into, he went through a period of despair and stopped caring about his life. He did heavy drugs (pot, mushrooms, LSD, cocaine, meth).
- His grandfather offered to pay for college and he got into Montana State University. He picked computers because he typed fast and loved video games, and figured he could make games.
- At first he mostly partied at school, with drinking, cocaine and LSD to the point of vision problems lasting years, and he dropped out.

## The Turning Point: Choosing a Different Path

- He describes a very real encounter with God where he saw himself standing at a fork. One side was more of the same unhappiness, the other was trying something different. He decided to try the other path.
- Nothing changed immediately. He was still addicted to porn and still did drugs. But something in his heart changed, and every time he did those things it felt like a conscience had woken up.
- He passed that semester with Cs and Bs, which he calls the first time he ever really passed anything.
- He later fell back in with old friends, intentionally dropped out again to avoid the wrong road, and tried a local school. He felt defeated and unsure who he was.
- He says avoiding people made drugs easier to drop, while cigarettes and porn were much harder because you can do them alone.

## The Calculus Moment: Hard Work Beats Smarts

- He returned to Montana State and took Calculus 1 after failing pre-calculus twice and barely passing it on the third attempt.
- His routine: 3 hours of calculus class five days a week, then 1 to 3 hours with tutors at the math learning center, which was open four days a week.
- By week three he was cruising. He finished the 2-hour final in under 30 minutes with the highest grade in the class.
- His takeaway: "smarts will get you far but hard work will get you further any day of the week."
- That started a love of learning and a drive to pursue excellence. He kept being among the best in his classes.

## Breaking Addictions and Going All In

- He says he quit porn roughly his "5,000th time" trying. Eventually his head and heart connected, it stayed gone, and smoking left with it.
- After that he felt "unstoppable" in the sense that he had proven to himself he could learn anything.
- He went nuts on programming, tried to start his own company and worked about 80 hours a week.
- He admits this hurt his relationship with his wife. He wishes he had been a little less enthusiastic and fostered the relationship better.
- He was rejected by Google and by local companies, going "from the top of the top to the bottom pretty quick." He then took a job at a local company.

## Choosing Joy Regardless of the Job

- He hated that first job and was bored. Then he committed that he would find joy in the situation regardless of how entertaining the job was: "I don't need to be happy because of my job."
- He made that decision on his way to work. While getting coffee, someone from a building next door asked if he programmed and offered him a job at 50% more money. He took it.
- That job was hard and awful but amazing. It leveled him up a lot, though it strained his marriage. He says he probably should have said no more often, but it was good long term for his career. He calls that a hard balance and says he had no good way of saying no then.

## How He Got Into Netflix

- He went to a conference where he learned about RxJS, put it on his resume, and kept learning and playing with it.
- Netflix was looking for people who knew RxJS, saw it on his resume, offered him an interview, and he got the job.
- He has been at Netflix around eight or nine years.
- He credits it to "hard work with a little bit of luck, preparation with timing."

## Advice: Daily Resolutions and Giving It Time

- He says there was no magic bullet and no single night things went from bad to good. It was a decision he had to make every day, multiple times a day, and it took years.
- He dislikes New Year's resolutions because there is no point in time when you suddenly become someone different. His alternative: make it a daily or even minute-by-minute resolution. "If you have to say no a thousand times, 10,000 times," you pursue the change you want.
- To people just learning to program who feel defeated, or junior engineers who hate their jobs, he says keep building skills, pursue what is good and valuable, and give it time.
- It took him about 6 years, and he says others may be smarter and take less. His message: don't give up quickly, keep pushing.
