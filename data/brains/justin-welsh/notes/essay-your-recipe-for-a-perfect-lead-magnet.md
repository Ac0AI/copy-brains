## Turn the lead magnet into an honest on-ramp: solve one early problem completely

Justin's complaint about common lead magnets is that they "teach you 10% and then sell you the other 90%." He agrees prospects can tell when information is being held back, and that feeling tricked "erodes any trust you've built." His alternative is a lead magnet that gives away a full solution and still leads naturally to the paid product. He shared this in response to a question from his Creator MBA community.

- The magnet should not look "incomplete on purpose."
- The paid offer picks up where the free one honestly ends.
- Trust is the asset being protected.

## The 'early narrow' problem concept

Justin's key idea is the "early narrow" problem. It is a crucial early step in the customer's journey that, once solved, unlocks the rest of the "wider journey."

- Solving it fully does not require holding anything back.
- It matters more than a late-stage problem: a mistake at the first step makes the rest "a lot more difficult - probably impossible," while a mistake at the last step is minor (his example: a pizza that is "a bit too burned").
- Problems of this type "make wonderful lead magnets."

## Step 1 and 2: map the journey, then pick the early narrow step

Justin's process starts with the customer's overall journey, the one your paid products and services help with.

1. List everything the ideal customer needs to figure out to reach their big goal.
2. Order the list into the most logical sequence.
3. Pick the early narrow problem from that sequence.

His worked example is someone teaching home pizza-making through courses and cookbooks. The steps are: make good dough, roll it out well, choose the sauce, choose the amount of toppings, heat the pizza oven correctly, get the pizza onto the peel correctly, rotate it correctly, and get time, temperature and doneness right. Dough is the early narrow problem. He notes the steps are "easy to read and VERY hard to do," and says he messed up his first ten pizzas himself.

## Step 3: solve it completely, then sell the rest

The lead magnet should fully solve the narrow problem. In the pizza example, it teaches excellent dough for a home pizza oven, start to finish. Justin lists two results of this approach:

- **No one is tricked.** The magnet promised to solve the dough problem and delivered.
- **The rest of the journey is unlocked.** The customer can do the first critical step successfully and carry on. You become "a trusted guide early in their journey," which makes you the logical choice to keep working with.

At the end of the magnet, mention the remaining problems and position the paid course or cookbooks as the solution to them. His sample close: "Now that you have the perfect pizza dough, you're ready to join 1,000+ other students in transforming your dough into world-class home pizza. If you found my dough lesson helpful, consider joining my course today where you'll learn the secrets to rolling your dough, choosing the correct toppings, and a complete masterclass on using your pizza oven..."

## The lead magnet recipe (summary checklist)

1. Write down all the steps prospects must take to reach their ultimate outcome.
2. Arrange the steps into a logical sequence.
3. Identify an "early narrow" problem: a crucial early step that, when solved, unlocks the rest of the journey.
4. Design a lead magnet that "100% solves that narrow problem" and give it all away generously.
5. Sell the remainder of the journey in a simple video, an email series, or inside the lead magnet itself.
