## Justin's path to $10M: timeline, channels and margins

- Passed $10M in total revenue after 5 years and 9 months, built solo with no paid ads and no employees.
- Milestones: 29 months to the first $1M, then 9 months to $2M, 6 to $3M, 5 to $4M, 4 to $5M and $6M (same week), 3 to $7M, 3 to $8M, 5 to $9M, 6 to $10M.
- Channels were 100% organic: LinkedIn, X/Twitter, Instagram, his newsletter and SEO.
- Profit margin is about 89% today, down from about 99% at the start, because he added software, outsourced some work (website redesign, personalization/segmentation) and travels for work.
- He argues 90% margins "aren't attainable in nearly any other type of business" but are realistic for a solopreneur.
- Current revenue mix:
  - Products: $6.75M
  - Consulting: $1.17M
  - Sponsorships: $795k
  - Subscriptions: $695k
  - Community: $630k
- Team: his wife helps a lot, an outsourced VA answers support tickets, and he partners with people when needed (e.g. Brennan Dunn). He no longer does anything SaaS-related.
- He says the path isn't a template ("you may not have the same luck or timing") and that he is an outlier. It took six years and 5K+ pieces of content.
- His long-game advice: start a side project, build it to 60-70% of your salary, then go all in.

## Steps 1-6: Content, signal, a service business and a niche

- **Create noise.** He wrote content daily before he had a business, just to find his voice. He began on LinkedIn about building a SaaS unicorn as CRO, with no strategy. The key is volume, not perfection.
- **Find the signal.** He tracked engagement metrics (comments, shares, DMs with questions), not vanity metrics, and doubled down on what resonated. He built a daily LinkedIn system that grew him from 2,000 to over 750,000 followers. At this point he had about 15,000.
- **Start with a service business.** Founders DM'd him about SaaS, he answered every DM, and then offered consulting. He argues your first business should be a high-touch, high-value service: you learn the market from customers and get immediate cash flow.
- **Find your ideal customer.** Look for the sweet spot between customers you love and those who love you. His was early-stage SMB SaaS founders in healthcare. "The riches are in the niches", and you can't niche down until you know who you serve best.
- **Double your rates.** With a niche, happy customers and testimonials, he doubled his rates, wrote content aimed at that niche and declined work outside it. Loop: higher rates = better clients = better results = even higher rates.
- **Reduce your time.** Instead of earning twice as much, he worked half the time for the same income and used the freed time to build more automated income. His rule: optimize for time, not just money.

## Steps 7-12: Productizing and pricing the first course

- **Listen to your inbox.** Rereading LinkedIn DMs to find problems to productize, he found repetitive questions about SaaS sales and many about LinkedIn itself. "Your customers will tell you what to build next."
- **Test cheaply.** At 21K+ followers, people asked how he grew. He made a short course, *The LinkedIn Playbook*, sold at $50 and promoted with posts. It was casual, with no high production value. Don't overthink the first product.
- **First sales.** Listed on Gumroad on April 16, 2020, it made $10,482 in the first month. It was a different niche than his consulting, which confused him; his advice is to follow what customers will pay for.
- **Run with what works.** Over the next 15 months the Playbook brought in $75K. He kept consulting through VC networking, events like SaaStr and guest posts on other consultants' blogs. He says to run in two directions if you must; experiments show the path.
- **Raise price after building trust.** He rebuilt it as *The LinkedIn Operating System* with a 3x-4x price increase. The $50 price was his "trust tripwire": charge $50, deliver 100x the value, build a loyal base, then raise prices as you deliver results. Customers should press play and see results within days.
- **Align content and product.** With 100% of his LinkedIn content on audience growth and a product directly tied to it, the second version grossed $186K in 3 months, versus $75K in 15 months for the first. "When you go 'all in' in one direction, revenue explodes."

## Steps 13-15: Affiliates and the community he closed

- **Affiliate army.** Halfway through the course, students are prompted to leave a testimonial and join the affiliate program, and the same automation runs at the end. Result: 3,247 affiliates generating $600K in revenue. "Your best customers can be your best marketers."
- **Community.** Selling about $2K/day in info products, his interest in consulting faded. He opened a private creator community at $199 per year, with course completers prompted to join.
- **Closing it.** It was a $15K MRR business, but he felt he had to be on Slack 24/7. After 15 months he shut it down. His takeaway: communities require constant attention, and "Sometimes the right business decision can feel wrong." Trust your instincts about the life you want.

## Steps 16-19: New channel, second course, MRR and newsletter

- **Twitter.** With the freed time he started writing on X and reached 480K followers in under 2.5 years, reusing his LinkedIn growth know-how.
- **Second course.** While on a podcast with Dickie Bush and Nicolas Cole, their audience was struck by his simple content process. That led to *The Content Operating System*. His rule: your audience tells you what they want to learn next.
- **Monthly Templates.** He was making content templates for himself each week, so he tested them as a $9 upsell on each course. After 8 months it had 2,584 subscribers and $23.2K MRR. "The best products solve problems you're already solving for yourself."
- **Newsletter.** With 10,000+ students and template subscribers he wasn't emailing, he launched *The Saturday Solopreneur* in January 2022: one actionable idea each Saturday, readable in 4 minutes. It reached 185K subscribers in 40 months while he prunes the list regularly. "Consistency matters more than perfection."

## Steps 20-22: Sponsorships and the Creator MBA launch

- **Sponsorships.** With 175K+ readers, he sells two slots per issue at $2,500 each, or $5,000 per weekly issue. Sponsors like Kajabi, Stan, Circle, Delphi and Taplio have booked 12x-15x each. Principle: build the audience first, monetize second.
- **Flagship product.** In 2023 he started *The Creator MBA*: 111 lessons, 19 hours of video, plus a 14-step framework you can watch in 2 hours to have your first business. He says a flagship "should be your best work. Don't rush it."
- **Personalized launch.** He brought in Brennan Dunn, an expert in personalized online product launches. This delayed release about six months. They built an 18-week personalized launch plan.
- **Result.** Launched January 16, 2024, it sold $1.6M in six days. His lesson: "The work happens before the launch, not during."

## Step 23: Unsubscribe Substack and membership

- Nearing $10M, he wanted to write creatively and missed person-to-person connection, so he started a Substack called *Unsubscribe* and launched a membership alongside it.
- The Unsubscribe Membership includes real-time chat, a live weekly Q&A, monthly workshops and in-person networking events.
- It became a $19K MRR business in about one month.
- His principle: never stop experimenting, since the best businesses evolve with your interests.
- Closing principles: pay attention to signals, follow the paid customers, build what people need, and stay consistent.
