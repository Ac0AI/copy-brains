## Pick a narrow audience before you create anything

- Justin cites streaming: in 2021 Netflix and Hulu fell from 65% to 48% market share, while niche streaming services grew at more than 2x the rate of the majors. His reading: "niche content resonates."
- He tells creators to build their own niche "streaming service" for the right audience.
- Target a specific person with a specific problem. "Help small businesses" is too vague. "SaaS companies in the finance vertical, below $10M in revenue" is the level of specificity he recommends.
- The tighter the audience, the more likely they are to find your "signal" in a world of generic "noise."
- He says most creators fail because they have no plan. They post daily, get no traction, and quit. He calls a growth plan the actual "how" behind an audience goal, and it replaces random content and products nobody buys.

## Stand out with non-obvious content (Category Pirate)

- A niche gets you a small group of followers. Over time, sharing only obvious tactics people can learn anywhere makes meaningful content harder to produce.
- His fix is non-obvious content: a brand-new thought process for your niche that makes you radically different from others.
- He endorses Nicolas Cole's "Category Pirates" idea. Cole's point, which Justin quotes: you don't need everyone to care, "just a small pirate ship of folks who agree with your unique perspective," and that is enough to get paid to do what you love.
- Warning: "if you just say what other people are saying, you become a commodity."
- He credits interesting, unique, non-obvious things to say for growing past 600,000 LinkedIn followers, which he calls the easiest place to build a brand.
- Questions he asks to find your viewpoint:
  - What does everyone believe that you don't?
  - What is nobody in your niche talking about that they should be?
  - What are the biggest mistakes people make without realizing it?
  - Why are you worth following when others aren't?

## Tell a real backstory people can see themselves in

- A resonant backstory, he says, is gasoline on the fire once you have a niche and a unique perspective.
- He calls this building a better backstory. Like a startup founder, you need a story that compels people to follow you, and they must "literally see themselves in you."
- It can't be made up or fake. It should be your real story, stitched together so it is easy and exciting to follow.
- He has a 7-step process for doing this and published it as a free guide ("Build a Better Backstory").

## Do unscalable things early

- Justin says the idea of putting this on autopilot early is "couldn't be further from the truth." In the beginning you must do things that don't scale.
- His own early list:
  1. Published daily to social media, often 2-3 times per day.
  2. Replied to every comment, every morning, for over an hour.
  3. Responded to 85%+ of the DMs he got on LinkedIn and Twitter.
  4. Commented on 10-15 people's content every morning for over a year.
  5. Attended over 300 Zoom meetings with followers in his first 2 years.
- He ties these activities to growth past 600,000 followers on LinkedIn and 495,000 on X/Twitter.
- He says he cannot overstate how important it is to be everywhere when you start. Many people call this "creating value," and he argues it needs a deeper definition, endorsing Daniel Vassallo's take on it (the quote itself is not included in the source).

## The 4-step plan to 1,000 true fans

1. Think like a niche streaming service: a specific audience with a specific need.
2. Become a Category Pirate: a unique, non-obvious perspective inside that niche.
3. Tell a story worth following: a real backstory your audience sees themselves in.
4. Do unscalable things: daily posting, replying, DMs, commenting, and calls with followers.

His claim: nail these four and "you are absolutely on your way to 1,000 true fans."
