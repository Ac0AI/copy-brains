## The 1-3-5 content method: one pillar piece becomes 16 pieces

Justin's 1-3-5 method is a repurposing system: create one substantial pillar piece, extract three core ideas, and turn each idea into five micro pieces. The aim is to produce enough content for hungry algorithms without burning out. He has posted on LinkedIn and Twitter every day for five years, and his framing is "Work smarter, not harder", because "we aren't rewarded by the hours we spend, we're rewarded by the results we produce."

- He uses his newsletter as the pillar piece of content.
- He says the idea is adaptable ("the sky's the limit"), so creators should tailor it to their own situation.

## Step 1: Create one pillar piece

Justin starts with one comprehensive piece that goes deep on a topic relevant to the audience.

- Formats that can serve as the pillar: a long Twitter thread, a detailed blog post or newsletter, or an in-depth YouTube video.
- Pick a topic that addresses a big pain point or question the audience has. His example: "A Step-by-Step Guide to Launching Your First Digital Product."
- He calls this the hardest part of the process, but it makes the rest of the production "much faster and easier" because most of the work is done first.

## Step 2: Extract three core ideas

From the pillar, he pulls three main concepts or tips. Each should be substantial enough to stand alone as valuable content.

Example from the digital product launch pillar:
1. Validating your product idea
2. Creating a minimum viable product (MVP)
3. Building an effective launch strategy

## Step 3: Turn each core idea into five micro pieces

For each of the three ideas, he creates five pieces of micro-content:

- Two short written posts, pulling impactful sentences from the pillar content.
- One short-form video summarizing the key point in 60 seconds or less.
- One carousel post breaking the concept down in 5-10 slides.
- One poll or question post: make a statement, then ask a question to engage the audience and gather information.

He says to mix and match based on the kind of content you like producing most.

## The math

- 1 pillar + (3 ideas x 5 micro pieces) = 16 pieces of content.
- The pieces can be used now and repurposed later.
- He hopes the math motivates creators to spend quality time making the best pillar piece they can, since everything else is derived from it.

## Why the approach works

- **Consistent messaging:** going deep on a topic and then breaking it into micro-posts means "beating the same drum with some variety," which reinforces brand and message.
- **Meets different consumption preferences:** some people prefer in-depth articles, others prefer quick visuals or short videos. The format spread reaches people where they consume best.
- **Smarter, not just more:** "This method isn't just about creating more content - it's about creating smarter content."
- **Sustainable cadence:** "consistency > perfection." The method lets creators provide value regularly "without running yourself ragged."
