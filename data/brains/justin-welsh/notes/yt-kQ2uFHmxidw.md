## Choosing a sub-niche: broad topic, niche, sub-niche

Justin says the biggest LinkedIn mistake is being unfocused, and the second is having no niche at all. His example of an unfocused week: sales leadership on Monday, a funny kid story Tuesday, Father's Day Wednesday, a Bob Marley song Thursday. Readers should know exactly who he serves and how he helps them.

- Formula: broad topic, then niche, then sub-niche. Example: athletes, then runners, then marathoners.
- "When you talk to everybody you talk to nobody." He cites Seth Godin: "Everyone is not your customer."
- Start with one niche and get very good at it before expanding. Splitting time between baking cupcakes and 401k tips leaves an audience asking "are you talking to me?"
- His daily content is always about growing on LinkedIn, so his 115,000 followers know what to expect each morning.
- Too narrow is also a risk (marathoners aged 35-50 in Des Moines). Aim for a niche where you can find "100 to 1,000 true fans", which is enough to build a business.
- Six ways to niche down: price and demographics, level of quality, psychographics, vertical and industry, geographics.
- Be patient: niching down loses some existing followers, but over time you become the go-to person. He quotes Godin: "No niche is too small if it's yours," though he says he doesn't fully believe that.

## Dollars over dopamine: what content should do

Justin argues that likes, polls and feel-good viral stories feel great but do nothing for a business.

- His example is the viral story of a UPS driver delivering Christmas presents during COVID: thousands of likes, irrelevant to a consulting or bakery business.
- Polls (ketchup vs mustard) and gimmicks produce reactions, then "zero" in your Stripe account.
- His rule: "dollars over dopamine." Engagement is not the goal.
- Everything you create should do one thing: move people into your tribe. Tribe members become buyers because they follow you for a specific reason.

## Three content types that pull people into your tribe

- **Polarize.** Not mean, but strongly opinionated. Example post: "You weren't born to spend 50 hours a week working on someone else's dream." About 30% hated it; the 70% who agreed became his tribe. He writes for his following, not for everyone.
- **Teach.** At least twice a week, with full steps and nothing hidden. Examples: an eight-step process for landing a client on LinkedIn, and a four-step, 12-sub-step process for raising consulting rates. Teaching builds trust because people apply it and it works.
- **Entertain.** One-liners like "Average action taker better than brilliant overthinker." Entertaining content is usually polarizing too.
- Expect 30-40% of readers to dislike his content. Aim for a 60/40 or 70/30 split. Content 100% like is bland; content 0% like is probably mean. Thick skin is required.

## Idea and writing tools, and beating "knowledge blindness"

- **Content matrix.** List topics in your sub-niche down the left. Across the top are eight post structures: actionable, motivational, analytical, contrarian, observation, X-first-Y, present vs. future, listicle. Add two more: upcoming week, and highs and lows from the week. Matching topics to structures yields many ideas fast. One attendee got 26 ideas in under an hour.
- **Writing process.** A three-step process he calls trailer, meat and recap.
- Many people think they aren't experts. He calls this "knowledge blindness." His test: your high school math teacher isn't Einstein, just ahead of the students. Don't compare yourself to Gary Vaynerchuk.
- "Create content for the you from two to three years ago." You only need to be ahead of the people you teach.

## Three ways to build a relevant tribe

- **Be a leader.** Share what you've already done. Ask: where was I 2-3 years ago, are there people there now, and can I get them where I am faster with fewer mistakes? Most of his own content is this.
- **Discover and curate.** If you lack expertise, do the research for your audience and package it. Example: Sumit Garg on Twitter breaks down Bezos's shareholder letters, Apple letters and mental models, and has about 40,000 followers at 22.
- **Interview and distribute.** Interview experts in your sub-niche and share the findings. Example: Chris Fladzook, a Yale senior, interviews famous founders through the entrepreneurs club and gets heavy engagement despite no tech experience.
- You can mix all three and test which your audience likes most.

## Inbound lead funnel: from LinkedIn to email

Justin wants to move people off LinkedIn because the algorithm can change any time. He wants a place where he controls the narrative and communication.

1. **Daily post.** Each morning he writes one post, hoping readers click through to his profile.
2. **Featured section with two links.** "TOF" (top of funnel) is a free LinkedIn advice article for people not ready to buy. "BOF" (bottom of funnel) is his course for ready buyers.
3. **Evergreen article for email.** A big, robust guide you update over time (still valuable in 5-10 years). Readers trade their email address for it, which opts them into his weekly newsletter.
4. **Automated emails.** Consistency matters, so weekly or bi-weekly (he usually writes once a week). He teaches using two or three offers to sell product, service or business. Each morning 50-60 people receive an email from him, and one or two buy. He calls it an automated machine that "takes some time" to build.

## Outbound leads with Google Alerts

His two-step outbound method needs only automation and no sales pitch.

1. Set up Google Alerts for ideal prospects. In his health-tech days, he alerted on every healthcare company raising a Series A (a company with fresh money to spend).
2. Save the alerts and, every Friday at 9am, message the CEOs who raised money, about 50 people a week.

- The message is only congratulations: no pitch, no PDF, no "here's why to pay attention to me." Example: "congrats on the round, I'm a healthcare technology veteran, love what you're doing, following you from Los Angeles."
- Replies led to calls and customers. He named Swell ($2.5M seed) and Okadak ($10M Series A) as examples that became customers.
- He usually lands a handful of customers from this.

He says he has helped 3,000 people start side hustles. His wife Jennifer grew from 2,500 to 40,000 followers in six months using his method.

- Outcomes drive content: identify your goal (consulting business, promotion, new job, speaking) and niche from there. His own advising serves only early-stage, transactional SaaS companies in healthcare technology.

Two hours, 30+ lessons, normally $250, offered here at $150 (he said he was about to raise the price).
