## Why Justin tests social media tactics constantly

- Justin treats social media as an experimentation lab. He is "always testing and measuring and modifying" his strategies.
- He credits a big part of his social success to trying new things to learn what works and what doesn't.
- His rule: social media algorithms are always on the move, and "as content creators, it's our job to adapt and experiment."
- He applies the same experimentation mindset to "everything else in your business."

## The 2022 Twitter-to-newsletter formula and why it stopped working

- Through 2022, Justin used Twitter to drive traffic to his website, with the goal of attracting newsletter subscribers.
- A November 2022 Tweet shows the formula. Engagement was low (158 vs. an average of 800+), yet it still produced 593 new subscribers.
- Takeaway: the Tweet's job was subscriber conversion, not engagement. He judged it on subscribers, not likes.
- Early in 2023, Twitter's algorithm changed and the formula stopped delivering. He couldn't get past 250 new subscribers on any given day, a sharp drop from a month earlier.
- He chose not to wait for things to "go back to normal." He wanted to recapture the old subscriber count and keep growing the newsletter, so he ran an experiment.

## The 1-Tweet CTA vs. 2-Tweet CTA experiment

- Justin named the test the "1-Tweet CTA vs. the 2-Tweet CTA."
- Hypothesis: the LinkedIn algorithm is known to dislike posts with links, and he suspected the new Twitter algorithm disliked Tweets with links too.
- Change: he moved the CTA (the link) out of the main Tweet and into a second Tweet, making a tiny thread of two short Tweets.
- Control: the original format put the link in the single Tweet.

## Results of the 2-Tweet CTA

- Tweet 1 had no CTA and generated 802K impressions.
- Tweet 2 had the CTA link and generated 39K impressions.
- The thread produced 870 new email subscribers, versus the roughly 250-per-day ceiling he'd been stuck at under the 1-Tweet format.
- The headline claim of the essay (the "4x conversion") rests on this comparison of the post-algorithm-change ceiling against this result.
- He kept using the trick afterward "because it seems to appease the new Twitter algorithm. For now, at least."

## Reusable playbook: keep the link out of the post that needs reach

- Put the hook or valuable content in the first post with no link, so the algorithm distributes it widely.
- Put the CTA link in a second post or reply. Only a fraction of viewers see it (39K of 802K impressions here), but it was enough to convert at scale.
- Justin says the same approach works for any CTA: newsletter subscribers, product purchases, "or whatever CTA you're after."
- Treat the tactic as temporary. Re-test when the algorithm shifts, since it is only known to work "for now."
