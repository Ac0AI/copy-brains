## The passive-selling model: don't sell on social, sell at the end of articles

Justin advises creators with big audiences and digital products (a course, recipe books) to stop asking for the sale in social content, because followers get turned off by "selling". His alternative is a micro-funnel:

- Short-form social content builds attention.
- A CTA moves people to the website.
- Articles or newsletter issues end with a "passive" CTA that sells the asset.

He frames the point as working "the numbers": get the product in front of enough interested people without saying "buy this thing". He also argues that after launch, sustained sales are what make a digital asset valuable, and that many entrepreneurs have no longevity strategy. A longevity strategy needs "a process and some data to get better over time", not complexity.

## Three parts of short-form content to track: hook, body, CTA

Justin treats every social post as three measurable pieces:

- **Hook:** Generally drives about 90% of success in impressions, meaning how many people see the post.
- **Body:** Where you show expertise. It should be helpful enough to show the reader you can solve their problem. He measures it by positive sentiment in the comments.
- **CTA:** Moves people from social content to the website. He measures it by how many people it sends over.

He says to publish enough content to have meaningful metrics on all three. Questions to track constantly:

- Which hooks drive the most impressions?
- Which body copy drives the most positive sentiment?
- Which CTA moves the most people to the website?

## Build "combos" from your top performers

After 3 to 6 months of publishing, Justin says you should know your top five hooks, body styles and CTAs. Then create "combos": the right hook with the right body and CTA. Using proven combos maximizes impressions and, in turn, website visitors.

His end-state is chain-linked content: best hook, then best body copy, then best CTA, then best "selling" article. He describes this as "maximizing every single piece of your entire micro-funnel."

## Send traffic to relevant articles, not the homepage

Justin says traffic from social should not go to your homepage. Send it to articles that expand on the specific problem and solution the social post covered. At the end of each article or issue, passively sell your asset, with no hard selling.

Example CTA, for an article on balancing fats, carbs and protein for a leaner body, ending with:

"If you're looking to make this easy, I have a recipe book with 250+ perfectly portioned, simple meals. Get lean, shredded, and healthy with these 30-minute or less recipes in my recipe book here."

He describes this as "Simple. Helpful. Non-aggressive. Really just a 'passive' CTA."

## Test which article sells best

Justin's process for finding the best "selling" article:

1. Put a passive CTA to your asset at the end of every article.
2. Rotate between about 20 different articles or newsletter issues.
3. Track the conversion rate from article reader to asset buyer.
4. Identify which article is set up to sell best.
5. Create similar articles for other topics.

He calls these articles "24/7/365 salespeople that cost you nothing other than the time it takes to write them", working while you eat, sleep and spend time with family.

## Illustrative math and who it works for

Justin's example of what a creator with a $30 recipe book might see by doing this effectively:

- 50,000 impressions
- 500 website visitors
- 20 purchases
- $30 per recipe book
- $600 in daily revenue
- $219,000 in yearly revenue

His framing: "Not a bad result for doing the same thing they're already doing." He says it is not just for big creators; anyone with meaningful traction online can do it. The case that prompted the guide involved two entrepreneurs in lower-margin coaching and product businesses, one with a 1M+ LinkedIn/X following and an online course, the other with 1M+ Instagram followers and multiple recipe books. Both were looking for higher-margin, more automated ways to increase profit.
