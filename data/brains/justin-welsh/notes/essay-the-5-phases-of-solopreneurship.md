## The 5 phases of solopreneurship

Justin frames a solo business as growing in five phases, each with its own priorities. His point: solopreneurs burn out by "doing the wrong things for the phase your business is in," so the first job is to zoom out and identify your phase.

- Phase 1: Stay Alive
- Phase 2: Build Systems
- Phase 3: Growth Focused
- Phase 4: Invest in Defense
- Phase 5: Automate the Business
- Each phase typically takes a year or two.
- He thinks about his business "in terms of years instead of days." His business moved faster than he expected, and he says yours doesn't have to (or need to).
- The differentiator is patience: "a willingness and determination to outlast most people."
- Planning cadence: long-term goals, yearly strategy, quarterly tactics, daily effort.

## Phase 1: Stay Alive (survive on low cost and free distribution)

- The start is about survival: keeping the "internet doors open and lights on." Justin argues this is where about 95% of people fail.
- Keep expenses low. Free or cheap website builders and email software are enough. No expensive software, fancy logos or brand systems yet.
- Leverage social media, which is basically free. Use Twitter and especially LinkedIn to share knowledge and expertise. The only investment is time.
- Stay away from ads and expensive marketing campaigns at this stage.

## Phase 2: Build Systems (once you have traction)

- Trigger: you have some traction, meaning you're making some predictable money.
- A system is a process you can follow to produce a predictable outcome. Examples he lists: idea systems, content systems, email and newsletter systems, customer support systems.
- This is where the structure of the business begins to form.
- Document every task you do repeatedly. Start by outlining the tasks in a document, so when it's time to do or delegate a task you have clear instructions. "No more forgetting how or when to do things."
- His own example: a documented system for writing his newsletter and turning it into 6-12 pieces of short-form social content, stored in his internal business playbook.

## Phase 3: Growth Focused (add weight to the foundation)

- Strong systems mean the foundation is less likely to crack when you add "additional weight," so this is when to start investing in growth.
- By now you should intimately know your customers and how to acquire more of them.
- Tactics:
  - Expand to another platform if you've been active on only one.
  - Start a newsletter, weekly video series or podcast to reach untapped channels.
  - Pursue strategic partnerships and collaborations with other creators, solopreneurs or business owners to extend reach without significant cost.
- His own path: for the first 2.5 years he created content exclusively on LinkedIn. Then he added Twitter, then the weekly newsletter, and more recently got serious about Instagram.

## Phase 4: Invest in Defense (build a moat against copycats)

- Success attracts copycats: content stealers, product copiers, and worse. That noise makes it harder to compete in crowded social media.
- Justin says he is currently in phase four. His defensive moves:
  - A professionalized brand system: improving brand systems, messaging and website, aiming to "set the standard for Solopreneur brands."
  - An SEO strategy: a 60-asset SEO campaign to bring more organic traffic to his website.
  - Email automation: he had never used it, and is planning a more customized journey for followers, subscribers and customers.
- The logic: rely less on social media and invest in things that give a "moat."

## Phase 5: Automate the Business (efficiency and predictability)

- Justin says he hasn't reached this phase, so his thoughts are speculative.
- Paid ads become a fit once systems are in place, branding looks great and landing pages convert at a high level. The goal is spending $1 to generate $2, "basically an automated cash machine."
- Consider a part-time project manager who can:
  - Organize assets: with thousands of social posts, hundreds of newsletters and articles, and many follower questions, have someone organize the archive for easy access.
  - Create a weekly plan: his goal is to own the creative work while a "taskmaster" tells him on Monday morning exactly what to do and why, keeping him on the most important priorities.

## Action step Justin recommends

- Reread the phases, work out where you are, find the relevant suggestions, and choose just one to move forward with next week.
