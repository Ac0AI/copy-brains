## Offer count and decision fatigue

Justin argues that too many offers lower sales through "decision fatigue": too many options wear out the buyer's brain and make a choice harder. His example is a Creator MBA Office Hours student with four service offerings, five digital courses at multiple price points and three community membership tiers. That business made a few thousand dollars a month. The student's reason was "I want to give people choices."

- Each added option is another decision point. It adds time and questions before the customer can move forward.
- "At some point, people would rather make no decision than the wrong decision."
- Analogies: an old-school diner with a 100+ item menu versus an eight-item menu (fewer choices means a faster decision), and spending 30 minutes scrolling Netflix and watching nothing.
- His question to ask of your own offers: "Are you really serving your audience by giving them more choices? Or are you just making it harder for them to say yes?"

## Why tiered courses stall buyers

Justin's example is a beginner course, an intermediate course, an advanced course and a bundle of all three. It looks logical, but the buyer starts thinking: "Should I start with beginner? What if it's too basic? Maybe I should get the bundle...but that's a bigger investment...which one is right for me?"

- The buyer does "mental gymnastics" and ends up choosing nothing.
- His own practice: each course he offers has one price, "That's it."
- The fix for courses: if you're just starting out, one course at one price is enough.

## Why tiered service packages stall buyers

Justin's example is three service tiers, such as $497, $997 and $2,997, each with a slightly different scope of work.

- The prospect compares packages and works out what they really need. They also wonder whether the extra $500 is worth it, or whether they could get some of the top option's work in the middle one. They probably choose nothing.
- His own practice when he offered consulting: one offer tailored to the most common needs of his clients, "Take it or leave it."
- The fix for services: a core service offering, with (maybe) an accelerated offer to get results faster.

## Recommended offer set: one course, one community, one service

Justin's guidance for cutting offers down:

- **Courses:** one course with one price when starting out.
- **Community:** at most two membership types, community and private mastermind.
- **Services:** a core offering, maybe plus an accelerated version.
- "Most of the time, one course, one community, and one service is perfect."

## Using the three offers as a natural value ladder

With one of each product type, Justin says you create a natural value ladder that matches how customers want to reach their intended outcome. The customer's choice becomes only how they want to "do it":

- **Course:** "I'm going to buy the course and do this on my own."
- **Community:** "I'm going to join the community and do this with other people."
- **Service:** "I'm going to hire you 1:1 and have you do it for me."

He frames this as the better way to give people options: choice between modes of doing it (alone, with others, done for you), not between near-duplicate products and price points.
