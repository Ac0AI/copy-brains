## The weekly preview ritual

Every Friday, Justin runs a "weekly preview": a short review of the coming week's calendar built around eight questions. It is not a to-do list or a calendar glance. The goal is a schedule that "supports what's truly important: my life."

- He pitches it for anyone "flying by the seat of your pants" or jumping on back-to-back calls with no room for creative work.
- He says to steal, adapt or drop questions; not all eight are needed.
- The point is to "have a process to ensure that your life isn't flying by without stopping to think about whether you're truly living."
- He frames it as aligning the calendar with his values, goals and the realities of a one-person business.

## Remove, automate, delegate

The first three questions cut the workload before anything else is scheduled.

- **What can I remove?** Schedules fill up with commitments that don't serve bigger goals. "Elimination is your best friend as a Solopreneur or Creator." Look ahead and cut time spent on things that don't support goals.
- **What can be automated?** Each week he looks for tasks to automate to free up time and mental energy.
- **What should be delegated but isn't?** Delegate tasks that cost more time than they're worth.

## Automation example: newsletter sponsorships

Sponsorships were once a clunky manual process: a form to fill out, then long email back-and-forth with the sponsor. Now the flow is:

Buy button → Submission of Copy → Calendar Invite → Newsletter Publication

- This saves him about 30 minutes a week.
- It is also a simple experience for the sponsor.
- The newsletter reaches 200,000+ entrepreneurs (per the essay's sponsor call-to-action).

## Delegation example: customer service

- He used to spend 45 minutes every single day on customer service tickets: invoices, login questions, "can't find something."
- He outsourced it to his virtual assistant, who handles every issue that hits her inbox. She has the time and focus to keep customers happy, and he no longer thinks about it.
- His regret: "I waited far too long to delegate anything. And I wish I hired her sooner."

## Protecting urgent and important work: urgency, meeting length

- **Urgent vs. important:** "We often mistake urgent for important." He scrutinizes the calendar for what is genuinely critical right now. A meeting that isn't important at this moment gets pushed 60-90 days out, making space for things that are urgent AND important.
- **Shorter meetings:** "Most meetings can be halved in length without losing substance or outcomes." The question he asks is whether any important meeting can be 50% shorter.
- If a meeting has no agenda, ask for one; the meeting can then likely shrink significantly.
- For podcasts, he tells hosts he can't do 60 or 90 minutes and that 45 minutes is his max. As a host, he applies the same principle.

## Life-side questions: screen time and fun

- **Get off the computer more:** He doesn't want his life lived in front of a screen, which is a daily challenge for someone who earns a living online. He prioritizes stepping away, engaging with the world and nourishing creativity offline.
- He built systems for creating Twitter and newsletter content (Content OS) and for growing quickly on LinkedIn (The Operating System) without spending exorbitant time online.
- **Maximize fun time:** Family time is "paramount," and it is easy to overlook in solopreneurship, so he carves it out deliberately.
- Each week he prioritizes trips, dinners, mid-week lunches, comedy shows and time with friends.
- His claim: he needs fun to keep creative juices flowing, and "the more fun I have with family and friends, the more creative ideas I get."

## Final check: is anything missing?

- After the first seven questions, he reflects on the new-look week, watching for gaps or opportunities missed on the first pass.
- It is a chance to confirm "with 100% certainty" that the schedule aligns with his values and immediate goals.
