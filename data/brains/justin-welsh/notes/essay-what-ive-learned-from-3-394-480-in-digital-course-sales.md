## Lesson 1: Building in public lifted conversion 2.7x and daily revenue about 8x

- Justin built his first course, The LinkedIn Playbook, quietly at home. It made $11,781 in its first month, about $380 per day.
- He built the updated version, The LinkedIn Operating System (released July 2021), completely in public. He shared each day's wins, losses, metrics and revenue.
- It took 91 days to build and made $94,651 in the first month, about $3,053 per day.
- Both products got roughly the same landing page views. The public build converted 2.7x higher, even at 3x the price, partly because people could see what he was building and were invested in the journey.

## Lesson 2: Price for accessibility to create word-of-mouth

- Justin notes creators with much smaller audiences selling a "masterclass" for $1,499. He thinks the trust and nurturing needed to close a deal like that is "pretty massive".
- His approach: price products affordably, make them as valuable as possible, and ask buyers to tell their friends if they enjoyed them.
- The result is a word-of-mouth affiliate engine. He has paid out over $138,000 in affiliate commission to generate $395,200 in sales.
- His rule of thumb: "It's easy to recommend a $150 product to your friends. $1,499? Not so much."
- He recommends pricing to be accessible so word-of-mouth does the distribution work.

## Lesson 3: Weekly testimonials are his strongest promotion

- Customer words convert prospects at a much higher rate than the creator's own words.
- Once per week he shares a simple testimonial or a DM from a happy customer. Course sales go up about 2x to 3x on those days.
- A normal day does $3,500 to $4,500 in course sales. A testimonial day often does $10,000 or more.
- He calls this the most effective promotion he has, and it is "extremely passive".
- Organic word-of-mouth compounds it: someone is talking about The LinkedIn Operating System online every single day, across 17,000+ customers.
- His advice: treat every testimonial as a chance to convert more prospects, and share them.

## Lesson 4: Keep an 80/20 give-to-ask ratio

- Justin creates about 50 pieces of content per month on LinkedIn and 55 on Twitter, 105 in total.
- He shares a testimonial about 8 to 10 times out of those 105, so he promotes less than 10% of the time.
- Weekly promotion therefore isn't excessive in the context of the whole content stream.
- His golden rule: give value 80% of the time or more, and promote 20% of the time or less.

## Lesson 5: Shorter courses get finished

- The LinkedIn Playbook runs about 75 minutes and had a 45% completion rate. He cites the average for course creators as 13.8%.
- Idea.Audience.Proof.Product runs 3.5 hours and had roughly a 25% completion rate. He didn't promote it much.
- He believes the longer course was the better, more helpful one, but that doesn't matter if people don't finish it. In hindsight he would have made it much shorter.
- He thinks the longer you've been in the creator space and the more well-known you are, the longer and more robust your courses can be.
- For beginners: teach people what they want to learn in 45 minutes or less to increase completion and adoption.

## Summary and takeaway

- His five lessons: build in public, price for accessibility, use testimonials, keep an 80/20 give/ask ratio, and keep courses short.
- His closing point: none of the lessons matter if you never get started.
- Headline figure behind the advice: over $3,000,000 in digital course sales (the essay title says $3,394,480).
