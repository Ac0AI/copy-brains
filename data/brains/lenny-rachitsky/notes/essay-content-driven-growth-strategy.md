## The five content-driven growth strategies, and Lenny's map of editorially generated SEO

Lenny maps content-driven growth on a 2x2. One axis is what you optimize for (SEO vs. virality). The other is who generates the content (users vs. employees). That gives five strategies:

- **User-generated SEO-optimized (UGSO):** users create public content that Google surfaces. Examples: Quora, Glassdoor, Reddit.
- **Editorially generated SEO-optimized (EGSO):** employees or contractors create content to rank for keywords. Examples: Ahrefs, Slidebean, HubSpot.
- **Data-generated SEO-optimized (DGSO):** a company auto-generates thousands of pages from UGC and proprietary data. Examples: Thumbtack, Zapier, Tripadvisor, Yelp, Grubhub.
- **User-generated virality-optimized (UGVO):** UGC that users share with friends, pulling them into the product. TikTok (watermarked videos) is the ultimate example, along with the Reface app. Airbnb listings and Zillow's Zestimate sit at the edges.
- **Editorially generated virality-optimized (EGVO):** one-off viral content from founders or the company, a.k.a. thought leadership or brand building. Examples: Spotify Unwrapped, Mr. Beast, Stir's one-off drops, Superhuman's early blog posts.

He had already covered UGSO/DGSO and the viral strategies in earlier posts. This piece zooms in on EGSO. He asked Twitter which companies do it best, and Slidebean, Ahrefs, Intercom and HubSpot came up repeatedly. Webflow is a bonus case. Intercom and Webflow aren't primarily SEO-driven, but he included them because their perspectives are instructive.

## Seven high-level takeaways on editorial content growth

Lenny distilled the lessons from the five companies into seven points:

1. **Start small.** Be scrappy and experiment with platform, topic and style until something works.
2. **Align on a clear goal.** Decide whether the goal is SEO traffic, virality, brand building or sales enablement.
3. **Hunt for opportunity.** Research topics where searchers are underserved, and deliver compelling, differentiated value to them.
4. **Give the reins to someone who cares.** The best content comes from people who care about the product, business and topic, usually founders or a passionate full-time employee.
5. **Think long-term.** Make sure there is a clear path to the investment making a significant dent in growth, and ask whether the upside is even worth it.
6. **Build a dedicated team.** Eventually you need at least five people dedicated to the effort, more if you do video.
7. **Be patient.** It took years for each company to see significant payoff.

## Scale benchmarks from the companies Lenny profiled

Lenny shared these numbers from each company:

- **Ahrefs:** 300+ posts, 1.5M visits/month. Top posts: Top 100 Most Visited Websites by Search Traffic, Affiliate Marketing for Beginners, Top Google Searches.
- **Slidebean:** 250+ YouTube videos, 160K visits/month. Top videos are company stories (Boosted Boards, Zoom vs. Skype, Spotify vs. Apple).
- **Intercom:** 870 posts and 262 podcasts, 100K+ visits/month and 75K+ listeners/month. Top posts cover RICE prioritization, design workshops and what a product manager does.
- **HubSpot:** in its early days, 30% or more of customers came from demand generated by blog posts. Its web traffic (organic, direct, referral) exceeds that of many business publications such as TechCrunch, so it treats other media outlets, not other SaaS brands, as its competition for online attention.

## Why these companies started investing in content

Lenny collected the origin stories:

- **Slidebean:** The "pitch deck" keyword on SEM was its best lead source. SEM was expensive and covered only about 5% of searches, so it moved to SEO. It published roughly 150 articles in a year and reached SERP positions 3-5 for most target keywords. That traffic drove growth for two years. It then judged that positions 1-2 were unlikely however many articles it published, so it moved to YouTube as "the next" search engine. It started with search-style videos, then moved to videos built for the YouTube algorithm (high CTR, high watch time, broad topics, sharing).
- **HubSpot:** It published before it had a product. Dharmesh Shah's OnStartups community showed that value-first content builds an audience. Inbound marketing means "create assets of value for your audience vs. pay money to interrupt them via traditional advertising." The media strategy has two parts: content to educate (blog posts found via search, with e-book, template or course upgrades) and content to influence (original stories on trends and case studies, via video and podcasts, aimed at a large audience).
- **Ahrefs:** There was no single decision. Tim Soulo inherited a small blog in 2015 and improved it gradually.
- **Intercom:** The founders came from the Web 2.0 blogging era, and Des Traynor wrote the first ~100 posts. Intercom deliberately chose high-quality editorial content to stand out from the SEO-driven norm. Customers who came through the blog and podcast spent more and stayed longer. Gated content must be a fair value exchange. More recently the focus has been enablement: making content usable by sales, account-based marketing and paid acquisition.

## Starting resources: cheap experiments and company-wide content incentives

- **Intercom:** For the first year, John Collins was the whole content team ("team blog"), backed by the brand design team. Everyone was incentivized to create content (blog posts, talks, podcasts) by adding content creation to personal and team goals, so it counted toward career growth. Nobody was paid extra.
- **Slidebean:** The founder edited the first YouTube videos, and team members with video backgrounds carved out time. Because experiments were so cheap, the team could trial-and-error for over 18 months before finding a format that worked. For about the first year the videos "sucked" and got no traffic.
- **Ahrefs:** A budget to find talent for the blog, plus borrowed designer time. Small steps, no tipping point.
- **HubSpot:** Early marketing hires all focused on creating content (blogs, social posts, webinars).

## Team structures at scale

- **Slidebean (YouTube):** a content/strategy manager, 1 lead animator and 2 junior animators (all full-time), 2 screenwriters (mostly research), and a community manager. Video production cost about $125K in 2020.
- **HubSpot:** a full media operation: blogs, content offers (e-books, templates, courses), HubSpot Stories, YouTube, Audio (podcasts, Clubhouse), the daily newsletter and Trends subscription (both run by The Hustle, which HubSpot acquired), and a Distribution team to plan promotion.
- **Intercom:** a team of 10 in Dublin, with three more roles opening. It has three pillars: Audiences (top-of-funnel content for support and success leaders, and where they get information), Enablement (reworking content for sales, such as turning commissioned research into slides, plus customer evidence), and Channels (SEO and the podcast).
- **Ahrefs:** a blog lead, two in-house marketers, one part-time technical writer who also sits on the product team, a video lead with three production helpers, and one graphic designer.

## How HubSpot operationalizes SEO content

- Full-time analysts build quarterly editorial calendars across all blogs, mixing search-first posts and influence-focused posts.
- The team knows how much net-new search traffic is available for each calendar, like a quarterly TAM. That tells them how much traffic they can expect and when they are running out on a topic.
- A team is alerted to posts losing traffic and optimizes them to recover traffic and conversions.
- Slidebean uses a tool to decide which videos to make.

## Advice for starting content-driven growth

Lenny's takeaways, using the founders' own words:

- **Choose SEO or viral deliberately.** Tim Soulo says SEO content is much easier than viral content because keyword research gives you topics. Viral content depends on creativity and unique ideas.
- **Master one channel before adding the next.** HubSpot started with a blog and focused on it for years. "There is always room to be better or different." Brands compete with every tweet, post and podcast, not just direct competitors. "Good no longer gets you results."
- **Set intentional goals and model them.** For demand generation, estimate available search traffic for your topics and apply rough conversion rates to size the demand TAM. A goal might be a blog worth 20% of needed demand, or a podcast that influences your audience.
- **Hold your nerve, then double down.** Intercom's John Collins says content "doesn't generate returns as quickly" as other marketing, and "content is a supertanker, not a speedboat." Once a format shows engagement, double down on it quickly rather than starting a second medium.
- **Video is expensive and long-term.** Slidebean says it takes months and dozens of videos to build an audience, and most startups need customers faster in a way they can control. When it works, the brand effect is strong. Slidebean says it wishes it had focused on brand sooner.

## Webflow: video education to lift activation, not just traffic

Lenny's bonus case shows content used for new-user success rather than top-of-funnel traffic. He notes that, unlike SEO, any B2B company can use video to increase activation or conversion.

- Webflow built Webflow University, free courses that help designers of all skill levels learn a powerful platform with no watered-down interface.
- Video was a priority from launch, when a founder recorded a full-site build walkthrough. In 2016 a community member posted a much higher-quality tutorial video and the community reacted positively, so Webflow adopted that direction. It eventually hired him to lead education.
- The team started as one full-time power user, working with support on docs and guides. Since 2016 it has added a dedicated team (producers, editors, designers), modern equipment, a soundproof studio and custom music and animation. The content marketing team uses freelancers and agencies, and plans to double headcount.
- People who enroll in the courses are "much more likely" to become subscribers. New courses attract new people and introduce use cases and features.
- Webflow's non-tutorial content has inspirational examples and useful resources for designers, with a fun, friendly social tone.
- Advice from Mischa Vaughn: "you need a devoted team with a clear charter." Find a leader, build a distinct vision and voice, and give them plenty of support.
