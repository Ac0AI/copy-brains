## Early-stage B2B: ramp to $1m ARR matters more than month-over-month growth

- Lenny asked about two dozen investors what counts as GOOD and GREAT growth by business stage. Across all of them, early-stage month-over-month (MoM) growth was not the headline metric.
- Reason given: percentages on a tiny base mean little. In Kimberly Tan's words (a16z), "10% vs. 20% growth at $100K ARR isn't substantial."
- Investors would rather see accelerating MoM growth (for example 10% to 12% to 15%) as a sign of momentum.
- If a MoM benchmark is needed below $1m ARR, answers typically fell in the 15%-25% range.
- What early-stage B2B investors focus on instead is how fast the company ramps to $1m ARR after going live.

## Benchmarks: 0 to $1m ARR after launch

- Lenny's summary: getting to $1m ARR within a year of launch is GOOD; getting there in three quarters (9 months) is GREAT.
- John Luttig (Founders Fund): "If you grew to $500k ARR in 3mo post-launch, that's more impressive than $1m in 24mo post-launch." He treats 0 to $1m ARR in 12 months or less as great. Exceptions exist, since not every company's success is measured in ARR since launch (Figma, open source).
- Sandhya Hegde (Unusual Ventures): "You start the growth clock the month you go GA."
- Grace Ge (Menlo): "Velocity is everything post-launch." As a company matures, it is less about convincing investors to dream the dream and more about proving it is real, executable and monetizable.

## Timing your launch strategically

- Once you have numbers, investors scrutinize them. Caitlin Bolnick Rellas (CRV): "The great irony is that once you start having numbers, investors start to really care what those numbers are."
- Luttig: launching with very little traction is worse than being pre-launch, because it shows the market or value prop is already demonstrably weak. "No data is better than bad/small data."
- Takeaway Lenny draws: be deliberate about when you launch, because the growth clock and the scrutiny start then.

## Slow starts can be forgiven if takeoff is strong

- Some investors don't care how long it took to get going, only how fast it grows once it does. One example: a business that grew revenue from $500k to $5m this year is attractive whether it launched 1 or 5 years ago. That investor noted some of their biggest winners took several years to find product-market fit.
- Kanyi Maqubela (Kindred): speed from $500K to $5M ARR has been less predictive-of-nothing than 0 to $1M; rather, it is more predictive than 0 to $1M. It indicates "minimum viable durability" of revenue that compounds over a longer period.
- During the 0 to $1M phase, companies are often still optimizing retention, experimenting with efficiency and segmenting customers.

## Without fast ARR growth, show retention and efficiency

- Rebecca Kaden (USV): revenue growth is an output of customer love and acquisition efficiency. The most important health factors are cohorted revenue retention and/or purchase behavior (ideally U-shaped cohorts where net revenue retention rises over a year) and marketing spend/revenue.
- Another investor looks for a leading indicator of ARR that compounds at a high rate: very productive salespeople, oddly short sales cycles, customers who rave and tell friends (typically giving oddly low CAC).
- Ellen Chisa (Boldstart): "It's a huge mistake to focus on the growth number before getting retention." Early numbers are small enough to hit "great" by brute force, which won't translate to continued growth and can leave you worse off than building fundamentals first.
- Quality of ARR matters as much as rate. Arra Malekzadeh (Craft) looks at the components (new, retained, expansion, resurrection, contraction, churn), customer concentration, sales efficiency, CAC payback, and new ARR versus sales and marketing spend.

## Early-stage consumer: engagement and retention before revenue

- For B2C, early investors look at intensity of use, sharing and retention rather than revenue growth.
- Mike Vernal (Sequoia): "For consumer, I focus on engagement and retention first and foremost. >50% DAU/MAU or >50% D30 retention is excellent. If you have both of those, only then do I worry about whether you can grow it."
