## Founder equity splits should reflect performance, not an equal cut
- Jason points out that Steph Curry doesn't make the same as the five players on the Warriors bench. Everyone is paid differently based on performance.
- He calls the equity split a hard conversation because people "lean socialist". Three founders means 33% each, four means 25% each, and that sours things when one partner stops showing up.
- He wants splits tied to performance and to what the team gets if it performs well. In his view, an even split is "just a math equation" and not a value-based one.
- This is his reason for treating the split as the foundation of the company, to be discussed openly at the start.

## Integrating outside hires into a close founding team
- Jason notes that senior hires from outside, who weren't part of founding the company, still need to feel like part of the core team.
- His example is Kevin Durant joining the Warriors. Durant never quite felt like part of the core team, which is the failure to avoid when an outsider joins a tight group.

## Founders are often the best early salespeople
- Jason says there is "honesty" and a transfer of enthusiasm when founders explain in the room why they built something. The buyer feels it across the table, or in his words today, directly through to the customer.
- The buyer's reaction he describes is "I want to be part of this."
- The hard part is getting later salespeople to carry the same conviction.

## Reputation compounds: how helping Travis Kalanick led to Uber
- Jason says someone at a party told him he was "lucky" to invest in Uber, and he agreed. Travis Kalanick, standing next to him, corrected the story.
- Travis said Jason had helped him with his first two companies, Scour and Red Swoosh. Whenever Travis needed help, Jason picked up the phone, had dinner with him and talked through the business.
- Travis's point was that this is why Jason got the investment. It wasn't luck.
- The tactic is to help founders early and for free, long before they are an investment.

## Repeat founders: take the call
- Jason's working rule is that second- and third-time founders who still have the energy are people you simply take the call from.
- His open question is how to tell whether a repeat founder is still hungry or just going through the motions.

## Small teams and revenue per employee in the AI era
- Jason argues that AI is letting companies earn huge revenue with very few people. His example is coding assistants like Cursor, which he says have reached revenue above $1M per employee.
- He cites Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke's stance on headcount: don't justify new hiring unless it can't be done with AI.
- He expects this to show up first in consumer businesses.

## Venture is crowded and moving earlier
- Jason invests through four funds and says investors everywhere are moving toward early stage.
- In his view the market is more crowded, Series B is "hot", and Series A is crowded too. Investors want to take the entire round, so everyone is pushing earlier.
- He treats the podcast as a learning tool and not just a place to talk.
- He is interested in programs like Sequoia's ARC as a way into early stage.
