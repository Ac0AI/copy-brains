## Replicants as the new default hire: OpenClaw at Jason's 20-person firm

- Jason spent 20 days obsessed with OpenClaw, an open source platform that runs on a Mac, PC or in the cloud. He calls it "everything we were told Siri and Alexa would be."
- Its difference from ChatGPT-style LLMs: once you authenticate it with your online services, it "does things" as opposed to "tells you things."
- He sees LLMs as great researchers and archivists. They make middle managers "100% more effective, instantly" by summarizing meetings, creating action items and preparing plans for humans to execute.
- He says LLM makers don't let their products execute because the companies are "scared to death of the mistakes they would make."
- With OpenClaw, "you assume that responsibility." If it makes a mistake after you gave it your password, "that's on you."

## What an OpenClaw agent actually does

- Podcast booking: it researches potential guests, finds their contact information, emails an invite, builds a CRM to manage those contacts, and schedules the appearance on the calendar.
- Errands: it can fill an Uber Eats cart and order lunch.
- Jason's 20-person venture and podcasting firm offloaded about 20% of its tasks to OpenClaw in 20 days.
- He says it keeps improving daily because of "skills" and memory.

## Skills and recursive self-improvement

- Skills are built by you or third parties and "function like apps."
- His favorite is /last30days by Matt Van Horn. It lets you instruct an agent (he calls it a "replicant") to research best practices for any skill on the web and fold the learnings into its skill stack.
- Example: for thumbnails, the firm's Producer Replicant runs a deep research project every Saturday at noon. It finds people discussing how to make better thumbnails on Reddit and elsewhere on the open web.
- His framing: unlike humans, the replicant "doesn't take breaks and doesn't forget. It just relentlessly makes itself better. It's recursive."

## The hiring hiatus and the economics behind it

- Jason doesn't expect to hire another human at his firm "for at least a year or two."
- He expects efficiency to rise at least 10% a month, which he equates to two new full-timers.
- His reasons: it's easier, faster and more cost-efficient to set up replicants.
- Cost: replicants burn about $1,000 in tokens a month and "never ask for a raise."
- Claimed performance: replicants "don't make mistakes," get 10% better at their jobs each week (which he says typically takes humans about a year), and "don't complain, they train."
- A new human hire becomes "a luxury spend for now," something you do because you can afford it and enjoy having another interesting person around.

## Team buy-in and the optimistic framing

- He says everyone on the team buys in because "chores are not fun."
- Each time a replicant takes over repetitive chores, the team gets more time with other humans: founders, podcast guests and one another.
- He rejects the dystopian read: "This isn't dystopian. It's nirvana!" He compares it to Star Trek, where humans solve problems alongside "Computer."
- His headline: "AGI is here. It's just not implemented yet."
