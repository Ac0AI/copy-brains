## Affiliate recruitment with AppyStash: turning a brand URL into a prospect list

- Vic co-builds AppyStash with Dustin Howes, a tool that turns a brand's URL into a list of potential affiliates and influencers using AI, machine learning and automation.
- Workflow: enter the brand URL, the platform extracts keywords, target audience and description, then you open "Discover partners" and pick a discovery method.
- His favorite method is the "AI Partner Finder": select the partner type and platform. It supports open 10 platforms and, he believes, is probably the only tool that searches affiliates and influencers on social platforms as well as the open web.
- Example: for an e-commerce flower brand it returned posts ranking for the brand's own top keywords, so there is no separate keyword analysis and no expensive SEO tool needed. Each result also shows traffic data, top keywords and geographic data.
- "Lookalike" search: pick a platform (SEO, YouTube, Twitter and so on) and it finds hundreds of similar affiliates posting on the same keywords. Dustin calls it the feature he uses almost daily to replicate a good affiliate.

## Building a YouTuber lead list with email and affiliate-link signals in under five minutes

Vic's process for the 800-YouTuber lead list:

1. Open the manual search engine and choose YouTube.
2. Add multiple "keywords to include", for example "best products for better sleep" and "sleep product review". Advanced filters allow excluded keywords and hashtags on social platforms.
3. Search (the demo returned 150 results of quality publishers ranking for those queries).
4. Click export. A CSV arrives by email after a couple of minutes.

- The export includes source, contact URL, contact email (scraped from public channel descriptions) and the referral link.
- Referral links are a buying-intent signal: Genius Link, ShareASale, Amazon and bit.ly links "highly indicate affiliate activity".
- Not every YouTuber has a public email, so sort A to Z to put leads with emails first and leave the rest for manual outreach.
- He says the whole list takes under five minutes.
- Dustin adds that clicking through YouTube to find emails behind a captcha is the annoying part this skips.

## AI-personalized cold outreach to affiliate prospects

- Vic recommends Instantly (used internally, "worked really well") and Smartlead for bulk affiliate outreach; both are affordable.
- Tactic: put a content example (e.g. the prospect's video title) into a custom variable and have a GPT prompt write an icebreaker complimenting that video.
- Example output: "I saw you have a video about fast and efficient car chargers, that's funny because I was just looking for one for my new car."
- Why it works: it reads far better than a generic bulk email ("join my affiliate program", "can you review this product"), and signals that their content already helped someone and that there is money to be made.

## Spotting fake followers with HypeAuditor before spending on influencers

- Vic calls fake followers a big problem in influencer marketing, and posted a LinkedIn carousel about it that people liked.
- He recommends HypeAuditor's free "check any influencer" feature.
- What to read:
  - Engagement rate, compared to the industry standard for that channel.
  - Subscriber growth chart: a sharp spike followed by much slower growth can mean bought followers or views, or can just be a viral video, so check which.
  - Total video views and average views.
- He demoed it on MKBHD and Marques Brownlee's channel; AppyStash also shows views, likes and audience interests when you fetch audience data.

## Onboarding new affiliates: email sequences and an AI support bot

- A new affiliate should enter an onboarding email sequence. Vic suggests MailerLite (very cheap and affordable) or Mailchimp for a more enterprise solution.
- Three things to cover: emails, onboarding materials and affiliate support.
- Boost Box (built by Ru from Australia, a community member, early stage) lets you train a chatbot on your materials, past affiliate work and policies, so affiliates ask the AI first and escalate to the manager or support team only if needed.
- Benefit: removes repetitive support emails from the manager's inbox.
- Dustin adds a caveat that partnership marketing must not lose face-to-face relationship building.

## Staying productive as a teen founder: AI leverage, outsourcing and avoiding burnout

- AI is what lets Vic ship features, launch new products and fix bugs fast while in high school; he says he didn't realize how much it helps until asked.
- He is cautious about burnout at 16 to 18, calling it among the unhealthiest things that can happen, and says to zoom out ("where we were a year ago or two years ago") on bad sales or feedback days.
- Advice for young founders:
  - Early on, focus on growth over profitability.
  - Outsource as much as you can: pay someone to run social media or implement a feature if the budget allows.
  - Build a quality team; he likes working with people around his own age.
  - Take advantage of low expenses while living with parents or newly moved out.
  - Travel and take breaks to keep balance.
  - Learning business fundamentals early is valuable even if the business is not doing big numbers, and the right people will join for part-time pay or for free to be part of a bigger mission.
- He also built Podkit, a media kit tool for podcasters, finished that month.
