## Building Affistash in 2024: growth from marketing and a co-founder

- Vic started 2024 by finding an "audience co-founder" for Affistash, a product he almost sold in 2023 ("so happy i didnt").
- The co-founder handled marketing work and revenue began growing, which fueled Vic's motivation to keep building software.
- First time crossing $2.5k MRR came in Q2.
- By autumn, income grew a lot from reinvested marketing money, and Vic started thinking about delegating and automating more marketing tasks.
- His year-end screenshot showed Affistash MRR up 442.4% versus the previous year.
- He still felt "hungrier, and more pressured than ever", partly because other people now depended on the project.
- Stated goal for 2025: reach "6 figs recurring".

## Affistash marketing tactics that worked in fall 2024

- **pSEO:** Vic wrote a Python script plus Framer CMS to index 5000 pages.
- **Cold email:** bought 6 mailboxes and got around a 15% reply rate, which he called "insane". Automating the reply process was still in progress.
- **LinkedIn and YouTube content:** he and his co-founder took LinkedIn content more seriously, and views grew noticeably.
- **Conferences:** the co-founder attended a few, which resulted in bigger deals.
- **Landing page:** moved from NextJS to Framer, and conversion rates "improved a lot".
- **Onboarding:** improved the onboarding process and started collecting more data about users and where they come from.

## Selling a SaaS via a single tweet: Coverposts exit

- In summer 2024 Vic sold coverposts.com, a social media marketing tool built with @ditu_dev. It was his first SaaS exit of the year.
- The process was simple: post a tweet (it went semi-viral), receive 10-15 DMs from interested buyers, and sell to a CTO based in California.
- Distribution via his Twitter audience did the work of finding buyers. He reached 1000 followers in Jan 2024.

## Developmently's business model and side experiments

- Vic runs a software company called Developmently. The main revenue model is building and selling its own SaaS products.
- In 2024 he gave the service model another try after almost a year and a half of stopping it: backend development and consultancy gigs with a designer/frontend developer friend.
- By late summer he had signed a couple of big clients for software dev services.
- A community marketing database, trygrouphunt.com, built with @ditu_dev, "failed lol".
- He considered starting a water labeling company with a friend and is keeping the idea to himself for now.
- In December he started building a new software product, with the outcome left for his 2025 essay.

## Motivation and mindset behind building

- The year began with "a new found motivation to build cool shit, with no expectations or intentions to please anybody".
- He never started with the goal of making money; the current situation feels "surreal".
- He values running an online business from anywhere in the world at a young age.
- Anxiety was his biggest demon: "a lot of second guessing, fear of losing and anxiety" since he started business at 13-14.
- He felt "always in a hurry" and workaholic, even when business and school were going well.
- "Money doesnt mean shit": losing relationships with people he cares about is "1000x more painful than any customer dispute, any churn or any lost money". He still chases revenue.
- Writing the yearly essay worked as therapy, and he plans to make it recurring.
