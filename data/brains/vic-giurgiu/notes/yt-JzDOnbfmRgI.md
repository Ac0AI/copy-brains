## Validating before building

- Vic says to "validate your idea and your concept before you start building it" and that he tells every entrepreneur he meets the same thing. He picked the habit up at his software development agency, where founders always validated before the agency built.
- For Affistash (affiliate recruitment software), he cold-messaged affiliate managers in affiliate marketing communities, subreddits and on LinkedIn.
- His pitch: he is an ex software-agency founder building something in their space, and asks what feedback they have and what product would make their life easier.
- He asked each manager to walk through their whole process, from starting an affiliate program to landing the first affiliates and partners and managing them.
- The research took about two to three weeks of calls with affiliate managers. Some of those managers became customers once the tool was built.
- Findings: recruitment and partner lead discovery were entirely manual (paying for SEO tools, checking who promotes competitors, scouting leads, judging fit). Finding 5 to 10 prospects could take hours, which made it a good fit for AI.

## Low-fidelity validation with a mockup deck

- Vic's process for every product or business: build a mockup or very low-level demo, even a quick deck in Canva or Figma. You don't need to be a pro designer or developer.
- Write to people in your industry and ask for 5 to 10 minutes on a call, then show them the deck.
- He says many people want to help in the early stage, and you can get roughly 10 to 20 opinions this way.
- You don't have to ask for payment up front. Offer a free account once the product is built as thanks for the feedback.

## Networks, mentors and picking a niche

- Vic resonates with "your network is your net worth". He says to surround yourself with the biggest, most influential people in your niche, because they help when you expand to new markets and industries and their referrals can mean a lot.
- A good network also means always having someone to consult before a business decision. He has two or three mentors he speaks to before any new venture or major decision.
- His mentors are SaaS founders with hundreds of thousands of dollars in MRR and seven-figure exits.
- He argues young founders have a better chance of connecting with such people, because many successful entrepreneurs started young and see themselves in them.
- His third lesson: work in a niche you are passionate about, because it keeps you motivated through the tough stretches (angry customers, lots of demos and meetings). He is passionate about AI and automation, so building an automation tool made sense.

## What Affistash does and who it targets

- Target user: affiliate managers and partner managers whose main task includes recruiting new affiliates and publishers.
- Customer mix: about 70 to 80% SaaS, mostly small and medium B2C SaaS companies with roughly 5 to 15 employees. The rest are e-commerce and info products. He chose SaaS because it is the industry he knows best, which made it easier to reach founders and managers there.
- B2C SaaS fits because those companies advertise heavily through publishers and content creators.
- He was starting to expand into enterprise teams with established affiliate recruitment and partnership departments, and had begun working with a few affiliate management teams at bigger companies.
- Core feature: AI-driven partner lead discovery. The user fills in company data (name, niche, website, business model, product description, target audience). The tool then generates leads for chosen partner types: content creators, media publishers, agencies, newsletters, or a mix.
- Platform options include SEO (top-ranked content for the niche and target audience), YouTube, LinkedIn and Twitter.
- Demo example: for a SaaS product (Sofa Brain) targeting interior designers and real estate agents, the tool generated 112 publisher leads automatically, such as "top 10 AI tools for interior design" lists monetized with AdSense and affiliate links.
- Leads can be refreshed by choosing a new type and platform and giving feedback.
- Each lead can pull contacts (email, LinkedIn, Twitter) and shows an accuracy percentage so managers can tell which are better fits.
- Outreach happens in the tool: connect a mailbox, then use an AI template library with selectable writing styles and word limits. Templates are generated from your company data.
- He advises personalizing every email because it improves conversion rates.

## Where the lead data comes from

- Vic says leads come from a mix of sources: a database of manually collected leads built by the Affistash team, plus scraping APIs and scrapers connected to specific platforms and networks (YouTube, LinkedIn, Twitter and others).
- The thesis behind the product: the AI can run the searches, grab and classify leads, filter them, and generate outreach messages far faster than a manager doing it by hand.

## Pricing at the time of the interview

- Individual (premium) plan: $37 per month billed annually, $47 per month billed monthly. Aimed at solo affiliate managers and freelancers, covers one company profile.
- Enterprise plan: $128+ per month billed annually, $257 per month billed monthly. Includes five team accounts and the ability to recruit partners for multiple affiliate programs, so it suits agencies and recruitment agencies working with several clients.
- He said they were still testing the market and were new, that pricing would likely change within months, and that he agreed they were underpriced given how expensive tools are in the industry.

## Where affiliate management and AI are heading

- Vic expects a lot of automation and AI in affiliate management. Recruitment is only one step of a successful program.
- Other areas he sees AI improving: onboarding (generating content and tools for partners and having conversations with them), commission optimization and data analysis.
- He also sees real-time interaction with partners during onboarding, for example an AI changing banner colors to match a partner's website theme.
- On the content side, he expects people to create far more affiliate content with AI (video, niche blogs) and an array of AI tools and AI-produced content in the industry.
- He described "endless possibilities" for AI and automation here and suggested AI banner generation for affiliates as a business idea.

## Early-stage habits

- Vic ran a software development agency before Affistash, building desktop and game software for companies, including enterprise-sized ones. Agency work relied on partnerships and referral commissions, which introduced him to affiliate and partnership marketing.
- He started Affistash at the beginning of 2023, with a co-founder, while still in high school, and held demos around his school schedule.
- He researched competitors' landing pages (for example, looking at what managers like to see) when redesigning his own landing page, often late at night.
- To stay visible he posts regularly on LinkedIn, shares demos weekly for B2B SaaS, AI SaaS and e-commerce companies, and offers tailored live demos with partner leads already loaded for a prospect's company.
