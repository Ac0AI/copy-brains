## Origin: from a $75 game dev gig to shipping software

- At 13, Vic made $75 from a game dev gig, which he says changed his life.
- He studies AI in college and builds software companies.
- His early path: launched 2 indie games that reached over 2,000 players, then ran a software dev agency with his closest friends and built products for IPOs.
- He turned the agency into a software studio and launched SaaS products. In his words, "Some were successful, some were not."

## Exits and wins

- **Affistash:** grew it to 6 figures at 17 with a co-founder (Dustin Howes), then sold it to FMTC at 19.
- **BrowserUnicorn:** built and sold it with a partner (@ditu_dev), and listed it on Product Hunt.
- **Coverposts.com:** built and sold with the same partner.
- **PodKit:** built and sold in 3 months to listener.com, working with Eric Melchor.
- Also won hackathon awards with Dialz.

## Pattern: build small products with partners and sell them

- Most of his listed outcomes are small products built with a co-founder and sold, rather than long-held companies.
- PodKit shows the speed of this approach: build to sale in 3 months.
- The agency and studio served as the base: client work first, then the studio launching its own SaaS products.

## Failed products

Vic openly lists what didn't work:

- **linklyft:** link in bio for freelancers.
- **grouphunt:** a community marketing tool.
- **characterstream:** FaceTime-like interactions with AI characters.
- **gitwall:** paywalls on GitHub repos.
- **browzy:** a productivity Chrome extension.

His stance: ship many products, publish the failures next to the wins, and treat both as part of the record.
