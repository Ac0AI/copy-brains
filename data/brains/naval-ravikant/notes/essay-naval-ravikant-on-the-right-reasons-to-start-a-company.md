## Naval's early motivations for starting companies were impure

- Naval says his first few companies were driven by wanting to be a founder: "I really just wanted to be a founder." That desire "kind of overrode everything" and "was not a pure motivation."
- His motivation then shifted to money and power. He says "nobody wants to talk about it, but [money and power] are fundamental drivers."
- He admits wanting both to make money and to have a company with influence, and says "that wasn't that great of a motivation either."
- He wishes he had the insight he has now "20-30 years ago."

## Genuine curiosity produced his best outcomes

- Looking back, Naval says he was most successful when he took on projects "because I was genuinely curious about them."
- Following his own intellectual curiosity gave him insight, and that insight led to good investments, startups, and outcomes.
- The implication he draws is that curiosity-led work beat founder-identity, money, or status as a starting point.

## The two questions Naval asks before building something

- Now that he doesn't need to work for money or status, he only works on products he wants to see exist.
- He asks himself: "What's a beautiful thing I can make that wouldn't exist if I didn't put effort into it?"
- He pairs it with a second question: "And what people do I want to spend all my time around?"
- Together these select for both the thing being built and the team building it.

## Why meeting material desires doesn't end the search for purpose

- Naval argues that once your material desires are somewhat met, "you end up extremely bored."
- When you no longer have to hustle for a living, you can lose your sense of purpose: "What do I do?"
- He rejects two escape routes:
  - Meditating in a corner for a long time "gets boring too."
  - Going completely hedonistic is "a death trap and just an empty lifestyle."

## Creating as self-actualization, done with friends

- Naval's answer is self-actualization: "I want to be the best version of myself." For him that means creating something.
- He holds that "it's better to create than to consume."
- He finds it "far more fulfilling to learn along the way and build."
- He wants to build with friends, "people that I really respect, admire, and enjoy spending time with."
