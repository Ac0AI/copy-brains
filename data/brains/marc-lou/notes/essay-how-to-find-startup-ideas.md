## Upgrade something you already use daily

- Marc says that when you are a generalist, "your #1 problem is finding startup ideas", so "solve your own problem" is not enough on its own advice.
- His alternative is to find something you do daily and improve it. Examples he gives: a better habit tracker, a better email drip tool, automating your coding flow.
- He argues "customers won't pay for your creativity". Do something boring: upgrade an existing product or service you use often.
- Three reasons this works:
  1. It increases the odds of making money, because people are already paying for solutions.
  2. It boosts conversion rate, because you care about the market and speak its language.
  3. It generates tons of new problems, which are more startup ideas.
- The habit he recommends is to question daily what could be improved in your workflow, and ideas will "germ".

## Ideas emerge from building, not from brainstorming

- Marc points to Pieter Levels, who builds on top of his own startups: AvatarAI created InteriorAI, which made PhotoAI.
- His rule: "Ideas emerge from building them."
- Each repeated pain point inside a project can become a product:
  - Reusing the same code boilerplate is a startup (Marc's own ShipFast is a NextJS boilerplate).
  - Struggling with DNS records for email deliverability is a course.
  - Launching in the same directories over and over is an info product.
- His tactic: start something boring, launch it, then look for what you can automate, fix, or teach. By doing this "you'll never run out of ideas."
- Context: in 2021 he had 0 startup ideas, and by September 2023 he had 50+ on his list.

## Start from universal needs

- Marc notes problems exist everywhere, and each one is a startup idea. His examples of universal needs:
  - X users want more followers.
  - Businesses want to make more money.
  - Anyone wants to be fit and healthy.
- His advice: start with a market you care about and think about how to fulfill its basic needs.
- He frames the question to ask yourself as: "What universal need can you solve with your skills?"

## Case study: escape room marketing tool (2018)

- In 2018 Marc was lost, with no income, no startup idea, and no coding skills. He loved video games.
- He cold-called escape room businesses and sold them a marketing tool: an embedded mini-game on their websites to increase conversion rate (the universal need).
- Someone bought it, and he "was shocked at how easy it is to sell something when you solve someone's problem". The software was not even built yet when he sold it.
- Outcome: the SaaS grew to $6,000 MRR. He burned out because he did not care about the market and closed the business (Acquire did not exist back then).
- He still values it: it kept him busy for 2 years, made money, and led him to where he is today.
- Lessons he implies: sell before the product exists, solve a clear conversion problem, and pick a market you actually care about to avoid burnout.
