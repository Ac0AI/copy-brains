## Finding ideas by scratching your own itch

- Marc says the most successful products come from solopreneurs solving their own problems. His example is Pieter Levels building NomadList while traveling the world.
- Problems already surround you, so build a habit of looking around. A 9-to-5 job is the best place to explore.
- Search for experiences that bother you, or knowledge you wish you had.
- If nothing comes, start something new: a new sport, a new daily routine, a new skill. New activities surface new problems.
- Universal problems also work: "Businesses want more revenue, and people want to be more productive."
- Another route is upgrading a product that you or your friends already use.
- Marc argues that when you get started, "ideas don't matter. Building them does." Building teaches you real problems and produces many promising ideas.

## Why long builds backfire: the 2017 failure

- In 2017 Marc spent a year building a startup that made $0. He says he threw away all the code, quit his girlfriend, and moved to a new country.
- His principle: the more time invested, the higher the expectations. Since entrepreneurship rarely meets expectations, spending months on one product is "a recipe for burnout."
- His contrast: in 2023 he launched 10 products and made $263,000.
- His claim: speed is a solopreneur's best friend.

## The 24-hour startup

- Marc says "every startup has a 24-hour version." His examples: Airbnb is a spreadsheet, and Instagram is a Google Drive.
- The tactic, step by step:
  1. Find the 24-hour version of your idea.
  2. Remove all non-critical features.
  3. Spend a week or two building.
  4. Write a good headline.
  5. Ship with a buy button.
- Putting a buy button on the first version tests real demand immediately.

## Mindset: repeated tiny failures

- Marc says "building a successful startup is a repetition of tiny failures."
- He advises being comfortable with imperfection and taking action now rather than waiting.
- Short build cycles keep each failure small, which protects against burnout and lets you try many ideas.

## Output and track record

- Title claim: he built 21 products in 2 years.
- Revenue marker: $263,000 from 10 product launches in 2023.
- He tied the speed to a build-in-public approach: he streamed the building of a micro startup over 8 YouTube lives, then planned to stream its launch on Product Hunt, Twitter and Hacker News.
