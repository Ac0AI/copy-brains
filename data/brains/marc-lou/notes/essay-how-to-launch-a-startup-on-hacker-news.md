## How Marc approaches launching on Hacker News

- Marc's Hacker News experience: after a post on Hacker News he got his first customers and started to get traction on Twitter, after months of "0 likes" since starting Twitter in November 2021.
- His numbers: 5 viral posts and 25,000 visitors from Hacker News in total.
- His first startup, Habit Garden, got 11,000 visitors and its first customers from Hacker News.

## Post in the Show HN category

- Of all Hacker News categories, only one matters to makers: Show HN, "a dedicated space for users to share their side projects."
- Add "Show HN:" to the title when submitting. The post then appears automatically in /newest.
- Promotion ladder:
  - Posts getting traction in /newest are moved to /new for more visibility.
  - Posts getting traction in /new are moved to / (front page) for maximum visibility.

## Make it personal and speak the audience's language

- Marc argues people don't care about a big corporation getting richer. They care about a side hustler shipping apps after a 9 to 5.
- Start the post with "I made..." or "After 6 months coding...".
- The audience is developers and tech enthusiasts, so speak their language. His example: "I made an SEO-optimized site entirely with GPT-4".
- Non-technical angles get ignored: courses, coaching, e-commerce. Unless you make it techy. Example: a habit tracker stands no chance, but a gamified habit tracker does. That is how Habit Garden got 11,000 visitors.

## Tell the outcome in the title

- Marc points to the list of best Show HN posts of 2023 (bestofshowhn.com). They all share one thing: "You know what you'll get by clicking on it."
- Be clear on what makes the product or service special and why it is worth the reader's time.

## Contribute and engage in the comments

- Hacker News has a lot of spam and people promoting themselves without contributing.
- Tactic: add a comment on your own post. Briefly explain who you are and why you built the product, and ask for feedback.
- Engage with users who comment. Marc says people claim Hacker News is full of negativity, but once you show you're a real person, users are much friendlier.

## Don'ts

- **Don't ask for upvotes**, at least not directly. He believes a real person decides which posts get featured and can tell when users arrive from a direct link to your submission.
  - If you want support from friends, tell them to find your submission by browsing Hacker News, not via a direct link.
  - Multiple upvotes from accounts with 0 karma look suspicious.
- **Don't overthink it.** Hacker News is very random. Unlike Product Hunt, there isn't much you can do to influence a post's success.
- **Don't use emojis.**
