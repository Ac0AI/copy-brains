## Why Marc still launches on Product Hunt

- Marc's record: of 21 products launched, 13 got the Top Product badge and one ranked #2 Product of the Month. He also won Maker of the Year 2023.
- He admits Product Hunt "isn't as big as it used to be", but still calls it one of the best places to launch a startup.
- Reasons he gives: about 4M monthly high-quality visitors, one of the biggest tech newsletters, and massive media coverage.
- His claim: unlike other platforms, if you work on the launch you get predictable traffic and grow your customer base.
- He launches as a solopreneur competing against VC-backed startups.

## Optimizing the listing for inbound upvotes

Inbound upvotes come from Product Hunt users casually browsing the site, so the listing must be on point.

- **Name:** crystal clear and memorable.
- **Tagline:**
  - Short, straight to the point, and ideally triggers an emotional reaction.
  - Trendy tech words like "GPT-4" are fine, but don't go too deep.
  - Designers, product managers, and marketers must all understand it.
- **Logo:** animate it as a GIF. It's "such an easy way to get attention because few are doing it".
- **Gallery images:**
  - Extremely concise, like a YouTube thumbnail.
  - One feature per image.
  - Zoom in on the feature as far as possible before taking the screenshot.

## First comment and launch-day conversation

- When the listing is scheduled, write the first comment.
- Lean into being a solopreneur rather than a VC startup, because it is inspiring. Explain why you came up with the idea.
- The comment section supports markdown:
  - Link your product name to maximize traffic.
  - Plug your Twitter to build an audience and make future launches easier.
- If you learned anything valuable while building, create a Product Hunt conversation (discussion post) on launch day and plug your launch link at the end. People are already browsing, so it's an easy upvote.

## Outbound upvotes from Twitter

Marc says you need help from outside Product Hunt to compete with VC startups. Twitter is the biggest source of outbound upvotes because the community is big and very supportive.

- Within the first 4 hours of the launch, tweet the announcement with the link.
- Make the tweet stand out, since there are more makers than ever. He always adds a launch video.
- Result: each launch tweet got 300+ likes and the startup got a Product Hunt badge, even when he had 1,000 followers.
- Retweet the launch tweet a few hours later.
- Schedule 2 or 3 unrelated tweets later in the day, so one might go viral and newcomers discover your profile. Pin the launch tweet.
- He built LaunchViral to help replicate the launch-video approach.

## Website badge and cross-posting

- Add a Product Hunt-branded badge to your landing page that links back to the launch page. He uses a subtle one, fixed at the bottom-left on desktop.
- Launch the product on other sites too: Indie Hackers, Reddit, and Hacker News.
- If one of those posts does well, visitors arriving on the landing page can click the badge and upvote.
- Other channels he lists for micro startups: launching on Reddit, launching on Hacker News, free tools marketing, and programmatic SEO.

## Choosing the launch day

- Monday to Friday are busier: more traffic, but more competition.
- If chasing the badge, launch on Sunday.
- If chasing traffic, launch on Monday. His reasoning: "VC startups don't work on Monday 0:01 AM".

## When you're ready to launch

- Get social proof first: ask a few customers or users for testimonials and add them to the landing page.
- Then launch as soon as possible, especially if you're just getting started.
- The first launch is unlikely to be a big hit, "And that's OK. It's a learning process."
- Options after a weak first launch:
  - Re-launch a few months later after adding new features.
  - Launch free tools that promote your product to sharpen your skills.
- Each launch builds an audience, so the next one gets easier.
