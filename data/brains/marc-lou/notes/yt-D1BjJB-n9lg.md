## Marc's portfolio of 10 SaaS: totals, margins and what each earned

- Marc says he built 10 software businesses that together made over $200,000 (the title says $237,631) at roughly 90 to 95% profit margin, with almost no customer support, so the income is largely automated.
- The video covers seven active products and three he no longer runs.
- Revenue by product (as stated):
  - Habit Garden: about $20,000 since 2022.
  - Indie Page: $43,000 since 2023.
  - Workbook PDF: about $2,400 (the transcript is garbled; he calls it "pretty much a failure").
  - Buy Dispute (Stripe dispute tool): almost $8,000 in about a year.
  - Zenvoice: $15,000 in its first year.
  - Poopup: close to $7,000 since March 2024.
  - Datafast (web analytics, beta): almost $6,000 in 3 months, MRR close to $1,400.
- Sold or retired projects:
  - Viralbot: started 2018, reached over $4,000 MRR via cold email, which he found boring and which burned him out.
  - Game widget: made $200, sold for about $4,300.
  - MakeLanding (AI website generator): about $15,000 revenue in 3 to 4 months, sold for $35,000 because he did not know how to grow it.

## Where Marc's ideas come from: build first, ideas follow

- He argues "you don't need a brilliant startup idea to get started. It's actually a trap." The best way to find an idea is to "create stuff, create a lot of noise and then you will find signal."
- Most of his ideas come from a personal need or frustration:
  - Zenvoice: auditing his Shipfast-style Stripe account in late 2023, he found Stripe charged 0.4% per transaction just to generate PDF invoices. He computed roughly $1,600 lost, then built a tool to avoid it. That loss became $15,000 in revenue.
  - Buy Dispute: Stripe banned him overnight in 2022 over many payments from one customer and email, so he built a tool that sits between Stripe and the business and flags likely fraud patterns.
  - Datafast: he needed one place to see web analytics linked to payment data and could not find an easy one.
- Ideas also come from audience demand and friends:
  - Indie Page: people DMed him asking for a site like his personal showcase page.
  - Poopup: the notification widget on Zenvoice's site drew comments, and his friend Dan told him to make it a product.
- "Every startup idea I get comes from the previous startups I built." If stuck, rebuild something you already use (a habit tracker, an alarm clock) or try a new hobby and meet new people.

## Free tools, viral features and badges as distribution

- Marc calls free tools his favorite marketing strategy.
- Habit Garden: visualizehabit.com lets anyone build a habit grid showing a year of progress. It is shareable, ends with a promo, and imports the habits into the tracker. A Reddit post about it got almost 6,000 likes.
- Indie Page:
  - A Twitter bot posts whenever a maker makes a sale.
  - A /leaderboard page, framed as "Can you make $1,000 online?", shows Stripe-verified revenue. It is inspirational and shareable, and he says it currently drives the most revenue and many new accounts daily.
  - Revenue spikes on the chart came when he focused on marketing.
- Zenvoice: each customer gets a unique link to share with their own customers, with a small badge pointing back to the product. A customer sharing it with an audience can lead to hundreds of dollars of sales within hours.
- Poopup: he replicated the same on-site notification mechanic that had worked for Zenvoice.

## Launch tactics: social proof, Product Hunt, viral videos and live streams

- Early audience: Twitter screenshots of the app gave Habit Garden its first 10 to 50 users.
- Fun promo videos: he made World of Warcraft-style promos (his wife as the character), which got attention on Twitter and YouTube.
- Indie Page social proof: he got well-known makers (over 10,000 followers) to use the app so the landing page had social proof, then launched on Product Hunt and got the number one of the day badge.
- Buy Dispute: a video with Leonardo DiCaprio explaining Stripe disputes went viral (about half a million views, 2,000 likes). He believes anyone making similar videos could do the same.
- Zenvoice live stream:
  - He built, designed and marketed the whole product in 9 days on a YouTube live stream.
  - YouTube pushed it to developers, and he got customers before the product officially launched.
  - He says live streaming builds trust because people see who is behind the product. It is a "cheat code" because few people do it, and you do not even have to talk.
- Poopup: he did no marketing beyond Product Hunt and other launch platforms, since it was a one-time product to maintain and leave.

## Pricing choices: one-time payments, lifetime deals and credits

- Zenvoice: $49 for one Stripe account, $69 for unlimited, no subscription even though it costs him a few cents a month. He says subscriptions are really hard to sell and one-time payments made him much more money than a $5 or $10 monthly subscription would have.
- Poopup: $9 for one website, $19 for unlimited, one-time, because he knew he would push few updates.
- Habit Garden: $5 a month or $47 lifetime.
- Indie Page: $25 a year or $45 lifetime.
- Workbook PDF: a 30-day pass at $29, like a monthly without recurring billing.
- Buy Dispute: credit-based, for example 100,000 protected transactions for $219.
- Datafast: usage-based subscription starting at $9 a month and rising with traffic or events. Its MRR is growing and churn falling, which he attributes to putting 80 to 90% of his focus on it.

## SEO, product-channel fit and what he would do differently

- Marc says to find a product where "marketing does not feel like marketing." He burned out in 2018 doing cold email. Programmatic SEO, free tools and fun viral videos make him happy.
- Workbook PDF: hundreds to thousands of indexed pages target keywords like "English workbook PDF for intermediate" and drive at least 100 clicks a day. He thinks the product suits social media (for example TikTok language-learning videos) better than Google.
- Buy Dispute: about 10 boring SEO articles on Stripe disputes bring roughly 5 to 20 qualified clicks a day from people already looking for a solution. He says he is not the right person to grow it: it needs cold email to bigger companies or fear-based marketing, which he does not want to do.
- Habit Garden: he thinks subscriptions for consumer apps are very hard in 2025. He would now use Instagram and TikTok with inspirational or challenge videos.

## Learning when to quit and testing fast

- Marc has built over 30 tiny businesses. His signal: after launching and getting about 1,000 visitors, he knows quickly whether a product is a good fit.
- He warns that "the longer you spend on something, the higher your expectations," and when results never meet them you burn out. He sees people spend a month building and months trying to grow with no significant numbers.
- If a product has some potential but you cannot find marketing that fits, he says to move on to the next one.
- Marc sold or gave up products when he could not grow them: he took a $10,000 DM offer for Habit Garden when running out of money, later got it back for free, and sold MakeLanding for $35,000.
- His closing advice: build something rather than watch more videos.
