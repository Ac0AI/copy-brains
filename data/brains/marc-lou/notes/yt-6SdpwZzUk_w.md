## Cold outreach grew a software business to $80,000 in revenue

- Marc grew a software business to over $80,000 in revenue in 2018 with cold email alone, spending $0 and not being a native English speaker.
- Most people fail because they try to sell the product. The goal is to get a reply and open a conversation.
- Finding leads: go where potential customers hang out (Tripadvisor, Yelp, Twitter) and collect the business owner's name and email. His escape-room example: read reviews, filter for terrible ones, because owners tend to reply to those, and sometimes the reply includes a manager's personal email.
- Track leads in a Gmail CRM (he used Streak) with stages: lead, email 1, follow-up 1, follow-up 2, and so on.
- Email format: about three sentences, with the owner's name and the benefit (not the product) in the subject line, a personalized opener, and a curiosity gap about a missed opportunity. The call to action is just "reply to this email if you want to learn more", not a website visit or a call booking.
- Follow up 2-3 days later, then once more about a week later, then move on.
- His emails got a 50% open rate and a 10% reply rate.
- The math: 20 cold emails a day gave 2 replies and 1 subscriber, at $19.99/month, so about one new customer per day.
- He calls it boring but "the fastest way to make money on the internet".
- He also recommends cold calling as a long-term way to learn marketing and human psychology. Use ChatGPT to write call scripts covering the opening and objection handling, and research each business so you have something that makes the person on the phone interested.

## Free tools as a marketing channel

- Marc calls free tools his favorite channel: free to build, minimal effort, and it can promote anything.
- A free tool is a standalone mini app that promotes a paid product.
- Logo Fast, a logo and icon generator he built in 3 days, made over $110,000 in revenue for his coding course, with 583 visitors in the last 30 days. The tool has a promo for his coding course on the side.
- Ways to find ideas:
  - Extract a paid feature from your product. Tweet Hunter's free tweet generator is the example.
  - Think about the steps before your customer uses your product. His "Visualize Habit" tool shows what you'll achieve in a year if you keep a habit, then flows into his habit tracker. It got over 10,000 visitors in 24 hours and hundreds to thousands of habit tracker users.
- Requirements:
  - 100% free.
  - One feature only. If you have two ideas, make two tools.
  - A smart name that people search for (a user persona generator, "logo fast").
  - No signup or email wall, so the tool works right on the homepage.
  - A seamless plug, where the paid product is the logical next step. A top productivity hack site with an ad for his course is his example of a bad plug because it looks like an ad.
- Publish everywhere: Hacker News, Product Hunt, directories, Twitter.
- Don't overthink it: build one great feature, ship, move on, repeat.

## Affiliate marketing: setup and rules

- Affiliate marketing made Marc over $50,000 in revenue across some of his products. It only works for a product that already makes money.
- How it works: power users sign up, get a unique link, and earn a commission on referrals, which brings more users and more affiliates.
- Three steps:
  1. Use affiliate software. He uses Rewardful (not paid promotion), which needs just a tracking script and integrates with Stripe.
  2. Build a dedicated SEO page addressed to affiliates, not customers, since they want to make money. Include proof (a photo of people already earning), an earnings slider based on number of referrals, and concrete stats (an affiliate with 167 visitors earned $358). Optionally add a brand-assets download with logo and headlines.
  3. Promote it. If you lack an audience, promote it inside your app at the right moment in the customer journey. His example is an apply button in the deployment section of his codebase, since users are at the end of the project. Another is putting an affiliate link in users' shared dashboard screenshots for his analytics startup.
- Rules:
  - Be generous: at least 30%, up to 50%, depending on margin.
  - If you copy an existing business that already converts, a 50% commission gives a competitive advantage.
  - Ban branded keyword bidding. When someone runs Google ads on "ShipFast", he deletes the referral cookie, and the terms say so.
  - Set a payout window of at least 30 days to avoid affiliates buying through their own link, then getting a refund or chargeback. He uses 60 days for new affiliates with zero commissions and slowly moves them to 30 days.

## Hacker News: how to get a Show HN to go viral

- Marc has driven well over 25,000 visitors to his sites combined from Hacker News. It costs $0 and can take 3 minutes. It works best for digital products, especially for developers or using trendy tech like AI.
- Audience: backed by Y Combinator, techy and nerdy, which should shape the message.
- Post only in the Show category: title starts with "Show HN:". New posts land in "newest" or "show new", and once a post gets roughly 3-5 votes it moves to the main Show page. Staying at the top can reach the homepage.
- Title formula: start with "I made XYZ", which tells a story and works regardless of karma (his first post had zero karma). Add the outcome the tool is for: "a movie recommendation app based on your mood", "a habit tracker to fight procrastination" (152 upvotes). Use techy, trendy words such as "gamified".
- For inspiration, he points to bestofhn.com, where the recurring pattern is "I made..." posts.
- Don'ts:
  - Don't ask for upvotes. Moderators see traffic coming from your link, and you get banned instantly.
  - Don't use emojis.
- To post multiple times, you need to be an active user, with about five engagements (comments and so on) per post.

## Programmatic SEO for startups

- Marc says programmatic SEO drove over 50,000 visitors to some of his sites, around 100-200 visitors per day to individual sites. It costs nothing, takes medium effort, and works for products with search intent.
- The idea: rank for many long-tail keywords that lead to your main product page.
- Steps:
  1. Find a main keyword and long-tail modifiers (locations, skill levels like beginner/advanced/pro). His example is a directory of rage rooms by city, using a free keyword generator with Ahrefs for exact difficulty and volume.
  2. Build a page skeleton template with variables. Example: workbookpdf.com reuses one template across languages, such as a personalized Japanese workbook or Ukrainian workbook.
  3. Collect and fill in data by hiring someone on Fiverr, or scraping it yourself (needs advanced dev skills).
  4. Optimize the template: image alt attributes, titles, and key keywords in the H1.
  5. Get backlinks, for example from Product Hunt or his Indie Page portfolio site.
  6. Submit URLs to Google Search Console to speed up indexing.
- Expect a wait. One site took months before it started to take off, then gave consistent traffic over the years.

## Meta ads (Instagram and Facebook)

- Marc spent around $14,000 in the last month on Meta ads, at a cost per purchase of around $48, and made around $6,000 from ads that month. As he states it, he is a developer who doesn't like ads.
- Only run ads on a product that already converts, as with affiliates.
- Setup:
  1. Install the Meta pixel (check it with the Meta Pixel Helper extension).
  2. Track purchases as an event. He keeps it simple, tracking only payments, sent from the post-purchase page.
  3. Targeting: cold audiences have never heard of you, warm ones know you. Warm converts better but is limited. If in doubt, start with retargeting people who already visited your site.
  4. Creative: the image is what catches attention. Don't start from a blank template. Use templates that already work (iOS notes, the Drake meme) or screenshot a competitor's ad and add "what you do right".
- He credits a friend, Nico, for helping him with ads.

## Personal brand, Reddit and Product Hunt/directories

**Personal brand**
- Marc grew to about 160,000 Twitter followers in 3 years and 120,000 YouTube subscribers in a year. Excluding direct traffic, Twitter is his second-biggest traffic source. It costs $0 but a lot of time.
- Pick the platform that fits you (he is bad at photos, so skip Instagram, and prefers text).
- Find your unique trait and turn weaknesses into strengths. He gets bored fast and builds many sites, so he is "the ship guy". Make the profile, banner and writing match.
- With zero followers, consume content first and learn the culture, then post: behind the scenes, results (a Stripe screenshot), learnings. One outcome per piece of content.
- Use templates from what works for others, such as "1 hour of X every day". Add your own signature.
- It takes time, and no one follows you without building cool stuff.

**Reddit**
- A post went viral and gave 50,000 visitors in one day. Reddit hates promotion.
- Use a subreddit made for sharing side projects with the "I built X" formula and a short demo video.
- Elsewhere, share stories with zero promotion, as in a viral r/entrepreneur post about selling his AI tool, and let people click through to the profile.
- His hack for free tools is a URL variant that hides the promo, so the tool looks like a pure free tool, with a link to his social profiles to build an audience.

**Directories**
- Product Hunt: he has launched over 20 sites and was Product of the Year 2023. #1 gives about 2,000 visitors in 24 hours, plus roughly 2,000 clicks from the newsletter, a backlink and a badge.
- Free alternatives: Uneed and TinyLaunch (solo-dev run). Paid AI directories: Futurepedia and There's An AI For That.
- Listing: a simple logo (don't pay hundreds), an outcome-oriented simple name (BuyDispute: shield with a dollar), and a 60-character headline about the person, not the product. Use curiosity or emotion.
- Votes: ask friends and family, and add a "we're live on Product Hunt" badge linking to the listing on your site.

## YouTube live coding

- Marc calls live coding streams underestimated: free, no extra effort, and good for developer tools and B2C apps.
- A 3-hour stream got 50,000 views when he had zero followers, even though he says he had no idea what he was doing.
- Benefits: productivity (people are watching so you can't scroll), audience growth, and customers who trust you because they've seen you live. You can earn before the product is finished.
- Unexpected things happen, such as a viewer who helped with a bug and reframed the whole app design.
- Tips: frame it as a challenge ("Day 1 of growing a software to $1 million") and make a clear thumbnail with code and tech icons (Next.js, Stripe).
- His closing advice is to try all the channels, and you'll never worry about traffic again.
