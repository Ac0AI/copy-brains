## Marc automates with AI agents and treats AI as a productivity multiplier for solo developers

- Marc says "AI is getting so good it's scary" and estimates he wrote "less than 10%" of the code for DataFast's new widget feature, using the Composer/agent mode in Cursor.
- His workflow: describe the feature in plain English to the agent, which builds it by following the existing components and designs already in his codebase.
- He argues the real payoff is time freed for other work that matters, such as marketing.
- He expects AI to eventually handle coding, design and perhaps marketing angles, which makes building simpler but also means "much more competition".
- He calls himself "just an observer" of the shift and recommends trying the Cursor agent.

## Marc's changelog page for DataFast: an SEO and retention asset built almost entirely by AI

- Marc built a public changelog at /changelog for DataFast, his web analytics product, using Cursor's agent mode.
- Purposes: keep a backlog of shipped updates ranked on Google (possibly for SEO as well), and show users the app is "being updated recently and consistently".
- Each entry has a date, title and a photo; the photo is extracted from the demo video he makes to present each new feature.
- Entries are written in Markdown, so he does not style anything by hand.
- The same Markdown feeds a small widget popup at the bottom of the DataFast main dashboard, so users see the same updates in-app.
- He notes the AI matched his existing designs, so he barely coded it.

## DataFast reaches $1,000 MRR, with a database cost problem

- Marc announces DataFast hit four digits in monthly recurring revenue ("a thousand in monthly recurring revenue"); it grows steadily but slowly.
- Revenue is up about 10 to 15% month over month.
- About 100 active subscribers pay monthly.
- He cites a churn rate of about 133% (as stated in the transcript) and calls it "not great but also not bad" and now steady. His read: the promise on the landing page matches what the product delivers.
- The "time bomb": his database cluster, which stores page views and events, is at about 150 GB, and the bill keeps rising. It costs him around 60% of revenue just for the database.
- He says he must reduce this usage or risk losing money, suspects an architecture issue with MongoDB, and asks for an expert to help.

- Marc collects feedback from his software users with a custom tool where people can upvote and suggest features.

## Marc's content workflow and a learning lesson from a Code Fast beta student

- Marc bought Justin Welsh's "Content OS" course after being inspired by his Twitter style; applying that approach around 2022 to 2023 made his Twitter following grow much faster. He says it is not an affiliate link and recommends it for anyone sharing thoughts in text online.
- His plan: dedicate a 30-minute time slot per day to writing, because he has many ideas to publish.
- He tells a story about a Code Fast beta student with a full-time job who studied part-time, started from the fundamentals (internet basics, React, Tailwind CSS) and showed up almost every day. With those skills he built a project that earned a freelance contract paid $60,000.
- His lesson: don't give up, keep creating and learning, because unexpected positive things can happen.

## Marc's view that it's the best time to start building, and his work setup

- He recommends Kevin Kelly's book on 12 technological forces (published about 10 years earlier), noting how right its predictions about AI and the internet turned out.
- His takeaway answers the common objection that "all ideas are taken" and "it's too late": we are "at the very beginning of something much bigger than us", so it is the perfect time to build "any business like a tiny little thing on the internet".
- Work routine mentioned: morning workout, breakfast, then about 3 to 4 hours of deep work; he is traveling as a slow nomad (New Zealand, then Japan, Korea, India, Europe, Canada, USA, South America), running community meetups along the way, and stays in each place a week or more.
- Lesson from a hard 16 km hike done in 4 hours instead of 6: keep going one step at a time and focus on the next step.
