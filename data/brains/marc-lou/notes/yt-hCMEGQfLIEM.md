## Marc's path: fired in 2021, 23 websites built solo, uneven results

- In 2021 Marc was fired from a software engineer job and went solo. He builds 23 websites and does the code, design and marketing himself.
- Results are very uneven: some products made nothing, some made well over five figures, and three were acquired.
- His first six years or so were spent on projects "nobody wanted to use". He credits shipping many small products, and an audience built along the way, for the eventual hits.
- Early learning products (2019-2020): Virally Bots, a Facebook Messenger game bot for escape rooms, charged $200/month and had about 20 customers at once. MRR peaked around $4K in December 2019 and it made roughly $69,000 in total. Covid killed it and he burned out.
- Game Widget, an on-site mini escape room for any business, sold pay-per-use at $19 per 500 website visitors. It made about $50-80/month and about $50 MRR (stated as 80 MRR when sold). He sold it for $4,300 as his first acquisition.
- He notes that Game Widget targeted a very wide market instead of escape rooms and struggled to get customers.

## Launch playbook: Hacker News, Product Hunt and a free micro-tool for each product

- Mood to Movie (movie recommendations by mood, never monetized): he posted on Hacker News as a nobody and it went viral, bringing roughly 10,000 visitors from that post and 100,000 in total. A joke "horny mood" created organic word of mouth, and TikTok resharing kept traffic spiking.
- Habits Garden launched on Hacker News and brought 14,000 visitors in April 2022 plus the first customers.
- Product Hunt results, across products:
  - 50 Hacks: second product of the day, about 45,000 visitors in the launch month and 200,000 in total.
  - Body Crush: third product of the day.
  - Hero Fit: product of the day.
  - Visualize Habit: Golden Kitty award and a badge.
  - Indie Page: product of the day, almost 200,000 visitors.
  - Logo Fast: product of the day, week and second of the month, plus a 2023 Golden Kitty.
- He credits Twitter community support for pushing launches higher, and says he built Hero Fit live on YouTube over 7 days with viewers helping debug and design.
- He builds small free tools that feed the main product. Books Calculator, Decision Game and Visualize Habit all promote Habits Garden. Logo Fast promotes ShipFast, and the fake-notification app promotes another of his products.
- Visualize Habit shares a card that links into Habits Garden, importing the user's habits into onboarding. Its launch landed at New Year's resolution time.

## Funny movie-parody videos as a launch engine

- He makes a short funny video for each launch, such as himself in The Wolf of Wall Street or playing World of Warcraft in real life.
- These videos give each launch an initial boost and bring the first visitors. The Wolf of Wall Street video, made for the fake-payment-notification app, is when his Twitter account started to grow.
- They did well on Twitter because people did not expect them from a mini startup founder.
- He uses ChatGPT to write the jokes ("I'm actually not a smart guy"), and AI to create fake backgrounds and characters.
- He turned this into a mini course teaching the whole process: script, shooting, editing and breakdowns of his videos. It made $6.7K one year and $88.7K the next, for a total of about $95K. The transcript says "about $15,000", which does not match, so treat the total as unclear. The course traffic comes from his Twitter.

## ShipFast, the biggest earner: $380K from a boilerplate

- He kept repeating the same setup for every project (landing page, login emails, Stripe event listeners), so he made a boilerplate for himself and then opened it to others.
- ShipFast is a codebase on GitHub with documentation, setup for the landing page and Stripe, and components. He adds new components as he builds new apps.
- Revenue: a little over $200,000 from the first Stripe account, another $176,000 from a second one, and about $8,000 from PayPal and other payments. Total about $380,000.
- Traffic comes mostly from Twitter, YouTube and word of mouth.
- He values the testimonials most. Hundreds of people told him it changed their lives, after years of being "a nobody".
- The page includes a demo video of him explaining the boilerplate.

## AI products and selling one: Landing AI / Make Landing

- Mech Ling started as Landing AI, a website generator. The first version was a one-time payment of about $30 per landing page.
- The editor reuses components from his previous sites. Users can edit text, buttons, fonts, colors and sections, use Unsplash images, and deploy to their domain or export HTML.
- Revenue: about $7,000 under Landing AI, then $8,600 after rebranding (the transcript also says about $167,000 total, which is unclear). About 10% of profit went to OpenAI and Stable Diffusion credits. The product ran at over 10,000 visitors per month.
- In November-December 2023 he realized he competed against Framer, Squarespace and WordPress. He listed it on Acquire and sold it within a few weeks for $35,000, for a total over $50,000.
- Workbook PDF (AI language exercise generator) used a $30 for 30 days day pass because he dislikes subscriptions as a customer. It made money at launch, then a few dozen dollars per month.
- Naval AI, a chatbot trained on Naval's tweets, was built with a friend, Martin. It made a few hundred dollars, and they shut it down because of cost and abuse.

## Pricing: no subscriptions, one-time payments, and removing the free plan

- Habits Garden launched at $9/month or $54/year. Revenue was a few hundred per month at first; after he removed the free plan, revenue went up.
- Habits Garden reached about $300 MRR, plus about 50% more from the iOS and Android apps (about $500 MRR at peak). It made roughly $6,000 on web and about $2,000 from the stores, and he sold it for $10,000 to a buyer who reached out on Twitter.
- He prefers one-time payments:
  - Indie Page: $25 for one year or $45 lifetime. It made a bit over $1,000, with $500-1,200 per month, and has about 5,000 solopreneurs.
  - ZenVoice: one-time $49, or $69 for unlimited Stripe accounts.
  - Poop Up: one-time $9 for one website or $19 unlimited.
  - Buy Dispute: pay per transaction, cheaper as volume grows.
- Monetization attempts that failed: ads on 50 Hacks made $350. The ad paywall was connected to his analytics dashboard. Gamify List's promotion slots got zero customers, and Hero Fit's paid avatars made about $30.

## Building products from his own pain points, and building fast

- Buy Dispute: the first five Habits Garden payments were from a fraudulent card and Stripe banned him overnight. A contact reinstated him because he had a Twitter audience. He built a tool that listens to Stripe events and applies rules, such as refunding the last payment if a card is reported stolen. It made $32,000 at launch and about $4,000 total (the transcript is unclear on figures). He uses it on all of his own products.
- ZenVoice: he realized he had paid Stripe over $1,000 (the amount is unclear in the transcript) just to generate PDF invoices, which cost up to $1 per invoice. He built a one-time-payment alternative, and it made a little over $4,000 in the first 2 months.
- Poop Up: a notification plugin built in 3 days after ZenVoice, because people loved the notification. It made about $32,000 (stated as "three 32,000") in a little over a month and got attention from Vercel's CEO.
- Logo Fast: a free logo generator built from his own process of editing SVGs. Every download saves a logo to a public directory, which is an SEO experiment. More than 2,000 of those pages are ranking on Google. It earns indirectly through ShipFast promotion.
- He uses Capacitor to wrap HTML and CSS in a web view and ship iOS and Android apps in days.
- Programmatic SEO: Books Calculator has over 300 pages, which brought a few dozen clicks per day after a month with no traffic. Gamify List got traffic from keyword rankings he did not plan, and directory pages ranked.
