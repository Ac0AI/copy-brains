## Why Marc stopped building AI products and built something he wanted instead

- In April 2023 Marc made two decisions: "Always have a buy button on every project" and "Build painkillers, not vitamins."
- At the peak of AI FOMO he built MakeLanding and WorkbookPDF. Both made money, and MakeLanding generated $12,000 in a few months. But it had low conversion, angry customers and a high refund rate.
- In August 2023 he came back from two weeks off with a goal: "Make something people want." Since he did not know what people want, he built something he wanted himself: a code boilerplate to ship faster. He built it in a week with no expectations and launched it as ShipFast.
- In its first 48 hours ShipFast earned more money than he made in all of 2022 ($6,000 of profit).

## Don't build AI products; use AI to improve your own workflow

- Marc asked "What can AI improve or fix?" A million ideas came to mind, but none fixed problems he actually had.
- He argues that if there is an easy way to find a startup idea, VCs and people who experience the problem are already on it.
- FOMO is real, and Twitter success stories keep feeding it. His advice is not to fall into the trap.
- If you want to work with AI, build something else and look at how AI could improve your workflow.

## Focus is overrated: launch many startups, then focus on what sticks

- Marc's track record of time versus results:
  - 365 days on his first startup: $0
  - 365 days on a habit tracker: $5,000
  - 7 days on ShipFast: $6,000
- He compares startups to relationships: you would not marry someone you met last week. Don't try to make a startup work when there is no initial traction.
- His approach is to create a bunch of startups, see what sticks, and then focus. He dated for 6 years, and his focus is now IndiePage and ShipFast.
- Counterexample he addresses: someone on Twitter focused on one startup and it took off after 2 years. Marc says that person probably worked in the industry for years and knew something others didn't. "Unless you're that guy, don't marry startups without product-market fit."

## Design is underrated: the design is the thumbnail, the copy is the title

- The most common feedback on ShipFast was "Landing page is hot."
- His framing: "Your design is the YouTube thumbnail. Your copy is the video title." Visitors are caught by the design and stay for the copy.
- He does not recommend weeks of SVG doodles. Good design is three things:
  1. **Padding**: the right space between copy, buttons and sections. He calls RefactoringUI by TailwindCSS "a masterpiece" for this.
  2. **Layout**: big headlines, smaller paragraphs.
  3. **Typeface**: he picked Bricolage Grotesque for ShipFast.

## Product-market fit means users pull out their credit cards like a no-brainer

- Two ShipFast signals he cites:
  - A customer apologized because a bug stopped him from accessing what he had paid for.
  - Another customer asked for Marc's bank details because he had no credit card, and wired the money.
- For 7 years Marc tried to convince users his apps were worth trying, which he calls "pushy." With ShipFast, users were pulled into the product.
- His rule: build something that makes users want to pull out their credit cards like a no-brainer, and don't waste time on the rest.
- His framing on time: "We live 4,680 weeks, you have 4,680 shots."
