## Why Marc launches on Product Hunt and what traffic to expect

- Marc says Product Hunt has about 4 million visitors a month, with a high-quality audience of designers, programmers, agency owners and solopreneurs.
- Track record he cites: "maker of the Year" in 2023, 21 products launched on Product Hunt over two to three years, 13 awards. He says the first launches got nothing and it got easier each time.
- Top product of the day: expect 2,000 to 3,000 visitors from the platform itself.
- Top 5 to 10 of the day: the product also gets shared in one of the biggest tech newsletters, adding another 1,000 to 2,000 visitors.
- Media and other platforms feed off Product Hunt, so spikes keep coming after launch day.
- He calls it a very good, free investment that needs no prior experience.

## How the Product Hunt ranking works

- A new batch of products is shown every 24 hours, and the timer resets at midnight PST.
- For the first 4 hours there is no ranking by upvotes. Order is random, to avoid winner-takes-all.
- From the fifth hour, products rank by upvotes. Whoever is on top tends to stay near the top, and the bottom rarely climbs.
- Marc's conclusion: the first four hours matter most, so the goal is to collect as many upvotes as possible in that window.
- There are two upvote sources: inbound (people browsing Product Hunt) and outbound (people you bring in).

## Winning inbound upvotes: name, tagline, logo, gallery, first comment

- **Name:** short, simple, easy to read. Use a logical name that says what it does (his example: "ShipFast", a tool to ship fast) rather than a VC-style name like Stripe or Tesla.
- **Tagline:** the most important part of the listing.
  - 60 to 70 characters maximum, one sentence.
  - It should say why a stranger would invest their time.
  - It should be about what the product does for customers, the pain it relieves and how their life looks afterward, not about the product itself.
- **Logo:** make it stand out. If most logos are blue, try green for contrast. The goal is to be remarkable.
- **Image carousel:** the first three images matter most.
  - One image per feature, kept simple.
  - Zoom in on a tiny part of the software instead of showing the full dashboard, because nobody reads small text unless it is a big headline.
- **First comment:** the founder writes their story of how the product came to be.
  - People relate to people. Marc frames the maker as a nice person quitting their 9-to-5, not a VC-backed company.
  - Explain why you built it, what it took, and how much time you invested.
  - Comments support markdown, so link the product and your social accounts (YouTube, Twitter journey) to start growing a following.

## Winning outbound upvotes: Twitter, other socials, users, and a website badge

- **Family and friends:** where he started, but he calls there more reliable ways.
- **Twitter:** it overlaps well with Product Hunt, and many solopreneurs there have Product Hunt accounts and are happy to help.
  - This comes down to writing good tweets: easy to read, a headline that catches attention, a relatable story, and a good creative.
  - His own start was fun launch videos, such as a fake podcast with Joe Rogan or a movie with Leonardo DiCaprio. He had a few hundred followers then. People watched, upvoted, and reshared, and that is how he grew an audience.
  - He sells a small course on how he makes those videos.
- **Other social media:** share the listing link on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok or wherever you are active.
- **Existing users:** if you have, say, 100 daily active users, email them or reach them through any channel and ask for support. They likely already love you.
- **Website badge:** add a banner saying "we are live on Product Hunt for the next 24 hours, we would love your support".
  - It brings Product Hunt visitors who land on your site back to the platform for an extra upvote.
  - It also converts visitors arriving from other platforms.
- **Launch everywhere at once:** he schedules the Product Hunt launch and launches the same day on Hacker News, Reddit and Twitter. Traffic from those platforms sees the badge and adds upvotes, which "brings an extra juice to Product Hunt".

## Choosing the launch day: traffic vs. social proof

- **For traffic:** Monday to Friday are the busiest days, with more browsers but also more competition, so awards are harder.
- He suggests launching at the start of the week, since "VC startups don't work on Monday at midnight" and so tend not to launch then.
- **For social proof:** if you want the Product Hunt badge to embed on your site, launch on a weekend. Sundays are usually the calmest, with less competition and a better chance of getting the badge.

## When to launch and how launching compounds

- Marc says launch once you have at least some testimonials on your site. Many new people will see the product, and it is easier to sell when others already validate it.
- You can relaunch the same product a few months later, and you can launch free tools to promote your main product.
- The first launch is hard and may not go as expected, but it is a necessary step for anyone serious about becoming a solopreneur.
- Audience loop he describes:
  - Launching products is why people follow you on Twitter.
  - A bigger audience gives more momentum on the next launch.
  - That in turn grows the audience further.
- He says that after a couple of launches you are almost guaranteed a Product Hunt badge and a few hundred to a few thousand visitors.
- His advice: "don't overthink it", launch, and repeat the same recipe "until luck finds you". The audience is built by shipping products, not created out of thin air.
