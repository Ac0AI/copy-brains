## Building TrustMRR from a tweet, and why it started as a 6-hour vibe-coded leaderboard

- Marc built the first version of TrustMRR in 6 hours by vibe-coding, after seeing a tweet from Pieter Levels saying many people fake their monthly recurring revenue screenshots.
- The idea: a public leaderboard where founders connect a Stripe API key so revenue is verified and shown on a public page.
- The tweet went viral and sent a lot of visitors to the site at launch.
- Eight months after launch it earns $44,000 a month, about 90% of it net profit. Marc runs it alone and says he has not touched a line of code.
- Within 3 months of launch he got a $1,000,000 offer and turned it down because he thought he could grow it much further. He also did not want to stay a month as an employee of his own former project.

## Sponsorships were the first revenue stream

- The first monetization was sponsor cards displayed on the site.
- Sponsor slots started at about $200 a month and went up to about $1,500 a month as traffic grew.
- That brought in roughly $20,000 a month consistently from launch.

## Ride existing trends instead of building from zero

- Marc says the idea was not built from nothing: it came from reacting to a tweet. It is much easier to build something tied to what is happening right now.
- A trend does not need to be a new technology. It can be someone saying "I have this problem" and you shipping a solution within an hour.
- Method: browse Twitter attentively and follow people who share problems or worries, then use that to ride the current trend and see if it goes viral.
- Example 1: his friend Rob saw Pieter Levels mention that many startups are vibe-coded, and built "Vibe Coded", a site listing SaaS you can replace by vibe-coding yourself. It made $9,000 in 4 days.
- Example 2: when OpenClaw launched at the start of 2026, someone shipped simple OpenClaw setup tools and went from zero to $30,000 in monthly recurring revenue in two weeks.
- Marc did the same with TrustMRR by building a directory of OpenClaw startups with Stripe-verified revenue, which brought more traffic.

## Quit fast and place many bets

- Marc says learning to quit quickly is crucial, though hard.
- His success rate is roughly 5 to 10%, so about 90% of what he works on goes nowhere because nobody wants to use it.
- It is painful to accept, but quitting fast buys time to make more bets, and more bets raise the chance one goes viral.

## Finding product-market fit after the launch spike faded

- After the launch spike, traffic and revenue fell to an all-time low at the end of January 2026. That is normal for anything that spikes.
- Unlike projects he never hears about, this one kept getting mentioned and copied on social media. Others building alternatives was a sign he was on the right track.
- He knew it could not stay just a leaderboard of verified startup revenue, so he tried many things: Tinder MRR (for fun), a Warren Buffett guessing game, and about 50 small features requested by people who mentioned the brand on social media.
- What worked: someone said they had a startup listed and wanted to sell it. Marc built an `/acquire` marketplace on the site.
- He then built a full dashboard for the acquisition process: listing, inbox with interested buyers, chat, file sharing, NDAs, letters of intent, and an escrow transaction where the buyer sends money, the seller transfers assets, and escrow handles the legal side.
- He expected it not to work because of the legal complexity of sending money to a stranger online, but acquisitions started happening.
- One company sold for about $85,000. Marc takes a 3% broker commission, which now brings in about $5,000 a month.
- Sellers sharing their deals on social media created a snowball effect. By August 2026, 144 founders had been acquired.

## Choosing products AI cannot copy

- Because AI makes copying easy, Marc looks for products with a competitive advantage that cannot be cloned overnight. A notes app or habit tracker is easy to copy.
- AI cannot copy 6 months of trust and a database of 20,000 buyers on TrustMRR. In his words, AI cannot simulate trust, which takes years of work to build.
- Focusing on something AI cannot copy means he expects to be fine 5 or even 10 years from now.
- Second example: "Stalker", a tool that tracks brand keyword mentions on social media. It is harder to copy because you need data access from Reddit and YouTube, and paid access to X and Reddit.
- He uses Stalker on "TrustMRR" to find feature requests, drops them into Codex to check if they make sense, then builds the feature.

## Getting 200,000 monthly visitors by building features people want to share

- Traffic strategy is always the same: make things people want to share. If a feature makes people proud to post it on social media, it works and he never has to do marketing.
- Users market themselves, and more people discover his product through them.
- TrustMRR features built for this: users add their texts and logos, add marketing channels, embed a landing page screenshot, and connect GitHub to share product updates. Founders share screenshots of their charts showing revenue over time.
- Same strategy for his web analytics tool DataFast: the "globe" shows real-time visitors and people share it. Others ask "how did you do that?" and the answer mentions DataFast, so they visit directly.
- He believes AI is good at everything except design. With the web flooded by sloppy AI sites and people tired of AI-looking content, careful, simple, good design is a competitive advantage. He builds shareable features partly for that reason.

## Picking what to work on now

Two criteria for choosing projects:

- The product must be immune to AI, meaning it cannot be replicated with a single prompt (TrustMRR's trust, Stalker's data access).
- Or it rides a current trend. At recording time (August 2026) the trend is AI visibility: whether ChatGPT mentions your brand, how many people see your keyword inside Claude. He is updating DataFast with AI visibility features, including a visibility score compared with competitors and seeing when AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude crawl your sites.
- Also new: "Trust Launch", a Product Hunt alternative inside TrustMRR. It is a page of startups launching today, ranked by votes, with basic financial metrics and comments. It uses the site's 200,000 monthly visitors to send traffic to indie developers.
