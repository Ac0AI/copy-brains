## Finding the idea: a pain with a money incentive

- Marc paid Stripe $1,600 for PDF invoices, turned the option off, and within days his inbox was flooded with customers asking for invoices.
- His rule: "A pain with a money incentive is promising."
- He searched for alternatives, found none, and built a self-serve invoicing tool for Stripe (ZenVoice).
- He built it live on YouTube, starting February 5th, 2024, in 31 hours. It made almost $2,000 in 5 days.
- His advice on ideas: "think less, build more."

## Pricing first, with an anchor plan

- Marc brainstorms pricing before building: "Pricing is everything." Customers look at price first, especially when they don't understand the product, and it is how he pays rent.
- ZenVoice used one-time payments because running it is almost free:
  - $49: unlimited invoices for 1 Stripe account
  - $69: unlimited invoices for unlimited Stripe accounts
- Costs: bandwidth under $1/month and emails at $0.001 each. A customer generating more than 49,000 invoices would lose him money, but he expects that never to happen across all customers.
- He offers 2 plans because "people have no clue how much a tool like this costs. Confused customers don't buy." The $49 plan is the anchor that makes the second plan make sense.

## One startup, one feature

- Marc's rule to avoid wasting time and burning out: "1 startup = 1 feature". He cites that 90% of startups fail.
- ZenVoice's single feature: customers of his users can generate invoices.
- He skipped the password reset form, invoice preview and account deletion.
- He did include a feedback button, so he learns what users need instead of guessing blindly.

## Landing page as important as the product

- Rationale: on average only 1 in 100 website visitors will see the product.
- He spent 11 hours on the landing page, 37% of the build time.
- Key elements:
  - **Problem agitation:** mock notifications of the annoying emails you'd get if you turned off Stripe Invoicing, reminding visitors why they are there. This got Vercel's CEO's attention.
  - **Headline:** a promise with an emotional outcome: "Focus on your startup, not the invoices".
  - **Demo video:** he demos the product and explains why he built it, because he's "not a cold VC startup".
  - **Social proof:** beta users tested it in exchange for a testimonial.
  - **Name:** ZenVoice is short, memorable and tied to the pain (in-voice).

## Launching everywhere to get the first customers

- He launches on every platform; Product Hunt, Reddit, Hacker News and Twitter have the most impact.
- He formats each post in the platform's language: cold tech words on Hacker News, friendly vibes with emoji on Product Hunt.
- For Twitter he made a launch skit to kickstart the post.

## Deciding what's next: dollars per visitor

- At 10,000 visitors (7,600 at the time of writing), Marc looks at one metric: **$ per visitor**.
- His worst has been $0.06 per visitor; his best is $0.93 (ShipFast).
- Decision rule for ZenVoice: above $0.5 per visitor, he focuses on it. Otherwise he lets it run by itself, fixes bugs and moves on to the next startup.
