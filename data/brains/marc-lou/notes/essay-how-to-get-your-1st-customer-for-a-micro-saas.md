## Four channels for landing a first customer with 0 audience and $0 budget

Marc's premise: assume a product, no audience and no marketing money. He lists four strategies he has used as a solopreneur to get customers for all his startups: launch, cold outreach, build an audience, and code.

## Launch platforms are the fastest free channel

- Marc says listing on launch platforms like Product Hunt is "the fastest way to get results. And it's free."
- Platforms he names: Product Hunt, Reddit, Hacker News.
- He spends at least 1 hour crafting the headline and calls it "80% of a successful launch."
- If the product is an AI tool and there is a little money to spare, he suggests listing on AI directories such as TIAAIFT and Futurepedia.

## Cold outreach: quality over quantity

- Marc notes that developers are scared of cold outreach, but says it works.
- He made his first $1 online, and grew a SaaS to $4,000 MRR, using cold emails.
- Cold DMs on Twitter, LinkedIn and Instagram work too "if you do it right."
- He receives about 30 cold outreaches per day; 99% are generic and 99% end up in the trash. "Quantity doesn't matter. Quality does."
- A good message is benefit-oriented, ultra personalised, and short (5 sentences max). He suggests spending hours crafting it.
- Cold outreach works best for high-ticket clients or B2B.
- If money allows, sponsoring niche newsletters is a way to do cold outreach at scale without being salesy.

## Building an audience: slow, not for everyone, life-changing

Marc says building an audience "changed my life", but unlike other channels it takes a lot of time and isn't for everyone. His 80/20:

1. Pick a platform where you are comfortable sharing your work: Twitter if you like writing, Instagram if you are good with photos, YouTube if you prefer video.
2. Build cool stuff. People need a reason to follow you.
3. Communicate using the platform's code. Learn from creators who are already successful.
4. Respect people's time. "Too short is better than too long."
5. Find your signature: start by getting inspired by others, then shine by being you.

## Code-driven marketing for developers

- For developers who "can't close VSCode," Marc points to marketing through building:
  - Build free tools you can launch anywhere and go viral.
  - Rank hundreds of pages on Google for free with programmatic SEO (pSEO).

## Diagnosing why none of the channels work

If all the strategies have been tried and nothing works, Marc says it is usually one of two things:

1. The product doesn't solve a real problem, so consider moving on.
2. The landing page copy isn't converting, so improve it.

He adds: "Your first endeavor won't magically be a success. But if you show up for long enough, luck will find you."
