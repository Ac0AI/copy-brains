## Marc's results and why validation matters

- In 2022, 25% of Marc's startups made money. In 2023, 71% of his startups made money.
- He credits the improvement to getting better at picking which idea to build, not just finding ideas. He calls picking the right startup "a skill" and uses a 3-step framework.
- His core belief: "There is no perfect idea. The only way to know is to launch it." The framework only raises the odds of success and gets him profitable faster.

## Test 1: Is it painful? Build painkillers, not vitamins

- Marc spent a year full-time on Habits Garden, a gamified habit tracker. It reached 10,000+ accounts and had many happy users, but makes less than $400/month.
- VirallyBot, a marketing tool for escape rooms, makes $600/month, and he hasn't touched it since 2019.
- The difference: Habits Garden is a vitamin (cool to have), VirallyBot is a painkiller (escape rooms need marketing).
- "People are much more willing to pay for a product that solves their problems."
- Tactics:
  - Focus on painkillers to reach profitability faster.
  - Turn a vitamin into a painkiller by explaining why the product is a must-have. Example: "Track your habits" becomes "Diet habit you won't quit".

## Test 2: Would you really use it yourself?

- Marc's wife is a Korean teacher. He typed "Korean workbooks" into Google, saw that people were searching for it, and 3 weeks later built WorkbookPDF (AI-generated language workbooks). He launched and made "a few bucks".
- The product is stuck because he doesn't use it. He has no idea how to write the copy or what features to add.
- Keyword research is a valid way to validate ideas, but it is "pale in comparison" to using the product yourself.
- Why being your own user matters:
  - Worst case, you improved your own life.
  - Chances are someone else needs the product too.
  - You understand the frustration, so your copy will be on point.

## Test 3: How long until launch? Think in weeks

- A solopreneur has limited time, especially with a 9 to 5 job.
- Marc's rule: if it takes 6 months to build, it's not a good idea, because it will actually take 12 months (overthinking and bugs), and you could burn out and quit.
- Think in weeks and ditch ideas that take longer than a month.
- Ways to lower time-to-market:
  - Presell the product.
  - Ditch code testing and dark mode.
  - Break big ideas into 1-feature-only products.
