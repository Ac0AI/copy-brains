## Relaunching the same product up to 100 times without changing it

- Marc has launched 30+ startups and still hates the feeling of launching a new product "and nothing happens."
- His core claim: "You can relaunch the same product 100 times without changing it. Nobody keeps track."
- He relaunches until one version takes off, using three levers: rebrand, reposition, repurpose.
- Each relaunch teaches more about what people want, making the founder "a better marketer, a better builder, a better founder."
- His closing rule: "Launch without moderation."
- Formula: Rebrand + Reposition + Repurpose = a whole new launch. Mix them until the product takes off.

## Rebrand: same product, new name, domain, colors and hero section

- A rebrand changes the name, domain, color theme and hero section while the product stays the same.
- The hero section matters most. Marc says "80% of people don't scroll past the headline," and in most cases that is where the bottleneck is.
- He says to radically change the offer in the hero, not tweak it.
- Design matters because "people judge a book by its cover, and your landing page by its hero section."
- Example: he rebranded his AI website builder from LandingAI to MakeLanding, then sold the SaaS in 2023 for $35,000.
- After rebranding, relaunch everywhere with the different offer until one clicks.

## Reposition: same product, new angle for a new customer segment

- Repositioning means changing the landing page copy to speak to a new customer segment while the product stays the same.
- Marc refines the words until they are "ultra-specific for your ideal customer and nonsense for the others." It works best when you niche down.
- The angle can change along three axes: a different pain point, a different level of expertise, or a different culture.
- DataFast evidence, conversion rate on the landing page:
  - 0.22% at launch a year earlier, positioned as an analytics tool.
  - 1.45% after repositioning to a revenue attribution tool (a niche).
- His takeaway: "Small changes in words = big changes in conversions."
- After repositioning, relaunch everywhere again.

## Repurpose: turn one feature into a free tool and launch that

- Repurposing keeps the product and landing page but opens a new vertical by extracting one useful feature into a free tool.
- Give the tool away with no signup and use it as a funnel into the main product.
- Example: a free logo maker (LogoFast) brought $20,000 in revenue "and counting."
- Reasoning: people love free tools and they share them.
- Launch the free tool everywhere and "repeat without moderation."
- Marc has a dedicated issue on free tool marketing aimed at product-obsessed developers.
