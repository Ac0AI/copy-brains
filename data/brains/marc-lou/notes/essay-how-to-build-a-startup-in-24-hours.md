## The "Minimum MVP": every startup has a 24-hour version

Marc argues that "we overthink startups." After 20+ startups and a year (2017) spent building something that got 0 users, he says there is always a version of your startup that can be published within 24 hours. His method:

- Find that 24-hour baseline first.
- Add only the bare minimum on top to reach a minimal MVP in days, not months.
- Examples he uses: "Airbnb is a spreadsheet", "Instagram is a Google Drive", "McDonald's is a pop-up store".
- He admits the product-obsessed developer in you will resist shipping this scrappy, and says he is the same.

## What is enough for a minimal MVP: headline, buy button, one feature

Marc's definition of a minimal MVP is "A headline, a buy button, and 1 feature."

- **Headline:** the most important piece. It works like a YouTube thumbnail: people decide to invest their time on your site based on that one sentence.
- **Buy button:** "You can only validate an idea if people pay for it." A waitlist is not enough, so the minimal MVP must have a way to pay.
- **One feature:** it should solve the customer's most painful problem. If that feature needs smaller supporting features, that is fine, but he says not to overthink it.

## What to ditch before launching

Marc lists three categories to cut from a first launch:

- Anything invisible to the customer: code testing, git branches, new frameworks.
- Anything that adds no direct customer value: forgotten password, dark mode, a professional logo.
- Any secondary features.

His framing: "Shipping startups fast isn't about working 12 hours a day. It's about removing noise." He advises shipping minimalist and being comfortable being imperfect.

## Why most effort is wasted: the launch funnel math

Marc illustrates why polishing before launch rarely pays, using a Product Hunt launch example:

- 1,000 visitors.
- 900 read the headline.
- 500 scroll past the first section.
- 50 sign up.
- 5 pay.

That is a 0.5% conversion rate, which he calls optimistic for a first startup. His conclusion: spending 3 days on a password recovery form was probably not worth the time, and "Most of your efforts have 0 financial outcome, that's entrepreneurship."

## Avoiding burnout by shipping fast

Marc ties speed to mental health as well as money. Spending months on a startup that ends up with 5 users and $100 in revenue is "a recipe for burnout", and he says he was there in 2021. Shipping a minimal MVP in weeks is, in his words, an alternative "for mental well-being and financial success."
