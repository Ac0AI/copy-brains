## A logo starts from the customer's life upgrade, not the product

- Marc says a solopreneur should be able to make a logo in about 5 minutes, with no back-and-forth with a designer, so more time goes to shipping.
- He describes himself as someone who "used to suck at design" but found a simple blueprint after creating 30+ logos. It needs no design skills.
- Step one is to define what the startup sells: the problem it solves and how that upgrades the customer's life.
- His examples:
  - Habit tracker: helps people who struggle with consistency get in shape.
  - Code boilerplate: templates that avoid writing code from scratch so developers build faster.
  - PDF AI chat app: saves time by not reading long, boring documents.
- He admits this step is hard, but once it is done a logo is "minutes away".

## Choose a shape that depicts the life upgrade, not the product

- The logo's shape "shouldn't be about the product at all". It should depict the life upgrade being offered.
- Examples:
  - Habit tracker: a seed or a strong bicep, representing transformation.
  - Code boilerplate: a lightning bolt or a Formula 1 car, representing speed.
  - PDF AI chat app: a clock, representing time saved.
- If the right icon is unclear, Google "Icon to depict [life upgrade]".
- For a workout habit tracker, a strong bicep is the fit.

## Source the icon from free SVG libraries and import it into Figma

- Marc always uses one of these free SVG libraries:
  1. Iconbuddy
  2. React Lucide
  3. Heroicons
- The workflow is to search for the shape, download the SVG, and import it into Figma.
- Using an existing icon removes any need for drawing or illustration skills.

## Use one primary color and skip color palettes

- He tells designers to "ditch color palettes", because solopreneurs lack the time and skills for them.
- Pick one primary color and stick to it.
- If unsure which color, search for symbolic color meanings, meaning how colors make people feel:
  - Red: passion or excitement (Tinder).
  - Yellow: hope or joy (McDonald's).
  - Green: nature or growth (Instacart).
- For the fitness habit tracker he would choose red, since it targets fitness, even though green was an option.
- Base recipe: put the primary color in the background and fill the icon with white. You can also swap the background and fill colors.

## Variations: gradients, black and white, app-style

- **Gradients**: trendy for AI products. Select the SVG and change the stroke color to a gradient. Keep the gradient colors in the same zone (red with yellow, green with blue) and avoid mixing hot and cold colors.
- **Black and white**: what Twitter did when rebranding to 𝕏; it looks "slick/noble". Play with the SVG fill color and background color to see what works best.
- **Mobile-first startups**: give an app feeling by adding a large border-radius to the edges and a shadow.

## Speed comes from repetition, and the logo can be productized

- Marc says the first logo takes some time, the second is faster, and the third takes about 5 minutes. "Keep shipping and you'll be unstoppable."
- He turned the process into LogoFast, a free tool to make logos in seconds.
- Building-in-public aside: he normally never changes his tech stack or learns new frameworks, because it keeps him from shipping. He broke that rule and added Supabase to ShipFast because users requested the same feature repeatedly.
