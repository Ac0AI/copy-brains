## Marc's view: good startup ideas can't be found, only discovered by doing

- Marc argues that good ideas are not something you can go and find. His test: "If good ideas could be found, there would be a marketplace."
- He compares successful startups to Nutella, which was an accident. During World War II there wasn't enough cacao, so Pietro Ferrero tried making chocolate with hazelnuts and ended up creating the world's biggest chocolate spread.
- His claim: "Successful startups are born by accident too."

## Where startups actually come from

Marc says a startup usually starts in one of three ways:
- The founder plays with new technology.
- The founder solves a problem they have themselves.
- The founder listens to what their customers want.

None of these starts with a sit-down brainstorming session to find the idea.

## Don't wait for a good idea, put your work out there

- Marc says you don't need a good idea to start.
- He suggests low-stakes starting actions:
  - Write an article about what you learned last year.
  - Rebuild your note-taking app.
  - Send a cold email to a business.
- The act of creating and publishing is what surfaces ideas: "The process of creating will help you discover problems."
- Those are real problems people would pay you to solve, and you may end up with a business you never imagined.
- His summary: "Less thinking, more doing."

## Marc's own chain of accidents

Marc describes his solopreneur journey as an accident, one product leading to the next:
- He was bored, so he played with AI and created MakeLanding.
- MakeLanding led to ShipFast.
- ShipFast led to ZenVoice.
- ZenVoice led to PoopUp.

## Products named in the essay

- CodeFast: learn to code in weeks, not months.
- ShipFast: a NextJS boilerplate, described as loved by 5,200+ developers, for shipping startups in days rather than weeks.
- DataFast: analytics to grow a startup with actionable data.
- IndiePage: a place for solopreneurs to showcase startups, with 7,000+ solopreneurs joined.
- ZenVoice: Stripe invoices without the 0.4% fee.
