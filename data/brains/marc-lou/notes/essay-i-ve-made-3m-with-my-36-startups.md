## The 10-year path: 36 startups, $3M, solo, 85% margin

- Marc reports $3M made across 36 startups over 10 years: solo, bootstrapped, 85% margin.
- He graduated in Computer Science in 2016 and spent a year on a "Tinder for sports lovers" idea. He never thought about how it would make money, and he was the only user.
- Next came an AI flight-price startup in Seoul ($100,000 from angel investors, 2 employees), which ended after 9 months with a bad product and no income.
- He then sold "Glovers" (couple gloves) knitted by a Korean grandma. He spent a few hundred dollars on Facebook Ads and made 0 sales, then sold about 2 gloves a day on the street.
- The pattern he drew from it: "Big goals, big failures."
- His later rules: think in weeks, build tiny projects fast, and quit even faster. "I don't think in years. I don't dream big."
- 90% of his 36 startups failed, and he says that's fine: "My goal is to build stuff that makes me happy, even if it doesn't make money."

## Getting the first $1 online: problems over products, sell before building

- In April 2018 he set a simple goal: "Make $1 online with code."
- To avoid another failure, he focused on problems, not products. His reasoning was that every business wants to grow, so he chose a gamified marketing tool for escape rooms.
- A marketer friend, Andrey, told him: "Why don't you sell it before you make it?"
- Marc is an engineer who loves building and dislikes marketing, but he listened. After a few cold emails, an escape room in Australia took a call.
- After a 42-minute pitch, she subscribed to the $99 monthly plan the same day. He built the product afterwards.
- That became VirallyBot, which made about $100,000 in total ($30K of it in an old PayPal account). In his words: "1 call paid the bills for 5 years."

## Lessons from VirallyBot and GameWidget: churn, the treadmill, and market risk

- VirallyBot was priced at $199/month. His daily routine was marketing in the morning (collecting leads, cold emails; cold calls didn't work for him) and product in the afternoon.
- It reached $1,500/month. Churn was high because customers only earned money if they actually used the software, and most didn't.
- He felt on a treadmill: new customer, new churn. A $1,999 yearly plan got some sales and revenue hit $3,000 in early 2020, but he was "just running faster on the treadmill."
- He built GameWidget in the same market with a different fulfillment: an embedded mini-game that gives discount coupons, installed with a script so customers didn't have to operate software. Beta results showed a +0.6% conversion rate.
- COVID closed all escape rooms in March 2020 and revenue went to $0 in a week, a risk of depending on one narrow vertical.

## Shipping lots of small startups with strict rules

- After a depressing period (a $9,000/month job with Tai Lopez, then being fired), he came back inspired by Pieter Levels building in public.
- Mood2Movie (December 2021) was a free, simple if-mood-then-genre app. It got 20K+ visitors via Hacker News, and a Reddit post brought 10K visitors. It kickstarted his build-in-public journey.
- In 6 months he launched 7 projects "just for fun" and learned coding, SEO, writing, launching and marketing. His Twitter reached 1,000 followers in July 2022.
- In 2023 he ran the strategy again, "ship startups like a madman", with three rules:
  - No free plan
  - Only painkiller products
  - Move on if there's no product-market fit
- Result: 8 startups in 8 months, 6 made money, 2 paid the bills, about $3,000/month by July 2023. His loop: "Ship fast, launch viral, repeat."
- His initial goal in building in public was 1 startup per month.

## Side-project marketing and launch videos

- Habits Garden, his gamified habit tracker, made about $200/month when he went all-in (August 2022). He didn't like marketing, and "Side-project marketing saved me: Ship free tools to promote a paid one."
- Example: VisualizeHabit, a free tool that visualizes the compound effect of tiny habits, plugged Habits Garden and brought 30,000 visitors in 2 months.
- For side projects to work he had to be good at launching, so he made fun launch videos (for example, a skit for ByeDispute). People shared them, creating a viral loop. Each side project grew his Twitter following and traffic.
- Habits Garden reached 10,000 users in January 2023.
- He sold a micro-startup making $80/month for $4,300, which gave him stories to tell, and his Twitter grew about 50 followers a day.

## Product-market fit with ShipFast and building for customers he cares about

- In 2023, 60% of revenue came from MakeLanding, an AI landing page generator. He couldn't grow it because he didn't use the product or care about the market, and built it out of "AI FOMO."
- Habits Garden's mobile app took 6 months and made $700/month: "I fell in love with the wrong product."
- After a holiday, he concluded: "I must build a business for customers I care about." He quit faceless AI projects and focused on products he'd use.
- He sold MakeLanding for $35,000 and the habit tracker for $10,000, which gave him time and clarity.
- ShipFast launched September 1, 2023 as a NextJS boilerplate that packed 2 years of shipping knowledge. He expected $100 and it made $40,000 by month end.
- On product-market fit: "It's hard to describe product-market fit, but when it hits, it's clear."
- He kept adding features, tweeting about it and collecting customer testimonials.
- November 2023 revenue was $65,400/month at 91% margin with 0 employees. ShipFast made almost $200,000 by end of 2023.

## Content, info-products and what burned him out

- His YouTube channel started in 2024 and grew to 100K followers "because I had a story and numbers to back it up." ShipFast hit $133,000 in monthly revenue in April 2024.
- Overconfidence followed: he doubled down on content and info-products.
- CodeFast (a learn-to-code course, prompted by questions under his videos) took 9 months at 6 hours a day and made $100,000 in 2 days.
- Revenue swung from $120K one month to $40K the next. He made $1.2M by end of 2024 but it "felt like a rat race where I had to work harder and harder to keep the numbers up."
- DataFast (October 2024) connects Stripe with web analytics to find the best marketing channels. It hit $300 MRR in 2 weeks, tiny next to CodeFast, but he found it more fulfilling.
- His conclusion was to go back to building apps "out of curiosity, not revenue opportunity."

## Unpredictability and the TrustMRR, DataFast and Stalkr results

- TrustMRR launched October 30, 2025 and made $20,000/month in ad placements within 24 hours. He vibe-coded it in 6 hours.
- The hype died the next month, so he built verticals. After 10 failures, the startup marketplace took off in January 2026. He rejected a $1M acquisition offer.
- 7 months later: 164 founders acquired, 200K monthly visitors, almost $400,000 made.
- DataFast kept growing through added features: $30,831 MRR and about 7% churn as of September 2026.
- Stalkr (August 2026) does $223 MRR. He builds it for himself, using it to find testimonials, feature ideas and bug reports for his products.
- His summary: "I have no idea what's going to work." ShipFast and TrustMRR blew up overnight, DataFast was slow and steady, and about 30 others never really made money.
