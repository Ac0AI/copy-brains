## Stepping away after a company ends

- After his company ended, Farza took a one-way flight out of SF with no plan. He didn't know if he would ever come back.
- He spent six months in places he describes as some of the most peaceful on the planet: villages in Morocco, remote forests in the Czech Republic, and the outskirts of Islamabad.
- He listened to a lot of the Beatles, spent most days doing nothing, and met "some wise people on the road".
- His caveat: "you don't just get on a plane and have your problems disappear."

## The low point: feeling like a flop

- When the company ended, fans, friends and investors sent an overflow of support along the lines of "it was a good run. you gave it your best."
- Farza notes that "you are always your worst critic." For most of the trip he felt sad, tired and confused all at once.
- He felt like a flop and wondered whether he had really burned out and whether "the story ends here."
- What scared him most was the thought that he would never feel the love of building again, as if the spark he'd had since age 13 was "gone forever." He calls that "the worst."

## Recovery came from time, not a fix

- His explanation for getting better is plain: "time just did what time does."
- By Feb 2025 he reported feeling "happier than ever" and "more excited than ever."
- He says the light came back while he was on the road. He describes finding again "the kid that see's the world as brand new and just wants to make cool things."
- He says the full story will come "one day" and jokes that it did not involve acid.

## Coming back to building

- By the time of the post (Feb 20, 2025) Farza was back in SF, living 5 minutes from the office, and "falling in love with making things again."
- His summary: "i really feel 13 again." He says he is ready to "build something really fucking cool again" and adds "i'm not done yet."
- He was hacking on new ideas every day and invited people in SF who wanted to work together to get in touch and meet up.
- He thanked the thousands of people who messaged or tagged him during the break. He didn't have it in him to reply, but says it "meant more than you'll ever know."

## How he uses his channels

- Farza posted the essay on X and on Substack. He thinks Twitter is "decent for long form," but sometimes a piece also deserves its own post on Substack.
- He describes Twitter, Substack and his blog as usually "polished."
- He was enjoying Instagram for "more personal, day to day stuff" and invited people to follow him there.
