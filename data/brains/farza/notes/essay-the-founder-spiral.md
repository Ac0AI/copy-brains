## Spiraling is a third reason startups die, alongside failed fundraising and co-founder conflict

Farza argues that most early-stage companies he has seen die did not fail from failed fundraising or co-founder conflict. They died because the founder ended up in a downward mental spiral and could not catch themselves. He says this applies to anyone who creates something from nothing, including creators, musicians and researchers, and that nobody warned him about it.

- Common advice he was given: companies die because 1) the founder couldn't raise or 2) co-founders had internal problems.
- His addition: "The founder simply ends up in a downward spiral, and can't catch themselves."
- He thinks it goes unmentioned because it is "just isn't as cool to talk about" as other startup topics.

## How the founder spiral unfolds

The spiral starts with physical depletion, which makes everyday startup problems unsolvable, which deepens the negative state.

- **Trigger example:** 4-6 days in a row of 5-6 hours of sleep a night, not eating proper meals, and long working stretches with little movement. This leaves most people very tired.
- **New problems arrive anyway:** a growth experiment fails, cash runs low, the product turns out not to be great.
- **The tired founder can't cope:** no patience or mental strength to solve them. Annoyance at teammates and loved ones grows, the fuse gets shorter, annoyance turns into sadness, then self-blame ("not good enough").
- **It compounds:** the founder returns the next day and faces a new set of problems they can't solve, "because you can't even seem to solve the ones inside your own head."
- **Typical ending:** after roughly 3-6 months of spiraling the founder quits and the company or initiative dies.

## Catching a spiral early: Farza's warning signs and check-ins

Farza says he has no general solution and is "not a psychologist or therapist", only what works for him. His approach is to catch the spiral in the first 7-14 days.

- **Signals he watches for:** 1) getting annoyed at others much faster than usual, 2) really low energy.
- **Daily journaling** is part of how he notices.
- **Self check-in:** a couple of times a month he asks himself "am I spiraling?"
- **Why speed matters:** "The longer it takes me to recognize the spiral, the worse it gets, and the harder it is for me to muster the energy to get out of it."

## Farza's reset protocol when he realizes he's spiraling

When he realizes he is spiraling, he does the following:

1. Stop everything.
2. Admit to himself that he has lost to his emotions and is spiraling, and that this is okay.
3. Go home.
4. Spend one day doing things that give him energy. For him that means watching movies in theatres, running, sitting in a sauna or cold plunge (most effective for him), or an hour in a sensory deprivation tank.

After that day he returns to a solid routine of eating, sleeping and exercising.

## His own history of spirals

- **Worst spiral:** nearly 2 years, from ages 18 to 20. He says he can't believe he made it out. What got him out was discovering "a deep love for programing", which gave him enough energy to leave a very dark place.
- **Last bad spiral (more than 45 days):** over 5 years before the essay (written Aug 2023). He spent 2 straight weeks doing nothing but exercising really hard, eating right and watching movies.
- **Since then:** "nearly spiral-free". He still spirals occasionally but almost always catches it within the first 2 weeks.

## Why founders are especially exposed, and what to do

- Farza says founders are "**very susceptible** to spiraling and getting very sick from it" because their days are long, hard and full of problems from outside pressure.
- His prescription: "build that internal system that protects you." Don't let emotions rule you.
- If you are spiraling now, he advises taking a step back today or tomorrow and being easy on yourself. He compares a spiraling founder to "a car that's overheating": slow down first, then do things that energize you or bring joy, then make space to plan how to slow the spiral further.
