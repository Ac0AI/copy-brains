## Pivot discussions and finding the real "why"

- Farza and cofounder Sharif had a long talk about potentially pivoting Tidbit even though it had "classic startup" green flags: paying users, a lot of attention, new users arriving daily. Neither felt drawn to the idea.
- After a weekend of thinking, Farza concluded Tidbit was still the best idea they had. They had just strayed from the original vision and needed to refocus. He texted Sharif that on Monday and Sharif agreed.
- His principle: "I think an idea should be able to survive a pivot discussion." If it does, it strengthens your confidence in the idea.
- When something feels wrong and you can't say what, a discussion doesn't hurt.

## Why B2B worked on paper but didn't fit

- The week before, teams came in and tried the product. Farza and Sharif built what customers asked for: MCPs (PostHog, GitHub, Linear, some custom ones) and ways to collaborate on data with teammates.
- Example: a Tidbit channel with a PostHog MCP lets a non-technical teammate (Hasan, the content person) ask "how many people came from TikTok" without learning PostHog's event names. Farza can hop in to help prompt since it's multiplayer.
- Sales happened in DMs. Owen at Boardy had a custom MCP and was using Claude Desktop MCPs plus shared team folders. This was the classic "customer already hacked together a solution" signal, and it became a paying user.
- Farza saw the B2B path clearly: "Talk to customers, build what they want. Find 5-10 more. Repeat." He says he literally saw the path to $1M, then $10M.
- But he "stopped giving a shit so fast." He didn't care about custom MCPs, team dashboards or agentic webhook channels that save support time. He was waiting for customer calls to end.
- His rule: if he doesn't deeply care, he has little faith he'll build a good product. After his previous company, he is wary of work that depletes his energy. Sometimes you power through, but this early in a company he thinks he should be getting energy from it.
- On opportunity size: "what's the point of working on a $100M opportunity if I'm not motivated about the opportunity itself? I probably won't even make it far + give up."

## Returning to the guiding light: build what you use daily

- Farza and Sharif realized they were no longer building a product they used daily, which was their number one guiding light. Customers could be made happy, but they had lost sight of why they were building it.
- They want to build for consumers: people like their parents, friends and past coworkers.
- The analytics backed this up. The heaviest users were normal people using the long-context feature for everything from #copywriting to #therapy to #tiktok-scripts. A text from Alec, founder of an app studio, reinforced it.
- They dialed back "multiplayer" and refocused on single-player consumer.
- New one-liner: "Tidbit is an interface for power users of Claude."
- Result: better ideas, better energy, more smiling, "naturally obsessed," more bullish.

## Product direction: long-context memory and channels

- The problem: people want Claude and GPT to retain context across sessions and build on previous work instead of "having amnesia every time." This matters most for content creation, research and any work of constantly refining ideas.
- Channels were kept, but got messy without a thread interface, so Sharif built threads. A channel like #design remembers past chats, so asking for inspiration on a new UI references past UIs he liked or disliked.
- Building ChatGPT and Claude imports: one click brings all conversations into Tidbit so users feel channels plus long-context memory immediately.
- Plan: launch it together with small features and a new landing page the following week.

## Shipping philosophy for a final-week swing

- With 7 days left before re-evaluating, the team decided to take "a massive swing."
- Farza's stance: "We rather the app looking massively different each week and we test new hypotheses vs make a bunch of small incremental changes over a longer period on a flawed hypothesis."
- Schedule: 6 days on, 1 day off, always Sunday off no matter what, office locked.

## TikTok content experiment

- Hasan, the content person, got almost 4,000,000 views in 7 days, making about 3-5 TikToks a day. Some pop, some flop.
- Conversion (view profile, go to link, make account) is about 0.5% on super Tidbit-specific content. Meme and trend content converts much lower but "juices algo."
- TikToks also get reposted by others on Instagram and gain results there.
- Farza says the content is unlike anything he'd have thought of himself. He trusts Hasan and treats the team as "actor bots" for him. So far it's working.
- Conversion takes time, but he is pleased with early numbers.

## Cofounder fit and the team plan

- Farza and Sharif visited YC and spoke with Aaron Epstein, who worked with Sharif in 2019 and Farza in 2020. Aaron told them: "I think you two should just work together forever," and explained how they balance each other emotionally.
- Farza notes this was third-party validation from someone who has known them for years and seen thousands of founders. It wasn't needed, but it boosted their confidence as a duo.
- Plan: after the final week, a roughly 2-week break (after about 3 months non-stop), then decide whether to keep doing Tidbit and whether to keep working together.
- Kamil (engineer) and Hasan (content) were on a planned 30-day speed-run and become free agents afterward.
