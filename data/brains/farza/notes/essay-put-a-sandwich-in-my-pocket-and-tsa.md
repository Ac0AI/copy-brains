## Why Farza avoids the fundraising topic but doesn't ignore it

- At buildspace, Farza usually stays away from raising money or finding investors. His focus is showing people how to make stuff.
- He sees alumni raise $50K-$500K nearly every week. His view: "If you ship, tell the world, repeat... capital finds its way to you." He adds that it isn't guaranteed.
- Chasing investors early can be "a terrible sinkhole for focus". Builders go from making cool products to "methodically begging others for money".
- He also thinks fully ignoring the money world is a mistake. The goal is to set yourself up to "get lucky" and possibly pursue investors later.

## What the builders who raise money have in common

- The alumni who raise money "magically" (a $25K grant, a small angel check) are usually very good at "telling the world" consistently over a long horizon, roughly 12-24 months.
- A one-off viral Twitter demo or a single Reddit blowup doesn't count. Those are hard to control and disappear fast.
- The real asset is trust built with an audience over a long time.

## The weekly newsletter as the most underrated way to tell your story

- Farza says writing a newsletter each week with your progress is the most underrated way to tell your story, and he can't believe every builder doesn't do it.
- Content can be anything: things you tried that week, how you're thinking about your work, how you're feeling.
- He wrote his every week for nearly 3 years (2017-2020), almost 150 issues. They covered his emotions and how he analyzed different markets to decide what to work on, giving readers a view into his head every week.
- He assumed no one cared and wrote it mainly so his Mom could understand what he was doing.

## What the newsletter produced

- The list had only 125 people after 3 years.
- Those readers deeply understood him and his journey. When he needed $25,000 to start buildspace, he got it from one person on that newsletter.
- Lesson: you don't need a big audience, you need a small one that follows your story over time.

## Why owned formats beat tweets

- Farza sees builders tweet something, watch it not blow up, and move on. His objection is that Twitter is "extremely ephemeral" and your story fades quickly.
- A newsletter or blog shows people your long-term history and lets you communicate more thoroughly.
- Alternatives work too. Some builders send him a monthly YouTube vlog on what they did that month. His advice: "Do whatever, just please tell your story."

## Mindset: your story matters

- "Your story matters. It's just your job to package it up nicely for others to also be a part of it."
- You never know who may read it.
- If you think you have nothing to say, he says you're lying, because you're living the story right now. It just takes practice to tell it.
