## Showing up daily with two rules, shipping to one person

- Farza started from zero in April 2025 and describes his first 21 days as "good, but fucking difficult."
- He has no rules about what to build. Some days he picks a new thing (a VR game), some days he continues a prior project.
- He is "insanely, stupidly disciplined" about exactly two things:
  - Show up every day.
  - Make something and show at least one person.
- Most days he starts at a blank page. He says he's not "that guy that has like 1000 ideas on an Apple Note."
- Day 1 was full of anxiety. For the first 5 hours he thought everything he did was stupid. Around hour 8 he started a simple text editor for himself, built the first version in 4 hours, and texted a rough Xcode build ZIP to a friend.
- He says he trusts his process, and that the two rules force output even when motivation and ideas are absent.

## Shipping small and embarrassing things anyway (Freewrite text editor)

- By the end of week 1 he thought the editor was "kinda cool" but was embarrassed that it was simple, small, and niche.
- He decided to ignore what people might say and ship it. He asked a collaborator (Josh) to make a launch video in two hours.
- He chose a video not to go viral or look cool; it had no rationale: "I just did it because it seemed fun." About 95% of the reason was that he likes working with Josh.
- Expectation: he'd have been happy with 10 downloads.
- Result: 1.8M views on X, thousands of downloads, and a community of "die-hard Freewrite fans" whose feedback he found heart-warming.
- Lesson he draws: low expectations plus fun and shipping beats waiting until something feels impressive.

## Making a YouTube video end to end in one day

- He messaged a collaborator (Aiden) about a day before and made the whole video in one day: script, shooting, editing, thumbnail.
- Expectations were zero. He'd have been happy with 100 views; the goal was "to get off 0 on YT."
- Results: almost half a million views across platforms (X, LinkedIn, YouTube), with 25,000 views on YouTube. About 75% of YouTube views came from the algorithm.
- He pushes back on the idea that he has a master plan for a "personal brand." He made it because it seemed fun and he wanted to work with Aiden again.
- Plan going forward: try to make 1 video per month for YouTube.

## Turning user learnings into a new product (voice agent)

- In week 3 he started a voice agent to help him talk through his day. It came from watching Freewrite users: people loved the AI feature he added last minute.
- His reasoning: "what if that was one entire product." He thought it was a stupid idea, but had no other ideas that Tuesday and had to ship something.
- Build, in about 3 hours:
  - Learned iOS/Expo.
  - Made the initial UI.
  - Added real-time voice with ElevenLabs and conversation with Gemini Flash.
  - Demoed a grandpa giving advice.
  - He admits he "literally ripped off" an ElevenLabs Expo React Native cookbook blog post and changed the background.
- He walked around the office showing the rough demo to about 20 people and was surprised by how some people spoke to it.
- For the next 2 days he improved it a little each day and showed 10-20 people each update. He now has "a ton" of ideas for something he first thought was stupid.
- Takeaway: "Just gotta ship. Just gotta show people."

## Put users in one group chat and mine it for ideas

- Tactic: "adding your users to a single GC is always a good idea."
- He learns a lot from watching how people talk about the product. The Freewrite group chat had grown to 1k people and gives him "an insane amount of new ideas."
- The voice agent was directly inspired by learnings from this user base.

## How ideas actually get generated: show people, react to their reactions

- His best ideas come from showing someone something, hearing their thoughts, and having that trigger a new idea. He describes the feeling of "you're on to something."
- His main goal for the first 3 weeks was to flex that muscle: build something small, show it, iterate on the reaction.
- His brain keeps building on prior days' work rather than starting from a blank slate each day (example: the voice agent came out of Freewrite user learnings). If he has to start blank each day, he'll keep doing it.
- He keeps things "loose as hell" with no rules, backed by a good level of base focus.

## Build with other people, even briefly

- Learning one: build with others even if only for 30 minutes and even if he barely knows what he's building.
- Examples of small collaborations: Josh and Aiden on videos, Dante on a YouTube thumbnail, Stavan on tweet copy, Jack on voice agent implementation.
- Why: these gave him energy, made him feel less alone, and made his ideas better.
- He notes you can work with friends in small ways and have fun; "it doesn't have to be all-in or nothing."

## Building a mental game for starting over

- Learning two: spending the previous 8 months deliberately building a new "mental" was worth it. He had told no one about this.
- Components: updated internal rules, new internal motivations, and new strategies for dealing with his mind. His goal was that his mental would never fail him again.
- He is very emotional (smiles, laughs, cries, and gets anxious easily) and wants to keep feeling all emotions because they make him feel alive and lead to his best work: "It's hard to be creative without being deeply in touch with your emotions."
- The goal was that emotions no longer change his state of mind while he works.
- The first 3 weeks were the test. Result: a majority of days were happy; emotions rarely controlled him; on down days he knew how to rebound. He called it a real personal breakthrough and said the challenge is maintaining it as things get bigger.
