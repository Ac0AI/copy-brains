## Proposing a business model: sell "teach people to build with your tool" to AI companies

Farza spent the week working out how Make Something (his AI-education project) could become a business. He sent proposals to two companies:
- A big AI tooling company, which agreed to pay $50,000 a month after about one call. The final contract is still pending.
- One of the big AI labs, which he is still talking to about the same $50,000 a month.
- Pitch: "I will use your technology. I will show people your technology. I will teach them your tools." The aim is to get people building real things with those tools.
- If both close, Make Something would be at $100,000 a month, about $1M a year. He notes it had existed for under a month.

## Why AI companies will pay for it: tools are known, usage isn't

- Farza argues the core problem AI companies face is that everyone knows the tools exist but few people build real things with them.
- His analogy is the computer. Most people were never taught to use one, but using it powerfully takes programming, marketing or design education.
- He argues the people building with AI are mostly those who were already building before. Few new people are coming in.
- IG ads and influencer marketing can't fix this. A million sign-ups on an AI builder product doesn't mean people will build real businesses or retain long term.
- His positioning is to help people build their own ideas and businesses with AI over the long term.

## Sponsorship and authenticity as the constraint

- Farza hasn't said yes to the contract. He doesn't want to be a shill for these companies.
- He doesn't want to do a stream or piece of content "just cuz someone's paying me". He wants to talk about tools he actually uses in his workflows.
- He prides himself on authentic material. He would "rather just not do any of this at all" than plug a tool in front of his landing page because of payment.
- He contrasts himself with MrBeast plugging NordVPN. MrBeast can do that at a scale of billions. Farza's audience (people who make things, education) is at the scale of millions.

## Step-wise ceilings instead of big-market thinking

- Farza doubts Make Something can make tens or hundreds of millions a year. He doesn't focus on that.
- He thinks in steps: can it make $10, $10,000, $100,000, a million? Each step is the next goal.
- He argues ideas rarely have a hard ceiling: "once you reach the ceiling there is always a way where you can break past that ceiling." Going from $1M to $5M a year would take more pushing and fresh ideas.
- He still treats Make Something as a side project and not yet a company. It might become one if the deals close.

## Productizing teaching: the AI screen-watching tutor

- Farza and Julian, a co-founder trial partner, are exploring how to put the teaching experience into software.
- The product is an AI that watches your screen and guides you through building your first app with Codex. It sits as a Mac sidebar, talks to you and unsticks you.
- It doesn't write the code. It guides step by step: go to codex.com, download it, write your first prompt, get your app off localhost.
- The idea is that the best way to learn is a friend who is an expert sitting next to you.
- Launched at the previous Saturday stream, Mac only, to a subset of users. About 40 downloaded it and about 10 built their first app. It was buggy and is still very early.
- He wants to keep a human element in the content, but doesn't want to teach "how to build your first app with Codex" live every weekend.

## Live streams, content and growth numbers

- Make Something runs one live stream every Saturday. Farza says the weekly deadline has been good for him: "I work really well with deadlines."
- Latest stream: about 1,000 signed up, about 400 showed up, about 200 built an app.
- Cumulative after about 3 weeks: more than 10,000 people reached, about 7,000 emails in the database, about 800 people shipped their first app, and a Discord of almost 1,000 people where they co-work daily.
- He hired a filmmaker friend in India to make Instagram videos. Farza says he loves writing content (scripts, hooks, the emotions) more than being on camera.
- He wanted more Saturday attendance and did only a little extra promotion.

## Paying users instead of influencers

- He offered users who shipped their first app $40 to make a reel about the experience, instead of paying random influencers.
- Some made good reels, and he plans to keep experimenting.
- He doesn't "necessarily believe in UGC". The principle is to empower people who enjoy your thing to talk about it, and he dislikes giving it an official name.

## Raising money and the Buildspace déjà vu

- Farza says he could raise roughly $500K to $4-5M now and keep control. He had a good, pressure-free experience with investors on his last company.
- He values that the market is open now, unlike 2021-2023, which he calls horrible.
- He doesn't want to raise "if I don't know what I'm working on".
- He feels like he is rebuilding Buildspace, a 5-year project. He likes that Make Something is a new brand with no baggage, so he can move fast and try things without pressure.
- He says there are no shortcuts: "how many cycles you put in" determines how much you learn and how fast you move. Experience doesn't let you recognize a great idea faster.
- He wants to work on what he loves and make it a good business, and says working with the right people matters as much as the project.
