## Speed-running money vs. building the dream: Farza's "both" position

- Farza says founder friends are debating whether to build "something generational" or just make money by "speed running to $1M ARR on some iOS AI app."
- He sees founders who have grinded for years on massive problems questioning their core philosophies. The choice they frame: "Do we build a money printer or do we continue to focus on building the dream."
- Part of the pull comes from seeing people on YouTube and Twitter hit $1M or $5M in under a year, which makes founders ask "Am I doing something wrong??"
- Another subset dismisses those apps as "just a wrapper grown by mindless UGC on Instagram," useless or a distraction. Farza pushes back on that dismissal.
- His conclusion: "Money, or dreams? Both. It must be both."

## The 2025 AI app playbook mirrors 2010 dropshipping

- Farza dropshipped in 2010, arbitraging random niches: wrap a product in cleaner messaging, run ads, profit.
- He says 99% of the AI apps launching in 2025 follow the same playbook: find a niche, wrap the latest model (built via Cursor) with cleaner messaging, and pay TikTok college kids instead of buying ads.
- The loop of find niche, build software, distribute gets easier every month, so more people do it.
- The result: in 2025 he sees the same thing as in 2010 at age 14, people aged 14-21 "just printing on the internet." He calls it "pretty awesome."
- He does not treat the playbook as trivial. Anyone can wrap a model, just as anyone could dropship, but hitting $100K a month on anything is "difficult as fuck." The winners are "truly obsessed," with relentless execution and genuine love for the game.

## Why most wrapper businesses die

- Farza's own dropshipping business is gone, along with most of the early-2010s dropshipping businesses.
- His team was full-time arbitraging a short-term opportunity until little was left to arbitrage. Facebook ad acquisition costs rose, CAC became "ridiculous," consumers realized everyone was wrapping Alibaba, and sales dipped.
- Almost nobody was building a true product that became core to people's lives. The dropshipping businesses that survived put effort into their own formula, product or secret sauce.
- He expects something similar to happen to many of today's explosive AI apps.

## Farza's own stance: find the large opportunity, but learn from the money printers

- For himself, he wants to find the large opportunity and have the patience to build the dream. He does not want to lower his long-term ambition just to hit a short-term money number.
- He is also "inspired as fuck" by 16-year-olds hitting $1M ARR on iOS apps they make in 30 days, and says he needs to learn everything he can from them.
- His view: "a great short-term money printer has a high chance of turning into a generational product with the right vision. I'd rather have an engine, than not."

## The Nike example: a money printer can fund the vision

- Nike spent its first 10 years as a "wrapper" around Onitsuka Tigers from Japan, dropshipping their shoes.
- Phil Knight kept growing the business, built a money printer, and kept growing the vision.
- One day Nike started making its own shoes. "50 years later we all wear Nikes."
- Farza uses this as proof that wrapping and reselling early can be the engine that eventually funds a real product.

## Ego and ambition as failure modes

- "Ambition can be a poison."
- Ego can stop founders from doing things that work in the short term because they believe their way of building products is "better," less cringe, or better for humanity.
- His position: "There is no right/wrong."
- If you are purely obsessed with speed-running money, that is great, but he suggests putting some "braincells" into the long term.
- If you are purely obsessed with being the next Apple and building a company that lasts decades but you are at $0 or close to it, that is great, but "perhaps go and make some money."

## Advice for founders grinding for years without a breakout

- "Keep your eyes and ears open 24/7. Constantly learn how this game is changing."
- When you see someone winning online, don't think "Damn what am I doing wrong." Think "What can I learn from this person and their work."
- "Just adjust your game plan vs crumbling."
- He would hate a world where people dream smaller or lower their ambitions just for a monetary goal, and equally hate one where people ignore reality and stay stuck in their dreams forever.
