## Farza rejoins Y Combinator at 30 with no fixed idea

- In April 2026, Farza joined Y Combinator for a second time. His first batch was in 2020, when he was 23. The second batch started the week he wrote the post.
- He says he doesn't yet have a clear direction: "Not fully sure what I'm working on yet."
- He is a little scared to start a company again but mostly excited. After his last company, he "never thought I'd start anything again in my life" because it was so tough.
- He says he'll "never be 'ready'", so he's going for it.

## Starting a company at 23 versus 30

- At 23 (YC in 2020), he had naivety. At 30 he knows how difficult building a company is.
- He describes himself as having "the naivety of a 19 year-old, but, with the mental of a guy who's been through a lot and learned a lot."
- He notes most people in YC are aged 19-24, and he is bullish on his mix of naivety and experience.
- He credits the last two years, which he calls "very healing", and a lot of work on himself for making a second attempt possible.

## Why he chose building over the safe path

- He acknowledges the safer path: most founders end up as VCs or in high-tier jobs at big companies. He says he doesn't blame them, and that this path would be easier and more secure, especially as he starts thinking about family.
- He has been offered many great jobs. Each time he considers one, a voice in his head says to give his visions a shot.
- He has no specific "this is the idea I'm excited about". It's more a feeling of "I should explore my ideas... I would regret it if I didn't."
- He thinks the timing is unusually good: "Especially in 2026, at the epicenter of one of the greatest inventions of my lifetime." He also says "There's never been a better time to work on the ideas in my head."
- His stance is to accept the risk: "Maybe I flop, maybe I don't, only one way to find out." He closes with "We must be brave!"

## Operating approach during the batch

- He plans to spend little time documenting and to focus mostly on building.
- He expects to launch a lot of random stuff over the following weeks.
- He will post one weekly update on YouTube, which has been more fun for him lately than writing on Substack. The first video covers how the YC opportunity happened, why he's doing it again, his imposter syndrome and how he thinks about it.

## Buildspace's legacy inside the batch

- Many founders in the batch told him they credit being at YC to buildspace, his earlier company.
- One example: a founder showed him a selfie of the two of them at a buildspace event when the founder was 15. That founder is now 18 and in the same batch.
- Some people who saw him in person assumed he was joining as a YC partner. They were surprised he was joining as a founder "back in the dirt alongside them."
- He says he often struggles to stop and appreciate the value of past work because he is so focused on the future, so this was a welcome moment.
