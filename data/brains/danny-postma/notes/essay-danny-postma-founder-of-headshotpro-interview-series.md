## From Landingfolio to Headlime: building small products first

- Danny started Landingfolio in 2015 at age 21, while working as a freelance Conversion Optimization Specialist. He struggled to produce effective landing page designs for clients, so he built a gallery of the best landing page designs on the internet as a side project.
- He did not know how to program at the time and built it on WordPress, which he found "much easier than I expected."
- The site still exists and gets thousands of visitors, but it is hard to monetize, so he moved on to other projects.
- Headlime started as a non-AI tool. While working on Landingfolio he wrote an interactive book of 200 headlines where users could input variables to customize the headline to their use case. He realised this would work better as software, and turned it into a simple copy spinner that did the replacing for you.

## Getting into AI early with GPT-3

- A friend introduced Danny to GPT-3 while he was working on Headlime. He reached out directly to Greg Brockman, then CTO of OpenAI, to request access, and got in as part of the first batch of users.
- He experimented with letting GPT-3 write the templates for Headlime and was "astounded by its ability to generate copy". That turned Headlime into an AI copywriting tool.
- He credits some luck: he was already building a copywriting tool when a copywriting AI arrived. He was one of the first people to implement GPT-3 in a production environment, alongside Copy.AI and a few other tools.
- The pattern: he was already close to a problem space, so a new technology slotted straight into an existing product.

## Pivoting fast: from Stock AI to ProfilePicture.AI to HeadshotPro

- Stable Diffusion launched in September 2022. Danny first built "Stock AI", a stock photo site, but quality fell short and he worried about legal action from image-rights owners like Getty Images, so he pivoted.
- He saw Dreambooth launching an AI service that could transform faces and realised he was building something similar. He launched his product within 30 hours, beating a competitor to market.
- ProfilePicture.AI "exploded on Twitter" and generated significant sales in its first week. He calls it a highly shareable product, and he also ranked well on Google for profile picture searches.
- When excitement faded and sales dropped, he pivoted again. He teamed up with David, a developer friend, and built a Python model that could manipulate photos into different poses, which pointed him at AI headshots.
- He launched Deep Agency and HeadshotPro at the same time. Deep Agency got a lot of press but no sales. HeadshotPro was "an instant hit", so he focused on it.
- His takeaway: "staying flexible and reacting to what the market is telling you." Press attention is not demand; sales are.

## Differentiating HeadshotPro: quality and a single focus

- **Photo quality is the primary differentiator.** Danny says competitors mostly rely on open-source models (Stable Diffusion with DreamBooth), which "simply aren't good enough for realistic photo generations."
- HeadshotPro runs a large backend pipeline, deploying dozens of extra open-source and custom models to, in his words, "10x the output quality". The team had worked on its own custom models for a full year as of early 2024.
- Models in the pipeline include LLaVa, used to filter bad user uploads and remove NSFW generations, and Codeformer, which removes AI artifacts from faces and makes them more realistic.
- His quality bet: if the model improves 1% each day, headshots can become indistinguishable from real photos within a year, widening the gap over less serious competitors.
- **Focus exclusively on professional headshots.** Many competitors expand horizontally into dating photos, cute avatars and a full photo studio. Danny sees those detours as a distraction and says his sole focus is the highest quality professional AI headshots.

## Product improvements on the roadmap

- Daily iteration on headshot quality is the main lever.
- Clothing generation: he expected "virtual try on" services to draw more interest in 2024 and wanted to use that tech for corporate customers, who often ask for matching suits across employees' headshots.
- User experience: the product requires 15-17 input photos, which he says has been "the magic number" for maximizing quality. The team is exploring ways to make upload easier without losing quality.

## Moving from solopreneur to building a team

- Danny's early fear of hiring pushed him to automate as much as he could himself, especially in pre-AI times.
- As HeadshotPro grew he saw three options: sell the business, stay a solopreneur, or build a team. He chose the team, despite it being scary, because he wanted to scale.
- He describes it as an identity shift: he had to admit he didn't have all the answers, and the goal changed from running a business to leading people to build something together.
- Tactics he used:
  - Told candidates from the first interview that he was inexperienced at managing people and that there might be bumps.
  - Started with contractors and part-time people who already had other clients, as a safety net while he learned team management.
- Looking back, he says he "should have started a team way earlier." His small team does things he "could have never ever done myself," and sharing wins makes the work less lonely and more fun.

## Selling off side projects to focus

- Danny has built many small AI products: Tattoos AI (tattoo generator), Alice (an AI social media experiment), Profile Picture AI, Deep Agency (virtual AI-generated models) and Meme Morph.
- Since HeadshotPro took off, these got little attention. He was in the process of selling most of them to focus fully on HeadshotPro.
