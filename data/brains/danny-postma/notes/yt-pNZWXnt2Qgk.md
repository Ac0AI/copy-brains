## AI plus SEO keyword generator sites

- Danny's core tactic: pair AI content generation with low-competition search keywords. He logs into Ahrefs, types in a seed keyword and looks for high search intent with very low keyword difficulty. Ahrefs scores difficulty 0-100; near 0 means little competition and you can rank quickly.
- The old bottleneck was producing the content. He cites his own inspiration gallery, Landingfolio, where ranking was quick but content creation took a lot of time. AI removes that bottleneck: "you can just rapid fire and let things generate."
- Worked example, AI tattoos: searching "tattoos" in Ahrefs returns thousands of long-tail keywords (butterfly tattoo, dragon tattoo, bird tattoo). Generate images for each with Stable Diffusion, Midjourney or whatever is current, and fill directory pages (for example a page of butterfly tattoos).
- The loop: build a tool users pay to use, then show their generated output on the site as user-generated content. This feeds the directories organically. He says you can generate "hundreds of thousands of pieces of content" in a month, Google rewards it, and traffic follows.
- His result: the AI tattoo site reached about $10,000 MRR before he sold it to focus on another startup.
- He says it works for anything AI-related combined with low-difficulty content.
- Possible upsell: a lower-ticket product (tool access) plus a higher-ticket done-for-you service. Entrepreneurs who want to build these sites are high-value customers who need tools and services.

## Timing and the window of opportunity

- Danny compares this to the early Apple App Store, when you could make money with a flashlight, calculator or fart app: "the golden age of AI and SEO combined."
- He expects it to stop working within about two years, because the niches will be found and filled by highly automated players.
- With SEO, he believes the first to market, first to build backlinks and first to claim the domains mostly stay on top.
- His own experience at Headshot Pro: when it started there were three or four competitors, then AI boilerplates led to roughly 1,000. He says Headshot Pro got lucky to be in the top three, and "the top three will always win."
- His advice: you can still compete in a busy market, but you must be fast. In AI tools, coming in a year later is probably too late. Find niches in Ahrefs with keyword difficulty of 0-5 before they are taken.

## ComfyUI template marketplace

- ComfyUI is a drag-and-drop node interface for building visual AI pipelines (inputs, data processing, outputs). Previously anything deeper than calling ChatGPT meant writing Python.
- Danny is rebuilding his own Python workflows in ComfyUI because it is easier, and it can now run in the cloud (he mentions Replicate).
- The idea: YouTubers already publish ComfyUI workflow tutorials. Instead, build a marketplace where people sell, rent out or charge fees for exported workflow templates. He expects ComfyUI adoption to boom within months, and compares it to Indie Hackers who sell code boilerplates.
- He says this is the idea he would build himself if he weren't busy, and he shared it publicly so it exists.
- He is open to partnering: he told listeners with the skills to DM him on Twitter.

## Fiverr/Upwork 2.0: hire AI workers instead of freelancers

- Concept: a clone of Fiverr or Upwork where you hire AI workflows (LLM-based bots) that feel like hiring a person. Danny doesn't know of anyone who has built this.
- Example: a translation job. Instead of finding a freelancer to translate an Excel sheet, the customer drops in the file and gets it back translated a day later, fully automated by AI.
- Why it works: normal people are afraid of AI or can't build APIs, so packaging it as a job marketplace is easier to sell. Customers don't want to babysit or fact-check; they dump the task and get the output.
- Supply side: let a community build workflows (possibly on ComfyUI) and offer them as services. The platform takes about a 5% fee; a builder might charge, for example, one cent per row.
- Getting customers: list the service on Upwork itself, and look at the most requested jobs on Upwork to decide what to automate. He mentions a known workaround for Upwork's anti-scraper blacklist. He compares seeding to Airbnb's fake Craigslist posts for its hosts.
- Operations: you may need real humans for checking output, such as an account manager.
- Exit angle: he says Upwork or Fiverr would probably acquire a working version. He notes Upwork's stock and Fiverr's stock are down heavily (Fiverr from about $320 to about $25 in his words) because of AI fears over low-level repetitive tasks.

## Humanize AI: replace a human service with AI, keep the familiar form

- Principle: to get products adopted, make AI feel like the real-world experience people already know, not "Tech based." He compares it to the early iPhone, where notes looked like notes.
- Headshot Pro example: instead of scheduling a photo shoot, driving there and paying around $300, customers take 15 selfies and two hours later get photos. "The AI is literally the photographer." The experience matches a photo shoot even though no human is involved.
- Branding: he would sell it as the old service, don't foreground AI, and make it about 10x cheaper.
- Method for finding ideas: call practitioners such as your dentist, auto mechanic or barber and ask what is repetitive and hated. Danny says there is little competition because these niches are boring and programmers don't know them, so whoever learns the market and can do marketing wins it.
- Case study he cites: medical scribes. A person listens in and takes notes; Whisper plus an LLM can transcribe and summarize. He says that job is likely automated within five years. A doctor's tool called Autoscribe (Mew2 Health, Toronto) had almost no competing search results.
- Spin-offs: nurses, dentists, and veterinarians. A friend built AI note-taking for veterinarians with no competition; niche users prefer a tool built for them over a broad consumer app.
- Timing: he says SaaS is already eating the world and this niche approach will be saturated in about five to ten years, so move fast.

## AI-run marketing agency with productized services

- Danny sold his first startup to Jasper (an AI copywriting generator).
- Idea: a marketing agency run almost entirely by AI. He owns the domain headline99, intended as a $99 landing page headline service.
- Flow: the customer fills in a questionnaire about their company; several LLMs summarize it and produce 5-10 headline ideas; a human in the loop picks the final three and you deliver the best-working headline.
- Reasoning: everyone uses software and SaaS now, but users don't know the craft, so you productize the extra layer on top. He sees broad opportunity in unbundling the marketing agency into autonomous SaaS-like services, potentially scaling with very few people.
- He says he likes the domain so much he is tempted to keep working on it, and notes projects with "head" in the name (Headshot Pro, headline99) have been successful.
