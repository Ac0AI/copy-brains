## The shift from pages to agents

Guillermo frames AI as "perhaps the most important transformation we'll see in our careers": the move "from pages to agents," or "from pixels to agents," or "from foreground to the background."

- Vercel started in 2015 to give everyone the internal infrastructure that Amazon, Google and Facebook used to build and ship fast. He cites Next.js at 13 million downloads per week and a platform serving over a trillion requests per month for over 10 million customers.
- He says the same wall-hitting situation is now repeating with AI, and that "even the giants haven't figured it out yet."
- His stance is that developer jobs are not going away, but surviving requires changing how developers think and create.

## The cloud is being rebuilt for agents

Guillermo argues the infrastructure built for pages does not fit agents.

- The old cloud: static assets on a CDN, optimized for page load time. Google rewarded fast pages with higher rankings, which created a flywheel of more business, conversion and organic traffic.
- The new need: a "CDN for tokens."
- Compute shifts from fast one-shot request/response to long periods of "thinking time" and background multi-step workflows.
- Search rankings give way to embedding intelligence everywhere, with agents "meeting customers where they are."

## Interfaces become generative, and the move from code-first to code-last

- Before, interfaces were deterministic: you designed a dashboard and "every pixel was specified." Now they are generative and adaptive, created on demand, with context, personalized to the user.
- Example: Snowflake announced an integration with v0, Vercel's generative UI agent, which creates data visualizations on the fly.
- His position: "Front ends aren't disappearing. We're just generating them on demand," and user experience matters more than ever. What changes is how it is created.
- He describes the decades-long trend away from the code itself and toward the end goal (great products and experiences) as a move from a code-first to a code-last world.

## Everyone can build, so the barrier is clarity of intent

- Software used to be limited to people who learned syntax, command lines, data structures, IDEs and complex infrastructure.
- Now "the barrier isn't technical knowledge. It's clarity of intent." Prompting and understanding how AI works is "the new literacy."
- He used to see JavaScript as a great democratizer: framework-defined infrastructure let millions of JavaScript developers become cloud engineers by pushing code and getting globally optimized infrastructure. AI is a superset, "because we can all yap. We can all talk. We can all prompt."
- Distribution tactic: put agents inside Slack, Zoom, Teams and Snowflake and treat platforms as primary prompting surfaces. This reduces friction and could take the field "from tens of millions of developers to a billion developers."
- Anecdote: a company founded in the cloud's early days has one rule, "they don't do code." Internal communication is just conversation about what to build, with no GitHub or infrastructure talk.

## Meta skills for developers: don't get attached to individual skills

- "Don't get too attached to your individual skills." Doing math in your head is a great asset, but a machine now does it better. He sees coding as one of those individual skills.
- The meta skills that matter are the ones machines can't easily replicate: what problems are worth solving, what experiences could be better, faster or more delightful, and what is about to become possible.
- Developers should uplevel by becoming more full stack (front end, back end, middleware, data). Data engineers can build interfaces; front-end developers can orchestrate complex back ends.
- The opportunity is to become an orchestrator of a variety of agents, without going as deep into each line of code.

## Shipping is the meta skill

- "Shipping is a distinct skill from coding. Shipping is the meta skill."
- Shipping means building end to end: messaging, designing, coding, QAing, teaching, marketing, selling, pivoting, iterating. Coding used to dominate because coding ability was scarce.
- "Shipping is about storytelling," including in data, and it starts with communicating your intent clearly.
- Shipping also requires taste: experience, craft, attention to detail. He treats taste as a developable skill, not an innate gift. His exercise: quantify how much time you spend watching people use your products, or other products.
- "In a world where AI can generate infinite variations, the ability to discern quality will be a gold standard skill."

## Move fast and raise quality

- His counterposition: everyone talks about AI for productivity, but AI must also serve quality.
- He cites former Snowflake CEO Frank Slootman's line that when velocity increases, excuses get made about quality ("We can't possibly move this fast and maintain quality"). Guillermo's response: "Move fast and raise quality."
- Advice to customers asking what to build: care about both productivity and quality. "Productivity without quality is just noise. And quality without productivity means you're probably getting left behind."
- Just because code generates faster does not mean standards should drop: "we should raise them."

## Which agents to ship, and keeping the AI cloud open

Agents Vercel has recently shipped:
- Support agents that resolve common customer questions instantly.
- Abuse agents that analyze threats and recommend actions.
- Content agents that turn conversations into structured drafts.
- A data analyst agent (announced with Snowflake) that turns natural language into SQL and visualizations.

How agents raise quality:
- They concentrate judgment, reserving human time for decisions that shape the product and business.
- The bar rises over time because the system learns from what the team accepts, edits or rejects.

Openness:
- He believes every organization and individual should be able to create and deploy their own agents, like owning a domain name and deploying to it.
- He criticizes the first AI wave as dominated by closed models, proprietary SDKs and centralized interfaces, and calls for an open AI cloud with access to models, platforms and infrastructure.
- Closing thesis: the future of developer experience "isn't about coding less necessarily. It's about shipping more": more ambitious products, more innovative solutions, more value for users.
