## Why Alex says to offer a lead magnet instead of "book a call" or "submit for a quote"

- Most sites send traffic straight to "buy now" or "submit for a quote." Most visitors aren't ready, so they leave and never return.
- A "book a call" or quote CTA only works with a warm, informed audience that already knows and trusts you. Its only job is lead capture, so Alex asks what else would capture more leads while still signaling buying interest.
- His fix is a mini offer: a complete solution to a narrow problem, free or low cost. Solving it reveals the next problem, which the core offer solves.
- People who take a free or cheap offer are more likely to buy a related higher-priced offer later. "When someone pays with time now, they're more likely to pay with money later."
- Illustration: of 100 people, "buy my thing" might get 1 buyer. "Let me help you first, then ask" might get 3 to 5 times as many.
- This needs no extra traffic or ad spend. It raises conversion on existing traffic, so it "just drops to your bottom line."
- He says he has used it in every business he has invested in, and that it has generated many millions of dollars across the portfolio. He covers it on page 31 of the $100M Leads book.

## Alex's 2016 breakthrough: swapping a failed webinar for a free case study

- In April 2016 he paid $25,000 to be in a group where everyone told him to run a webinar. His webinar didn't work, because he lacked the skill to run one.
- He then saw an ad for a "free case study" on turning $1 into $120,000 in a weekend. The opt-in was a screen recording of how the guy did it.
- He replaced his webinar with a video headlined "Free case study, how we added 213 members to a gym and $112,000 in San Diego."
- The next morning Leila told him her calendar was full.
- Lesson: the more advanced the audience, the more likely they see a webinar as a sales pitch. "Consume it on your own time, let me show you what I did" got more opt-ins from business owners.
- Webinars work, but he lacked that skill. A screen-record walkthrough of a campaign and its results was easy to make.
- Once someone opts in, he called them to ask how they liked it, then offered to do it at their location with a risk-free offer: no pay if he doesn't perform. That was far easier than making a webinar close.
- If a lead magnet fails, that magnet failed, not lead magnets in general. This is like a bad ad headline not meaning ads don't work.
- Bad lead magnets don't work, and beginners don't realize theirs is bad.

## Why lead magnets work and how to handle objections

- Costco samples work mainly because people taste something good and want more, not just from reciprocity.
- Three types of lead magnet, which can be combined:
  - A sample or trial.
  - One step in a multi-step process.
  - An assessment that reveals a problem.
- "Freebie seekers and tire kickers": don't give it to everyone. Add a qualifying dropdown, send qualified people to the good thing and the rest to something else.
- "I don't want to give away too much value": don't solve the problem your core product solves. Solve a problem that leads to it.
- Sell at the point of greatest deprivation.
  - Restaurant analogy: someone who just ate an entree won't want another, but may want dessert.
  - Water analogy: right after you give water, they aren't thirsty, so that moment says nothing about whether the water was good.
  - The free "entree" should be free or at cost, and the "dessert" should cost significantly more.
- Packaging matters more than contents. Changing the lead magnet's headline can change demand 2x, 3x or 10x, provided the contents clearly solve a problem.

## The three lead magnet types in detail

**1. Reveal a problem**
- Alex loves this type. It creates deprivation by showing a problem and how bad it is.
- Bonus: also show what solved looks like and the delta.
- B2B example: a free website speed test. His example claim is that each second of load time costs 3% of conversion. Going from a 9-second to a 3-second load time is an 18% increase. Against the price of a website, that gain is small, so he'd ask "How soon do you want me to start?"
- It works best for problems that worsen while waiting: posture analysis, termite inspections, financial audits (back taxes, cash flow). Worsening problems build urgency.
- Ad example: one of his highest-converting ads for Acquisition.com's software company was "four reasons why you'll never have a million-dollar agency," aimed at small lead-gen agencies. Many readers thought "all four of these reasons I'm also suffering from."
- Be very specific on the negatives and vaguer on the positives. If you nail someone's problems, they assume you can fix them. This also makes marketing more compliant and expectations more realistic.

**2. Free trial**
- This is the Costco sampler or the fitting room. It has the lowest barrier.
- Give a full loop of value, then remove it so the person feels deprived and converts.
- Limit the number of uses, the quantity, the time, or a combination (for example "x-day trial" or "this many hits").
- Gym Launch example: customers bought the monetization system, and Alex gave them agency services (leads) free for four months. Afterward they wanted leads to continue, and could now pay for them. It was a built-in back-end upsell.

**3. One step of many**
- Best for complex products and services.
- Examples: the first coat of several on a garage, a first laser hair removal session (six to eight are needed), or the first two videos of a course.
- Ashley (a personal stylist) could give a color analysis first. It leads to questions about tops, bottoms and formal vs informal wear.

## Four ways to deliver a lead magnet

**1. Software or tools**
- Spreadsheets that calculate, assessment tools, templates or software.
- Example: Neil Patel's tool where you enter a URL and it assesses the site while collecting your information.
- A tool can reveal a problem, offer a trial, or be one step of many.

**2. Information**
- Mini courses, guides, expert interviews, static templates.
- Costs nothing, scales infinitely, provides value and creates deprivation, with no operational drag.
- Example: his $100 million scaling roadmap, a tool plus information built from 200+ hours of studying businesses. It tells you what scaling stage you're at and how to solve your problems. Opt-ins can book a one-on-one call and may be invited to the headquarters.

**3. Services**
- "People sleep on this so hard." Do free work to create goodwill, but only for qualified prospects: budget, authority, a clear need, and ready to act now.
- Math example: an hour of labor costs $25 hard cost, but the work is worth $250 or more. If 1 in 4 recipients buys, customer acquisition cost is $100. That is worth it if a customer is worth much more.
- Examples: free audits with some implementation, same-day service delivery, any done-for-you component.
- First business: he trained people free for a year in the "free training project" to collect testimonials. After a year he said he had too much demand and asked whether they wanted to pay. Almost all said yes.

**4. Physical products**
- His books are lead magnets, and they lead to calls and to investing in or partnering with businesses. This is a very long game.
- Example: give a CEO hat or shirt only to people who prove they're CEOs. The item costs $3 to $4. At 1 in 20 converting, that is about $100 per customer for a qualified CEO.
- Ask what your ideal customer would want, and give that.

## How to name a lead magnet and test the name

- Naming is hugely underrated. The name drives engagement more than anything else.
- Advertise the result, not the vehicle.
  - His first gym ran a "Big Booty Boot Camp." The content was a six-week deadlift, squat and hip thrust program.
  - A "six-week deadlift and squat seminar" didn't convert his mostly female audience.
  - Different names attract different people, for example "tight and toned glutes" or "Bubble Butt Boot Camp."
- How he tests names:
  - Define the avatar clearly.
  - Run small ad tests comparing headlines.
  - With no budget, poll your audience via Stories, texts, or "comment A or B" posts. Only qualified people should answer.
- He split-tested six headlines for $100M Leads, plus the cover image and subheadlines, and shows the results in the book. If it takes two years to write a book, he can spend two days testing the headline.
- Short term, the title drives sales. Long term, the contents and word of mouth do.
- Naming formulas:
  - Number + outcome + time frame: "three emails that can turn cold leads into clients in 24 hours."
  - How to [good thing] without [bad thing], even if [greatest insecurity]: "how to build a funnel without hiring a copywriter even if you've never done it before."
  - Adjective + good thing: "the lazy funnel template that converts like crazy."
  - X mistakes that prevent [good thing]: "four mistakes keeping your business under a million dollars a year," or "five ad copy mistakes that are killing your conversion rate."

## Turning the lead magnet into sales with CTAs

- Most people make a great lead magnet and forget the call to action, or don't ask often enough. Salespeople who ask the most times get the most deals.
- To ask often without turning people off, keep providing value between asks. Maximize your ability to ask times the number of times you ask.
- CTA formula:
  1. Clear, not clever. State exactly what you want.
  2. The exact next action.
  3. A reason to act now.
- Use scarcity, urgency or both when you have them. Any reason beats none, as in the line-cutting study where "because I have a dog" still worked. His examples: a one-year anniversary promotion, or National Dog Day.
- Service businesses have capacity limits. Say so, for example "four more customers will get us to capacity." Customers don't know how small you are, so use that for scarcity. He notes 78% of businesses are service businesses.
- Larger businesses can run rolling cohorts, for example "we start only this many people per week" or "join this Friday."
- Example: each chapter of his books ends with a CTA to a free gift on acquisition.com, which leads on to a monthly workshop and the chance to become a portfolio company.
- Operational summary:
  1. Give value first by revealing a problem, offering a trial, or giving one step of many.
  2. Deliver through software, information, services, physical products, or a mix.
  3. Name it compellingly, or ask your audience.
  4. Put CTAs inside the lead magnet, and before and after it.
