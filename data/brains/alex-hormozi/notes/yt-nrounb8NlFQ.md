## Reading a business's economics before prescribing fixes (Ashley, AC Styles case)

Alex's first pass on any business is to check the unit economics. If those are healthy, the fix is more volume, not a redesign.

**Ashley's numbers (personal styling for luxury clients):**
- Revenue about $309K a year, profit $130K, 42% net margin.
- Four years in, over 1,000 clients served, 40 current clients, 1 churned in the last year.
- Funnel: about 12 leads a month, 8 appointments, 100% show rate, 4 sales, 50% close rate.
- Blended CAC $600, LTV $10K, which is a 16:1 LTV to CAC ratio.

**Alex's read:**
- Losing 1 of 40 customers in a year (about 2.5% annual churn) is "phenomenal".
- Quality of a product or service is "dictated by the percentage of customers that want to buy it again and again."
- The aim is to "acquire a customer once and have them pay you for the rest of their lives."
- Recurring means a membership or subscription. Reoccurring is like Coca-Cola, where people rebuy without a subscription.
- When economics are good, "the N is low": she just needs far more volume.
- He asks what percentage of customers come from each source and where time is spent. Often one source gives 90% of customers while half the time goes to a source giving 10%. Then he asks whether the winning source is tapped out. Most times it isn't.
- Ashley could track this because she had data. Alex says data shows where the leaks are.
- Her mix: about a third from affiliates, about a third from paid (Google and Thumbtack), about a third from referrals.

## Pricing, ascension and continuity for a high-end service

**Her offer structure:**
- $8,500 onboarding over 60 days, which includes a closet teardown, an inventory and new outfits (5 to 10).
- After that, $2K a month or $4,500 a quarter.
- About 10% of revenue comes from vendor commissions.
- An earlier $3,500 one-time tier was dropped, because the luxury tier made more money.

**Alex's views:**
- He likes that she serves high earners (minimum around $1M income, about $25K a year for the service plus clothing spend). Many owners fear going after high earners. She "doesn't compromise her quality to meet lower-end customers."
- He kept the more expensive monthly option as a price anchor. Even if only 10% take it, it lifts the take rate on the quarterly plan.
- Most existing clients were grandfathered at about $1,500 a quarter (about $500 a month), and only about two had moved to the new pricing.
- Pricing method: "I just keep edging up the price until people stop saying yes," then back down slightly. That gives the price the market will bear for that avatar, offer and message.
- Her continuity was working, so he left it alone. She is small, with limited time and capital, so he recommends only the highest-likelihood, lowest-cost moves first. Other opportunities can wait for more volume and cash flow.

## Avatar clarity

- Alex asks who her dream customer is and works backward from there.
- Her avatars are mom socialites (kids with many social events, wealthy, private) and men who are CEOs. It's about a 50/50 revenue split, and the wife often starts first.
- He checks the split because some owners serve two types, hate one and love the other, and keep the one they dislike because it pays bills. If 30% of revenue came from a customer he hated and 70% from one he loved, he'd cut the 30% and double down.
- Ashley likes both, so he doesn't make her pick. At $3M to $5M he'd revisit which type stays longer, is worth more and refers more.
- Buying triggers are life transitions: a baby, a changed body, divorce, wanting to start dating, or a promotion to the C-suite or VP.

## Fixing the paid funnel: lead magnet, qualification and friction

**The leak:** Google ads produced about 1,800 clicks for 8 sales, at $3,400 for the year and a cost per click of $189 as stated. The page had no prices, no video and no reason to opt in. Alex says that if she fixes this, "you could probably 5 to 10x the sales of the business." Thumbtack (about $6K spend, 58 leads, $38 a lead, 8 sales, CAC $750) is 90 to 95% unqualified.

**Benchmarks and lead magnet rules:**
- A qualified-traffic opt-in page with a clear lead magnet gets 20% to 50%, sometimes 60%, opt-ins. He used 30% as the target for her high-intent search traffic.
- A lead magnet should be risk-free, easy and fast. "We don't want the Avatar to work to get the value."
- Work more on the front end so the prospect works less. It's like "pre-digesting the food."
- Between a more valuable lead magnet that takes longer and a less valuable one that is immediate, customers prefer the immediate one.
- For her, he suggests a color analysis by skin, hair and eye color, or a style direction. Another option is "how to best use your existing closet", which sets up a gap she can sell into.
- A costly or unscalable lead magnet can go only to qualified people, a "honey trap for the right type of people."

**Qualification:**
- He uses BANT: budget, authority, need, timing. Ask the fewest questions needed to decide.
- Budget was the biggest one for her. Don't show prices on the page, but describe the service. Ask a few questions and it screens out about 80%.
- Unqualified leads get a free resource instead of a calendar slot.
- Good friction removes only bad leads. Bad friction, like slow page loads, removes everyone. A photo upload is too much friction up front, so move it to follow-up.
- Asking skin, hair and eye color up front lets her prepare one of about 10 palette templates before the call.

**Sales call logic:** A free-sounding offer can get 10x the calls. Closing 30% of 10x the calls still gives about three times the deals. Pre-sell with videos (one on the page, a second between booking and the call) so calls are spent on the person's decision, not repeating the pitch.

## Scaling spend when LTV to CAC is strong

- With a machine that returns roughly $16 for every $1 within 30 days, "why would you have any kind of limit on that budget?"
- Cheap-channel math as Alex framed it: $3,400 a year at 23x would generate about $78,000.
- Plan: fix the funnel first, then raise spend from $300 a month to $600 to $900 a month. Her capacity was 12 calls a month, and she could double or triple that. CAC may rise a little and that's fine.
- Attribution first: "the first step of running ads is know what happens otherwise you're just spending money in the dark." Alex was unsure which of her sales actually came from the ads versus search.
- He didn't push Facebook ads, because she already had a profitable channel.

## Affiliate program: from 6 to 60

**Definition (from $100M Leads):** Affiliates are independent businesses that tell their audience to buy your stuff, in exchange for money, free stuff or both. They have their own businesses and their own advertising.

**Her results:**
- About 6 affiliates (4 matchmakers, 2 private clubs) send about a third of revenue, roughly $100K a year.
- She found them with a Google search, then did manual outreach by email, about 40 attempts in all.
- Six closed, about 15% on cold outreach. Alex says normal cold outbound gets 0.5% to 1%, so that's 10 to 15 times normal efficiency.
- Alex's math: each email was worth about $2,500, and a 10x gives roughly a tripling of the business.

**Alex's plan:**
- Send 400 emails at 20 a day. Don't change the manual process or buy software.
- Build four segments: matchmakers and dating, executive coaches, high-end personal trainers, and divorce attorneys.
- Sources: Google, social media, then list brokers narrowed to a city. Re-send to non-responders.
- Remote service is fine (about 50% of her work).
- Affiliates compound: the work is done once and each sends one or two clients every month.
- Keep them active by treating them like customers. Hold 1 to 3 events a year, ideally one event with all of them in the same room, pitched as collaboration rather than competition. Alex's plan is one event per quarter.

## Sequencing and goal setting

- Her goal was $5M. Alex said that's 17x from $300K and proposed $1M first, then more. "Systems break when they triple," so he thinks in triples. He cited the "Chinese rule of three and 10."
- He held off on her corporate-clients idea. Reaching $2M to $3M from these fixes first might change her mind.
- Priority order, easiest and fastest first:
  1. Video sales letter, plus lead magnet, plus BANT qualification, plus skin tone personalization. It's about half a day to a day of work.
  2. Attribution tracking. He labeled it 1.5.
  3. Raise ad spend to $600 to $900 a month. It's "literally clicking a button."
  4. 20 affiliate emails a day, an event each quarter, and the four affiliate types.
  5. Leave continuity alone.
- His estimates: funnel and ads 2 to 3x (lead flow possibly 2 to 5x), affiliates 3x. Combined, about 12x, which takes $300K to $3.6M.
