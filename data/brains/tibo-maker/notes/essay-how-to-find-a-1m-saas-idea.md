## How Tibo reverse-engineers a $1M SaaS from the math

- A $1M valuation at a 2.5x yearly multiple ("pretty normal for a saas") means $400k ARR, which is $35k MRR.
- $35k MRR is 350 customers at $100/month, or 700 customers at $50/month.
- "Your idea doesn't need to change the world. It just needs to find 350 to 700 people who will pay you consistently every month."
- Tibo says he has built a $1M SaaS five times: Tweet Hunter, Taplio, Revid.ai, Outrank.so and SuperX.so.
- Market-size check: "If you can't find 700 people who'd pay $50/month for your product, the market is too small. Start there before you write a single line of code."

## Why copying X trends gives you the same ideas as everyone else

- "If you have the same process as everyone else, you'll have the same ideas as everyone else."
- Scrolling X, spotting a trending problem and building it puts you in competition with everyone who saw it at the same time.
- He describes two types of founders when it comes to finding ideas:
  - Founders with years in a specific industry, who know the pain better than anyone.
  - Founders with no deep industry knowledge and no obvious idea. Tibo was in this group in 2021.

## Industry insiders see what everyone else treats as normal

- Existing companies often have deep incentives not to fix problems, because fixing them disrupts workflows or management structures.
- Examples he gives:
  - Elon saw that rocket parts cost a fraction of what NASA paid for finished rockets, and SpaceX was born.
  - The Loom founders were frustrated explaining bugs over email at their jobs. Screen recording existed, but nobody had made it seamless.
- "Your industry knowledge makes you see something obviously broken that everyone else has accepted as normal." He calls this the ideal scenario.

## The 10% rule: build many products instead of going all in

- Tibo expects a success rate of 10% at best when creating new products, and says the same holds for Levelsio and Marc Lou.
- Going all in on one idea is "the worst thing you can do."
- A 10% success rate across 10 ideas means something works.
- He did "one product per week until something sticks" back in 2021, and says others have done it too.
- Why it works:
  - It forces you to ship instead of plan.
  - You get real feedback fast.
  - You move past the roughly 80% of ideas that will be garbage before they cost a year of your life.
- His summary: "Build more. Validate faster. Kill quicker."

## What makes an idea fit you specifically

- An idea that could work won't necessarily work for you. It needs to fit:
  - Your skill set.
  - What you enjoy doing.
  - Something you can work on for a long time without getting bored.
  - The marketing style you're naturally good at, whether SEO, content, ads or 1-1 sales.
- Example: if you hate writing and hate SEO, a product that lives or dies on those channels will drain you fast. He mentions his tools Outrank (SEO on autopilot) and SuperX (content distribution) in this context.
- You can't know all of this from day zero. "The more you build, the more you understand what fits."

## The validation test: two weeks, four questions

- Timeframe every idea, with a maximum of 2 weeks.
- At the end, ask four questions:
  1. Are people signing up without you chasing them?
  2. Are they coming back on their own?
  3. Are they paying without heavy convincing?
  4. Am I even getting feedback?
- Yes to all four: keep going.
- No to any: kill it and move to the next idea, unless there's an obvious fix.
- His conclusion: "A $1M SaaS idea isn't a flash of inspiration. It's what survives this process."
- Related rule: 2 hours of building for every 1 hour of content consumption.
