## Know your edge before picking an idea

Tibo defines business success in one phrase: "You have to be better than others at something." He starts by asking what makes the founder unique, then doubles down on it. His checklist:

- What skills do you have that others don't?
- Do you already have an audience?
- Any special market knowledge?
- A network you can leverage?
- A unique combination of skills?

He calls this your "unfair advantage" and treats it as the starting point of any new business.

## Build an idea bank because knowing what to build is the bottleneck

Tibo argues that in 2025 "building is not a bottleneck any more. Knowing what to build is." He says 2025 is "ruled by idea guys", so founders should keep collecting more ideas, and he lists where to find them:

- Reddit questions ("goldmine of problems")
- Feature requests on Shopify, Webflow and similar platforms
- The Chrome extension store (ask what could be standalone)
- Amazon reviews, especially 3-star reviews of popular products
- App store complaints

His principle: watch what people complain about. "Their ignorance is your opportunity."

## Validate with a landing page and an email field before coding

Tibo says to gauge demand before building, because there is no point building if no one is buying. His process:

1. Use V0 or Lovable to create a landing page.
2. Add one email input and a "Request Access" button.
3. Share it in DMs, tweets, Reddit and similar places.
4. See who gives you their email.

If people leave their email, that counts as validation. If there is no interest, skip to the next idea. His reasoning: if people won't commit to a product for free, you can't expect them to pay when you release it.

## Use the one-week build challenge to force a narrow core

Tibo picks the landing page that collected the most emails and sets one constraint: "What can I build in ONE WEEK?" He insists on one week, not two, not a month, not a quarter. The limit forces the founder to narrow the product to its core value proposition.

- Build it, ship it, and get it in front of real people.
- He accepts that the first version will be "crappy", because the learning is worth it.

## Find the real problem behind the one you think you're solving

Tibo says most founders miss that "the problem you think you're solving usually isn't the real one." He argues most problems come down to one of three things:

- Money
- Health
- Sex (relationships and status)

The founder should work out which one the product actually addresses, then go deeper, iterate, collect feedback and repeat.

## Expand acquisition fast once positioning is clear

Once the founder has found the core reason to buy and the positioning, Tibo says to turbocharge client acquisition. Speed matters most, because "categories get saturated and sophisticated before you know", and you want to be the leader when that happens. His channel list:

- Build in public on X
- Start with basic Meta and Google ads
- Activate SEO, using Feather.so or Outrank.so to start quickly
- Create mini-tools for lead generation
- Work with influencers and set up an affiliate program (he names Rewardful)
- Build virality into the product by making it shareable

His rule: each channel properly built compounds.

## Fix retention because it beats acquisition

Tibo warns that once there is traffic and some revenue, retention will likely be poor. He says this is where most founders fail: they keep acquiring users who just leave. His stance is "Retention > acquisition." He tells founders to work on it obsessively ("It's your oxygen"), because that is how a business compounds and how a founder reaches financial freedom.

He closes with the view that the difference between success and failure usually isn't the idea but "consistency. Persistence. Adjustment." He also notes that most people quit right before things get good, and that the playbook is simple but "simple ≠ easy."
