## Build the MVP in days or weeks, not months
- Tibo says to use shortcuts: no-code (he built some MVPs on Bubble.io) and boilerplates. He accepts skipping things that expert developers refuse to compromise on.
- His reasoning is the failure rate: "You will have a 90% failure rate." At one year per attempt, finding a winner could take 9 years.
- He compresses the cycle so he can ship a new project every week and reach success much faster.

## Find 5-10 truly relevant users and build a relationship
- After the MVP, he finds five to ten people who are the core target audience, reaching out by tweet, subreddit or email.
- Relevance is the point. If the tester is not in the target audience (his example: your mom), the feedback, positive or negative, teaches nothing.
- He then builds a real relationship with each person to understand their life, their pain and their workflow, so the product delivers real value.

## Talk to users every day and be the user yourself
- He wants recurring usage, so he talks to users daily to learn why they come back or don't, then reproduces that for others.
- Until a product reaches 10K/month in revenue, the support link in each product points to his Twitter DMs. This creates a daily flow of users and makes them feel close to him.
- Fixing something in 5 to 10 minutes can turn that person into a customer for life. Shipping a requested feature within an hour or two makes the user an advocate who talks about the product.
- He uses his own products, which makes him "10 times more relevant" on the core problem. Any tiny thing to fix, he just fixes.
- He states the core job as understanding the user's core need: keep a constant communication channel (social works well) and use your own product.

## Understand the user's ultimate goal, then iterate until they can't live without it
- Knowing how far you can help users reach their ultimate goal lets you generate 10x or 100x more value.
- Build features that solve users' problems, not your own.
- Repeat the talk-build-fix loop while staying active on social media, where he sees new requests daily.
- He warns against going to acquisition too early. With low retention, you spend energy pushing people in and "99% of those people are going to flow away."
- Complaints are a good sign: a user who takes time to complain is committed and wants you to fix it.

## Distribution: go broad, then become a media company
- Once stickiness exists, he tries many acquisition channels to see which work for that product.
- Launching on Product Hunt and talking about the product on social (building in public) is free. For him it is the default until 10K of revenue and is enough to reach the 1-3K/month needed to live and reinvest.
- At some point a company must become a media company: pick socials, SEO or cold email and build a content pipeline.
- That means having something to say about your industry, testimonials and case studies of very successful users, and an engine that fuels everything else.

## Scale with sustainable channels, then concentrate
- For scaling he picks SEO, ads and affiliation, because you can set them once and scale to high limits. If ads work at 1K/month budget, they can often go to 10K or 100K with ongoing optimization.
- Example: Outrank grew from 0 to 20K/month by building in public. To keep the rhythm past 20K they started ads, invested in SEO with a dedicated hire, and ramped the affiliate program by making recommending Outrank easy. That took it from 20 to 200.
- Step 12 is to scale what works and kill what doesn't. Though he goes broad earlier, growth for each product comes from one or two channels, and he goes all in on those. Once SEO works, there is endless SEO work: new keywords and queries.

## Portfolio strategy and his products
- Reported portfolio: Rabbit.ai (video making, about 400K/month, growing about 10% month over month), Alfronk (blog generator becoming an all-in-one SEO tool, just passed 200K/month), Super X (X audience tool, 13K/month), Post Sinker (posts to 10 platforms, 1.5K/month), and Feather (Notion-to-blog tool, about 10K/month, acquired for 250K).
- Combined: about 700K/month, 50K paying customers, growing about 20% monthly for over 6 months.
- He runs multiple products for resilience, driven by fear: he has a wife and two kids and AI moves fast. If OpenAI kills one product, it is not the end of the world.
- He cites X's takeover almost killing Tweet Hunter while it made 200K/month.
