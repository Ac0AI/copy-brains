## Free SEO mini tools as the main acquisition engine

- Tibo built mini tools for Taplio and Tweet Hunter. Taplio is an AI-assisted LinkedIn scheduling and management tool.
- Content on Twitter and LinkedIn was everything at first. Then it "was just not enough".
- The team shipped "more than one per month" and ended with about 40 SEO-optimized mini tools.
- Together those tools brought roughly 70% of traffic.
- Each free tool ranks for many long-tail keywords. Visitors then discover and buy the main product.
- Tibo's test question: what free tool can I create that generates top-of-funnel traffic, where people who use it are naturally the buyers of my product?
- Best performer: "download video from LinkedIn post", a low-intent keyword for Taplio but about a million visitors a month. It ranked first.
- That tool was an evening's idea, built without even telling his co-founder. It was also the task they gave a developer they wanted to hire.
- Well-researched tools did not perform. His takeaway: "we know nothing basically", so ship many and see which wins.
- Result: downloaders are content creators, so the CTA was "schedule this on Taplio" or "on Tweet Hunter".

## Mini tools as features of the main product (the Vid.io pattern)

- Tibo cites Vid.io, a video editor, as inspiration. It has around 50 tools such as crop video and compress video.
- Each tool takes your file as input, then redirects you into the main editor with the file already loaded. You are inside the product with no signup and no payment.
- Why it is cost-efficient: a new tool is just a feature of the main product, so it is cheap to build.
- Why it converts: users get a taste of the editor and think of it next time they need one.
- Tibo says he now runs Revid.ai, a video editor for short-form videos.

## Why tool-based SEO survives AI search

- Tibo argues that "Google is dying because of GPT" matters less for action-based queries.
- Someone who wants to compress a video or download a LinkedIn video is looking for a tool. They are unlikely to ask ChatGPT and will search Google and click through.
- So he would "double down" on the mini-tool strategy.
- He is skeptical that tools get embedded in chat answers soon. Plugins and custom GPTs, in his words, "seem to be a failure", with only small use cases.
- On the host's view that LLMs will embed tools: Tibo accepts that Google now has more incentive to improve user experience, but is unsure.

## Reddit and Quora answers as an LLM-visibility play

- Tibo would invest heavily in answering Quora and Reddit questions, because LLMs likely draw answers from them.
- Example: to be the answer to "best sushi restaurant in Paris", get your pick to the top-voted Reddit answer.
- He believes AI plus proper context and instructions could eventually produce human-sounding replies, with a human touch, emotion and even errors. He sees it as a cat-and-mouse game between LLMs and content.
- He mentions Peter Levels blocking anyone he suspects of AI replies, and wonders about the error rate.

## Anti-AI positioning as an arbitrage

- Tibo is working on SuperX, a Chrome extension for X with strong analytics. Adding AI replies would label it a gray-hat tool that makes Twitter worse with "shitty contents".
- He is therefore considering an anti-AI positioning.
- Idea: a suite of tools with no AI that people are happy to promote, because promoting them signals they are genuine humans.
- His competition argument: AI is brutally crowded, with "10 competitors" jumping on any AI idea. Levels and Danny Postma said their next product will be a no-AI product. Anti-AI has far fewer competitors.
- He points to 37signals / Basecamp (Jason Fried) as likely to keep making no-AI products, and wants to see their next one.
- He calls anti-positioning "so underrated" because it stands out when everything else looks the same.

## Brand and creator sponsorship marketplace ideas

- Tibo thought the brand-influencer matching space was dead and saturated until he noticed Passionfruit. It offers creators ready-to-book sponsorship packages (tweet, YouTube video) with payment and calendar. Brands book with no signup via an email flow.
- His lesson: when you think a space is saturated, "you can just do 100 more things".
- Idea: flip it for brands, with a link-tree-style page where brands list the sponsorships they want and creators apply.
- Evidence: Tweet Hunter opened such a page for Twitter creators. It showed a rough calculation of payment and asked creators to plug in their Twitter handle and apply. Applications arrived by email and the team started the conversation there.
- Result: hundreds of applications after one tweet, which became "a nightmare to sort". Moderation and sorting is the real problem to solve.
- Product: AI to sort, grade and moderate inbound creators, and handle the back-and-forth of guidelines and validation.
- Pricing: charge for moderation and booking handling. Alternatively take a cut of sponsorships or sell a subscription by brand size, but not at the very beginning.
- Getting brands: scrape brands currently sponsoring creators. Keywords like "sponsored by" or "together with" appear in most sponsorship posts and newsletters. Reach out to them with this.
- SEO tool angle: build a free, SEO-optimized library of past sponsorships that worked, with pages per niche (e-commerce, AI, coaching). Rank for long-tail keywords, then sell the main product.
- Link-tree-style products generate static pages for clients. With custom domains, linking back to the main product builds backlinks fast.
