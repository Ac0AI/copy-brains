## Price backwards from a range, not forwards from a trick

- Tibo dismisses X pricing advice such as "Put $7 at the end of your price!", "Price it under $10!" and "Use psychological pricing!". In his words, "these tricks don't work."
- His rule: "You work backwards from the price point."
- He prices his SaaS in a range of $29-$99, then decides what to build, and how to build it, based on that range.
- Instead of obsessing over psychological pricing, he asks three questions:
  - What results do we deliver?
  - Who needs these results badly?
  - How much are these results worth to them?
- "That's your price."

## The low-price trap

- Most founders start at $4-$9/month, believe it will attract more customers, and hope to raise prices "later". Tibo says "Later never comes."
- A low price is a low-quality signal. Customers perceive a cheap product as lower quality, so the price hurts the product.
- A low price does not compensate for low value. You can't price at $5 and expect users to tolerate a bad product.

## High-value clients don't care about price, if the problem is burning

- Customers with a burning problem care about the result, not the monthly fee.
- Keyword research tools: if the data and opportunities are the best, it doesn't matter whether it costs $50, $100 or $200/month. The advantage outweighs the software cost.
- Newsletter software: deliverability matters more than saving $50/month. He says this is part of why many email companies scale to 7 figures quickly.
- The condition is that the product solves a burning problem. That is why his headline carries an asterisk: "High-Value Clients Don't Care About Price*".

## Tweet Hunter: raising prices 5x

- Tweet Hunter started at $9/month and ended at $49/month, a 5x increase. The increases applied to new customers only.
- Each price increase had two effects at the same time:
  - A small drop in conversion.
  - Lower churn, which he says the team did not expect.
- Fewer people bought, but those who did stayed longer.
- "EVERY SINGLE TIME we raised prices, the LTV bump made for the conversion drop."

## Complaints about price are a good sign

- People who complain about a price bump often love the product, because they rely on it.
- Most complainers don't actually churn.
- Real churn comes from customers who don't find the product valuable and cancel silently.
- His heuristic: "If no one's complaining about your price, it's too low."

## Solve a real problem first; higher prices create a upward spiral

- The number one focus should be solving a real problem for the customer. That gives you the authority to charge more.
- Higher prices change how you operate:
  - You think bigger.
  - You build better features.
  - You deliver premium experiences.
  - Your users get better results.
- He calls it a win-win spiral upward.

## The pricing framework

1. Price for results, not features.
2. Target people who value those results.
3. Don't fear raising prices.
4. Build for premium, charge for premium.
