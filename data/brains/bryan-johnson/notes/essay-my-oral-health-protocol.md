## Baseline measurements Bryan asks a dentist to take

- Bryan says that, as with anything in Blueprint, you need to know your baseline first. He asks his dentist to evaluate three things.
- **Attachment loss:** the loss of supportive tissue around the teeth. Severe poor attachment can lead to tooth loss.
- **Pocket depth:** measured from the top of the gum line to the bottom of the pocket where the gum attaches to the tooth. Healthy gums are 1-3 mm. Three years ago Bryan had several pockets that were 5 mm deep.
- **Plaque index:** the thickness of plaque on the tooth surface. His dentist stained his mouth with a solution that reveals plaque, then assigned numbers to the plaque levels and the parts of the teeth affected.
- He also checks whether the gums bleed. He wants gums that don't bleed easily. His dentist kept poking his gums trying to make them bleed and couldn't.

## Results after three years on Blueprint

- Bryan started Blueprint with receding gums, eroding teeth and a sugar-heavy diet, and says his teeth caused him a lot of anxiety. His message: caring for your teeth is manageable and reversing damage is possible.
- His dentist reports "you have the gums of a healthy teenager".
- Plaque index of 24.4%, which he calls ideal.
- Gingival and calculus index of 0, the lowest possible and equal to an 18-year-old.
- Attachment loss reduced by 41%.
- He resolved destructive bruxism he had dealt with for 20 years.
- He argues oral health matters beyond teeth: optimal oral care can prevent tooth decay and gum disease, and can lower the risk of heart disease.

## Bryan's nine-step oral care protocol

Steps 1-6 are done morning and night.
1. **Waterpik:** the first step. A high-pressure water stream removes food, plaque and bacteria from hard-to-reach places.
2. **Floss:** used after the Waterpik has loosened particles. He uses Dr. Tongue's floss because it is thicker and showed a 25%+ increase in plaque removal in studies Blueprint reviewed.
3. **Brush:** an electric brush with soft bristles, which cleans effectively without needless harshness on teeth or gums. He says a manual brush is fine if you prefer it.
4. **Toothpaste:** he currently uses two types, neither containing fluoride.
5. **Tongue scraper:** he calls it "a game changer" and encourages everyone to try it for better oral hygiene.
6. **Mouthwash:** a xylitol mouthwash.
7. **Tea tree oil:** he previously used 2-3 drops in 2 oz of water. It helped his oral health years ago. He stopped recently because his baseline is back to ideal.
8. **Bruxism device:** worn while sleeping. A dentist on Blueprint recommended the SomnoDent bruxism device (SomnoDent Avant). For the first time in 20 years of night grinding, his bruxism stopped. He strongly encourages anyone with bruxism to address it.
9. **Professional cleanings:** at least 2 visits a year, to catch problems early and remove hardened plaque. Even with diligent brushing and flossing, plaque is hard to fully remove at home.

## Daily oral hygiene habits

- **Reduce sugar:** use alternatives such as trehalose, monk fruit, xylitol and allulose.
- **Limit acidic liquids:** avoid extended exposure to acidic fluids such as coffee and lemon water.
- **Avoid dry mouth:** stay hydrated. Saliva washes away food debris, neutralizes acid and carries ions like calcium that remineralize and strengthen enamel.
- **Wait to brush:** he doesn't brush for 30 minutes after eating, because enamel is temporarily softened.
- **Travel toothbrush:** on days on the go, he carries one and sneaks away to a bathroom during the day for a clean.
