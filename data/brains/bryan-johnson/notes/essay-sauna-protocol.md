## Dry sauna as a core longevity protocol

Bryan calls dry sauna one of the most efficacious longevity protocols he has done, and now saunas daily. He ran a 30-day test measuring hundreds of markers. He cites long-term studies linking sauna use 4+ times per week to about a 40% lower risk of dying from any cause versus no sauna use. Sauna takes about 20 minutes a day and is available in most gyms. He says it can improve heart, metabolism, skin and brain health.

Bryan summarizes these effects (sessions of at least 19 min for the mortality figures):

| High blood pressure risk (healthy men 42-60) | -17% | -47% |
| Dementia risk | -21% | -48% |
| Systemic inflammation | -17% | -31.5% |

- Psychotic disorder risk is 77% lower at 4-7x/week.
- Over 80% of users reported better sleep, and 33% reported less muscle pain.
- Inflammation markers that fall: hsCRP, fibrinogen and white blood cell count.
- Metabolic effects: lower total cholesterol and LDL, a transient drop in triglycerides, and a small rise in HDL (10 sessions of 3 x 15 min).
- In a low-powered study (12 days at 212°F / 100°C, 5 x 10 min, over 4 weeks): muscle mass +1.07%, bone mineral density +7.7%.
- Sweating increases removal of arsenic, cadmium, lead and mercury. Sessions of 15-20+ min may help clear stored fat-soluble toxins.

## Why dry sauna works and why alternatives do not

- Skin heats faster than the core, which inverts the skin-to-core temperature gradient.
- The heart pumps up to 70% more blood, similar to intense aerobic exercise (zone 2).
- The skin makes 0.6-1 liter of sweat per hour.
- Heat tolerance improves, with a lower core body temperature.
- At a core temperature of 102.2°F (39°C), the body releases heat shock proteins that repair misfolded proteins.
- Steam baths, hot tubs and infrared saunas do not replicate this. They do not reach the needed temperatures, induce the same sweating, create the inverted gradient, or redirect blood to the skin.

- **Without ice**, sauna hurt sperm markers: total motile count -56%, concentration -30%, motility -50%, morphology -48%, count -9%.

## How to sauna at home

- **Temperature:** 176-212°F (80-100°C). Beginners should aim for the low end, since higher heat can cause headaches and severely dry nose and eyes.
- **Duration and frequency:** 15-20 min, 3-5x a week (his key takeaway range is 3-7x a week).
- **Skin:** apply moisturizer before. Hot dry sauna raises transepidermal water loss and stresses the skin barrier.
- **Head and nose:** wear a sauna hat or a 100% natural cotton towel, or put an ice pack on a towel. Breathe through a towel if needed.
- **Hydration:** never sauna dehydrated. Rehydrate with 16-32 oz (0.5-1 L) of fluid plus electrolytes. To be precise, measure sweat volume and saltiness with a patch (e.g. from Gatorade), since some people have saltier sweat.
- **Toxin avoidance:** wear cotton or bamboo, or go naked. Avoid synthetic towels and heavily treated fabrics. Do not pour water on the rocks. Shower after each session.

## Who should skip sauna, and the fallback

Skip it if you:
- have serious heart issues or uncontrolled blood pressure
- are pregnant (consult a doctor)
- have an infection, fever or feel unwell
- have a history of seizures
- have respiratory conditions such as asthma or COPD
- have irritated or inflamed skin
- recently consumed alcohol or recreational drugs
- take beta-blockers, stimulants, anticholinergics or diuretics

If you have no sauna, his best alternative is exercise, which raises body heat and gives many of the same cardiovascular benefits. For maximum benefit, do both.
