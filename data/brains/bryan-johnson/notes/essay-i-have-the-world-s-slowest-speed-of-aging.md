## Speed of aging as the core measurement: DNA methylation and PACE

- Bryan chose "speed of aging" as his headline metric because of how robust and reliable the signal is.
- DNA methylation is a whole-body health marker that integrates thousands of biochemical cues into one measurable signal. The speed of aging algorithm distills it into a single number: an individual's PACE score.
- He contrasts it with narrow markers such as blood glucose, hormones and cholesterol. DNA methylation, and specifically the PACE clock, is "a comprehensive assessment of one's health."
- He says this new biological aging measurement science has begun "nipping at the heels of the best predictors of all-cause mortality" after just a few years.
- The test is an easy at-home finger prick.

## Why measurement matters: replacing storytelling with data

- Bryan argues that before Project Blueprint and Don't Die, health influencers and fads were mainly storytelling-based and anecdotal. What was missing was "rigorous science, measurement and data."
- When health decisions run on storytelling, nobody knows what works or who is telling the truth. This leaves people confused and paralyzed.
- This inspired him to make his project the most scientifically rigorous and quantifiable ever, and he says he became "the most biologically measured person in human history."
- His principle: if the goal is Don't Die, you need robust, science-based systems with quantifiable results.

## The Rejuvenation Olympics: rejuvenation as a sport

- Early on, people couldn't categorize him and blurted words like vampire, Patrick Bateman, techbro, biohacker, Prometheus and Dorian Gray.
- He began calling himself a "professional rejuvenation athlete." His reasoning: when LeBron James eats and sleeps well and sets records, he is praised, but when Bryan did the same, "hate rained."
- Framing rejuvenation as a sport gives the practice respect and formality. He created the Rejuvenation Olympics, a leaderboard for people competing on the slowness of their speed of aging.
- He says it sets a level playing field for anyone in health and wellness, since humans love points, status and leaderboards, which any competitive sport needs.
- The leaderboard has also helped the wider health community see which things increase and decrease speed of aging. He promised to post more on this.

## Genetics vs effort

- Talking with others on the leaderboard, Bryan learned some people naturally (genetically) have a very low speed of aging.
- He met a top-10 competitor with a speed of aging fairly close to his own who does nothing for his health. Bryan says "He is genetically gifted. I am not," while lowering his own speed of aging is a primary focus of his life.
- His takeaway: even without genetic gifts, methodical hard work can meaningfully slow speed of aging and even outperform the genetically gifted.
- He speculates that gene editing might eventually make lowering speed of aging a simple fix, or that it could become a pill.
- Until then, his approach is to understand the science, design evidence-based protocols, and "execute with precision."
