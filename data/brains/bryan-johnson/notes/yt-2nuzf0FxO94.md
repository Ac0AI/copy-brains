## Skin damage and why Bryan treats skin as an organ to measure

- Bryan calls skin "the largest organ of our bodies." As a kid he "destroyed" his, spending lots of time in the sun with no sunscreen, and in his 40s he is "paying the price."
- Blueprint's goal is to measure every organ, including skin, slow the speed of aging, and reverse existing aging damage.
- His rule is the same as for food: on his 2,250-calorie diet "every calorie has to fight for its life." Each skin treatment must hit its target markers (UV damage, pore size, reds, browns and others) or it is out.
- His reported progress: when he started, his skin was around the 98th percentile for damage, and his skin age on one marker was 64. His most recent measurement put it between 37 and 42.
- His first multi-spectral imaging scan was "pretty terrifying" ("I look like a zombie face"). He notes his son's 16-17 year old friends had the same shock at their sun damage despite always wearing sunscreen, living in Southern California.

## Measurement tools in the clinic

- **Multi-spectral imaging:** A clinical-grade, dermatology-office system that gives a dashboard of roughly 8-10 biomarkers, including UV damage that is invisible to the eye, browns, reds and pore size. It is his baseline and tracking tool for skin.
- **AGE reader (advanced glycation end products):** He puts his arm on it to measure "junk build up in the skin." Results plot against age; one standard deviation below the line is better than normal. His first readings were in the red. He treats it as a whole-body view of skin and cardiovascular health.

## Morning routine and creams

- He washes his face with CeraVe, which contains salicylic acid, "just enough to keep my face blemish free."
- He "micro doses" Accutane: two 40 mg pills per week. He needs acne control because other anti-aging therapies, such as rapamycin, have acne as a side effect at his dose.
- He applies a cream trialed for about 3 weeks (a "fat cell feeding" cream) aimed at restoring facial volume. He says it could end up "in the cream graveyard on my counter" if it does not prove itself on his markers.
- Sunscreen is the last step: EltaMD.
- His main finding on creams: "creams don't move the needle on data." For crow's feet or UV damage seen on multi-spectral imaging, he says you need devices such as lasers and IPL.

## Devices and what he concluded about each

- **BBL / IPL (intense pulsed light):** Wavelengths of 515, 695, 800 and 640 nm. He does full-body treatments, motivated by a small Stanford study suggesting IPL shifted skin gene expression toward a younger profile. He admits the study was too small to give huge confidence, but it was enough to try. It eliminated most of his sun damage on face and upper body, which he burned and tanned often as a shirtless kid.
- **Exilis (radio frequency):** Roll the warm tip over the face to boost collagen and tighten skin. He holds the temperature at exactly 40-42°C; above that is for fat burning. He has paused it out of caution, since he is trying to rejuvenate facial volume and worries about fat loss.
- **Tixel:** Thermal mechanical ablation, with a titanium tip delivering intense heat pulses. He has recently become positive on it, finds it safe and effective for firmness and collagen, and is buying a device. He is three therapies in and likes the results.
- **Solawave-style LED device ("Saluma Pro", $1,500):** Red light for collagen and healing, blue for acne. He used it while medication caused breakouts, but it comes in and out of the protocol because its effect is subtle next to BBL or Tixel.
- **Facial volume:** He lost a lot of facial volume early in Blueprint from caloric restriction. He has since raised his calories, and his photos from a year ago versus now show he has revolumized quite a bit.

## Red light therapy

- He stands between red and near-infrared panels 3 times a week for 12 minutes (36 minutes a week).
- He cites benefits for skin health, healing, mitochondrial health, and recovery from workouts. He also notes red light on the forehead has been shown to help with depression.
- He keeps it because the evidence supports it, even though he cannot measure its effect directly, as it compounds with his other therapies.

## Sun avoidance and home setup

- His windows have a tint that blocks about 99.9% of UV. He gets lots of morning and evening sun at home and wants the light without the damage. He accepts the comments about his pale skin ("I'm a vampire").
- His objective is not to follow cultural norms such as a tan. It is slowing aging and reversing damage, including sun damage.
- Outdoors, he walks to a nearby cafe carrying an umbrella for shade. He says this is odd in the US but common and normal elsewhere.
- UV is highest roughly 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and higher in summer than winter. He spends sun time early morning or evening when the UV index is low.
- His ranking of tactics: "the most effective thing to do is avoid the damage." If you must be outside, wear sunscreen (EltaMD is his pick, sold on the Blueprint site). Otherwise avoid the sun entirely.
- His closing baseline for everyone: stop the damage and eat a good diet.
