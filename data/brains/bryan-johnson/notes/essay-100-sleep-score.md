## Core principle: build life around sleep

Bryan calls sleep "the most powerful drug available – for performance, mental health, and well-being." He says it is the topic he gets asked about most. After years of struggling, he reports achieving the best sleep score in the world: "Eight months of perfect sleep."

- Sleep is treated as an afterthought in culture (after the movie, after work, "when you feel like it").
- He argues it deserves the same seriousness and devotion as work, because it "demands your best performance."
- His framing: build your life around sleep, not sleep around your life.

## Final meal timing and resting heart rate

Bryan's rule is to finish the last meal at least two hours before bed.

- Example: with a 10 PM bedtime, finish eating by 8 PM.
- To adapt, move the final meal back 30 minutes each day. By the end of the first week, the last meal lands around 5 PM.
- The purpose is to lower resting heart rate by giving the body time to digest and prepare for sleep.
- His claim: "The lower your resting heart rate, the better your sleep."

## Light: avoid blue light before bed

- An hour or two before bed, avoid blue light.
- Options: turn off screens, or remove blue light from them using apps and settings on computers and mobile devices.
- Replace it with red and amber lighting.
- He says light significantly affects readiness to fall asleep.

## Wind-down routine and separation from work

Bryan argues you can't finish work and put your head on the pillow and expect great sleep. You must calm body and mind, and create "separation from work."

- Options he lists: read a book, go for a walk, do a breathing exercise, take a bath, meditate, or journal.
- His own method is an internal dialogue where he splits himself into characters such as "Ambitious Bryan" and "Anxious Bryan."
  - Each part is allowed to voice the pressing things on its mind.
  - He writes them down and tells each part he hears it and will address it tomorrow, after a great night's sleep.
  - He finds it helpful to separate into parts and empathize with each one's concerns and objectives.

## Consistency of bedtime

- The body loves routine and rewards it with strong sleep performance.
- Go to bed within +/- 30 minutes of your chosen bedtime every single day.
- Build the habit and stay with it.

## Stimulants and caffeine timing

- Caffeine has a six-hour half-life. Example: coffee at 4 PM leaves about half a coffee in your system at a 10 PM bedtime.
- People metabolize caffeine differently, so the right cutoff is individual.
- His guidance: err on the side of caution and take stimulants earlier in the day.

## Mindset on sleep difficulty

- Bryan says "Sleep is hard work" and success takes time, like all things.
- His closing advice: invest in the habits and the body will perform.
