## Food-grade stainless steel for meals and pills

- Bryan eats from food-grade stainless steel containers rather than plastic, to avoid toxin worries.
- Their qualities: sturdy, keep food fresh, airtight seal, easy to clean, don't corrode.
- Meal planning: his chef makes three to four days of food at a time, puts it in the canisters, and he refrigerates them. This lets him plan meals on a weekly basis.
- Pill tins: he uses food-grade stainless steel pill tins for his supplements. They suit travel and his rotation, and keep things organized.
- The tins were discontinued by the maker and he has not yet found a replacement.
- His pill count is now around 35, down from about 100 at its peak.

## Air quality monitoring

- He did not realize before how important air quality is.
- He uses an IQ Air device that is quantified and certified. It shows indoor kitchen air quality and local outdoor air quality (Marina del Rey), cycling through screens of toxin levels.
- He gets a monthly report on indoor and outdoor air quality.

## Nervous system: vagus nerve stimulation and calming devices

- Framing: two modes, fight or flight (amped up) and chill. The goal is to spend more time in chill.
- Neurosym: worn on the left ear, attached to the tragus. It delivers a very light electrical pulse to stimulate the vagus nerve, which runs from the left side of the head down the neck and chest. Sessions can run 60 minutes.
- Sensate: a teardrop device placed on the middle of the chest, where the vagus nerve is also reachable. It follows a vibrational sequence synced to music.
- He uses Sensate when he wakes at night and can't fall back asleep, holding a pillow wrapped around it against his chest. He finds it calms him down.
- Other devices exist; he encourages experimenting with HRV to find what works for you.

## Measure everything: wearables, temperature and body composition

- He says Blueprint has become best in the world at "measure, measure, measure." People often do things without tracking whether they work.
- Wearables: Whoop, Oura and Apple Watch are all fine. He uses Whoop for its form factor, and says the choice is "honestly not a big deal."
- Wearables helped him build intuitions about what causes what. They helped him reach the best sleep score in human history, 8 months of perfect sleep.
- Body temperature: he measures it every day with an ear thermometer, carefully positioned. Over the past couple of years his temperature has dropped from 98.7 to 93.9, which he reads as his metabolic systems becoming more efficient.
- Body composition: he uses a Withings scale several times a week for weight, muscle, body fat and bone density. Any brand works.
- Purpose: long-term body composition data shows how weight and muscle respond to dietary and exercise changes, and gives a record to piece together how to avoid repeating past mistakes.
