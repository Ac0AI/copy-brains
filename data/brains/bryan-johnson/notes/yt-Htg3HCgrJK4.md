## Bryan's core framing: "Don't Die" and why he refuses to trust his own judgment

- Bryan argues every human is already playing one game: "don't die." People look both ways before crossing the street and install carbon monoxide detectors, so he takes the same logic to its limit.
- Blueprint's method: measure every biological process in his body, find where each cell is aging, identify every behavior that contributes, and eliminate it. He describes the aim as "no excuses ever for anything."
- He calls everyone a "self-deception machine," himself included. He does not trust himself around junk food, so he removed all self-harm options from his house rather than rely on willpower.
- His stated goal is to find "where I'm in error in thought and action constantly."
- He argues that people who are sleep deprived or living with bad habits are effectively inebriated, so their stated wants should not be trusted. He says he doesn't trust his own responses either.
- His argument for extreme measures now: superintelligence and the ability to engineer biology may be close, so trading years of life for cheap pleasure may mean missing the most remarkable existence in history. He says he is unconvinced by any argument against Don't Die.
- He frames current life-extension limits this way: a ceiling of about 120 years exists, and lifestyle alone will not break it. Punching through requires genetic-level work such as gene therapy.

## Sleep: the most important thing a human does each day

- Bryan calls sleep "the single most important thing any human does on any given day."
- He completed six months of 100% sleep scores, as judged by his Whoop. He says no one had previously shared data showing a run like that, and many long-term wearable users never hit 100% once.
- He framed it as a demonstration of human ability, like the four-minute mile: once one person shows it, others know it is possible.
- His schedule: bed around 8:30 pm, last meal before midday (about 11:00 am), no meetings before 11:00 am.
- He argues the "sleep under your desk" entrepreneur mythology is backwards. Sleep-deprived leaders are impaired to a degree comparable to alcohol intoxication, yet make decisions for large groups.
- On social friction: prioritizing sleep means turning down drinks, adjusting meal timing and bedtime, and going against social norms. He says every person who brave enough to leave early makes it easier for others in the group to do the same.
- For parents, he says a sleep culture can be set: once the child is put to bed, the expectation is that they stay in their room. He gives a fire or a real threat as the only valid reasons to leave it, not a lost toy or blanket.
- Kate reported a 19% increase in restorative sleep in her first 30 days.

## Measuring your speed of aging and key baseline numbers

- Bryan says everyone should know three things: how much they weigh, how fast they are aging, and the duration of their nighttime erections.
- Speed of aging test: a finger-prick blood card is mailed to a lab, which reads chemical signatures to produce an aging-clock result. At a speed of 1, you age at a normal rate. At 0.6, "you would get September, October, November and December for free."
- His suggested levers for slowing aging: olive oil, good sleep, exercise, a good diet, and not smoking.
- He argues that false advertising is rampant in longevity. Blueprint's website lists three years of effort to find what has scientific evidence, test it on himself, measure it, and then communicate it.

## NAD+: why IV drips do not work and what does

- Bryan says NAD+ drips don't work because you need sustained intracellular NAD levels, and drips don't provide that. He also notes half-life as a factor.
- What he uses: oral NMN or NR, twice daily. He tested both along with other modalities.
- Result: his intracellular NAD levels were equivalent to about age 47 when he started and are now reliably at an age-18 level. He dialed in the dose by measuring.
- His rule: if you don't measure it in your body, treat it as story, marketing and "what your friends are doing."

## Nighttime erections, shockwave therapy and hair loss protocol

- Bryan treats nighttime erection duration as a meaningful indicator of psychological, sexual and cardiovascular health. He measures it with a wearable ring device worn on the penis during sleep (typically 3-5 nights in a row, for a month or two at a time).
- His average: 2 hours 12 minutes per night, roughly equal to his chronological age in minutes. His target for 18-year-old levels: 3 hours 30 minutes.
- Intervention: focused shockwave therapy applied with an acoustic wand, which creates micro-injuries the tissue then rebuilds. He says it is used for ACL, joints, knees and shoulders and also for erectile dysfunction.
- Experience: roughly 7 out of 10 pain, up to 9 out of 10 at the tip. After two months he reports subjectively feeling about "15 years younger," plus improved erection strength and orgasm pleasure, but says he needs data before believing it.
- Hair protocol (all published on his site):
  - Platelet-rich plasma every 30 to 60 days: plasma plus a cell product and dutasteride, 13-15 ml total, injected across areas that would be balding.
  - Daily red light therapy cap for 6 minutes.
  - Nightly topical concoction including minoxidil.
- He started losing hair in his early 30s and says treatments only slow the process; he points to follicle cloning as a promising emerging technology.

## Gene therapy, muscle stimulation and other interventions

- Follistatin gene therapy: two injections in his obliques. It does not alter his genes but increases follistatin expression. Follistatin suppresses myostatin, which limits muscle growth. His levels went from roughly 8-9 to a target in the 20s-40s. He tracks follistatin in blood and uses MRI across muscle, fat, bone, speed of aging and DNA methylation. First results were due the following week and another gene therapy was planned.
- Electromagnetic muscle stimulation on his abs: he says MRI showed muscle being broken down and regenerated.
- Air quality: he runs monitors measuring indoor air in every room and outdoor air quality, with air filters in every room. On bad Los Angeles air days he avoids significant outdoor activity because fine particulates (PM2.5) lodge in the lungs and are hard to clear.
- Kernel: his brain-measurement helmet. He ran a 15-person ketamine pilot, measuring his brain 10 minutes a day for 5 days before, during and 14 days after. He saw his brain activity patterns scramble and then reform around days 3-4, suggesting a one-to-three-day window of openness to new patterns.

## Lessons from Kate's 90 days on Blueprint

- Kate, a 27-year-old, was the first woman to follow the protocol. Bryan said she did a 30-day trial first and he told her there was no pressure and that it was entirely her decision.
- Her protocol: 100% sleep every night, perfect nutrition on the same ~1,700 kcal meals as Bryan (as stated by Kate), over 60 supplements daily, and a weekly cardio and strength target.
- Results reported in 30 days: V2 max moved from the 51st percentile to the top 7% for her age and gender; leg press one-rep max rose from 220 lb to 360 lb.
- Her baseline: fitness-test age around 60-70, with low vitamin D and zinc and high oxidized LDL despite looking healthy.
- Bryan's takeaway: the protocol exposed her self-destructive patterns (people-pleasing, back-to-back meetings, McDonald's at 10 pm). He praised her for being transparent and for not missing a day.
- Bryan's sleep-and-data rule shown in her case: when HRV and recovery dropped, the data said rest, but her conscious mind pushed to hit heart rate targets for public image.
- Women's protocol is still being tuned for hormones and tracked data; Bryan said his own protocol took two to three years and millions of dollars to stabilize.
