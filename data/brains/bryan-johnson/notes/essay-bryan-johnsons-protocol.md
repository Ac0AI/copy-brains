## Core thesis: lower resting heart rate before bed

- Bryan's answer to "what is the one thing I can do?" is to lower resting heart rate (RHR) before bed. It is free and within anyone's power.
- His reasoning: "sleep is the best performance enhancing drug in the world." A low RHR gives better sleep, which makes exercise and good eating more likely. A high RHR gives poor sleep, which "destroys your self control," a vicious cycle.
- Stopping eating around noon gets his RHR to about 39 bpm before bed, which he says gives a perfect night. If RHR is 56 because he ate an hour before bed, he says sleep quality is roughly 30-40% worse.
- How to lower RHR before bed:
  - Final meal or snack 4 hours before bed.
  - Screens off 60 minutes before bed.
  - Read a book about 10 minutes before bed. In the last hour, do calming things: hobby, podcast, walk, journal, meditate, breathwork, call a friend.
  - Watch stimulants. Caffeine has a 6-hour half-life, so a 4 pm coffee leaves half a cup in the body at 10 pm.
  - Red and amber light in the evening (f.lux, blue-light-blocking glasses, red mode on the phone).
  - "No fights after 5 pm." Fight in the morning when well rested.

## Sleep protocol: 10 habits

- Bryan reports eight months of 100% perfect sleep. He says getting in bed at 8:30 pm and asleep within 3 minutes is the result of years of building habits.
- Sleep-deprivation figures he cites:
  - 18 or 24 hours awake is equivalent to a blood alcohol content of 0.05% or 0.1%.
  - 6 hours of sleep or less makes you 4.2x more likely to develop a cold.
  - One night of sleep deprivation raised S100-B by 20%, similar to a traumatic brain injury.
  - One night of 4 hours' sleep cut natural killer cell activity by 70% in 18 of 23 participants. One normal night restored it.
- The 10 habits:
  1. Reframe your identity as a professional sleeper. Make sleep the top priority and plan the day around it.
  2. 30-60 minute wind-down routine (reading, warm bath, breathing, soothing music).
  3. Morning light within 15-30 minutes of waking, or a 10,000 lux device.
  4. Dim lights 1-2 hours before bed, use warm (red is best) light, install blue-light filters, limit screens for an hour, black out the bedroom.
  5. Bedroom at 65-68°F (18-20°C). Breathable bedding, fans, a warm bath beforehand, or a temperature-controlled mattress (Eight Sleep).
  6. Consistent bedtime allowing 7-9 hours in bed, including weekends. Set an alarm for winding down.
  7. Quiet, dark, cool environment, comfortable mattress and pillows (he uses a copper pillowcase), noise machine or earplugs.
  8. Last meal at least 2 hours before bed, and experiment with 4 or 6 hours. Late eating causes metabolic load and glucose swings, reduces melatonin and disrupts the drop in core temperature.
  9. Avoid caffeine, alcohol and stimulants 8-10 hours before sleep. Choose herbal tea in the evening.
  10. Track sleep with a journal or tracker and adjust.

## Five habits and the "Evening Bryan" method for bad habits

- The five habits:
  - Sleep, which he calls the world's #1 longevity drug.
  - Nutrition: "every calorie must fight for its life." Build systems so the mind never makes eating decisions.
  - Exercise: 6 hours a week is ideal, or even 20 minutes a day if that is all you can do.
  - Family, friendship and community.
  - Avoid fast food, junk food, smoking, vaping, excessive alcohol, social media and anything addictive. "Addiction makes you a prisoner."
- His story: at 42 he stress-ate every day at 7 pm and was 60 lbs overweight. He "fired" Evening Bryan.
- The method:
  1. Name the version of you that does the self-destructive behavior.
  2. Write down the persuasion tactics it uses ("tonight is the last night," "tomorrow we'll burn it off").
  3. Prepare rebuttals ("this is not what we really want," "we know this makes us sad and regretful").
  4. Give authority to the version that should be in charge (Morning you).
  5. Make firm rules. "None is easier than some."

## Exercise protocol

- Target: 6 hours a week.
  - 3 strength sessions.
  - 3 cardio sessions: 150 minutes of Zone 2 (can hold a conversation) and 75 minutes vigorous (HIIT, cannot converse).
  - Include flexibility, balance and mobility.
- Tips:
  - Avoid injury, even if it means skipping certain things.
  - Make it a daily habit with no decision to be made.
  - Move 5-10 minutes after each meal, and get up every 30 minutes at work for 2-3 minutes of light activity.
  - Don't get paralyzed by nuances.
- Premenopausal women: prioritize high intensity and strength in the follicular phase. In the luteal phase use Zone 2 and lighter strength.
- Postmenopausal women: prioritize strength and HIIT for muscle, insulin sensitivity and bone density, plus weight-bearing exercise.
- Evidence he cites:
  - Regular activity reduces all-cause mortality 26-31% and cardiovascular disease 28-38%.
  - The weakest third for muscular strength had about 20-35% higher risk of premature death in a ~1.1 million Swedish male cohort.
  - Combining moderate and vigorous activity gives nearly the maximum mortality reduction (35-42%).
  - Exercising 2-4x the minimum vigorous amount (150-299 minutes) is linked to 21-23% lower all-cause mortality.

## Sauna, with fertility protection

- Protocol: dry sauna at 200°F (93°C), daily, 20 minutes, with an ice pack on the groin the whole session. He rehydrates with 36 oz of mineral water.
- He cites Finnish studies showing 63% lower cardiovascular mortality and 40% lower all-cause mortality.
- His results:
  - After 15 sessions, large drops in measured toxins: 2,4-D down 65%, MEP, MEHP and perchlorate to undetectable.
  - After 23 sessions, central pulse pressure down 28.6%, augmentation pressure down 50%, RHR down about 2 bpm.
- Fertility:
  - 15 sessions without ice: motile count down 54%, motility down 57%, normal morphology down 55%.
  - 27 sessions with ice: total motile count up 57%, concentration up 26%, motility up 16%, morphology up 15%.
- Ice method: reusable non-toxic ice packs between cotton boxers and shorts, kept in place for the whole session.
- Tips for others:
  - 3-5 sessions a week of 15-20 minutes at 175-194°F (80-90°C), ideally after workouts.
  - Hydrate well, and cover the head with a damp cloth above 194°F.
  - Skip if you have heart issues, are pregnant, ill, have a seizure history, asthma or COPD, or have taken alcohol or certain medications.
- He argues dry Finnish sauna beats steam and infrared because it heats skin faster than the core. This gives up to 70% more blood pumped, 0.6-1 L of sweat per hour and heat-shock protein activation with only about a 1°C core rise.

## HBOT, red light and shockwave

- He calls hyperbaric oxygen "one of the highest value health therapies I've done."
- Protocol: 100% oxygen at 2 atmospheres, 60 sessions, 5 per week, 90 minutes each (20 minutes on, 5 minute air break, repeat). He uses an HPO Tech hard-shell chamber with oxygen through a face mask. He warns against chambers with 100% ambient oxygen: eye irritation and cataracts, higher ignition risk, and harder emergency stops.
- Results after 60 sessions:
  - hsCRP below detectable levels.
  - Telomere length up 2.6% (10.3 to 11.4 kb).
  - Short-chain fatty acids up 250% and n-butyrate up 290%.
  - VEGF up 300%.
  - Vitamin D up 235%.
  - pTAU217 down 28.6%.
- Red/NIR light: 2 x 6 minutes daily.
- Shockwave: 4500 shocks, 3 days a week, level 14, on the cervical spine, shoulders, ankles, elbows, forearms, lumbar spine, hips and glutes, and knees.

## Female protocol, pregnancy, water and measurement

- Follicular phase (day 1 to ovulation):
  - High intensity and strength training, train more often.
  - Higher carbs, protein at 1.5 g/kg.
  - Standard stack plus calcium 1200 mg.
- Luteal phase:
  - Zone 2 and easier strength training, with protein at 2.0 g/kg, more fats and fewer carbs.
  - Sodium 200-400 mg, potassium 200-400 mg, magnesium 100-200 mg.
  - Ashwagandha 300-600 mg, Rhodiola 200-400 mg, melatonin 1-3 mg.
  - Light training in the late luteal phase.
- Ovulation signs: a 0.5-1.0 degree basal temperature rise, and egg-white cervical mucus.
- Perimenopause and menopause: strength and HIIT, protein 2.0 g/kg, calcium 1200 mg, magnesium 100-200 mg as needed.
- Pregnancy:
  - Avoid Longevity Mix, ashwagandha, curcumin, red yeast rice, NR, CA-AKG, glucosamine, creatine, hyaluronate and NAC.
  - Consider prenatal vitamin (at least 600 mcg methylfolate), magnesium 500-600 mg, iron 30 mg, calcium 1200 mg, omega-3 1000-2000 mg.
  - At least 100 g protein and 30 g fiber daily, caffeine at most 200 mg.
  - 150 minutes of moderate exercise weekly.
- Water: a ~$1,300 filtration system (carbon, reverse osmosis, deionization, remineralization, coconut carbon), with cheaper ~$300 options. He tests water with SimpleLab.
- Routine measurement:
  - Blood draw every 3-6 months.
  - Annual full-body MRI if over 40 or high family risk.
  - Annual mole check and eye doctor, dentist every 6 months.
  - Continuous glucose monitor.
  - Monthly breast or testicular self-exam.
