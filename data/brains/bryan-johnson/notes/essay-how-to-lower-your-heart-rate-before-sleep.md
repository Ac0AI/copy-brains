## Heart rate before sleep (HRBS) as Bryan's top health metric

Bryan's #1 health tip is to lower heart rate before sleep (HRBS): "I've spent millions of dollars developing health protocols backed by science. I do dozens of things a day for my health. But if you're going to focus on one thing, it would be this." He calls it "the most consequential health lesson to learn," and says it can be improved immediately and for free.

- HRBS is the number of heartbeats per minute while lying quietly right before sleep.
- He treats it as a single-number biomarker of overall health. It reveals fitness, metabolic health, food habits and stress management.
- After five years of health experimentation, it is the marker he focuses on most, because it "mirrors how I'm doing in life generally."
- He also treats it as a longevity therapy in its own right, not just a measurement.

## Why a low HRBS matters

- A lower heart rate reflects activation of the parasympathetic ("rest and digest") system, which promotes relaxation and sleep onset.
- Lower HRBS makes it more likely to fall asleep faster and get deeper, higher-quality sleep.
- Example he gives: after a large meal before bed, heart rate stays elevated as the body diverts energy to digestion, making it harder to fall and stay asleep.
- He calls sleep "the #1 longevity therapy." Poor sleep is linked to:
  - higher risk of death
  - increased hunger and weight gain
  - increased risk of hypertension
  - irritability and impaired judgement
  - lower functional IQ by 12-15 points
- His personal data point: resting heart rate before bed is the strongest predictor of his sleep quality. At 40 bpm he is "guaranteed a perfect night's sleep." At 57 bpm he loses about 30% of sleep quality, "every time."

## How to measure HRBS

- Lie flat on your back and relax for sixty seconds. He finds a few deep breaths helpful to calm the body.
- With a wearable, check the app.
- Without one, count your pulse for 15 seconds and multiply by 4 (e.g. 70 bpm).

## What counts as a good score

- Around 50 bpm is a "fantastic" HRBS. It reflects an efficient heart and a nervous system in "rest" mode, common in aerobically fit people and athletes.
- It can signal good stroke volume, higher vagal tone and better cardiorespiratory fitness.
- His own HRBS usually hovers between 39-44 bpm.
- If yours is in the 60s, 70s, 80s or 90s, he says meaningful progress can come very quickly. Measure baseline tonight, then lower it gradually.
- Safety notes he gives: if the score is consistently below 40 or above 80, especially with symptoms, check with a physician. Seek care for dizziness or fainting. Women typically run about 3 bpm higher.

## The general HRBS protocol

Bryan notes his own protocol is "over-the-top for most people" because he is intensely focused on longevity, so he recommends this baseline for others:

- Finish all food about 4 hours before bed (earlier is better).
- Avoid heavy or large foods at the last meals (e.g. pasta).
- Avoid alcohol entirely.
- Avoid blue light, including screens, at least 1 hour before bed (screens off 60 minutes before).
- Avoid stress close to bed (fights, working late).
- Finish all caffeine or stimulants by 12pm.
- Finish high-intensity exercise 4 hours before bed.
- Have a calming routine 1 hour before bed (read, walk, journal).
- Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet.
- Keep the same bedtime every night.

## What raises resting heart rate before bed

Bryan lists approximate increases in bpm:

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Alcohol before bedtime | +5-10 bpm |
| Anxiety, stress, rumination | +5-25 bpm |
| Late-day caffeine | +2-5 bpm |
| Intense evening exercise | +4-10 bpm |
| Large evening meals | +10 bpm |
| Nicotine | +10 bpm |
| Dehydration | +5 bpm |
| High room or body temperature | +5 bpm |
| Certain medications | +7 bpm |

## Bryan's extra personal tactics

- Slight caloric restriction at 2,250 calories daily.
- Finishes all food by 12pm.
- Uses only red light in the house at night.

## Results, compounding and adoption

- Results come quickly because the body responds almost immediately, and the benefits scaffold: a lower HRBS tonight means better sleep tomorrow, which means a lower heart rate tomorrow night.
- He describes it as "simple, measurable, and entirely within your control." Friends tell him it has changed their life.
- He admits he is asking a lot, such as turning off screens and eating earlier in a culture that eats late. His promise: "You will feel more vibrant and energetic."
- Social tactic: at events where you need to fit in, eat lightly. He advises small daily steps, and argues individual behavior can shift culture toward a new norm.
