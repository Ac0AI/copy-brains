## Exercise priorities and weekly structure

Bryan says no single exercise is best; a mix is. Each week he focuses on four categories:
- strength training
- low-intensity cardio (zone 2 heart rate)
- high-intensity cardio (zone 4 or 5)
- mobility, flexibility, and balance

He cites a study linking 11 minutes of daily exercise to a 23% lower risk of early death. His message: a small daily habit produces meaningful benefits, and consistency matters more than going all out once a month. "Even 10 minutes a day is a big win." He also says: "Don't get caught up in all the nuances and rabbit holes of exercise."

## Ground rules for training

- **Strength:** aim for 2-3 sessions a week using compound movements (squats, deadlifts, lunges). If three is too much, even one session is a win.
- **Progressive overload:** gradually raise the weight or reps over time. Consistency takes you a long way.
- **HIIT as best return on time:** 4-6 reps of 30-second all-out efforts with rest between, 3 times a week, can match the cardiovascular gains of 5 weekly sessions of 40-60 minutes of steady cardio. He notes cardiorespiratory fitness is one of the strongest predictors of lifespan, and that intervals are the best trade when time is tight.
- **Timing:** avoid high-intensity exercise within 4 hours of bed, since research suggests it reduces sleep quality and recovery.

## Bryan's own weekly targets

He does a mix of strength, cardio, balance, and flexibility every day, aiming for:
- at least 150 minutes of zone 2 cardio per week
- at least 75 minutes of high-intensity cardio per week (about 10 minutes of that at near-max effort)
- mobility, flexibility, and balance training every day

## Sample week: strength days (Monday, Wednesday, Friday)

**Monday (~60 min, strength plus moderate cardio):**
- 5 min general warm-up, 5 min dynamic warm-up
- 20 min strength: squats 3x10-15, push-ups 3x8-12, single-arm dumbbell rows 3x10-12, kettlebell swings and/or farmer's walks 3x30 seconds, planks 3x20-30 seconds
- 5-10 min stability work (single-leg balance, bird dog, single-leg RDL, dead bugs, plank with shoulder taps)
- 25 min moderate cardio (brisk walking, jogging, cycling, swimming, elliptical)

**Wednesday (60 min, strength, flexibility, balance, mobility):**
- 10 min dynamic stretching
- 30 min compound strength targeting areas not sore from Monday
- 15 min yoga (cat-cow, cobbler's pose, tree pose, cobra pose)
- 5 min static stretching cooldown

**Friday (45-60 min, strength):**
- 5 min general plus 5 min dynamic warm-up
- lunges 3x10, overhead press 3x10, dumbbell chest press 3x10, side plank 20-30 seconds each side, step-ups 3x10 each leg
- 5-10 min stability work

## Sample week: HIIT days (Tuesday, Thursday)

**Tuesday (~10 min HIIT plus moderate cardio):**
- 8 rounds of 20 seconds all-out, 20 seconds rest or very low intensity (40 seconds rest for beginners), about 5 minutes total
- run, cycle, or use bodyweight exercises
- fill remaining time with moderate cardio at 60-70% of max heart rate, a pace where you can hold a conversation but still feel the effort

**Thursday (~40 min, Norwegian 4x4):**
- 10 min warm-up building to a light sweat
- 4 rounds of 4 min hard at about 85-95% of max heart rate (zone 4-5, unable to hold a conversation by the last minute), then 3 min easy at zone 2
- 5 min cooldown with a few static stretches
- use joint-friendly modes: cycling, rowing, incline treadmill walking, stair climber

Pointers: the hard 4 minutes should feel "uncomfortable but controlled, not an all-out sprint," paced so the fourth interval matches the first. If four rounds is too much, start with two or three. Finish at least 4 hours before bed.

## Sample week: weekend

- **Saturday:** play with friends (hiking, biking, swimming, pickleball with caution, basketball).
- **Sunday (35-50 min active recovery):** 20-30 min gentle yoga or stretching with extra mobility work, plus 15-20 min mindfulness meditation.

He says you could instead do some strength and cardio every day, or spread it out: "Do what works for you."

## Pre- and post-workout nutrition

General guidance: eat 1-2 hours before exercise (complex carbs plus some protein), and eat complex carbs plus protein ideally within 1 hour after.

**Bryan's pre-workout:**
- an 8-oz drink with Longevity Mix and Creatine
- Longevity Protein eaten with Blueberry Nut Mix, Collagen Peptides, extra virgin olive oil, and berries
- then he exercises

**Bryan's post-workout:** a meal with veggies, legumes, extra virgin olive oil, berries, nuts, and seeds.
