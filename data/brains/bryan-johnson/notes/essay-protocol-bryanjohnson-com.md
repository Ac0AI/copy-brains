## Sleep and resting heart rate as the foundation

Bryan says the single most effective free thing for health is to lower resting heart rate (RHR) before bed. His logic: low RHR gives better sleep, better sleep makes exercise and eating well more likely, and poor sleep "destroys your self control." He calls sleep "the best performance enhancing drug in the world."

- Approximate RHR increases he lists: alcohol before bed 5-10 bpm; anxiety, stress, rumination 5-25 bpm; late-day caffeine 2-5 bpm; intense evening exercise 4-10 bpm; large evening meals 5-15 bpm; nicotine 5-15 bpm; dehydration 3-8 bpm; hot bedroom or body 3-8 bpm; certain meds such as ADHD stimulants 3-10 bpm.
- He stops eating around noon, which gets his RHR to about 39 bpm before bed and gives a "perfect night's sleep." An RHR of 56 from eating an hour before bed makes his sleep quality about 30-40% worse.
- Wearable-reported result: eight months of 100% perfect sleep.
- He frames himself as a "professional sleeper" and treats the sleep appointment as the most important item on his calendar.

## Ten sleep habits

1. Make sleep the #1 priority and plan the day around it.
2. Final meal four hours before bed (lower RHR, fewer glucose swings, protects melatonin and the nighttime drop in core temperature).
3. Screens off 60 minutes before bed.
4. 60-minute wind-down: walk, journaling, breathwork, meditation, family time, calling a friend, bath, or a book. Reading is his favorite and, he says, "as effective a sleep med as actual sleep drugs." No work, texting, partying or fighting.
5. Consistent bedtime. He is in bed at 8:30 pm and wakes around 4:30-5 am; others should pick any time and stick to it. "No fights after 5 pm."
6. Red/amber light in the evening (red bulb lamp, blue-light-blocking glasses, f.lux, phone red mode).
7. Bedroom quiet, dark and 65-70°F (18-21°C); noise machine or earplugs; a temperature-controlled mattress such as Eight Sleep.
8. No caffeine, alcohol or other stimulants for 8-10 hours before bed. Caffeine half-life is about 6 hours, so a 4 pm coffee leaves about half a cup at 10 pm.
9. Morning light within 15-30 minutes of waking, or a 10,000 lux light device (he uses it 3-4 minutes before sunrise).
10. Track sleep with a journal or wearable and adjust.

Cited sleep-loss figures: 18 and 24 hours awake are like 0.05% and 0.1% blood alcohol; 6 hours or less of sleep a night means a 4.2x higher chance of catching a cold; one night of 4 hours cut natural killer cell activity by 70% in 18 of 23 participants, restored after one normal night.

## Breaking bad habits ("Evening Bryan, you're fired")

He used to overeat every day at 7 pm and was about 60 lbs overweight. His method:

1. Name the version of you that does the self-destructive thing (e.g. "Evening Bryan").
2. Write down that persona's persuasion lines ("tonight is the last night", "tomorrow we'll burn it off").
3. Prepare rebuttals ("this is not what we really want", "this makes us sad and regretful").
4. Give authority to a better version (e.g. Morning you) to decide for the evening version.
5. Set firm rules. "None is easier than some."

He revoked Evening Bryan's authority to eat between 5 pm and 10 pm.

## Exercise protocol

- Target 6 hours a week: 3 strength sessions plus 3 cardio sessions, with flexibility, balance and mobility. Cardio goal is 150 minutes of Zone 2 (can hold a conversation) and 75 minutes of vigorous work (HIIT).
- If 6 hours isn't possible, 20 minutes a day is still great.
- Tips: avoid injury above all; make it a daily habit with no decision; move 5-10 minutes after each meal; get up every 30 minutes (he does 2-3 minutes of light activity); don't get lost in exercise nuance.
- His own session is 60-90 minutes starting about 6 am, covering balance, flexibility, strength and cardio, with weekend hiking, pickleball, cycling or climbing.
- Premenopausal women: high-intensity and strength training in the follicular phase (estrogen helps performance and recovery); Zone 2 and lighter strength in the luteal phase. Postmenopausal women: prioritize strength training and HIIT plus weight-bearing work for muscle, insulin sensitivity and bone density.
- Evidence he cites: regular activity lowers all-cause mortality 26-31% and cardiovascular disease 28-38%; combining moderate and vigorous activity gives about 35-42% mortality reduction; weakest-third muscular strength in Swedish adolescent males had roughly 20-35% higher premature death risk.

Philosophy: "every calorie must fight for its life." He rejects camps (plant-based, carnivore, keto, paleo) and follows evidence and his own biomarkers.

- Daily macros: 2,250 calories (10% caloric restriction), protein 130 g (~25%), carbs 206 g (~35%), fat 101 g (~40%).
- All liquids are mineralized (tea, electrolytes, Longevity Mix) and he stops fluids at 4 pm to avoid waking to urinate.

## Skin protocol

His skin started at the 98th percentile for damage. He reports age 44 with skin age 40, then age 48 with skin age 39, about a 9-year reversal, measured with Canfield's VISIA multispectral imaging.

- Basics: sunlight early morning and late afternoon; avoid direct sun when UV is high (10 am-4 pm); use clothing, a hat, a UV umbrella or mineral sunscreen; UV-tinted windows (blocks 99% UV); prioritize sleep; avoid junk and fried food.
- Collagen peptides 20-30 g daily paired with vitamin C; niacinamide, polyphenols, antioxidants and omega-3.
- Daily topicals: Blueprint cleanser, serum and moisturizer plus tretinoin 0.025-0.1%.
- Red light: 6-minute full body sessions.
- Procedures, each about once every 6 months: 1927 nm laser (tone, texture, sun damage), 1550 nm laser (collagen and elastin), Sofwave (ultrasound tightening), Everesse (6.78 MHz monopolar RF lifting).

## Hair and oral care

**Hair** (he says he is genetically predisposed to baldness, started thinning and graying in his late 20s, and at 46 has a full head of hair):
- Start before you notice loss; adequate protein, omega-3, biotin, vitamins C, D, E, iron and zinc.
- Blueprint peptide shampoo as a scalp treatment after regular shampoo: 1-2 pumps, lather, leave 2 minutes, rinse. Then daily peptide serum on a dry scalp (one dropper), optionally with the 655 nm red light cap for 6 minutes.
- Nightly topical 5% minoxidil plus other ingredients (1 mL), massaged with a silicone scrubber for 1-2 minutes.
- Oral minoxidil 3.75 mg a day (started at 2.5 mg). He cites a 90-man trial where 5 mg oral matched 5% topical twice daily at 24 weeks; side effects include excess hair growth and headaches.

**Oral care:** Waterpik on waking and at night, floss, soft electric brush, tongue scraper (morning and night), dentist twice a year. He wears a SomnoDent device and says it stopped 20 years of nighttime grinding. He used tea tree oil (2-3 drops in 2 oz water) earlier but no longer needs it. Reduce sugar (trehalose, monk fruit, xylitol, allulose), limit long exposure to acidic drinks like coffee and lemon water, and stay hydrated.

## Sauna, light and shockwave therapy

- Finnish studies cited: dry sauna at 175-212°F linked to 63% lower cardiovascular mortality and 40% lower all-cause mortality.
- His protocol: dry sauna at 175°F (93°C), daily, 20 minutes, then 36 oz of mineralized water. He puts an ice pack on the groin throughout.
- Tips for others: 3-5 sessions a week of 15-20 minutes at 175-194°F; good after workouts; never when dehydrated; skip it with serious heart issues, pregnancy, fever, seizures, respiratory conditions, alcohol use, or beta-blockers, stimulants, anticholinergics or diuretics.
- Results he reports: after 15 sessions, 100% drops in MEP, MEHP and perchlorate (undetectable), 65% drop in 2,4-D and 56% drops in NAPR and HEMA. At 23 sessions, central blood pressure fell 12.6% and augmentation pressure 50%.
- Fertility: with ice, after 27 sessions total motile count rose 57%, concentration 26%, motility 16%, morphology 15%. Without ice over 15 sessions, motile count dropped 54%, motility 57% and normal morphology 55%.
- Why dry sauna: it heats skin faster than core, with heart output up to 70% higher, 0.6-1 L of sweat per hour and heat shock protein activation.
- Red and near infrared light: 6 minutes twice daily. Shockwave: 4500 shocks, 3 days a week at level 14, across spine, shoulders, ankles, elbows, forearms, hips and glutes, and knees.

## Hyperbaric oxygen (HBOT)

He calls HBOT "one of the highest value health therapies I've done."

- Protocol: 100% oxygen at 2 atmospheres, 60 sessions at 5 a week, 90 minutes (20 minutes oxygen, 5 minute air break, repeated). He uses an HPO Tech hard-shell chamber with oxygen through a face mask and warns against chambers filled with 100% ambient oxygen (eye irritation and cataracts, higher ignition risk, harder to stop a session).
- Results after 60 sessions: hsCRP below detectable levels; telomere length up 2.6% (10.3 to 11.4 kb); short-chain fatty acids up 250% and n-butyrate up 290%; VEGF up 300%; vitamin D up 235% with no supplement change; pTAU217 down 28.6%.
- Daily schedule has either HBOT (60 or 90 min) or IHHT (42 min) at about 9 am.

- Perimenopause and menopause: strength and HIIT, protein 2.0 g/kg, calcium 1200 mg.
- Pregnancy: avoid several stack items (Longevity Mix, ashwagandha, curcumin, red yeast rice, NR, CA-AKG, creatine, NAC and others); add prenatal vitamin, magnesium, iron 30 mg, calcium 1200 mg, DHA/EPA 1000-2000 mg; at least 100 g protein and 30 g fiber daily; caffeine under 200 mg.
