## Shoes off and indoor air quality

- Bryan takes his shoes off at the door. Shoes carry in lead, asbestos, pesticides, feces, metals and microplastics, which are then far more likely to get into the body.
- He runs 2 monitors. One tracks CO2, PM1 (ultrafine particles that can enter the bloodstream), PM2.5 (reaches deep into the lungs) and PM10 (coarse dust). The other shows indoor and outdoor AQI.
- The monitors show him whether his air purifiers and HVAC filter are working.
- Quick win: a portable HEPA air purifier in the bedroom, running while he sleeps.

## HVAC filter and safety sensors

- He treats the HVAC system as the first line of defense for air quality. He upgrades the filter and cleans it regularly.
- MERV rates an air filter's particle capture. A MERV 8 filter captures ~30% of PM2.5.
- He goes as high as the system is rated and no higher, because exceeding the rating can cause problems. His system handles MERV 13.
- With an air monitor, the effect of a better filter shows up within minutes.
- Smoke alarms: 3 in 5 home-fire deaths involve dead or missing alarms. He says to press the test button once a month.
- He recommends a carbon monoxide detector (CO kills 400 Americans a year).
- He recommends a radon test. Radon is an invisible, odorless gas from the earth and the second-most prominent cause of lung cancer after smoking.

## Water: reverse osmosis

- Tap water can contain heavy metals, PFAS, microplastics, pesticides, volatile organic compounds and pharmaceutical residues.
- Bryan uses a reverse osmosis system. Tap water has over 300 mg/L of dissolved solids; with RO it drops to 25 mg/L.
- He says the RO system should include a remineralization step for electrolyte balance.
- He cleans or replaces filters every few months, or as the filtration company recommends.

## Plastics, cookware and the stove

- He has spent months removing plastics from his house and still occasionally finds more.
- Swaps: wooden chopping boards, stainless steel utensils, glass storage containers.
- Non-stick pans contain PFAS ("forever chemicals"), which can last up to decades in the body. He uses stainless steel or cast iron instead.
- Gas stoves are linked to increased childhood asthma risk. Even 1 gas burner spikes nitrogen dioxide and fine particles.
- Options: replace with an induction stove, use a ducted vent hood, or cook on the stove less often.

## Cleaning products and carpets

- Scented sprays and air fresheners release volatile organic compounds, which react with oxygen to form formaldehyde, a known carcinogen.
- His rules for cleaning products:
  - Avoid phthalates, ammonia, quat, chlorine bleach and formaldehyde.
  - Look for EPA Safer Choice, EWG Verified or Green Seal certifications.
  - Avoid scented sprays and air fresheners.
  - Open the windows when cleaning.
- Carpet holds 10x the dust, allergens and microbes of hard floors. With carpet, he says to use a HEPA vacuum 2x a week.
- A good HEPA filter removes 99.97% of microplastic particles.

## UV from windows

- Non-tinted glass lets ~37-73% of UVA through. UVA contributes to skin aging and skin cancer risk.
- Bryan has window film that filters 99% of UVA and UVB.
- He is not anti-sun; he wants controlled exposure, and all-day sun through windows can exceed it.
- Cheaper alternatives: close blinds during high-UV hours (10am-4pm) or move to a room without direct sun.
- His principle: "Be sun smart in your home like you would be outside."

## Evening and bedroom environment for sleep

- Red light: blue light lowers melatonin and makes falling asleep harder. Bryan uses a ~$30 red lamp in the evening, dim, ~2 hours before bed.
- Darkness: even 10 lux (very dim street lighting) can halve melatonin production and fragment sleep. In his testing, a blacked-out bedroom substantially improves sleep quality. Options are blackout blinds or curtains, or an eyemask.
- Noise: when he lived next to a busy road, a noise machine smoothed out the traffic noise and improved his sleep quality.
- He suggests white noise (TV static), brown noise (rumbling thunder) or pink noise (wind), and says pink is best because it is less high-pitched than white.
- He advises measuring your sleep to see whether it works for you.

## Mindset on home toxins

- Bryan says to avoid feeling anxious about toxins: "They are everywhere… but you can remove the big ones."
- He says to be patient. It took him 5 years to improve his home environment, and he advises baby steps.
- Some steps are quick and free, such as shoes off at the door and closing blinds at peak UV.
