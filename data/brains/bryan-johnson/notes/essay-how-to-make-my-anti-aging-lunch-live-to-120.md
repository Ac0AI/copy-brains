## Super Veggie: what it is and why Bryan eats it

- Bryan eats "Super Veggie" every day as his lunch and says "I love it."
- He frames the food as delicious to him and "one of the most exciting moments of my day."
- He notes that newcomers may need a period of adaptation, and some will want to tweak the recipe or texture.
- He adds that Blueprint "breaks all kinds of social norms," which predictably triggers human reactions.

## Super Veggie ingredients and quantities

- Black lentils: 45 g dry, about 150 g cooked
- Broccoli (head and stalk): 250 g (broccoli sprouts can substitute)
- Cauliflower: 150 g
- Shiitake or maitake mushrooms: 50 g
- Garlic: 1 clove
- Ginger root: 3 g
- Lime: 1
- Cumin: 1 Tbsp
- Apple cider vinegar: 1 Tbsp
- Hemp seeds: 1 Tbsp
- Extra virgin olive oil: 1 Tbsp, drizzled after prep
- Seasoning: NuSalt (potassium chloride)

## Cooking the vegetables

- Weigh the vegetables first.
- Place broccoli, cauliflower, mushrooms, ginger and garlic in boiling water.
- Boil until tender, 7-9 minutes. Steaming is also acceptable.
- Keep the cooking or steaming water, since it can thin the final texture.

## Cooking the lentils

- Bring water to a boil in a medium saucepan and add the lentils.
- Reduce heat to low and cook uncovered for 18-20 minutes, until "al dente."
- Drain in a colander and rinse under cold water.

## Blending, serving and finishing

- Bryan allows either blending or keeping the dish whole.
- To blend: put 1 Tbsp dried cumin, 1 Tbsp apple cider vinegar, 1 fresh lime, the cooked black lentils and the strained cooked vegetables into a high-speed blender until it becomes a thick soup.
- If needed, add some of the vegetable (or steaming) water to thin the texture.
- Top with 1 Tbsp hemp seeds, then drizzle 1 Tbsp of extra virgin olive oil.
- Season with NuSalt (potassium chloride).

## Cooking method rationale: minimizing AGEs and ALEs

- Bryan cooks by "low temperature, high humidity, high acidity, high antioxidant" methods.
- The stated goal is to minimize formation of Advanced Glycation End-products (AGEs) and Advanced Lipid Peroxidation End-products (ALEs).
- This is why he boils or steams the vegetables and lentils rather than using dry, high-heat methods, and why acid (apple cider vinegar, lime) and antioxidant-rich ingredients are part of the dish.
