## The five pillars of exercise for longevity

Bryan frames exercise as many systems working together, so the question is which combination keeps you alive longest, not which single type is best. He cites regular exercise reducing all-cause mortality risk by 31%. He names five pillars: strength training, low intensity cardio, high intensity cardio, mobility and flexibility, and balance. His advice is to pick one pillar and start integrating it daily, and "the most important principle isn't perfection or intensity, it's consistency."

## Bryan's personal exercise schedule

- Trains 6 to 8 hours per week.
- Each day: 30 minutes of strength training and 30 minutes of cardio.
- Mobility, flexibility and balance work are layered in throughout the day.
- Goal: a body that stays strong, resilient and injury-free "at 60, 80, and 100," beyond looking hot and jacked.

## Pillar 1: Strength training

- Muscle health is one of the strongest predictors of longevity. He cites a Swedish study of more than 1 million young men: the weakest third had a 20 to 35% higher risk of premature death than the strongest.
- Muscle acts as "metabolic armor": it stabilizes joints, improves insulin sensitivity, supports bone density and decides whether you can live independently in old age.
- Protocol: at least three strength sessions per week.
- Focus on compound movements for the whole body: push, pull, squat.
- Apply progressive overload: add a little weight or one to two reps week over week, and use an app to track it.
- He rejects the myths that lifting is only for bodybuilders, kills endurance, or requires being young, and says "strength today is independence tomorrow."

## Pillar 2: Low intensity (zone 2) cardio

- Zone 2 is a pace where you can still hold a conversation (the talk test).
- It improves mitochondrial function, efficient energy production, fat burning and metabolic health. He calls it one of the best ways to extend lifespan.
- Bryan's target: 150 minutes of zone 2 per week through walking, jogging, cycling or swimming.
- Habit: a 10-minute walk after meals, which improves blood sugar control and digestion and uses up glucose from the meal.
- He rejects the claims that cardio kills muscle gains, that it must be exhausting, or that walking doesn't count.

## Pillar 3: High intensity cardio and VO2 max

- Zones 4 and 5 "expand and test the limits" of the engine that zone 2 builds. Short bursts dramatically improve VO2 max.
- VO2 max is one of the strongest predictors of longevity. Higher VO2 max is consistently associated with lower cardiovascular disease and death risk.
- He cites a large treadmill cohort: adults in the lowest 25th percentile had about 5 times higher all-cause mortality risk than those in the top 2%.
- Bryan's target: 75 minutes of high intensity cardio per week. Start where you can and build over time.
- In zones 4 and 5, heart rate should reach about 80 to 90% of max. Online calculators can find your personal zones.
- Options: sprinting, cycling, battle ropes, and his personal favorite, the Norwegian 4x4.
- Norwegian 4x4: 4 minutes of zone 5 effort, then 3 minutes of zone 2, repeated for four sets. You shouldn't be able to speak comfortably during the hard intervals.
- Wear a good heart rate monitor to stay in the correct zones: "measurement and data is everything."

## Pillar 4: Mobility and flexibility

- Mobility determines how long the body stays functional. With age joints stiffen, connective tissue tightens, posture deteriorates and falls become a leading cause of injury and death.
- Protocol: stretching and mobility work daily, 5 to 10 minutes a day.
- Focus areas: hips, spine, ankles, hamstrings, shoulders.
- He says it doesn't have to be complicated.

## Pillar 5: Balance

- Balance is "possibly the most overlooked pillar of longevity fitness." It reflects coordination, neuromuscular control and brain health.
- He cites research that people who can't balance on one leg for 10 seconds have significantly higher mortality risk, with one study showing 84% higher all-cause mortality among middle-aged adults.
- Protocol: daily balance practice with stability drills and slow, controlled movements.
- His favorite is single-leg stands, ideally with eyes closed for as long as possible.
