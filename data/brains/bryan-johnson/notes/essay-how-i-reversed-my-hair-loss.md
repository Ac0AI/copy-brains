## Why Bryan started his hair protocol early and what he credits

- Bryan says that genetically he "should be bald." He began losing hair and going gray in his late 20s. At 46 he reports a full head of hair and about 50% of his gray gone.
- His main lesson: start early and be proactive. He made the mistake of acting only after he noticed the loss.
- Context he cites: by age 20 about 20% of men have visible hair loss; many people lose up to 50% of their hair before noticing thinning; by age 50 up to 40% of women have noticeable loss.
- His advice is to consider starting a hair health protocol before loss is visible.
- He frames his protocol as open experimentation. He is doing several things at once, but says others need not. Topical minoxidil 5% alone may be all that is needed for good results.
- He stresses humility: "We're still in the early days of anti-aging and likely know far less than we think we know."

## Nutrition for hair health

- Bryan says proper nutrition matters for hair.
- Nutrients he lists: adequate protein, omega-3 fatty acids, biotin (vitamin B7), vitamin C, vitamin D, vitamin E, iron and zinc.
- He states the Blueprint supplement stack contains all of these.

## Bryan's custom topical formulation and how he applies it

- **Dose and routine:** 1 mL applied to the scalp each morning (before bed is also fine). He then massages the scalp for 1-2 minutes with a silicone scrubber to stimulate blood flow.
- **Personalization:** the formulation is based on a genetic test that predicts his response to various ingredients. The company then supplies a custom Rx formulation.
- **His ingredients and strengths:**
  - Minoxidil 7%
  - Cetirizine HCl 1%
  - Latanoprost 0.004%
  - Dutasteride 0.25%
  - Melatonin 0.1%
  - Caffeine 0.2%
  - Tretinoin 0.0125%
  - Vitamin D3 1,000 IU/mL
  - Vitamin E 10 IU/mL
- **Budget option:** topical minoxidil 5%. He notes some people get side effects such as itchy skin or headaches, so monitor for them.

## Why he went back to once-daily topical application

- Bryan tried applying the topical twice a day (morning and night).
- His recent blood results then showed DHT too low at 5.8 ng/dL, which he says can create side effects.
- He returned to once daily and plans to retest his blood in 90 days.
- The takeaway: track blood markers when using DHT-lowering ingredients such as dutasteride, rather than assuming more application is better.

## Red light cap and oral minoxidil

- **Red light cap:** six minutes a day. He says you can wear it during your morning routine.
- **Oral minoxidil:** he takes 3.75 mg a day. He started at 2.5 mg and evaluated for side effects before moving up.
- **Evidence he cites:** a double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial in 90 men with androgenetic alopecia found oral minoxidil 5 mg daily had similar efficacy to topical minoxidil 5% solution applied twice daily after 24 weeks.
- **Safety:** generally considered safe at low doses. Common side effects are hypertrichosis (excess hair growth) and headaches.
- He points readers to Dr. Marjon Vatanchi at Irvine Dermatology as a source for an oral minoxidil prescription.

## What did not work: PRP and PRF

- Bryan did PRP and PRF, where blood is drawn, the plasma separated, and the plasma reinjected into the scalp.
- He did it diligently for 12 months and saw "nearly no benefit."
- His verdict: it is painful, expensive and time consuming. PRP seems to work for some people but not all.
- He turned to a different treatment method that Blueprint is assessing and may release later in the year if it passes.
