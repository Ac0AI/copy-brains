## Treat luck as a skill you can measure and manage

- Shaan and Ben have worked together for 5 years and created nine projects that each made over $1M.
- He argues luck is something you can influence: "we actually treat like creating luck as a skill."
- Applying Peter Drucker's "what gets measured gets managed," Ben keeps a "luck sheet." Each day he logs the 5-6 things he did to create luck, then logs which ones later paid off (a tweet that became MFM content, a deal he chased and wired into, a hire, an intro).
- The annual goal for Ben is 100 lucky breaks, which takes roughly 500-700 interesting actions.
- Shaan traced the best outcomes of the last two years "like 23andMe, ancestry.com." Each key customer, intro and hire for a business that crossed $10M ARR, profitable and bootstrapped, in under 18 months came from "Ben doing random [stuff]."
- Measuring it changes decisions. It gives the green light to shoot shots, like flying out for 2 days to hang with Jesse Itzler with no agenda.
- Most attempts lead nowhere, and that is fine. None of it is transactional; the goal is to be useful.

## The relationship-guy model (the Nick Dio / Gary Vee idea)

- Nick Dio is Gary Vee's relationship guy: he hosts dinners, takes people on trips and hangs out with people Gary likes, often without Gary there.
- Shaan compares it to LeBron spending about $1M a year on his body. His version is "what's my version of spending a million dollars a year on my body," meaning the business asset is relationships.
- Gary builds a magnet with his content, but he can't personally meet everyone it attracts, so he hired someone to do it.
- Shaan is an introvert who creates content from his bedroom. Ben is extroverted, replies to everyone, checks in, and congratulates people on milestones (for example sending a cake with their number on it).
- Why it works: the extrovert turns the content's attraction into relationships. Roughly 1 in 20 people ends up teaching them something, becoming an investment, or helping in another way.

## The barbell for relationships: light touch or long hang

- The model is a barbell of two extremes: constant lightweight texting (memes, tweets, quick notes), or long in-person time.
- Shaan, relaying an idea from John Coogan, says the middle is death: "death by a thousand Zoom calls." A 40-45 minute Zoom costs energy but builds little depth or bond.
- A 5-hour hang (lunch, walk, coffee) is worth far more, "the equivalent of like 200 Zoom calls."
- Example: Chris Sacca moved out of San Francisco to Truckee, bought a cabin plus a guest house, and invited interesting founders for 48-hour weekends (hot tub, skiing, cooking, talking). That is how he got into Uber and Instagram: he understood the founders and businesses in depth and knew to "back up the truck."
- Going to someone's home also shows how they live and whether you want that life.

## Find your "first believer"

- Shaan says everyone successful had someone who irrationally believed in them before there was proof. His was Michael Burch, who gave him the CEO job around age 23-24 when he wasn't qualified.
- He calls it belief as an angel investor, before money. It matters more than cash as a starting ingredient.
- How they pay it forward: they say it out loud. They text people they believe in ("I'm buying all the Billy Oppenheimer stock on the market right now"). Shaan also suggests thanking whoever believed in you.
- How Ben and Shaan started: Ben was a listener in a CEO mastermind group Shaan ran (a test that became Sam Parr's Hampton). Ben's business had one customer, Anheuser-Busch, paying about $1M a year. Ben was bored, had hit a wall growing it, and said yes to a 90-day project.
- That led to the All Access Pass, which did over $1M. For 90 days Shaan emailed subscribers daily what he worked on while building a business (an ecom brand, a fund, a course).

## Pick a 3-6 month "main mission" and learn from a curated peer group

- Shaan and Ben choose a main mission roughly every 3-6 months (longest 1-2 years): raise a fund (about $20M raised for startup investing), grow his ecom brand (from about $5M to $25-30M in revenue over about 12 months), grow MFM, and so on.
- Neither knew ecom, so Ben built "Club LTV": a free, curated group of ecom owners doing over $5M in revenue who met every other Friday.
- Format: speed-dating style intros and one founder presenting what's working (TikTok, email), with groups reshuffled.
- It drew about 150 owners in the first month. Any problem they had, someone in the group had already solved.
- The plan to bring it back: a free, curated group of the top 1% of MFM's roughly 1 million listeners ("entrepreneurs with a sense of humor"), meeting monthly in shuffled groups. Shared fandom makes people likely to get along.

## Half-baked ideas with real mechanics

- **Courtside YouTube channel:** buy courtside seats and interview a different person each game (what they do, how they make money). Estimated cost is about $100K in Orlando and $400-500K in New York. Shaan thinks sponsors like FanDuel or SeatGeek could fund part of it. He'd also pay about $20 a month for a service that identifies who sits courtside, a "business meets basketball" paparazzi.
- **Lunch bounty marketplace:** people bid on a one-hour lunch with a notable person and proceeds go to the person's chosen charity. It could be launched permissionlessly and use public pressure. Reference points: Monish Pabrai paid $650K for lunch with Warren Buffett. At two lunches a week averaging about $25K, it raises about $2.5M for charity.
- **"Everyone needs their Birkin bag":** collectibles are huge (Whatnot, live pack-opening commerce, is valued at about $5B and has done about $5B in sales; Shaan and Ben missed the chance to invest at about $50-60M). Open question: which niche doesn't have its Birkin yet. Shaan likes tech collectibles and one-of-one musician pieces.
- **Real-life Survivor:** for "post-economic" people (making money no longer drives decisions) who crave voluntary hardship and digital detox. Shaan says $10K with about 20 people living as a tribe is more scalable, like the new Tough Mudder. The same logic applies to a Love Is Blind pods tour.
- **Coach K in a box:** AI-made custom motivational videos for a workout or sales call. Shaan's version of the story: Coach K's locker-room videos spliced Braveheart-style footage with your own game film.
