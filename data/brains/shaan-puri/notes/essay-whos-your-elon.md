## Choosing role models: Elon is the default north star, but Shaan wouldn't want his life

- Many founders treat Elon Musk as the north star. Shaan agrees he is probably the GOAT of entrepreneurs: richest person in the world, most famous founder, and fearless about going after the biggest, hardest problems.
- He still says "I'd never want to be him." His reasons: Elon's family life is a mess, he neglects his health, and he lives a high-stress life.
- He frames it with a line he attributes to "some wise monk, probably": "One man's heaven is another man's hell."
- The question he asks instead: whose life and career do you look at and say "gimme one of those"? He says it's never an exact copy you want, but a useful guide.

## Shaan's role model type: the creative polymath

Shaan admires creative polymaths, people who succeed across many unrelated fields. His first example is Ben Franklin:
- Science: invented bifocals and the Franklin stove.
- Media: built a successful newspaper, the Pennsylvania Gazette.
- Politics: founding father who helped write the Constitution and the Declaration of Independence.
- Business: ran the US postal system as CEO and made it efficient and profitable.
- Music: invented the glass armonica, which influenced composers like Mozart and Beethoven.

His summary: "Science, Media, Politics, Business, Music… what a legend."

## Modern example: Jesse Itzler

Shaan's modern-day polymath is Jesse Itzler:
- Started as a white-boy rapper.
- Wrote famous jingles and won an Emmy.
- Entrepreneur: coconut water, a private jet company.
- Prolific runner and endurance athlete.
- Lived in a monastery with monks, and had David Goggins live in his house.
- Wrote a couple of great books.
- Married Sara Blakely, has 4-5 kids, and seems like a good dad.

The appeal is a career that is wide and also seems to include a good family life, which is the part Shaan finds missing in the Elon model.

## Why this career shape appeals: "ride all the rides"

- Shaan thinks it's because part of him feels life is short. His image: "Disneyland will close soon and I wanna ride all the rides before we get kicked out."
- The goal is breadth of experience across many domains rather than maximizing a single metric like net worth or company size.

## The price of the polymath path

- He cites Scott Adams (creator of Dilbert): "If you want to be successful, find out what the price is and then pay it."
- Looking at his examples, the price Shaan wrote down for himself:
  - Reinvent yourself and not be afraid to start from scratch (beginner's mind).
  - Become a very quick learner.
  - Be unafraid to do things you're clueless at.
  - Be willing to change your career label, friend circle, and habits every few years.
- He says these all sound fun and sound like who he wants to be.

## The exercise to run

- Pick your own "Elon": the person whose life you'd actually want, not just the most successful one.
- Use them as a guide rather than an exact copy.
- Work out what it costs (Scott Adams's "price"), write it down, and check whether paying it sounds fun to you. His closing question: "who's your Elon?"
