## Why "down" is the one trait Shaan looks for in a partner

- Shaan says the most important trait in a business partner (and a life partner) is being "down". He compresses this from Warren Buffett's three criteria (intelligence, integrity, energy), which he used to follow.
- He frames partner choice as the highest-stakes decision: a median marriage (age 30 to 76) is 46 uninterrupted years (16,790 days), and divorce costs about 50% of net worth in a day. A business partnership has no structured "divorce" and unwinding it is messy.
- He grants that other traits matter (intelligence, unselfishness, energy, integrity), but says "the most important trait for me is how down they are."

## The three meanings of "down"

- "Down for whatever": flexible. You can throw any plan or idea at them and they roll with the punches.
- "Down for the cause": puts the team and mission ahead of their own needs.
- "Down to do it": an action word, not a personality trait like "smart". Down people act now. They don't wait, don't overthink, they start and figure it out.
- Their filters for action are simple: Does this sound interesting? Do I like this person? Can I be useful here? Does anything smell fishy? If fine, "OK, I'm down."

## Ben as the example of a great partner

- Shaan has worked with his business partner Ben for nearly 5 years and wants to do 20 more. They have built and sold companies together, done side projects and traveled.
- Ben is "smart, but not the smartest" and has a good work ethic but isn't "David Goggins". What defines him is that he is down: for the cause, to try it, to start right now.
- Ben was a 25-year-old mid-level employee at FanDuel, not a coder or a sales star. When a FanDuel founder wanted to leave and start a new company, he picked Ben because he was down.
- When Shaan met Ben, Ben had a SaaS business making about $1M a year in profit but was bored. Shaan pitched a half-baked side project that would initially pay him less. Ben decided on the spot: "yea, I'm down."
- Shaan asked if he needed to sleep on it. Ben replied he hadn't been listening because he had already gone to GoDaddy to buy the domain and set up the site that night. Shaan's takeaway: being down gives you an incredible bias for action.

## Other examples of down partners

- Greg Brockman and OpenAI: Shaan cites Sam Altman's essay about Greg. He commits quickly and fully. After Sam pitched him on OpenAI, he asked questions for the first 30 minutes of the drive to San Francisco, declared "I'm in" halfway, and spent the last 30 minutes starting the job. He was a brilliant coder (former CTO of Stripe) but also took on non-technical grunt work like getting meals, cleaning up and ordering supplies.
- Airbnb: Brian Chesky and Joe Gebbia weren't coders or growth hackers, but when they needed cash, Brian floated turning their apartment into an "air bed and breakfast". You need a partner down enough to float an idea like that, let alone pull it off.
- Uber: when Travis and Garrett needed someone to run a weird black car, off-market taxi service, Ryan Graves answered their tweet. He had a modest background, wasn't a tech genius or polished CEO, but was ready to start yesterday.

## Why speed of commitment beats overthinking

- Shaan compares it to two people jumping from one cliff to another: it's not about who has stronger quads, but who can mentally commit to the jump.
- Even if a down person misses something 1 time out of 10, they move 10x faster than over-thinkers and get 10x more shots on goal.
- Being down drives action and reduces fear, doubt and judgment.

## Why a partner who isn't down kills high-upside ideas

- New ideas are uncertain and the odds look slim. A partner who flinches at uncertainty is contagious and pulls you toward conservative, safe, proven paths, where there is very little upside.
- The damage is unspoken. Like having a very conservative friend, you pre-filter what you'll bring them. With a non-down partner, you filter out high-upside paths before they ever see daylight.
- You need a partner who is down for off-the-beaten-path ideas.

## Pivots require a down partner

- Nearly every great business pivots at least once. Examples he gives: Instagram started as a location app, Slack was a multiplayer game, Twitter was a podcasting app.
- Product, market, price point and marketing strategy are "a giant guess up front". Pivoting means saying on day 458 "we were wrong, everything we've said the past year and a half is wrong... the name is changing, the plan is changing."
- You can only pull off that mid-air U-turn if your partner is down. "Weak stomachs can't handle the sudden shifts."

## How to spot down people and when it matters

- Shaan uses the Reticular Activating System as an analogy: just as you can't name 5 red things in your room until you look for them, then find them easily, once you look for down people you'll spot them.
- You can get away with 90% of life with someone who isn't down, because most things are safe, predictable and figured out.
- For life's great adventures (traveling, starting businesses, making art, having kids), which require daily leaps of faith, he says to pick someone who is down.
