## "Someday" and "later" kill dreams: do it now

- Shaan's core claim is that the "safe path" is not actually safe. It is "dangerous to your dreams."
- He frames safety as a slow killer: "Safety murders your ambition. Murders your sense of adventure. Safety is a serial killer. But it operates in a slow, boring fashion."
- The usual script is "first, let's settle down, take the job, play it safe," on the assumption that you can "always do that later."
- Examples of dreams that get postponed: traveling the world, falling in love, starting a company, a trip to Europe "until things settle down."

## Why "later" never comes

- "Things never settle down. You simply stay busy. And 'later' never comes."
- He calls "later" "one of the most dangerous words in the english language." When he hears it, he puts his "dukes up ready to fight."
- "Later" and "Someday" have "murdered more dreams than you can count."
- The safe path also keeps people stuck in an "OK" relationship or in the city they grew up in.
- People become "pavlovian dogs," trained to stay put in what they're already doing. They lose the muscle to go chase what they actually want.
- This is why, he says, "youth is wasted on the young."

## The Ramen Era vs. the Mortgage Era

- Ages 20-27 are "the Ramen Era": a golden window where you don't mind sleeping on the couch, can live off ramen and free refills, and have nobody to take care of but yourself.
- You may be broke and unskilled, but you have "maximum flexibility and nothing to lose."
- Life swings between the Ramen Era and the Mortgage Era, which he illustrates with a chart.
- On the value of youth: "Warren Buffett would give every dollar he has, just to trade places with the average 22 year old dipshit who spent the last year doing beer runs for Sigma Chi."
- Young people get seduced by "Someday" and "Later," which whisper that there is plenty of time to do the real thing after the current thing.

## What to do instead

- Start the business, tour with the band, backpack through Thailand, whatever it is: "Do it. And do it now."
- Don't wait until you're 31 with "a wife and kids and a mortgage." His line: "You gotta risk it for the biscuit."
- If you're 31, now is still the right time. It beats waiting until 41, "with an achy back," as someone who hasn't taken risks for 20 years.
- Rule of thumb: "If you're called to do something, the only right time is right now."
- Go on adventures and quests. "Find the people you love, and the projects you were put on earth to do."
- Resist "the illusion of safety."
