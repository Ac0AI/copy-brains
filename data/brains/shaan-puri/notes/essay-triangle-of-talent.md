## The core claim: a company's value comes from its right-side talent

- Shaan opens with a mentor's line: "everyone sucks in their first 5 years as a manager. It's up to you if you're going to suck for the next 15 too."
- Every company has a distribution of talent, from problem employees on the left, to average employees in the middle, to star employees on the right.
- A manager spends about 90% of their time on the left (problem employees) and the middle (average employees).
- The right side (star employees) creates about 90% of the company's value.
- If you work at a company, he says, be a "right side" player. If you run one, get as many of them as you can.
- He paraphrases Elon's idea in plain terms: "the team you build is the company you build… so build a great team."

## The Triangle of Talent framework

- He calls it the Triangle of Talent. It "saved my a** many times as a manager."
- Forget job titles: all employees are problem solvers.
- Problem solving has two parts:
  - Identify the right problem to solve.
  - Solve it, either yourself or by coordinating others.
- Star performers live at the top of the triangle.
- He says this makes a simple guide: "That's it. That's the whole game."

## Two questions to test whether someone is a star

- Are you identifying the A+ problem on a daily, weekly, monthly and annual basis?
- Are you able to solve that problem, either alone or by coordinating other people?
- He applies the same test to himself as a manager and to anyone he is evaluating.

## Picking the problem matters more than solving it

- He argues most people think the job is about solving the problem, "and it kinda is."
- But the easier and more impactful skill is getting good at figuring out "what problem needs to be solved right now?"
- Practical takeaway: spend effort on choosing the highest-value problem at each time horizon (day, week, month, year) before spending effort on execution.

## How many level 5s a CEO should have

Level 5 is the top of the triangle (the star performer). His targets by company size:

| Company stage | Target share of level 5s |
|---|---|
| Just started (cofounders) | 100% |
| Small team (5-20 people) | At least 30% |
| Medium team (20-150 people) | Shoot for 15% |
| Big company (150+ people) | 5-10% |

- On big companies he adds: "honestly just quit, big companies are no fun."
- The framing for CEOs: count your level 5s and compare against the target for your size.
