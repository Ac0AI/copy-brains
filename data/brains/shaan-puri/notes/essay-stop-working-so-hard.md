## "Hard work" is not the key to success

- Shaan rejects "hard work is the key to success" as incomplete. His test: if hard work determined pay, construction workers sweating all day would out-earn the architect who drew the house or the banker who pushed a button to approve the construction loan.
- He argues that wealthy people like to credit "hard work" because it sounds fairer than the truth. He jokes the honest version is "I'm just smarter than you, and my father paid for private math tutoring while you were stuck picking boogers in daycare."
- His position: "Hard work helps. Certainly. But hard work alone doesn't equal success."
- He mocks the endless list of "keys to success" on Instagram and Pinterest ("passion", "hard work", "discipline", "determination"): "how many god damn keys are there? I need a janitor's keychain."

## "Find the product that is you pushed out"

- The best career advice Shaan says he got came from his personal trainer, when he asked what to do next in his career: "Find the product that is you pushed out."
- His definition: find a thing so natural to you that doing it is just an extension of being yourself.
- His own example is his podcast. He and his friend Sam already loved nerding out about business and making dumb jokes, and did it for fun with no microphone. Adding a microphone turned it into a job.
- His reaction: "Hard work? This feels like play. I can't believe my luck. I get paid to do this?!"
- The key idea, which he frames with a Naval quote: the work "looks like work to others" but feels like play to you.

## Examples of work that looks hard but feels like play

- **His personal trainer:** does 5-6 workouts a day with clients, which looks exhausting to Shaan. The trainer loves it and is full of energy. That energy makes him a magnet for clients, and he has a waiting list.
- **Nick Gray:** a social butterfly who loved it enough to write a book, "The 2 Hour Cocktail Party", on hosting gatherings. He now gets paid $10K+ to fly to parties or events and host them.
- **Joe Rogan:** broke the usual podcasting rules. Three-hour interviews ("too long!"), no clear niche (one day MMA, the next comedy, politics or conspiracies about the pyramids). Shaan's read is that the show is simply his tastes and interests pushed out for the world to see.
- **A house-cleaning creator on TikTok:** makes millions of dollars and has millions of followers who watch her clean and organize her house. One video of her organizing a fridge got 17M views. To Shaan it looks like work, but she loves it and the internet lets her turn it into a career.

## Why being the best at your thing pays: the internet finds you

- Shaan's claim: "If you become the best at anything - you get paid handsomely."
- Examples he gives: the best croissant maker in LA can make more than a hedge fund guy in NY, and the best pre-school teachers (Ms. Rachel, Blippi) make millions on YouTube.
- Mechanism: the internet lets the best people get found, and they get huge rewards.
- Implication: authentic enthusiasm is a distribution advantage. People who love the work show visible energy, which attracts customers and audiences (the trainer's waiting list, Rogan's audience).

## The career goal Shaan recommends

- Stop optimizing for hard work or for finding a "good job". Find the product that is just you pushed out.
- His stated career goal, which he invites others to steal: "Get paid a King's ransom for something that I would do for free."
