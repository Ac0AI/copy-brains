## Elon's decision framework: the "Elon Method"

Shaan distills how Elon makes high-stakes bets into three steps:

1. Figure out the most existential problem.
2. Identify the key lever (e.g. Pennsylvania as the swing state).
3. Go all in. "Burn all boats. Throw your entire weight at it."

His one-liner: "If you play - play to win." He frames Elon's 2024 election push as a game-theory choice. Doing nothing risks losing, doing a little may not matter, and going all in can tilt a 50/50 race. Elon "doesn't process risk the same way the rest of us" do. Most people look at a coin flip and call all-in "too risky."

## Why Elon went all in on Trump: the +EV math

Shaan treats the $150M election spend as an expected-value bet, not a gamble:

- Elon spent about $150M out of pocket (about $1M per day, plus moving to Pennsylvania, speeches, X town halls, and getting Trump on Joe Rogan).
- His companies are worth about $1 trillion.
- If the move gives them even a 1% goodwill boost, that is about $10B in gains, or "$150M to get $10B." Shaan calls it "a +EV decision."
- Upside he lists: a pro-tech, pro-crypto, pro-business, anti-regulation government, a giant "I-owe-you" from Trump, and "the tears of his enemies."
- Downside Shaan acknowledges: the polls were tight, and a loss would have made Elon "screwed" with Democrats who already disliked him.

## The 22-event backstory: pressure that pushed Elon into politics

Shaan's narrative of how Elon went from apolitical engineer to campaign volunteer:

- 2020: Elon is "not political," just wants to build cars, rockets, and tunnels.
- A California congresswoman tweets "F*ck Elon Musk." He replies "message received" and moves his companies to Texas.
- Biden holds an EV summit and invites every car maker except Tesla. Shaan's explanation: unions back the Democrats, and Tesla is the only major non-unionized car maker.
- Twitter bans the sitting President. Elon sees free speech at risk and buys Twitter for $44B.
- He releases the "Twitter Files." Shaan says they showed Democrats trying to censor social media during the election.
- Government lawsuits and over-regulation of his companies follow. Advertisers boycott Twitter, revenue drops 80%, and Shaan puts the asset's value loss at $20B. Elon fires 80% of staff.
- A holder of 9 Tesla shares sues, and a judge revokes Elon's $56B Tesla pay package. Shaan notes the package was already earned, the metrics were hit, and the stock was up 10X.
- Shaan's read: Elon was "backed into a corner," so he went all in on Trump for three months.

## Credibility is not likeability

Shaan flags his own bias, 1,100 words of "30 different flavors of 'omg, i love Elon'," then states a principle: "I just don't mix up credibility with likeability."

- He doesn't like Elon's jokes or all his opinions.
- He still calls him "undeniably the greatest entrepreneur of our lifetime."
- The takeaway: learn from people's results and methods without needing to agree with or like them.

## Why Elon's work stands out: "the hardest hardware"

Shaan notes the Silicon Valley saying "Hardware is Hard" and lists what Elon works on:

- Self-driving electric cars
- Reusable rockets
- Tunnels
- AGI
- Chips in brains

His contrast: "Normal founders celebrate app launches. This guy launches rockets and catches them on lily pads in the ocean like it's a YouTube trick shot video."

## xAI: responding to a loss by going all in on speed

Shaan's "single favorite Elon story":

- Elon co-founded OpenAI and gave it $40M of his own money, yet ended up with zero ownership and zero say.
- Instead of "taking an L," he created xAI, despite a big disadvantage: OpenAI had a 10-year head start and $10B+ in Microsoft funding.
- His response was to go all in, building the biggest GPU supercluster in the world in 19 days.
- Shaan points to Jensen Huang (NVIDIA CEO) talking about it as proof of how unusual that speed is.

## Change the game instead of playing the opponent's

Shaan closes with a chess analogy from a new xAI hire that he says sums up Elon's last four years: "it doesn't matter if you're mate in 3...If there's a laser beam from space that shoots down your opponent's king, you win."

- When behind, Elon doesn't try to out-play the leader on the same board.
- He changes the rules with a bigger, faster, more extreme move, using the same all-in approach as the election and xAI.
