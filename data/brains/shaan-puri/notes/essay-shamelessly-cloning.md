## Copy great ideas shamelessly

Shaan's core claim, via Mohnish Pabrai (the "Indian Warren Buffett," $100M+ fortune): his whole strategy was "Two words… Shamelessly Cloning." Shaan loves the phrase and treats it as a working principle for building.

- Mohnish read a Tom Peters book (Peters was a big 1980s management guru) arguing that humans are surprisingly bad at copying successful ideas.
- He made a promise to himself to become "a great copier of great ideas."
- Shaan's takeaway: you may not be the smart operator who figures everything out, but you can choose not to be the one who ignores what is visibly working.

## The story of two gas stations

Shaan retells Peters' example. Two gas stations with snacks sit across the street from each other, with the same concept.

- **Owner A** pays a guy $150 to improve his signage and installs brighter evening lights. His traffic goes up a bit. He also walks outside to give regular customers a complimentary window wash. He keeps making small service improvements and eventually earns 2-3x more per week than the station across the street.
- **Owner B** calls the signage "a waste of money" and calls the window wash unsustainable ("you can't do it for everyone. And they're not even paying for it!"). He never changes.
- The interesting question, per Shaan, is not what the smart owner does but "why the heck is owner B not doing what he sees is working??" He asks whether it is ego or ignorance.

## Competitors won't act even if you tell them everything

Shaan relays Peters' argument that this behavior is common and that information alone does not change behavior.

- You can go to direct competitors and tell them all your trade secrets and everything you have learned.
- They will listen, but there will be "no change in behavior."
- Implication: the edge of copying is rarely about access to information. It is about willingness to act on what you can already see.

## The right way to copy: take the idea, not the art

Shaan cites Picasso: "Good artists borrow. Great artists steal." He is explicit that there is a bad way to copy.

- Don't copy people's art or the exact execution.
- In the gas station case, don't copy the exact signage. Copy the idea ("I should make my business look more approachable too") and put your own twist on it.
- The unit to clone is the underlying insight, then adapt it.

## Copying is often positive-sum

Shaan gives examples where copying is encouraged by the source and benefits everyone:

- Religion is people copying the morals and beliefs of others, and religions want you to copy them.
- CrossFit is people copying the exercise habits of fit people.
- Grandma's spaghetti recipe is a copy of someone else's spaghetti recipe.

## Shaan's own practice and a hiring tactic

- He keeps a list of great ideas he plans to "shamelessly clone" and shares it with readers.
- He recommends hiring top 1% talent from South America at $7-10 per hour for "MBA level talent."
- He offers intros to his contact for this: email one line describing the talent needed (e.g. "I need an EA").
