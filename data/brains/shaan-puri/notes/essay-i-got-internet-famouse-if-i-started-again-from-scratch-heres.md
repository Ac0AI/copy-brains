## Quality beats follower count

- Shaan says to focus on WHO follows him, not HOW MANY follow him. He tries to ignore the numbers, because platforms shove them in your face (on YouTube everyone else can see them too).
- Ignoring view counts and prioritizing quality is a competitive advantage, since most people fall into the numbers trap.
- He cites Tim Ferriss: "Would you rather 100,000 Americans, chosen at random, read your book? Or be invisible to them - but every attendee of Davos loves your work?" He picks the latter.
- Car analogy: General Motors sells 2.6M cars a year and is worth $50B. Ferrari sells 13,000 cars and is worth $80B. Ferrari has 200x fewer customers yet is more valuable, because each customer is worth 200+ of a competitor's.
- Applied to content: if Elon Musk loves his blog, that one reader is worth more than 100,000 TikTok views from random viewers.
- Rule: "Quality matters more than quantity."

## Find your inner nerd to decide what to talk about

- He quotes his trainer: "the best product is just you, pushed out to the world." He calls this finding your inner nerd.
- Ask what you nerd out about most: the topic that looks boring or like work to others but feels like play to you, and that you have an unquenchable thirst for.
- Example: his uncle's basement model train set. Shaan thought he was a dork, but says that for content he wants to be the basement model train guy, doing it publicly for the world.
- Example creator: the "die, workwear!" guy, who writes 1,000-word analyses of what pant length men should wear on suits, and enjoys it.
- Two benefits:
  - It eliminates competition, since very few people share your taste palette.
  - It makes success a guarantee. If success only means "am I famous with millions of followers yet?", you may be unhappy forever. If you nerd out and have fun daily, every day is a win. He says this shifts the odds of success "from 1% to 100% because I win by playing (vs. only winning by winning)."

## Build a magnet, not an audience

- He thinks of content as a giant magnet that attracts the type of people he wants in his life.
- Every blog, podcast and video should be a honeypot for like-minded people.
- "The internet is a geography vaporizer." He loves old Berkshire Hathaway meetings, basketball and building startups, and nobody in the neighborhood he grew up in likes those things. The internet lets him find the 10,000 people who share his flavor of weird.
- His goal: "build a magnet, not an audience."

## The 5 D's: be known well, not well known

- His goal is not to be well known but to be "known well", which means sharing stories, hopes, dreams, fears and obsessions.
- The 5 D's:
  - Done: what you've done, your track record.
  - Deliver: what you offer people who follow you.
  - Do: what you do for work and for fun.
  - Dreams: what you're shooting for (e.g. Gary V wants to buy the Jets).
  - Dork Out: what you're really into, what you collect.
- Two wins:
  - The audience feels more connection.
  - You get luckier, because fans know what to send you: links (obscure stories of rich people), book recommendations (he's studying comedy and storytelling), and deals ($1m+ annual cash flow, bootstrapped, needing growth help).
- He also argues there's no such thing as too long, only "too boring."

## Prioritize content over packaging; build a binge bank

- "A+ content with C- delivery is a great starting point. C- content with A+ delivery is a death trap."
- He didn't waste hours on packaging. He spent years living an interesting life so he had something interesting to say.
- Example: Joe Rogan's first podcast episode had an ugly webcam, snowflake video effects and a rainbow background. Rogan focused on content first and packaging later, and is now the king of podcasts.
- Binge bank: when numbers were small and he doubted the point, he told himself he was building a binge bank, "a personal Netflix binge-worthy show", about 1 hour of content that, if someone consumed it, would make them love him.
- The trick is to picture future people discovering his content, clicking to learn more, and falling into the binge bank, instead of thinking about the 8 people viewing today.

## Sell a feeling, not information

- He thinks of his channel as a little shop at the world's most crowded flea market, and says he's not selling things but a feeling.
- Examples of feelings sold:
  - David Blaine: awe.
  - James Clear: self-control.
  - UFC: "holy shit!" moments, live on PPV.
  - Tony Robbins: motivation.
  - CrossFit: a satisfying sweat.
- Give the feeling once or twice and you get a follow. Give it every day and you get a lifelong fan.
- His method: figure out which feeling he wants to sell, then deliver it consistently.

## Be so good they can't ignore you (MrBeast's advice)

- Shaan, a friend of MrBeast's, shares his advice for aspiring YouTubers: "Make 100 videos, and every video, try to do one thing better. Maybe it's the hook. Or the thumbnail. Or storytelling. Whatever. Just make one thing better, every time, then come back and talk to me after 100."
- Why it works:
  - It shoos away unserious people, since almost nobody takes the advice.
  - Those who do take it have figured it out themselves by video 100.
- On blaming the algorithm, MrBeast says to replace "algorithm" with "people": not "the algorithm doesn't like my video" but "people don't like my video."
- Shaan's takeaway: the algorithm serves people videos they like to watch, so failure is "a skill issue."
- Content is a skill, a muscle developed through reps. The strategy: "become so good they can't ignore you."
