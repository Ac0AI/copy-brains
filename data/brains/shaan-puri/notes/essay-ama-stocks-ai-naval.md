## Holding through a market crash: leverage, patience, and selling early

Shaan describes the nausea of a market crash: "There's a unique nausea that comes from losing 20% of your net worth over breakfast."

- After selling his first company to Twitch (owned by Amazon), he received a chunk of Amazon stock.
- Rich acquaintances advised "don't sell your stock, just borrow against it": hold the stock and still get liquid cash. The catch is a margin call if the stock crashes.
- He built an Excel calculator and found he could borrow ~60% of his stock and stay safe from a margin call unless something extreme happened.
- The pandemic then hit, and he came very close to a margin call. He was so stressed he "basically margin called" himself: he sold some stock, paid off the margin loan, and slept well, despite having lost about 30% of his net worth overnight.

## Three lessons from a crash

1. "Leverage is like botox. Use with caution. Too much of it will f*ck your face up."
2. When a market crash happens, "respond, don't react." His rule is to do nothing for 30 days.
3. The most costly mistakes come from selling early, not buying wrong. A bad purchase caps your loss at 1x your investment. Selling early can cost you 2-10x of upside.

## Reading Naval's way: reading as a spark, not information intake

Shaan's favorite under-the-radar Naval line: "Reading isn't about putting information in your mind. Reading is about sparking a fire in your mind." He aims it at people who listen to podcasts at 2X or brag about how many books they read in a year. His view: the point of books is sparking new thoughts, not collecting info. Reading is to the brain what exercise is to the body.

## Shaan's three reading habits

1. **Read a wide range.** He follows a management book with *Chimpanzee Politics* (power and sex among apes). Unrelated books spark new combinations of ideas.
2. **Reread the best stuff.** Rather than only reading new books, he rereads good ones several years later. He's a different person, so he takes something different from the same book. He cites the quote: "No man steps in the same river twice, for it is not the same river and he is not the same man."
3. **Pause while reading.** Speed reading is a mistake. If the goal is to spark a fire in your mind, "that fire needs air to breathe."

He also quits boring books: "There is no honor in finishing a bad book." He calls himself a "strategic quitter," which he says makes reading more fun and interesting.

## Reading the OpenAI departures (August 2024)

Shaan says OpenAI "is starting to smell fishy" like a midwest sushi restaurant, and admits "I don't get it."

- Signals he lists: the top 2 safety leaders (Ilya Sutskever, Jan Leike) left, and the CTO was reported on "extended personal leave" (he names Greg, as written in the source).
- Co-founder John Schulman leaving for Anthropic, the nearest competitor, is highly unusual. His analogy: the Uber co-founder joining Lyft.
- His two hypotheses:
  - **Safety:** people joined for the mission of safe AGI, and the safety people are leaving because they think Sam is prioritizing winning and profit over safety first.
  - **Money:** people may be vesting out and getting huge offers at competitors, making it financially irresponsible to stay. OpenAI burning ~$5B a year in cash could be a worry, but he doubts it's the concern because "the size of the prize is too big."
- He says he doesn't know what is going on, but it will make a great Netflix doc in about 5 years.
