## Customers want to become big Mario, not hear about the item

- Shaan's "Rule #1 of marketing": "nobody cares about your product, they care about themselves."
- He frames it with a Mario analogy:
  - Your customer is little Mario: not powerful, needs help.
  - Your product is an item Mario can use.
  - The result is big Mario, shooting fireballs and kicking everyone's ass.
- The marketing job is to sell the big Mario outcome, not the item.

## The common mistake: talking about yourself and your product

- Shaan says most people "talk endlessly about yourself / your product."
- His alternative: tell customers how awesome they will be after using the product.
- This is the core framework. In his words: "The day you understand this framework, is the day you start learning marketing."

## Why the product is an obstacle, not the draw

- Shaan stresses, with a "🚨 I REPEAT 🚨", that nobody cares about your product.
- He argues the product is an obstacle for the customer:
  - It has a cost.
  - It takes time to learn.
  - It is "a necessary evil on the way to them getting what they _really want_."
- What people do care about is their own quality of life improving.

## Worked example: breakfast bars

- Shaan's example applies whatever the business: "If you're selling breakfast bars, don't tell me about the granola."
- Instead of ingredients, lead with outcomes:
  - It saves 20 minutes in the morning by being a grab-and-go breakfast.
  - The buyer loses weight in the process.
- The pattern is to swap product features (granola) for the customer's after-state (time saved, weight lost).
