## Constraints as a creative engine

Shaan argues that creativity thrives under limits, not freedom. His line: "Think outside the box" was wrong. What great creators do is put themselves into a tight box to do their best work.

- Self-discipline unlocks personal freedom (the Goggins/Jocko insight). By the same logic, constraints unleash creativity.
- He backs this with two quotes: "Creativity starts when you cut a zero from your budget" (Jaime Lerner) and "The enemy of art is the absence of limitation" (Orson Welles).

## Dr. Seuss and the 50-word bet

A case of a constraint deliberately imposed to force a better product.

- In the 1950s Dr. Seuss was on a hot streak with Cat in the Hat and How the Grinch Stole Christmas. Cat in the Hat used only about 236 total words.
- His publisher, Bennett Cerf, upped the ante with a bet: write a children's book using only 50 unique words.
- Seuss won the bet. The result, Green Eggs & Ham, became his best-selling book ever, with 8M+ copies sold.
- Lesson: a tighter limit than his already-minimal style produced his biggest hit.

## Jaws: when the constraint picks you

Sometimes the limit isn't chosen. Shaan uses Spielberg's Jaws as the example of embracing a forced constraint.

- Attempt 1: use real sharks. The production team said no.
- Attempt 2: build a mechanical shark. It cost $250,000 and broke 3 times because of the water.
- With production nearly paused, Spielberg's reframe was: how do I make a terrifying shark without ever showing it on camera?
- His answers:
  - Triple down on music and lighting.
  - Shoot from the shark's point of view so it's never seen.
  - Show only the shark fin ("a swimmer's nightmare").
- Result: a smash hit, about $500M at the box office on a $7M budget.
- Spielberg later admitted: "If the mechanical sharks had worked, Jaws probably would've been a worse movie."

## The "One Minute Blog" as a self-imposed constraint

Shaan applies the principle to his own writing.

- His essay series is called the "One Minute Blog." The constraint: write something insightful that takes the reader 1 minute 59 seconds max to read. (This post's read time: 1 min 34 sec.)
- His observation: the "One Minute" posts are far more popular than his no-limit posts.

## How to apply it

- When facing a blank page or open-ended brief, add a hard cap: word count, time, budget, number of elements.
- When a resource you counted on fails (budget, tool, access), ask what the constraint forces you to do better, rather than how to work around it.
- Treat a tight box as a feature of the work, not a handicap to remove.
