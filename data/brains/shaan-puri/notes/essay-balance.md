## Life balance is the wrong goal; integration is the right one

- Shaan says "there is no such thing as 'Life Balance'", and that it's not even the right goal. He treats "balance" as polite code for "I want everything".
- Most people have about 5 top priorities and a fixed budget of "life points" (energy, time, money, attention). Balance-seekers ration them.
- Rationing gives every area roughly 50% effort, so nothing hits 0% and nothing hits 100%. He calls this "a recipe for mediocrity & disappointment" and a zero-sum game of constant trade-offs between work, family, fitness, friends and fun.

## Three doors: balance, zero expectations, integration

- **Door 1, "Balance":** living with an expectation of balance. He says Reality - Expectation = Unhappiness. These people feel like they're doing something wrong, or blame the boss, the government or the system.
- **Door 2, "Zero expectations":** blast your life points at whatever matters right now. Typically fun and work in your 20s, family and work in your 30s, travel and health in your 60s. Still zero-sum, but happier.
- **Door 3, "Integration":** what Shaan says the "winners of life" do. They have the money, body, family, fun and peace of mind, and you rarely hear from them because they're too busy living.
- The core idea: instead of choosing between two buckets, make one activity fill several. "Integration multiplies your time." About 16 waking hours a day, but one hour filling 3 life buckets multiplies it.

## Shaan's own integrations

- **Health + friends:** works out with his buddy John every morning and plays basketball with friends once a week. When people ask for a coffee meeting, he proposes a hike, walk or workout instead. He says the shared struggle builds relationships faster.
- **Friends + work:** most of his friends are entrepreneurs, so hanging out yields marketing, operations and investment ideas.
- **Wife + work:** he started a business with his wife and they built a ~$25M company together. Caveats: it only works because she is a strong business partner, and they learned to "turn it off" so they stay life partners, not just business partners. He warns it "can be disastrous if done wrong".
- **Fun + health:** a weekly basketball league and workouts with buddies make exercise a fun part of the day instead of solo gym grinding.

## Work is the biggest bucket, so fix work first

- Most people spend 8 hours a day at work. If work isn't fun, isn't done with people you like, and doesn't give you schedule freedom, integration is very hard.
- His analogy: a power outlet with no holes, so nothing else can plug in. After his career change he's "a power strip", "swiss cheese", "putt putt golf" with holes everywhere.

## The "time test" for jobs

- Test: if you worked for one hour, closed your laptop and left, having done as much as a co-worker who sat there 8 hours, would your boss be mad at you or at the co-worker?
- He says in 99% of jobs the boss would be mad at you, even though they claim to be "results driven". That means you're paid for time, not results.
- Shaan held a job for 1 year and disliked it for this reason.

## How Shaan restructured his career

- He then spent 8 years as an entrepreneur, mostly failing, but kept going because: he enjoyed it (never knew what day of the week it was, a green flag), his friends were his coworkers, and a big hit meant lasting financial freedom.
- Two people made him jealous, "like they had cheat codes for life":
  - **An investor** who gets paid to have lunch with smart people and picks about 1 in 20 to give money to. He notes most funds use a 2/20 model: 2% of the fund per year in fees (on a $50M fund, $1M a year for 10 years) plus 20% of profits.
  - **Tim Ferriss**, whose fireside chat drew nearly 1,000 ticket buyers to hear one person talk in a chair, with no music or pyrotechnics.
- After selling his company he skipped starting another one and told people he was now a podcaster and investor, with no podcasting skills and a small investing bankroll. He turned down every job offer and term sheet to start a new company.
- Four years later: his podcast My First Million is mostly calling his friend Sam and talking business for an hour. Halfway through he reads a Hubspot CRM ad. He gets paid more than he ever imagined for something he'd do for free. As an investor, founders call him, do the hard work, and he takes a piece of the upside.

## Checklist for finding your "thing"

Find work that:

- You'd do as a hobby and can't believe you get paid for.
- Pays for results, not time-in-chair.
- Has top performers earning 10x or 100x the average (he says this is almost every field: plumbers, chefs, doctors).
- Gives you control over your schedule.

His conclusion: "If you find that, then you can do integration, and never need balance." Examples of "work that feels like play" he gives: writing books (Ryan Holiday), teaching people to code, owning a gym.
