## Spending a year "strategically broke" after college

Shaan skipped the post-college job track and made a year of no job, no salary and no plan on purpose. He called it his own "grad school": a year to work on projects, travel, and figure out what he wanted to do. He had heard a line from a guy at a casino 16 years earlier that stuck with him: "I don't know much, but I know one thing: the ordinary life isn't for me." He wanted a creative life, with friends, and to "get filthy rich in the process."

He says the trade was worth it:
- A year of free time in your 20s is worth a decade of free time at 65.
- Skills and relationships built in your 20s have 20+ years to compound.
- Young travel (backpacking, hostels) is easy and cheap. By the 40s it is "damn near impossible", and with a wife and 3 kids it is "more like a U-Haul move, with fist-fulls of diapers."
- "Nice stuff" was cool, but "freedom of time was much cooler."

His tweet on this got 200,000 views. Older people agreed, and younger people asked how to do it without money. He says he didn't want to be "that guy" who tells people money doesn't matter, so he describes what he actually did.

## Living scrappy: the cost structure

Shaan lived in Boulder, Colorado with two best friends, Trev and Dan. Boulder has outdoors, a university party scene and lots of entrepreneurs (Techstars was based there).

- They rented a bad 2-bedroom apartment for about $1,400 a month, roughly $466 each.
- Trevor slept on an air mattress in the living room, which was also the bedroom, office and gym.
- Furniture was free Craigslist desks and Target air mattresses.
- Their pet was a free mouse named Marty.
- The menu was Costco frozen grilled chicken ($25 for 10 pounds), eggs, avocados (the luxury item), rice, ranch style beans, and "tons" of Frank's Red Hot.

## Calculate the minimum, not the maximum

Shaan didn't try to maximize income. He "calculated the minimum money needed to live with max freedom."

- He decided he needed about $1,000 a month.
- His goal was to earn that in about 5 hours of work a week.
- He tutored math at $35/hour, first for a girl he met at a bar and then her friends. When she asked if it would be free if she were his girlfriend, he said "I'll take the $35/hour, thanks." His takeaway: "you can't hustle a hustler."
- He coached basketball at a school for autistic children, $35/hour for 2 hours a week.
- These gigs brought in about $700/month for roughly 5 hours a week.

## Getting paid for the pitch: business plan competitions

Once he hit his 5-hour cap, Shaan asked: "what's something easy I could do once, that would give me my entire year goal?" His belief is that "constraints breed creativity."

- He was already brainstorming business ideas. The shortcut was to pitch the idea and get paid for the pitch, without building the business.
- He found niche business plan competitions with decent prizes and low competition.
- He entered a dozen, got paid by 2, and he and his team won $25K+ in prize money.
- That covered about 2 years of living costs.

## Master skills and projects, not jobs

The goal was not to stay unemployed. It was to use his 20s to find what he loves and is great at. He started by learning "master skills", which he compares to the one master key on a janitor's ring of 573 keys that opens many doors. His list:
- Making something out of nothing (hustle)
- Selling stuff online (sales/marketing)
- Pitching ideas and getting people on board (persuasion)

He didn't know how to learn these, so he did projects. He borrows Pixar's process: start with something that sucks, then "remove the suck." Every version 1.0 sucks, so you edit and tweak until it no longer does. His job was to make each project, each "dot", as bold as possible and trust they'd connect later (the Steve Jobs line that you can only connect the dots looking backwards).

## The projects and what each taught him

- **E-commerce store for custom wristbands to frats and sororities:** made $1K in the first weekend. Taught him selling online, finding manufacturers on Alibaba, drop shipping and cold email.
- **Game shows:** he and Dan got cast on Wheel of Fortune and Dan almost won a trip to Italy. Being young gave him "the license to do irrationally dumb things."
- **Video editing for real estate agents:** turned property photos into an animated slideshow video with music for $500, about 15 minutes of editing. Agents were hard to sell to. He learned video editing, more cold email, and that he should favor work where earnings are not tied to his time (unlike tutoring).
- **Poker:** won $10K in a tournament one night and used it to travel Australia. Taught him strategy, thinking in probabilities, controlling his mind and spotting when others are lying.
- **Delivery-only sushi restaurant:** taught him how to make sushi, run a restaurant, negotiate a lease and get bank financing.

None were big financial successes. A few made money and most went nowhere. At the time it felt like striking out repeatedly, but he kept going because it was fun. The skills paid off much later.

## How he decided what to do: Permission to Wander

When asked how he went from sushi, poker and e-commerce to a career, Shaan says he used a 3-step system he calls "Permission to Wander" for figuring out what you want to do in life. This essay only introduces it and defers the steps to a follow-up piece, "the 3-step plan to figuring out what you want."
