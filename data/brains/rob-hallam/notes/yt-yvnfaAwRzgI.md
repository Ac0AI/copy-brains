## From zero income to an agency to SuperX

- Rob studied computer science and became a software engineer in 2022, based in Reading, UK. He took the traditional path but disliked the job: isolated, overworking, no friends nearby, bad weather. He calls it "the closest I was to being depressed."
- He was laid off in November 2022 with a small pay package and took it as a sign to go all in on building his own thing. He looked at e-commerce, dropshipping and SaaS.
- In 2023 he joined a small startup through a friend. It ran out of money, partly through poor management.
- Seeing Marc Lou make millions through his X audience with ShipFast showed him "there's another world out there."
- In September 2023 he began "indie hacking" and sharing publicly on X. His first product was Indie Index (a directory of indie hackers). A tweet asking who'd want it got about 50 likes, when he had around 100 followers, so he treated it as validation. It took about 4 months to ship.
- Indie Index did not make money because he never monetized it. His lesson: "you need a buy button on something to be able to take people's money otherwise it's not a business."

## Solving cash flow with an agency

- Rob's core early-stage problem was cash flow. Without it you either work a job and build on weekends, or you're short on both cash and time.
- After launching Indie Index he made $0 for a full year while backpacking and building. He tweeted that he'd made no money in 12 months and offered himself as a freelance developer.
- A reply suggested he sell his skills as an agency rather than freelance, so he spun one up off the back of that viral tweet and launched immediately.
- He landed his first client for $3,000 for one month. It was the first money he'd made in a year, and it compounded from there.
- The agency gave him both validation and runway.
- His advice for new builders: either keep a job and build on the side until it pays enough to quit, or run an agency (as he did) to build runway.
- He applied the same pattern recently with Can I Vibe Code?: take the momentum of a viral tweet and launch immediately.

## Marketing: get in front of your ICP and test many channels

- Rob defines marketing as getting in front of people, specifically the right people (your ideal customer profile).
- His channel for Indie Index and the agency was X, where his audience (indie hackers) already lived. All his agency clients came inbound: he posted wins, people DM'd, they booked a meeting, he signed them, then posted about that to attract more.
- He says X works for him because sharing his journey feels natural and fits his ICP. He also notes he prefers the marketing side over "being in the weeds coding."
- Options he lists:
  - SEO (dying, turning into AEO). Cheap but slow to compound.
  - Outreach. Works for some, for example Goji Berry (about $4M a year) does outreach plus X and ads.
  - Ads, if you have cash flow, which many indie hackers lack.
  - Building in public. The downside is that anything that works gets copied if you lack a moat. Mobile app founders often have the viral content format as their moat, so sharing it gets it ripped off.
- He cites Nevo (Postiz) that what worked two years ago doesn't work today.
- He doesn't think one channel fits everyone. When starting, he says to try as many channels as you can and see which works.
- His formula for doubling down: a painful enough problem, a marketing channel that works, and something you enjoy enough to stay motivated. Indies work alone, so self-motivation matters. When those three line up, "it just cascades."
- He does not recommend using AI to automate your personality on X.

## How SuperX came about

- While running the agency, Rob wanted a tool to help craft tweets and learn what goes viral by analyzing data. Existing tools like Tweet Hunter hadn't integrated AI yet.
- He built a product to scratch his own itch, knowing the market was validated because competitors existed. He called it Brandcast.
- During competitor analysis he found SuperX, a Chrome extension. A few months later Tibo (now his co-founder) acquired it from the original founder, and the collaboration with that founder didn't work out, so Tibo took full ownership and posted looking for a co-founder.
- Rob sent Tibo a Loom of Brandcast, they worked together for a week, then agreed to partner.
- It then took about 6 months of grind before SuperX started working. The title claims $20K+/month.
- Rob credits luck for the opportunity but says he acted on it: the chain ran from the $0 year to the agency, the itch, Brandcast, Tibo's tweet, and the grind. His takeaway is to keep going because you never know when something will work.

## Lifestyle and location notes

- Rob lives as a digital nomad. He calls Thailand his favorite country: safe, modern, excellent hospitals and infrastructure, cafes with 300-400 Mbps Wi-Fi, mountains and beaches, and cheap, beautiful scuba diving in Koh Tao.
- He applied for Thailand's DTV (a five-year visa allowing six-month stays) and is currently in Vietnam.
- He found Latin America (Brazil, Colombia, Guatemala, Panama) enjoyable but felt more on edge there because of safety.
- He recommends Southeast Asia as a starting point for travelers, and Vietnam and Chiang Mai for settling long term. He says he's past the stage of moving somewhere new every two weeks.
