## Rob's week-two challenge: getting the first paying user (what separated the builders)

Rob set up a six-week challenge with three strangers who had never made a dollar online. Each got a $100 budget and was told to build something from scratch with AI. The week-two task: get someone to actually pay, not a free user and not a friend, but a stranger handing over a first dollar.

- Rob's framing for the week: "Not a free user, not your friend, someone actually giving you your first dollar."
- Outcome after two weeks: one builder (Kate) had five paying users, one (Davis) had none, and one (Manaj) had gone silent.
- Rob's stated stakes for week three: Davis "either has to get his first paying user or pivot," Manaj "has to actually show up," and Kate "has to prove that this week wasn't a fluke."

## Why Kate got paid: a waitlist plus a low-friction, lifetime-style offer

Kate built MoodMark, an app that was not yet live on the App Store, around a 9-to-5 job. She still got five paying users in two weeks.

- She emailed everyone who had signed up to her waitlist.
- She offered a small one-time price (about $5 at first) for lifetime access once the app launched.
- Five people ended up paying $1 each, which Rob counted as $5 total and "amazing."
- Rob called her "the first one in the series to actually make money" and noted she got it "from her wait list that she's built around 9:00 to 5:00 in just two weeks."
- Takeaway Rob draws: the waitlist was the asset. She had warm people who already raised their hand, so she did not have to find strangers cold.
- Charging before the product exists is fine here. Her buyers were paying for a lifetime deal on an app that was not yet live.

## Kate's mindset: share the journey and think about "hooks"

Kate described this as her toughest week. She started overwhelmed and scared of the uncertainty, then sat back and asked what hooks would make people believe in the product enough to pay $1.

- Her answer: she shared her building journey publicly, and people came to her and were interested.
- Rob asked how the first payment felt. Her words: "It felt insane... I think I'll remember this day till the end of my life." The first Stripe notification came through at 10:30 a.m. while she was at her day job.
- Rob's takeaway: the first payment feels like "some different life" from a 9-to-5, which is the motivation to keep going.

## How Kate fits building around a 9-to-5

Rob asked how she managed the time. Her routine:

- She is in a coffee shop by 7:30 a.m. and works on the product before her job (that morning, onboarding).
- After work she returns to the same coffee shop and keeps building.
- The baristas know her and sometimes give her free coffee because she keeps coming back.
- The point Rob highlights: she did all of this in roughly two weeks around full-time employment, so a day job is not a blocker if the time is protected daily.

## Why Davis stalled: a $1 offer to a cold audience with no replies

Davis was building a daily sensei app. He posted an offer on X: the first customer gets a $1 lifetime deal as long as they agree to give feedback and actually use the product. Nobody replied, and Rob notes that it "technically should be super easy" to find someone to pay a dollar, but it had not worked.

- Davis's assumption was that $1 would be an impulse buy. He later suspected the real issue was not enough audience.
- He was up at 4 a.m. building and still had no paying user.
- Rob's diagnosis of the pivot point: "You're at this spiral moment where either you spiral downwards or you start getting feedback and you start iterating." The advice is to take the opportunities available rather than spiral.
- Rob's pricing point: "when you're saying something's $1, when you're saying it's $30, you're saying that this is worth $1." A $1 price tag signals the product is worth about $1. He added that "in Europe you can't even get a coffee for $1."
- Davis's plan: use direct DMs, which should at least produce an answer about why people say no. The challenge Rob frames for him is to reach out to 100 people before the next week ends.
- Rob's rule for the next check-in: Davis either gets a first paying user or pivots.

## Consistency as a builder's real bottleneck

Manaj, 18, had a rough week: food poisoning, no video call, and no video diaries submitted, after a week in which, per Rob, the entire internet had an opinion about him.

- Rob's view: talent is not the issue ("the talent is there... I've seen what he can do"), but "talent without consistency doesn't build anything."
- After two weeks Rob had no consistent footage and no first paying user from him.
- Rob's takeaway across the challenge: the person who showed up daily around a job (Kate) got paid first, while the most talented builder who did not show up had nothing to show.
