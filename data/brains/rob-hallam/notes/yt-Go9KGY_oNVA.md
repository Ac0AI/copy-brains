## Why Rob chose Cyprus as a base, and what it offered

- Rob had nomadically travelled for about 3 years (Southeast Asia, South America, Africa, North Africa, Europe) and wanted a permanent base. His family is in the UK; he wanted something between Southeast Asia and the UK.
- Trigger: his startup Super X grew from just under $1,000/month a year earlier to over $22,000/month, so he wanted a tax-efficient base.
- He considered the UAE and Singapore before landing on Cyprus.
- Cyprus corporate tax is 12.5% to 15%. If you own the intellectual property (e.g. software you license out), the "IP box regime" brings the effective rate to about 2.5%. He also cited 0% dividend tax.
- Other draws: great food, friendly people, beautiful country, in the EU (but not Schengen as of 28 April 2026).
- He spoke to four or five lawyers online, picked one, and flew out for a month to find an apartment, meet lawyers, and set up personal and company bank accounts, the company, and residency.

## Residency mechanics for a non-EU (British) founder

- Post-Brexit, Rob is a non-EU citizen, so tax residency requires immigration residency first (the "pink slip"). It renews yearly at about 1,000 euros. A 12-month apartment lease is required.
- Alternatives for those with capital:
  - Invest 200,000 euros in your own company (a faster, less restrictive route).
  - "Golden visa": buy property worth 300,000 euros or more.
  - Rob did not have that savings level, so he went with the basic pink slip.
- Deal-breaker: the pink slip requires returning to Cyprus every 90 days, and not just for a day; roughly a couple of weeks. He called it "a leash", given he wanted to base from Vietnam/Thailand (about a 10-hour flight).
- EU citizens only need to spend 60 days a year in Cyprus to be tax resident. He says this suits Europeans who spend summer or winter there, keep under 183 days elsewhere, and pay tax only in Cyprus.
- Rob said if he had fallen in love with Cyprus he would have accepted the 90-day rule.

## Cost and housing in Limassol

- Limassol is billed as Cyprus's tech and entrepreneur hub, central between Nicosia, Larnaca and Paphos (airports at Larnaca and Paphos, each about an hour away).
- It has the highest rents in the country. He wanted a modern two-bedroom, northeast-facing, high-ceilinged apartment. Realistic cost: 2,000 to 2,500 euros/month, roughly US prices.
- Elsewhere in Cyprus, rent is about 60% of Limassol's (food and coffee cost about the same across cities).
- His logic for accepting the cost: with enough income, tax savings effectively pay for the apartment.
- A month in an Airbnb while searching cost over 2,000 euros.
- Agent experience: his first agent (Charalambos Constantinou, introduced by a friend) was great. The last agent was shady: demanded cash and deposit before showing the contract, and wanted the landlord's daughter as the lawyer. After roughly seven or eight viewings, this was the "straw that broke the camel's back".

## Why it did not work: community, infrastructure, lifestyle

- Loneliness: as a single 24-year-old who doesn't drink or party, he found it hard to make friends in Limassol. A community existed but was hidden, with no hub.
- No co-working spaces for remote founders (mostly 9-to-5 offices). He paid 80 euros/month for a tiny but new gym that closed Sundays, early Saturdays and for four days at Easter. He contrasts this with 24/7 availability in Asia.
- Limassol felt less Cypriot to him, with a large Russian community and many non-Cypriots; he wanted to integrate, learn Cypriot Greek and build local community.
- Weather in March: cold, rain, grey skies and Saharan sandstorm pollution, despite the "300 days of sun" claim.
- Decision moment: after the last agent, he slept on it, walked the beach, and decided Limassol was not for him "right now".
- Context: he booked his flight just before the Iran situation; Dubai cancellations left him stranded in Bangkok and overstaying his Thai visa, which Thai immigration waived.

## Which Cyprus city he'd choose, and who Cyprus suits

- If he returned, he would pick Nicosia: more Cypriot culture, "hearty" and "homey", with a friendly local group (via X contact Dudufolio). Paphos felt isolated for his age group, with more retirees and British tourists.
- Cyprus can suit:
  - Europeans needing only 60 days a year.
  - Founders who can invest 200,000 euros or buy 300,000 euros of property.
  - People with families who care less about building a new social circle.
- He stresses he is not bashing Cyprus: locals were among the kindest people he has met, and the first-24-hours tweet (over 600,000 views) was honest, but first impressions differ from living there.
- Lesson he states: first impressions are very different from the reality of living somewhere; he wishes he had had this video before going.

## UK tax timing and next steps

- UK tax year runs 6 April to 5 April. He rushed to establish residency before 5 April, but later found that as long as he stays out of the UK and avoids UK tie tests, he has until the 2026-27 tax year/April 2027 to settle a new arrangement (about 11 months of slack).
- Current plan: Da Nang, Vietnam, as his Southeast Asia hub, back to growing Super X; he likes lifestyle arbitrage there (Vietnamese coffee, a growing tech hub, learning Vietnamese).
- Options he is weighing: corporate tax in Singapore with personal tax elsewhere (Malaysia, UAE).
- Trigger to revisit Cyprus: if Super X grows from $22,000/month to over $100,000/month, he could invest the 200,000 euros.
