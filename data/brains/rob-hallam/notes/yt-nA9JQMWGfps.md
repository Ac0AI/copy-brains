## Why Rob made video his highest-leverage skill

- Rob credits video as the reason his business reached $30,000 a month and the reason for the life he lives now.
- He has had multiple videos on X pass 1 million views. One video about his experience in Cyprus did over 700,000 views in a week.
- He started a year before this video by filming with the iPhone front camera. Those videos were basic, and "Nobody watched them."
- His belief: video is the highest-leverage skill to develop in 2026. AI can generate video, but it cannot capture "the human soul part" or replicate stories a person has lived. A 10-20 minute human video about your own experiences has no AI equivalent right now.
- One good video can reach more people than you could meet in a lifetime and open doors you can't predict.
- He says it is not about talent, it is about reps. His framing: choose to "suck 1% less every single day."

## Bring energy by putting in reps and setting up your routine

- Nobody enjoys watching a flat, sleepy presenter. Energy on camera was one of his hardest early problems because he is a self-described introvert who gets energy from being alone.
- Nobody, Mr. Beast included, is born comfortable talking to a camera. Comfort and energy come only from reps.
- Tactic: find what dials you up.
  - For Rob, caffeine helps, and so does the time of day.
  - He films around sunset, which is why the lighting in his videos looks the same each time.
  - His routine: wake around 11:00 a.m., move his body, eat something filling, have caffeine, work slowly early and ramp up later, then film.
  - A camera in front of him at 11:00 a.m. right after waking "would be horrible."
- Order matters: put in the reps first. Only after that can you record when you feel like it.

## Make the video about the viewer, not yourself

- Viewers are always asking, often subconsciously, "How is it helping me?"
- Easiest fix: use less "I" and more "you." Frame points as what the viewer can do (for example, "you can make your own videos better by doing X, Y, Z").
- He says the more you make videos about the viewer, the more viewers you get.
- Value does not have to be education. Three kinds of value he names:
  - Entertainment, such as Mr. Beast, who checks the engagement graph on every upload, removes whatever does not perform, never repeats it, and iterates toward highly produced, engaging videos.
  - Education.
  - Connection, by sharing a story vulnerably.
- What is not value: talking without context about your day when nobody knows who you are.
- Before every recording, ask what the viewer gets out of it. This applies to short form and long form.

## Speak with intention, and don't over-polish

- Viewers give you their time, so respect it. Think about what you'll say before you hit record.
- Rob uses a list of talking points, not a word-for-word script, and then riffs. He cuts the "ums" and "ahs."
- Reps must be intentional. His gym analogy: swinging a dumbbell randomly builds nothing, while learning what a bicep curl targets and thinking about each rep does. Speaking reps work the same way.
- Replace filler words ("um," "uh," "like," "but") with silence. Pausing to think is fine.
- Don't polish too much, especially in long form. Occasional ums, pauses and mistakes make you relatable. Over-cut video puts people off because they can't relate to the person.
- Short form needs more polish than long form, though he notes a trend where less polished, more real videos do better.
- As a benchmark, he points to Higher Up Wellness on Instagram: people who talk naturally for hours with no filler words, which he says is highly engaging. Practice avoiding filler words and it becomes natural.

## Framing: look at the lens, not yourself

- Talk to the camera at eye level, as if eye to eye with the viewer.
- Don't glance at the preview screen, and don't read a script with your eyes moving across it. Even if viewers don't notice consciously, they pick up on it and it signals you don't believe what you're saying.
- Looking at the camera creates the best connection with the viewer, which makes the video more engaging and therefore more viewed.

## Hooks: the first seconds and the three things to cover

- Hooks are the most important part for getting people to keep watching.
- Length: about 3 seconds (possibly 2) on short form; 5 to 15 seconds on long form.
- On this video's own hook, Rob says he deliberately covered three things:
  - What you will learn.
  - Why it is worth learning (the return on investment of getting better at video).
  - Why he is qualified, so he isn't "some random guy who's never made a video." Pair good quality with credibility.

## Audio, then captions

- Sound is the most important part of a video, more than the picture.
  - People will watch grainy camcorder-style video on Instagram if the audio is good, and will scroll past instantly if the audio is bad.
  - Also make the video look decent (lighting matters), but audio comes first.
  - Rob uses the DJI Mic Mini, which clips on and connects to his camera. He says it is not sponsored and is reliable.
- Captions: he cites that approximately 75% of people watch videos on mute (subway, bus, gym, casual scrolling).
  - A video without captions loses those viewers, because they won't turn on sound to find out what you're saying.
  - He calls captions "the single best thing you can do" for retention and viewership.
- Where to use them:
  - Add captions on X, LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok, Threads and any non-YouTube platform.
  - Skip burned-in captions on YouTube. YouTube has auto captions, and many viewers are on TV (which YouTube is pushing heavily), so viewers can turn captions on or off themselves.

## How to improve: one change per video

- What prompted the video: Rob noticed several small issues in a newer creator's videos, messaged him, and the next video was much better. It showed Rob how much he had picked up from hundreds of daily videos over a year.
- Rob's plan to avoid procrastinating:
  - Pick one point from the list and record a video now, applying it. Options include captions, eye contact or fixing audio.
  - The next day, keep that change and add another.
  - Keep stacking daily. His claim: after 100 videos you will be "completely transformed."
- "It only takes one viral video to change your life."
- He plans more videos on founding startups, making videos and building SaaS products.
