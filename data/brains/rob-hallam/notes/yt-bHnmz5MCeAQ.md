## How Rob built canivibecoded.com in a few hours from a tweet

- The idea came from a reply to a tweet by Peter Levels (Levels.io), who had posted about re-engineering projects like WhisperFlow, Granola and Woop.
- Rob replied that someone should build a tool that shows which subscriptions you pay for and which ones you could vibe code yourself.
- Levels answered "It's a great idea. Can you build it?" and Rob started within the hour, around 11pm.
- The product is a web app called canivibecoded.com: a list of tools or subscriptions you can build yourself. Each entry has a prompt you paste into Claude Code, Codex or Cursor, so you don't pay (for example) $20 a month.
- Rob bought the domain 19 minutes into the build.
- He decided to make the whole site open source, because he thinks the idea works better that way.
- He wanted it to be easy to use and a bit funny, with a question-style hook such as "Can I vibe code this?" (Resend for sending emails is one example).
- Design was done in Paper, with a separate prompt per page. The site works well on mobile.

## Launch sequence and the time cost

- Rob stayed up until about 3:45am to finish and ship before sleeping, then recorded a video and posted a tweet around 5am. He had planned to be in bed at 1am.
- He chose to publish immediately instead of waiting, because he wanted it out before he slept.
- Eleven minutes after posting, the tweet had 11 likes. He was unsure it would work: "It could flop, who knows. We'll keep going anyway."
- After waking up, the tweet had 65K views and 34 retweets. A poll on Granola had 23 votes.
- The launch rode on an existing audience built over years on X and through his own brand.

## Early traffic numbers

- After 48 hours: more than 35K views and 9K visitors.
- After about 3 days: roughly 14K unique visitors and 1,000 prompts copied.
- On day 3, about 5,000 page views from midnight to the time of recording.
- He says retention looks high, and people keep returning to the site.

## Monetizing with sponsorship slots

- About two days after launch, Rob launched sponsor slots.
- First sponsor: Martin Lasak of Wishkit. He announced it on X, and the second sponsorship came in right after, from Peter's Post Fast.
- Third slot went to Goji Berry. Simon of Founder Stack bought a $599 slot at the same time. At that point he put the run rate at about $1.8K per month.
- Slot prices he mentions across sales: $299, $399, $499, $599, $799 and $999.
- Within about an hour he went from the first slot to roughly $2,000.
- Three more sponsors followed on the Sunday: Posters, Bullseye, and Browser Blast (Indexsy).
- Run rate moved from $0 to $7.6K per month in 48 hours, on a project he started on a Thursday.
- The title claims $9k a month. The transcript only supports the $7.6K per month figure.

## The retention problem and what Rob did next

- Rob's stated challenge after the spike: keep traffic up so sponsors get a return on investment.
- He says the site seems to be something people revisit, which helps.
- He had already launched a new weekly newsletter as one way to keep visitors coming back.
- He says he has "a lot of ideas" for keeping traffic going but does not detail them.

## Mindset notes on building alone

- Rob has spent years building his personal brand and posting what he builds. This was the first time something he built got attention on its own.
- He previously built Super X with co-founders, so credit was shared. He says he never knew whether he could have done it alone.
- He argues that confidence changes when you actually do it: "the alignment between what you were always telling yourself and what became concrete reality."
- His advice to people who feel stuck: reaching the top is "just a matter of time".
