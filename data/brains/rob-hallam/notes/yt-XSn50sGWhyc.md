## Why Rob left the UK for Brazil and what he is building

- In 2025 Rob moved from the UK to Pipa, a remote beach village on Brazil's northeast coast. He moved somewhere he could "actually afford to live" while building a startup.
- A viral tweet (750,000 views) led him to partner with X creator TBO on a new product called Super X, a tool to help people grow faster on X.
- His goal is to scale Super X to over $10,000 a month, and he is documenting the journey publicly.
- He says the only reason he can afford this is X: he built his last business by posting and sharing the whole journey there. The money he made came from that bet on himself.
- With Super X he gets to "share the power of X with everyone else".
- The partnership started with a trial period to see whether he and TBO work well together before committing fully.

## Building in public as a distribution engine

- Rob credits sharing his last business's journey on X as the reason it made money, and the same viral reach is what produced this partnership.
- One viral tweet created a co-founder-level opportunity with a large creator. Audience can turn into partners as well as customers.
- His new product is built for the same channel that created his opportunities, so he has first-hand proof of what it does.
- He is now also documenting the build on YouTube. This was new to him, and he is still working out what works.

## Rob's trial task: shipping an AI tweet generator on a large existing codebase

- To test the partnership, TBO gave Rob a challenge: build a feature that uses AI to generate tweets tailored to the user.
- Rob has coded for years and used AI before, but the Super X codebase already existed and was huge, so it took a few days just to get oriented.
- He spent 4 hours staring at a black screen with no error indication, digging through files to find what was wrong. He eventually asked AI for help.
- After roughly a week of work, the feature was deployed on the Super X domain. Users can generate tweets, manage the context, and customize the output.
- His lesson: "if you're struggling with something, if you're finding something difficult, stick with it because odds are you're probably closer than you think you are."
- He frames it with the meme that the person who sticks with it the longest is the one who gets the diamonds.

## Remote-work friction and how Rob handles it

- Time zones: TBO is in France, 4 hours ahead of Rob, so coordination is tricky.
- Infrastructure: the Wi-Fi is usually good but went down for a whole day.
- Self-motivation: working for himself means he has to drive himself, which is harder in a place that tempts him to relax.
- Health: he was sick with a virus for about two weeks, through Rio and his first week in Pipa. He suspected the house's filtered water machine, switched entirely to bottled water, and felt back to normal afterwards.
- He says the upside of the trade-off is worth it, and he is "super excited" about the product.

## Why Rob moves every two months

- Rob's pattern is two months in one place, then a move to re-spark things. Before Brazil he spent two months in Morocco, and before that two months in Jalta.
- He needs routine and "a sense of monotony" to be productive and focus on one task, such as his business or videos.
- Over time that monotony drags him down and time seems to blur. He says this is the problem with the UK at the moment: an environment where "you get burnt out where there's no creativity, no drive, no excitement."
- Travel and new experiences are his most extreme form of feeling like he is making progress. But constant novelty alone means no work gets done, so he needs both.
- Two-month stints reset the environment without losing the ability to build a work rhythm.

## Lessons from starting to vlog: don't plan your life around content

- Rob fell into the bad habit of planning his life around the vlogs rather than planning the vlogs around his life.
- He wanted the videos to be authentic and show everything he was doing. Once he felt he had to plan life around filming, "everything just went through the floor."
- He recorded a lot he never posted or edited because it "just didn't feel like me."
- His fix is to document what he is already doing instead of staging activities for the camera.

## Community as a substitute for stable friendships

- Moving every two months means he cannot keep the same social bonds for more than two months, unless someone travels with him between places.
- Remote communities such as the digital nomad group in Pipa make him feel "like you're not alone", full of people who work remotely on interesting things.
- Travelling lets him meet many different people. His roommate James is someone he first met over two years ago in Gran Canaria. They are both from the UK but never met up there.
- He argues you would not make these kinds of connections if you were stuck at home.
