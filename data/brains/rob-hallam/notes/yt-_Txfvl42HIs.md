## Rob's challenge: six weeks, $100, first dollar online

- The challenge is a series called "My First Dollar" (this is episode zero). Rob recruited participants by tweeting: "If you're ambitious, driven, and want to change your life, message me." He got 100+ replies within an hour.
- His credibility framing: over three years he founded five startups. Four failed completely (no users, no payments, no profit). The fifth now makes over $30,000 a month.
- His thesis is that today's tools make building anything online and earning from it easier than ever, and the challenge is meant to prove it.

## The seven rules for going from zero to first dollar

- Six weeks total to reach the first dollar.
- Maximum spend of $100, covering everything.
- Start from absolute zero: no previous builds and no existing audience. New product, new customers.
- One project only. Pick it and commit; no hopping between ideas.
- Work in public and film weekly, including successes, failures and low moments.
- Weekly milestones: landing page live in week 1, first user in week 2, with a weekly call to Rob to show progress.
- Participants keep 100% of the revenue. Rob takes nothing.

## Diagnosing the real blocker: confidence, not technology

- Rob picked an 18-year-old applicant who already runs about 10 AI agent workflows in parallel daily and has spent heavily on coding tools. Rob's conclusion: "it's not about the technology, it's about his ability to trust himself."
- Rob's view is that technical capability is no longer the bottleneck for new builders. Self-trust and willingness to ship are.
- He was struck by how self-aware that applicant was about the gap.

## Narrow the customer before building: the ICP test

- Rob's objection to the "AI social media manager for everyone" idea was that it was too generic.
- His fix for getting validation fast is to ask who the ideal customer is. The answer he liked: small business owners who know they should post but don't, and who considered hiring a social media manager but can't justify the cost.
- He said a clear ICP lets you picture the landing page copy immediately.
- Week 1 goal is a live landing page: know who you are speaking to and what problem you solve.
- On breadth, he said: "If you build something everyone likes, no one will like it." Excluding most people feels frustrating, but he treats it as necessary.
- Landing page test: the right visitor should read it and think "this is for me" and be able to buy.

## First customers: it's fine to start with people you know

- Rob rejected the idea that selling first to a family friend is cheating on the first dollar. He called it a great first step for getting feedback.
- Condition: the person must be the genuine ideal customer with the actual problem, not a parent or friend with no connection to the problem.
- Use that early feedback to shape the first version, and get the product in front of people as quickly as possible to validate the idea.

## Building for yourself: the minimum viable product

- Rob called it ideal to be your own first customer, especially at the start. He said many people should aim to start from there.
- Example MVP discussed (a mood-tracking iOS app): one tap a day, one color on a calendar, mood logged daily, plus reminders.
- Differentiator he accepted against "just use ChatGPT": the app is proactive (it reminds you), whereas ChatGPT is reactive (you must open it).
- Before building, he asks the same question each time: who is it for, and how will it reach them?

## Consumer products: the cheap-price problem

- A participant with B2B experience worried that consumer products are low priced, so you need either a lot of users or many apps (iPhone developers say things like "I have 50 apps and three make money").
- Rob's response was to commit to a narrow person rather than a broad audience, and keep a single project.
- A related AI coaching idea was framed as a personalized "sensei" for learning AI, aimed at people who can't afford a human coach. Rob's prescribed research: look at Reddit, Facebook groups and public information to find the problems people actually have, then address those on the landing page and define a specific customer in mind.
