## Rob's goal: SEO and AEO playbook for beginners

- Rob frames the target outcome as an "SEO playbook for beginners to get your first 10 to 15 articles published" so a new product can rank in AI search and Google and start getting customers.
- His premise: "it is easier than ever to build," and the hard part is getting customers for what was built.
- He calls the approach "search everywhere optimization": optimizing for search on Google, LinkedIn, Reddit and YouTube, not just a website.
- He says his playbook helped him bootstrap three companies to multiple millions in revenue.
- He cites a study (company name not recalled) showing roughly 17 times higher conversion from ChatGPT and other LLMs than from Google. His reasoning: people treat LLMs like a personal therapist and trust them.
- His practical timeline for a brand new product: power through the 10 to 15 pieces in about two weeks.

## How a product shows up in AI search: retrieval, not training data

- Rob names two ways to appear in AI answers: being in the model's training data, or retrieval. Training is hard to influence because models are only retrained occasionally, so he focuses on retrieval.
- Retrieval happens when a prompt forces the LLM to run a web search, such as product comparisons and pricing queries. These are very bottom-of-funnel.
- The flow he describes: user prompt, the LLM turns it into search queries, checks a search engine like Google or Bing, chooses URLs to cite, scrapes and summarizes them, then answers the user.
- His rule: at the stage where the LLM chooses which URLs to cite, you must either show up with your own content or be mentioned on content that already exists.
- You can see the sources behind a ChatGPT answer by opening the sources list at the bottom of the response.

## Four ways to get mentioned on other people's content

- Identify authoritative sources the LLMs already cite. Reddit is the most obvious.
- Pursue genuine coverage: expert quotes and contributions to pieces already being cited by LLMs. He did this as a journalist.
- Pay to get on listicles. Aim for the top one to three spots on pages like "best payment processing SaaS." If the LLM scrapes and summarizes that listicle, you get recommended.
- Keep your brand description consistent across every site that mentions you, so mentions portray you accurately.
- On paid placements: he says even publications like Forbes can be paid to feature you, which yields a high-quality backlink, and the same mechanism works for AI mentions.

## "Owned mentions" cheat code: rank through LinkedIn and YouTube

- Most cited domains by AI, per Rob: Reddit first, LinkedIn second, YouTube fourth.
- A new site has no domain authority, so ranking on your own site is hard. Leveraging LinkedIn or YouTube authority can be easier.
- Example: for a hard, bottom-of-funnel keyword ("programmatic SEO 2026" for their agency), his co-founder Jake wrote a LinkedIn Pulse article that ranks number one. It was an experiment, not planned for SEO. A LinkedIn post by Jake was also cited by ChatGPT for the query "Cleo versus Stanley." The team now produces this kind of content for clients.
- Backlinks come from odd places (a Product Hunt comment gave them one). He says links in LinkedIn or YouTube content are good but not worth sweating: write the piece, put it on LinkedIn, and link back to the homepage.
- On YouTube, the bar is low. Using his tool Mentions (tracking a form builder, Tally.so), he shows cited YouTube videos with a watermarked background, slow cadence, and about 117 views and 2 likes. His point: you do not need to be an S-tier YouTuber, you just need a video that is good enough.

## YouTube SEO checklist for being cited

- Title the video with the target keyword, for example "best form builder 2026."
- Make sure the video has a transcript and closed captions.
- Fill the description with the target keyword plus related secondary keywords (for example "best form builder for professionals"). Make it genuinely descriptive and link back to the page you want to rank.
- Name the thumbnail file with the target keyword.
- Embed the video on the matching landing page on your website. This makes the page more comprehensive and creates a cycle that helps YouTube, AI search and Google.
- If you are reviewing competitors, you can list yourself first, but be fair to them. His review of Stanley used a real signup and screenshots, so crawlers and Google see it as a fair review.

## Which content to write first: versus, alternatives and review pages

- Rob recommends beginners skip fancy keyword tools and start with three bottom-of-funnel types: "[you] versus [competitor]," "[competitor] alternatives," and "[competitor] review."
- Reason: a user searching you versus a competitor is at the end of the buying journey and just needs a push.
- Proof: his bootstrapped SaaS Cleo (zero funding) ranks number one and number two against VC-funded competitor Stanley, and the AI overview is all Cleo.
- Reviews should be honest: sign up for the competitor's tool and include screenshots. Interlink the review to the versus page, which helps both rank.
- Reviewing competitors lets you ride their existing authority and redirect attention to your product.
- Day-one plan for a newly shipped product: list competitors, create versus, alternatives and review content on your site, then on YouTube and LinkedIn add "best [category]" content (for example "best personal branding tool"). LinkedIn is lower effort: "get in your computer and start writing."
- Reverse-engineer what to write: use a citation tracker (his Mentions tool, or Profound) to see which URLs LLMs cite for your target prompt. Create content at similar URLs, add unique information, and make it better. He did this for client Beehiiv by finding content competitors had that the client lacked.

## Using AI to write content without being a copy of everyone else

- Rob says you can use Claude for a first draft, but he argues that fully AI-generated content fails: competitors are doing the same thing, and the model just summarizes content already ranking. His logic: "why is any search engine going to rank your content over the source material?"
- Fix: edit as a human and add unique information that is absent from currently ranking pages: first-party or proprietary data, video, testimonials, firsthand experiences.
- Do as many of these as is easy. A brand new tool may lack data but can add a video with firsthand experience and commentary.
- Validation from the host's experience (not Rob's claim, so treated here only as context): content built from his own users' data ranked best. Rob's reaction: "That is a piece of content that only you could create."
- Do not spam: no private blog networks, no spraying brand mentions everywhere. Focus on high-quality content that LLMs use as source material.

## Three phases of AI visibility and how to structure pages

- Phase 1: check whether you show up and how ChatGPT describes your brand. If positioning is wrong, change the copy on your site and the pages being used as sources.
- Phase 2: compare how you are described versus competitors. Look at their pages being used as sources, copy what works, and make it better.
- Phase 3: appear and be framed as a leader for generic category prompts like "best form builder."
- If pages are indexed but nothing happens, create more content or improve it. LLMs summarize in short, digestible paragraphs, so write that way.
- Include a facts section answering longtail conversational questions (for example "what is the best form builder for building hospital admission forms"). Use H1 and H2 for primary keywords and H3s plus facts for longtail.
- Many users do not read your page; they read the LLM's summary, so write for the summary.

## The 80/20 recipe for your first 10 to 15 articles

- Map the competition: list content ranking on page one for your target keywords and prompts.
- Audit each for elements (facts section, comparison tables, word count) and for weaknesses such as missing information or no embedded video.
- Match the competition's comprehensiveness, then one-up them as many times as possible. He compares it to tabloid covers: "10 tips" versus "11 tips."
- Add a short, lightweight one-minute video reviewing each tool if competitors lack one.
- Summary checklist: audit AI visibility; track citations and mentions; pursue earned media intentionally (paid listicles, or Reddit posts where the founder contributes useful information); create bottom-of-funnel content; target conversational queries with long variations; add unique content; include LLM-friendly elements (digestible paragraphs, facts, subheadings); one-up competitors.
