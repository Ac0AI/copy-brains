## The "First Dollar" challenge setup

- Rob recruited three people for a challenge: 6 weeks and a $100 budget to build something from scratch and make their first dollar online.
- Week one task: build a landing page in 48 hours.
- Week two task: get someone actually paying. Not a friend, not a free user, but a real paying customer who also gives valuable feedback.
- Rob defines "first dollar" strictly: money from a product the person built and sold themselves, not income from working for other people.
- He says the deadline pressure matters. One participant said having Rob set weekly timelines "is way more different" than self-imposed ones and has "really helped me progress with the pace."

## Build in an area you know, or at least care about

- Rob would advise 99% of founders to stay in an area they are expert in.
- Reasoning: an expert builds with the least effort a product that is "so much better than everybody else" in that field, and is self-motivated to build and scale it.
- He agrees with a participant (a finance-industry veteran) who refused a suggested business helping young people break into finance. The participant had left that industry for personal reasons and wasn't interested in recruitment.
- His rule: if it's something you don't care about or aren't interested in, passing is fair. Honest answers about what you actually believe are better than chasing a "logical" idea.

## Validate the idea with real people before building

- One participant (Daily Sensei) planned a Telegram bot teaching AI daily to non-technical solo founders.
- He posted in groups asking for feedback. The main objection was not the idea but the channel: Telegram isn't widely used by non-technical people.
- The idea morphed into an iPhone app, with payment through the Apple ecosystem so people "don't feel that they're paying" (just a double tap).
- The landing page went live at dailysensei.com with a waitlist.
- He ran an open call meetup in Latvia: 170 people applied for 30 spots. He plans to send the MVP to those applicants first.
- Rob's takeaway across the participants: talk to users first, use the feedback to adjust the landing page and product.

## Pricing: charge more from the start

- The Daily Sensei builder said he likes the idea of charging more from the start but that charging more is "a big mental block."
- He also had a fixation on getting somebody to pay exactly $1.
- The source shows the block, not Rob's counsel on it. Rob's own stated rule here is simply that the goal is a real paying customer.

## Landing page packaging: outcomes over functionality

- Rob's critique of the "Cadenza" landing page (a SaaS for small business owners' social media publishing cadence): the headline "Generates a week of Instagram posts in 60 seconds" is functionality-focused.
- His fix: make it outcome focused. Ask "why do I care about generating a week of Instagram posts?" The answer (building social presence to drive customers) is what to lead with.
- His reasoning: "All the businesses care about is more money."
- A business's success depends on delivering results, but also on getting users in the door and setting expectations for those results, which is "having the right packaging."
- Rob says the packaging should be tested with users rather than assumed good.

## Reddit for early feedback

- The Cadenza builder stitched the landing page together from components and templates found online.
- Rob endorsed his plan to use Reddit and speak to small business owners to get critical feedback on initial reception, then fine-tune the landing page and product accordingly.
- Rob called it important to commit to speaking to users to adjust the landing page.

## Controversy and attention

- A clip from episode one went viral. It showed a participant (18 years old) describing 12 Codex subscriptions running 10 agents in parallel, and critics said he was wasting money and not building anything real. A large streamer commented on it live to about 2,500 people.
- Context Rob gave: the participant has multiple clients for whom he builds free SEO tools at scale, ranks them on Google, and earns more than the rest of his household combined.
- Rob distinguishes this client income from the challenge goal, which is revenue from his own product.
- The viral attention brought inbound requests from people wanting SEO growth help.
- Rob told him "who cares what these people are saying" given he supports his family. The participant's view: attention can't be orchestrated, "all I can do is show up and stay true to myself."
