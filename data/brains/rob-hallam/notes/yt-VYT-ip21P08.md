## Rob's background and the failures that came before SuperX

- Rob studied computer science, became a software engineer, and was laid off by his first employer. He decided he was done with corporate and started his journey on X in early 2023.
- Over the next two and a half years he built and launched five products that made "a whopping zero dollars" in total.
- He says those failures taught him three things: distribution matters, monetization matters from day one, and you should build painkillers, not vitamins.
- His biggest takeaway is that sharing the hard times along with the wins built trust in the community. He posted every struggle and failure along the way, and that led to his breakthrough.

## How a vulnerable viral post became a $3,000 client and a dev agency

- After the five failures, money was scarce and he had made none in over a year. He posted about all of his failures, and the post got about 150,000 views on X.
- One comment on it suggested he start a development agency using his skills. He announced the agency on the momentum of that post and landed his first $3,000 client from it.
- All of his agency clients came inbound through X DMs from viral posts.
- He had no process for creating viral content, so he built one. He used AI, data and analytics to systematically create viral content on X, and made the most money of his life.
- That process led to his partnership with Tibo, the former co-founder of Tweet Hunter, who had acquired the SuperX Chrome extension. Rob built the SuperX web app, combining his analytics and content process into his "dream X growth tool".
- His view on why the vulnerable post worked: "so many people feel this way too."

## SuperX: the product, pricing and key numbers

- SuperX shows which posts went viral, why they worked, and helps users create their own versions. Its parts:
  - **Inspiration page:** daily generated post ideas built from what is going viral now plus the user's context.
  - **Chrome extension:** works on any profile, shows top posts sortable by impressions or likes, and estimated X earnings. Users send viral concepts into SuperX to write and schedule content.
  - **SuperX Engage:** a newly released feature that walks users step by step through replying to posts from people in their community and tracks the replies in one place.
- Pricing is $29/month subscriptions, with about 450 active subscribers.
- He took the business from about $1,000 to over $13,000 MRR in four months after the public launch, growing about 25% month over month. The video quotes both $12,000 and $13,000; the $13,000 figure is his own.
- Revenue is split between Stripe and Lemon Squeezy, after a recent migration. Trial rate is about 30%, with about 60 active trials. Churn is close to 30%, and he is working to bring it down.
- About 95% of traffic and most users came from X. Since he started using SuperX properly (around April), he has held about 100,000 impressions a day, versus inconsistent impressions when he posted randomly. He reached over 34,000 followers in just over a year.
- He used SuperX to grow SuperX, which he calls living proof the system works.

## Rob's launch timeline and what actually drove signups

- In January 2025 he made a New Year's resolution to start vlogging. A post about filming in public, written at the Amsterdam airport on the way to Rio de Janeiro, had over 1 million views when he landed. He closed his agency and began building SuperX around the same time.
- The first seven months were brutal: two hospitalizations from food poisoning (Guatemala and Colombia), constant X API changes that broke SuperX, and tens of "SuperX is down" messages every day. He kept building and posting.
- The July launch post was a standard screen recording with a breakdown of features. It got 32,000 views and 26 signups.
- He then posted a selfie of himself in front of his laptop. It got over 46,000 views and did not advertise the product, yet it drove far more signups than the launch post. It added $1,000 in MRR in 24 hours.
- He took that as a signal to keep going and applied the system built into SuperX to grow SuperX.
- His takeaway on why story beats product posts: with so much AI "slop" content, "people want to follow people. People want to follow stories." Sharing your story, personality and struggles honestly is how you build an audience.
- He also says: "You don't need followers to start. You need consistency, a story line, and a system."

## Rob's 7-step playbook for building an audience on X from zero

1. **Set your foundation before posting.** Use a real, friendly, smiling face as the profile photo and don't change it often. Write a one-sentence bio. Pin a post with your goal or elevator pitch. His was: left my job and home to live on savings and try to build my dream SaaS to $10K a month. People who click through from the feed must instantly understand what you're doing.
2. **Pick one specific goal and document it daily.** This gives consistency (people forget you otherwise), a storyline (people follow journeys), and authenticity from sharing ups and downs, which builds trust.
3. **Run the content loop.** Content falls into four types: entertaining, educational, inspirational and convincing (selling). Post them in order, with intention:
   - Entertaining first: funny, relatable, vulnerable, or a fun tool launch. This earns engagement and warms up the algorithm.
   - Then educational or selling, pitched from goodwill, for example "here's how you grow on X with SuperX".
   - Then inspirational: screenshot the views, followers or sales and explain what it means to you (why you left your job). Write it from the heart so people root for you.
   - Repeat from the start. The loop builds trust, educates and sells without being annoying.
4. **Systemize what works with data.** Don't guess what to post. Analyze top-performing posts of successful profiles in your niche (he uses the SuperX Chrome extension, but any tool works). Copy the concept, not the post word for word, and restyle it with your personality.
5. **Double down on what works and kill what doesn't.** Track every post's performance, then find patterns in format, style and topic. For Rob, video and feature releases plus authentic moments work best. The algorithm rewards consistency. Kill underperformers, even a viral one.
6. **Build real connections.** Don't chase virality alone. Reply with real value to people you genuinely want to connect with: share experience and stories, ask thoughtful questions, and show up daily. Avoid AI-generated spam replies like "Great post". He treats many followers as real friends and has met them while traveling.
7. **Leverage what the platform wants.** X currently pushes video because it keeps people on the platform longer. Pay attention and adapt as this changes.

## How Rob used data to choose what to build and what to cut

- Concrete example: he used SuperX to analyze what was going viral in his niche and found that a "curse for tweets" and an "algorithm simulator" were performing. He built both as SuperX features, posted a demo of them dancing, and it worked.
- His formula was to combine a proven viral concept with his own personality.
- He later removed the algorithm simulator from SuperX even though it went viral, because most users didn't use it.
- Switching from text to video format gave him 10 times the reach of text posts.
- His conclusion: it isn't about luck. It comes from consistent storytelling that builds trust, systemizing virality with data and analytics, and building intentional, real friendships. "If you do this, you won't need paid ads."

## Rob's tech stack, costs and building philosophy

- Philosophy: pick one stack, learn it deeply, and ignore the noise. Too many people chase new technologies instead of shipping.
- Stack:
  - **Development:** Next.js, Node.js, Tailwind
  - **Database:** SQLite
  - **Data source:** X API, about $2,000 to $3,000/month
  - **AI:** OpenAI and Anthropic Claude Sonnet APIs, about $1,000/month
  - **Servers:** AWS, about $200/month
  - **Semantic search:** Zilliz Cloud for vector embeddings, for semantic similarity search on tweets, about $200/month
  - **AI coding:** Claude Code, about $200/month
  - **Demo videos:** Screen Studio, about $10/month
  - **Landing page:** Framer, about $10/month
  - **Payments and analytics:** Stripe and PostHog

## Rob's closing advice

- His advice to his younger self: breathe, and do things with intention, not with emotion.
- Figure out what you want, cut out the noise, and then do "an insane amount of volume with intention".
- Take care of yourself. If you do all of this, "it's just a matter of time."
- He is clear that building in public is not for everyone, but if you are willing to put yourself out there and "embrace the cringe", the chances of building a successful product are much higher.
