## Why Rob left the UK at 23 to build a business abroad

- Rob argues the UK in 2025 makes it hard to build: a soaring cost of living, unaffordable housing and a stretched NHS.
- He says the same lifestyle abroad costs about half as much with far less stress, so the goal is to "actually live and not just survive".
- He is leaving friends, family and his comfort zone to build a business from a cheaper base.
- The downside is capped and accepted. If he fails, he burns through his savings and returns to the UK.
- His first stop was Brazil: Rio first, then a digital-nomad city in the north that he expected to have better Wi-Fi and be safer.
- He treats discomfort as the point: "If it wasn't uncomfortable I wouldn't be growing."

## From two years of failed products to a client agency

- For almost two years Rob built project after project for himself, spending weeks designing and coding each one. Each got zero users and zero revenue.
- The turning point was to stop building products for himself and build them for other people.
- One project led to another, and in a little under 4 months his agency brought in over $38,000.
- That agency income is what pays for his travel and gives him runway.

## Pausing the agency to take a real shot at his own SaaS

- Rob put the agency on pause to try building his own SaaS business.
- He is starting from scratch in a new country, with a new language (Portuguese), on his savings.
- He frames the move as using agency cash flow to fund a bigger bet, not as abandoning client work for good.

## Filming a build in public when it's all new

- Rob had never made content, vlogged or filmed himself before this trip. He is learning by watching people who have done it successfully and reading articles.
- Filming felt uncomfortable and carried a high resistance barrier, because everything about it was new.
- His workaround is to film in quiet moments, such as an empty airport gate, and add B-roll later.
- The upside he sees is sharing what he learns in real time while he learns it.

## How one post gave him an audience: the viral X post

- Rob posted on X while flying to Rio. He expected maybe 2,000 to 5,000 views if it went well.
- It passed 750,000 views, with about 4,000 likes, after levels.io quote-retweeted and retweeted it. Rob describes levels.io as the "grandfather" of digital-nomad, build-in-public founders on X.
- He saw it taking off mid-flight, on paid in-flight Wi-Fi, at 38,000 ft.
- By the time he landed 12 hours later he had gained almost 5,000 followers.
- His YouTube channel had no videos, yet it gained more than 300 subscribers.
- Afterwards he got countless DMs on X and LinkedIn, plus messages from friends, including a lot of offers.

## Why Rob argues a personal brand is leverage in 2025

- His claim is that with AI replacing services and mundane tasks, an authentic personal brand "cannot be copied because no one can copy you for being you".
- He says that brand has "insane power and leverage" and opens doors, as the inbound offers after the viral post showed.
- He accepts that content creation isn't for everyone. His advice is still that if you are thinking about it, you should just do it, because "something can happen that can change everything for you".

## Working while traveling: friction and the operating cost

- Constant movement adds uncertainty and variability. He had to find groceries, a gym, a new culture, a new language and new people, all while keeping up with work.
- Crossing many people and public spaces raises the odds of catching a bug. Rob was sick for a couple of days in Rio, which cost him some working time.
- Local gaps also bite. Food delivery in Brazil needed a CPF (a Brazilian social security number) or a Brazilian phone number, so a local contact had to order for him.
- He picks destinations partly for infrastructure. He chose his next stop in part for its better Wi-Fi and access to what he needs to work.
- He kept a work routine through all of this, putting in long days in his first days in Rio and fitting in a couple more hours before sightseeing.
