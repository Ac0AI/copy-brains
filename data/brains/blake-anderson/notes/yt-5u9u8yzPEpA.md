## Building iOS apps with AI: ideas, validation, and the marketing engine behind three apps

Blake taught himself to code with ChatGPT in May and June 2023 and launched his first app in July 2023. He has since built or co-founded three AI apps. Rizz GPT is an AI dating assistant that reached about $2.2M in annual revenue. Umax is an AI looksmaxxing app that did almost $5M in revenue in a year. Cal AI, co-founded with two partners who run operations, did over $1M per month in revenue within about six months of launch. Blake came up with the Cal AI idea and helped with designs, but did not build the app. He set himself a goal right after college of making $50,000 on his own within 12 months, while broke and living at home.

## Why consumer apps are lucrative right now

Blake gives two reasons.

- **AI:** LLMs let a single builder solve problems that were previously impossible or too expensive. "It would have been virtually impossible to build an AI dating assistant without the use of LLMs."
- **Novel distribution:** TikTok and Instagram are "very solvable right now". Because the platforms are in "bloodbath competition", they have to incentivize new creators and push more views to new accounts than they did a few years ago. That makes influencer, user-generated content, and organic account strategies work for funneling millions of people to a product.

## Finding and validating app ideas

Blake used a different method for each app.

- **Rizz GPT:** a problem he had observed directly. Friends kept posting in fraternity group chats asking what to say to a girl, so he built an app to answer that.
- **Umax:** found more opportunistically. When people want to become more attractive they spend heavily on skin care, hair care, and gua sha, but no software existed for the problem.
- **Niche signal:** "When you find a niche that's heavily monetized with physical products, that's a good indicator that the niche converts." It is also a sign that software can solve a problem inside it.
- **Validation:** he deep-dives the niche on social media. He creates a fresh account and consumes only that niche's content (for example calorie counting, dieting, and weightlifting for a calorie app). He becomes the target demographic, works out what he wants, and builds that.

Idea areas he thinks are still open:
- A career AI platform for students, rolling fragmented tools (AI LinkedIn image generator, AI resume analysis) into one brand, as a mobile and web app.
- Language learning built on ChatGPT advanced voice mode as a personal tutor. Duolingo makes about $33M a month, roughly $400M a year, and 1% of that market would be a $4M-a-year app.
- An all-in-one AI glow-up app. Umax was a first iteration, and he believes a more valuable one could still make millions.

## The Rizz GPT launch story

- The first version was bad. It had no notifications, no reviews, a messy design, and a terrible paywall. Early promos produced few downloads and a few dollars.
- He then found two underground, undiscovered creators and paid each $50, $100 total.
- Result: 5 to 10 million views overnight, 45,000 downloads on the first big day, 200,000 downloads that week, and 500,000 that month.
- The app reached about $80k MRR from the start, scaled to a little over $250k MRR, then settled at roughly $150k to $200k MRR.
- Takeaway: an MVP plus a smart marketing strategy is enough to start earning. The app does not need to be perfect.

## Build stack and process

- **Design:** Figma. He calls learning it one of the biggest unlocks, even for someone who will hire designers off Upwork. His method is to collect reference apps with similar functionality and design, and use them to guide his own.
- **Code:** React Native with Expo, using Cursor as the IDE. Backend is Node.js.
- **Other tools:** CapCut for content, Mercury for startup banking, Upwork for occasional contract work.
- **Hiring on Upwork:** hire five to ten people to do an hour or two of work each, then stick with the best one.

## Marketing: influencers, in-house UGC, and persistence

Blake uses two main methods.

- **Influencer marketing:** pay someone with an existing audience to promote the product.
- **Internal UGC:** you or a hired creator makes content about the app and posts it on accounts branded around the app. Early on he makes it himself in CapCut, mixing slideshows, faceless video, and talking-to-camera content.
- **Iterate ruthlessly:** try different strategies, and when one produces profitable returns on the time or money invested, "double down and double down and double down."

Most people fail at outreach because they DM ten influencers, get no replies within 24 hours, and give up. Blake's approach is to get creative about reaching them.
- The most profitable Rizz GPT creator was a teenager, 18 or 19, posting for fun, and hard to reach.
- Blake found a Discord link hidden in the creator's Instagram bio, joined it, and sent a message every 10 minutes until the creator replied.
- He also DM'd some creators' mothers asking for a contact to their sons.

## Monetization, margins, and costs

- **Model:** subscriptions. He says everyone he knows who makes serious money on apps uses them.
- **Pricing:** he generally goes for lower price points. More people can use the app, they tell friends, user sentiment on social media is better, and it is more sustainable for long-term growth. Many apps charge much more.
- **Paywall testing:** he recommends Superwall to split-test offerings (weekly, monthly, yearly), price points, and placements within the app. He says this can take revenue per download from X to 1.5X or 2X.
- **Apple's cut:** 15% officially but closer to 20% in practice on the first $1M of earnings. Beyond that it is 30%, closer to 33% in practice.
- **AI costs:** people overestimate them. Unless an app does image generation or advanced voice mode, backend AI costs are under 3% of revenue. The bulk of expenses are marketing and staff.
- **Margins by marketing channel:**
  - Internal UGC: about 50% to 80% profit margin.
  - Influencer marketing: about 25% to 70%.
  - Paid ads: about 0% to 30%.
- **Profit figures:** Umax does a little over $100k profit a month consistently (it used to do more). Cal AI does a few hundred thousand dollars of profit a month. He declined to share Rizz GPT's.

## Mindset: urgency and first principles

- **Urgency:** his most productive periods came when risk and urgency were high. Building Rizz GPT, he was at home making $0 while his parents' house was on the market and friends took six-figure jobs. Building Umax, he had just split from his Rizz GPT team and felt he had something to prove, coding alone on his brother's couch over Thanksgiving. He says to create that sense of urgency if you have "that dog in you."
- **Rizz GPT split:** he left after he and a co-founder kept clashing over who led the company. He told the co-founder that one of them runs it and keeps equity while the other leaves, and the co-founder chose to keep running it.
- **Think from first principles:** step away from what you hear on social media and don't just do what others say. He cites a Naval quote: "If you want to make the wrong decision, ask everybody."
- **Current project:** Apex, an all-in-one self-improvement ecosystem that combines content, a free community, free mobile apps, and low-margin, high-quality physical products. It was about a month and a half old at the time.
