## Blake's background and path to Cal AI

- Blake Anderson is 25 and from Philadelphia. He was co-founder, investor and advisor at Cal AI.
- He began building apps at Tulane. His first apps mostly failed.
- He then switched to building AI apps. After his first two apps, he got in contact with Zach, and the two began building Cal AI together.
- He describes the hand-off as Zach's DM to him, which started the partnership.

## Why a copycat pitch would not have worked on Blake

- He says copying an existing app and attaching a strong distribution strategy can get you to $10,000 a month or even $100,000 a month.
- He argues the outcomes are much bigger when you build something new, and says he finds that "much more inspiring" on a philosophical level.
- He uses his own story as the test. If Zach's first DM had been "Hey, I created a copycat of this," he says "I wouldn't have responded and we never would have worked together."
- He says many legitimate professionals feel the same way. The people worth building with tend to be drawn to original ideas.

## How big the iOS app opportunity is in 2026

- Cal AI looks like a huge win (about $50M a year), but he says it was a tiny slice of the whole market.
- The App Store as a whole is about $100 billion a year. Cal AI was roughly 1/12,000th of that total opportunity.
- The market is growing over 10% year over year. He expects it to pass $200 billion in annual spend on the iOS App Store alone within about 6 years.
- Most people picture the opportunity as a fixed pie. He says that once you apply new technology, AI or otherwise, the real opportunity is much larger than that picture suggests.

## Where Blake says to look for ideas

- Take existing ideas and put a differentiated spin on them. The spin can be different features, a different approach or different distribution.
- He thinks iOS apps will keep getting bigger every year. If he were starting now, building iOS apps is what he would do.
- His answer to "is the app store too flooded?" is yes, the opportunity is still available, as long as the app is differentiated.

## Blake's next project: 10X App Builder

- After the Cal AI sale he is working on 10X App Builder, which he describes as "Shopify for apps."
- It goes beyond writing code for the user. It helps with ideation, hosts user backends, and helps users design their Instagram page in an aesthetic manner.
- He works from the 10X office. 10X Studio runs there and has several subsidiary companies, and friends' startups share the space.
- He recommends it to anyone who is new to building apps.

## Why he keeps building after a big exit

- He describes entrepreneurship as a video game he does not want to stop playing.
- His analogy: if you were playing GTA, you would never put your character down and watch TV for 4 hours. For him, entrepreneurship is that game.
