## Origin story and the "last shot" mindset

- Blake grew up outside Philadelphia. As a kid he ran lemonade stands and wrote an ebook, "how to make money as a 7-year-old". He has never had a normal job or internship.
- His father was a successful entrepreneur until Blake was about 12-13, then neurological issues (including secondary progressive MS) sank the businesses. By graduation from Tulane his parents had sold the house and he moved back home. His older brother was lending him money for groceries.
- He started building Riz GPT as he graduated, choosing between a normal job and one last shot on his own. He describes it as a "burn the boats" bet.
- He says he'd "rather live at the extreme with a 50% chance that it all crashes and burns" than live a monotonous life.
- He admits that framing sounds dramatic for "a [__] app called Riz GPT", but says that's how it felt at the time.

## Riz GPT launch and distribution

- Riz GPT was his first app. He coded it about 12 hours a day for over a month, only coding.
- Launch tactic: find two underrated, unknown TikTok creators and pay each about $50.
- Result: overnight virality, one video at 2 million views and one at 4 million, with hundreds of thousands of downloads.
- Riz GPT hit about $200,000 a month very quickly, and he says he was still walking to and from Starbucks at that point.
- It still makes around $200 a month with little effort.

## Revenue across his apps

- Blake's tally (iOS plus a small Android amount): about $10 million in total sales.
  - Ci app: $2.4M
  - Improvement Tech / Umax (looksmaxing app): $4.2M
  - Riz GPT, lifetime: $2.6M
- Profit attributable to him is roughly $2.5-3M, after Apple's 30% cut and after giving money to his parents and brother.
- The apps keep earning without him. He estimates that if he stopped working today, the profit attributable to him would still be in the range of $5-10M.
- Riz GPT still does about $200 a month and Umax $50-400 a month (his figures, unclear on timeframe).

## Acquisition channels for the apps

- Primary channel: influencer marketing.
- Secondary: internal UGC (user generated content) and paid platform ads on Meta and TikTok.
- His view: everything works, but influencers are the most profitable strategy.

## How to learn to code and build fast

- Blake says coding returns are nonlinear (even "exponential or quadratic"), like surfing or video editing.
- Coding once a day for 50 days beats once a week for 50 weeks, even though both are 50 sessions.
- He recommends long, concentrated stretches. His Riz GPT month was 12 hours a day.
- He codes in Cursor, an AI-powered IDE, and says he is "not very good at coding" himself and leans on AI tools.
- At the time he was building the Apex Improve application.

## Thinking long-term and running a team

- He doesn't set goals like "make $10 million". He asks how to maximize his impact and the footprint he leaves, and sees money as the resource that funds a long-term vision.
- He works with two close college friends as co-founders. Each owns a primary domain and its processes and stack.
- He wants to pass lessons from earlier ventures to co-founders as fast as possible, with "no ego" and focus on "the information and decisions at hand".
- Team of about five lives and works together in a rented house in San Diego ("the Apex house"), with a daily check-in at a whiteboard.
- He picks surroundings (big views, nature, ocean, hikes) that help him "think clearly".

## Funding Apex from the old apps

- His next company, Apex, is self-funded from app cash flow.
- The team projects losing about $60-70K a month minimum for the foreseeable future.
- He plans to put at least a few million into making Apex succeed, and says about $1 million is in so far.
- Past app income keeps cash flowing, which lets him fund the bigger vision without outside money.
