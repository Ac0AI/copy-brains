## Cal AI, RizzGPT and Umax: building viral apps with AI and creator marketing

- Blake's first app, RizzGPT, was built when he had almost no money. His family had run out of funds and his parents sold their house. He moved back home and borrowed grocery money from his older brother. He calls RizzGPT his "last real chance".
- The app lets users upload screenshots of conversations and returns suggested replies, pickup lines and conversation topics.
- He taught himself to code with AI help and no formal training. He spent about 12 hours a day for over a month "just behind the computer coding, only coding". He says he is "not very good at coding".
- He started building it senior year at Tulane, torn between a normal full-time job and building something of his own. He has never held a real job.

## Launching with two $50 creators

- To launch RizzGPT, Blake found two relatively unknown, underrated TikTok creators and paid them $50 each to make promotional videos.
- The videos went viral overnight, with millions of views and hundreds of thousands of downloads. He did not need large creators to get started.
- Afterward he realized he could repeat the same formula to build more apps.
- His view of the economics: "these businesses don't just die". RizzGPT was still doing about $200k a month.

## Reusing the formula: Umax and Cal AI

- Umax is a looksmaxxing app. Users upload a selfie, and the AI suggests physical improvements, drawing on beauty standards from around the world and the internet.
- Blake picked a topic already trending among young people (looksmaxxing, rizz) and paired it with AI.
- Umax had made $4.2 million on the Apple App Store alone and was still doing $350k to $400k a month. Apple takes 30%.
- Even after Apple's cut, he estimates that if he stopped working today, the profit attributable to him would be at least $5 to $10 million.
- Cal AI is his third app. Users photograph meals and AI estimates calories.
- Across the apps, he says they still cashflow "a couple hundred" thousand a month, which funds his bigger ventures.

## Distribution: influencer marketing first

- Blake says the apps grow "primarily by influencer marketing".
- He also runs internal UGC and paid platform ads on Meta and TikTok.
- He uses everything, but influencers are the most profitable channel.

## Tools and the skill barrier

- Blake works in Cursor, an AI code editor, which he describes as "like an AIDE".
- He taught himself by learning basic sequences, through trial and error, and with more capable AI tools over time.
- His position is that coding experience is not required to build profitable apps.

## Long-term vision over short-term money

- Blake says he does not think about business as "how can I make $10 million". He thinks about "how I can maximize my impact in the footprint that I leave on the world".
- He says it is "nonsensical to think about money in the short term". You need the resources to build out the long-term vision.
- App profits fund that vision.

## Apex: reinvesting app profits into a community

- Blake started Apex, a community teaching how to build and market viral mobile apps. It has two courses, App Building 101 and the Web App Blueprint.
- He says he intends to put "at least a few million" into making Apex succeed and had put in about $1 million so far.
- The community is expected to lose about $60k to $70k a month minimum for the foreseeable future, partly because he pays his co-founders a solid salary.
- His co-founders are his best friends from college. Each owns a primary domain and stack, and he wants to transfer lessons from his entrepreneurship to them quickly.
- On risk: "I would rather live at the extreme with a 50% chance that it all crashes and burns."
- He gives money to his parents and his older brother, who lent him money in college.
