## How Blake built Riz GPT (now Plug AI) with ChatGPT and found its first growth channel

- Riz GPT is an AI dating assistant that launched in July 2023 and is now named Plug AI. It reached $2.5M ARR.
- The idea came from a college roommate who kept asking what to say to girls on dating apps. Blake pasted a transcript into ChatGPT, found the answer too "AI-like", and decided to fix it through prompt engineering.
- He had zero software experience, only a little Python. He says "ChatGPT taught me how to build the app and then ChatGPT provided the main functionality for the app". About 99% of the code was written by ChatGPT.
- The app was simple: a gradient background, a logo and two buttons. You upload a screenshot and get responses.
- An incumbent Riz app existed but had not gone mainstream or viral.
- At launch, marketing was not sticking. Blake found an untapped niche on TikTok and paid $50 for each of two promos, $100 total.
- Result: over 200,000 downloads in six days and $80,000 MRR right away. It scaled to a little over $200,000 MRR within a couple of months.
- The app launched without notifications and without review requests. He backfilled those afterward.

## Launch early on single-function consumer utility apps

- Blake's lesson from Riz GPT: "you can learn that [stuff] along the way, the most important thing is just get your product out there."
- He applies this to consumer utility apps with a few functions. It does not apply to social networks.
- The reasoning: ship, get the insights, find out whether it works, then backfill what you need.
- He says there is "no reason to delay the launch" for a few-function tool.

## Marketing consumer apps: a ruthless cycle of iteration

- Blake rejects one-size-fits-all advice such as "you need to use this TikTok strategy" or YouTube Shorts, Reels, Reddit or X. Certain channels may work better at certain times.
- He calls the most important factor for buzz and virality "a ruthless cycle of iteration", meaning constant testing of new strategies.
- Time to find the winning formula:
  - Riz GPT: a couple of weeks.
  - UMAX: about a month and a half.
  - Cal AI: more than a month and still not found at the time, though it was already near $100K MRR.
- Finding the first channel is not the end. Crossing the first threshold of views "doesn't mean that you figured out your final form". He says to keep iterating on marketing until you are finished with the company.

## Building an app with ChatGPT: his process

- Building Riz GPT took him about a month. He says it would have been quicker had he known the IDEs.
- UMAX was built in about a month. For UMAX, roughly 85% of the code was still written by ChatGPT.
- Steps for a consumer mobile app:
  1. Start with the designs. Decide what the product looks like.
  2. Pick the stack: React Native, SwiftUI or Kotlin. He builds in SwiftUI.
  3. Describe the design and the button functionality very clearly to ChatGPT. You can also upload a screenshot of the design.
  4. Ask ChatGPT to write the code, paste it into Xcode and preview it.
  5. If the spacing is off, adjust it yourself or ask ChatGPT to adjust it.
- Takeaway: "it's a lot easier than you think, you just have to start." Early growing pains (learning the IDE, code that won't compile) pass quickly. He describes ChatGPT as having your own professional tutor at your fingertips.

## UMAX: finding the idea and building it fast

- UMAX is a looksmaxing app. You scan your face, get a rating, and receive personalized recommendations to become more attractive. It launched at the start of December 2023, two days after Thanksgiving, and reached a little over $6M ARR.
- Blake left Riz GPT in mid-to-late October 2023 after a disagreement with a co-founder over product direction. They split amicably and he kept his equity. He was effectively unemployed, hopping between friends' couches and his brother's couch.
- Idea finding: he saw looksmaxing growing and judged it the next step after lifting content, since most men lift to appeal to women. He became "a student of the game" by engaging with all the content and thinking like someone interested in looksmaxing.
- Two unmet needs he spotted:
  - People wanted to know how attractive they were. Whole subreddits and "rate me" comments existed for this.
  - People wanted personalized tips, but the tips online were generic.
- The GPT Vision API was coming out, so he could prompt engineer face analysis instead of training an ML model. Early tests recommended shaving a patchy beard and skincare for forehead acne.
- He coded round two with ChatGPT and launched in about a month and a few days. December revenue was about $100,000. By the first half of January the app was around $200K MRR.
- His brother came up with the Madden-style rating system, which Blake calls the most pivotal decision for the company's trajectory.

## Responding to a copycat by deploying all revenue into influencers

- In January, a competitor copied UMAX completely: the app and the marketing strategy.
- Blake's original plan was to grow slowly and improve LTV and the offering before "burning the forest" through core users. He had done a couple hundred thousand downloads.
- As a bootstrapped company, he faced a choice: go slow and risk being overtaken, or fight back. Fighting meant trading some long-term conversion, since pushing millions of downloads before the product is in final form makes users hard to reconvert. Given the market, he decided to fight back.
- Within three days he deployed over $200,000 on influencer campaigns, which was all the revenue and earnings to that point.
- Results over about five days:
  - MRR went from $200K to over $500K.
  - About 100,000 downloads in one day.
  - Daily revenue of about $50K, $40K, $15K and $25K.
- He did not sleep much during this period. He was on the phone with influencers, coding, and working with engineers and his social media manager. Afterward the app plateaued at a little over $500K MRR.

## Reinvesting profits, no plan B, and team and co-founder choices

- Blake says he is very risk-taking and has no Plan B: "either this works or it goes bad and if it goes bad we'll assess that then."
- His pattern is doubling down: Riz GPT money funded UMAX, and UMAX money will fund his next big play.
- On co-founders, he has no formula. He chooses based on the role and the gaps in his own skill set.
  - Riz GPT: two co-founders from his college marketplace app.
  - UMAX: a near solo start, then his older brother and an influencer as co-founders.
  - Cal AI: two 17-year-old co-founders, Zach Yadegari and Henry Langmack, with equal ownership.
- On solo versus team, he cites the "85% of a watermelon is better than 100% of a grape" idea. If two people each make 80% of decisions right with no overlap, combined they make 96% right.
- On motivating a team, he says he does not have it fully solved. His approach is to play to who you are and not imitate someone else. He admits he can be hard, difficult and stubborn, but he really cares, and that shows.

## Cal AI and where he sees opportunity in AI

- Cal AI is an AI calorie tracker. The thesis: calorie trackers take too much time and he quit every one he used after a couple of days. With Cal AI you take a picture and get the calorie analysis.
- It also keeps the standard features: barcode scanning and manual macro input. Photo capture is the differentiator.
- It was around $100K MRR at the time, in a space with about 50 big competitors. He was comfortable sharing it early because a copycat would have to beat all those incumbents too. UMAX remained his main priority.
- Opportunity areas he points to with GPT-4o and native multimodal voice:
  - Audio and social simulation, including dating simulation, to counter people becoming more antisocial as typing becomes the main way to socialize.
  - Learning apps, such as a language tutor or a voice quiz tool that tests what you know and recommends resources.
- Package and reskin: even if ChatGPT can do something natively, "the vast majority of people" won't know they can use it there. So there is still a large opportunity in packaging or reskinning an LLM capability.
- Advice to builders: deviate from the norm in what you think, say and do. He warns that the Silicon Valley bubble is an echo chamber where too many people work on the same talked-about ideas. He says "a lot of the alpha lies when you push past the veil of the public domain". Nobody was talking about an AI looksmaxing app when he built it.
- He says video games, such as strategy and life simulations, give rapid repetitions of economic and strategic tradeoffs without putting real capital, time or stakes on the line.
