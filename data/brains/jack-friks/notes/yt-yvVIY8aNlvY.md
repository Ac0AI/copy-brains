## Doing something 100 times as the default way to get good

- Jack's core advice: if you want to get better at something, or find out whether you want to do it at all, do it 100 times. He calls it simple, direct and unglamorous, and says simple advice is usually the most effective.
- The idea comes from Visakan Veerasamy ("visakanv") on Twitter. Jack applies it to his own work.
- Examples of the rule: to get better at public speaking, give 100 talks. To get better at talking to people, talk to 100 people.
- Jack calls it a numbers game. Do a hundred things and you either get better at it or some of them turn out good.
- He says almost nobody succeeds overnight. People know this but don't want to accept that the answer is just doing it 100, 1,000 or 100,000 times.

## Examples of volume producing quality

- **Picasso:** he made about 50,000 pieces. Jack puts the ones that were true masterpieces at roughly 100, so under 1% were good enough to still matter today. The lesson is not to do fewer things. It is to do lots of things, keep trying and iterate.
- **Running:** a runner who keeps running improves, and one who stops doesn't. Jack says the choice of intervals, speed work or program matters less than continuing to run.
- **YouTube:** he mentions PewDiePie's reported advice to make 3,000 videos, not 300. Three videos give you low odds of a hit. 3,000 raise your odds and make you better at it.
- **Writing:** a great writer who "appears from nowhere" has usually written thousands of pieces before the one that hits.
- **Podcasting:** someone with 1,400 episodes (Joe Rogan is his example) is rarely in the bottom tier of hosts.
- **Music:** Kanye West said to lock yourself in a room and make five songs a day for three summers.
- **Ed Sheeran:** "when you turn on a dirty faucet, dirty water comes out for a long time. Then, eventually, clean water starts to flow." Jack's takeaway is that much of your output will be bad, but gems are buried in it, and doing many reps is how you find them. Even when it doesn't feel like progress, it is incremental progress.

## Applying reps to business and creative decisions

- **Investor pitch:** build the deck 99 times and use the 100th version, since it is likely better than the 1st, 2nd, 3rd or 10th.
- **Starting a business:** sell 1,000 of something. His example is "shut up and sell 1,000 chocolate bars". He thinks the best advice for anything you want to create is to do it and repeat it many times.
- **Book title:** write around 400 possible titles and pick the best. The 400th is likely the product of iteration and refinement.
- Across all of these, quantity gives you more chances at quality and makes you better at the craft.

## You don't have to finish all 100

- Jack says you don't need 100 reps of everything you start. Five, three or ten reps can be enough.
- His analogy is pizza pockets: you don't need to eat 100 to know whether you like them. Maybe you try six flavors and they're not for you, or maybe they are and you continue.
- The point is that you tried something and did something. Once you are doing things, the rest gets easier and falls into place, and you learn from it.
- Visakan's framing: drop the excuses and expectations, make 100 bad pieces with no reason and no agenda, at whatever pace feels comfortable. They can be bad or about things you don't care about. You will likely find something useful for your life.

## Progress shows up sooner than expected (the owl example)

- Jack shows Visakan's owl drawings. Visakan set out to draw an owl 100 times, and by the 9th owl the drawing was noticeably better than the first three.
- Jack says the biggest barrier is expecting that you can't do enough reps to see progress. Nine reps was enough for a striking improvement.
- Each rep is a step forward, not always upward, because you move past what you did before.
- After nine owls you might be much better, or you might not have improved and keep going. Or you might decide you don't want to draw owls and stop, which is what Visakan did. You don't need the full 100 to learn what you need to know.
