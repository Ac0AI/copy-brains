## Why Jack says attention is the real problem to solve

- Jack argues that attention is increasingly controlled by algorithms, mainly from social media but also other media that needs you present to make money.
- He says money is not the problem ("maybe the love of money is the bad part"). The problem is the incentive: many platforms, including YouTube, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook, are rewarded more for "screen time than lifetime".
- Result: there is more incentive to take up your lifespan and life force than to give it back.
- He treats attention as a commodity that these companies must treat as such to make money, and says engineers are "paid handsomely" to keep you on the screen.
- He frames this as the background for the app he was building at the time to curb doom scrolling (he said he had been working on it for "210 days", a figure that was garbled in the transcript, so treat it as approximate). He put the practical steps first and the app second.

## Step one: know what you want out of life

- Jack says the key to escaping the cycle of being hooked back into attention-taking apps is knowing what you want out of life. The answer differs for each person.
- Once you know where you want to go, it is much easier to put the phone down and notice that "this thing in my hand is not getting me to where I want to go".
- It is not a one-time fix. Even when you know what you want, the other side of the screen is still engineered to keep you. He says "nothing is like that", meaning no single decision cures it.
- The answer can change, even daily, and that is fine. The goal is to find something you want, try to get it, stay interested and "make it enjoyable".
- He recommends asking yourself the question often, then giving yourself time to rest so your brain can think about it.

## Step two: do nothing and let your brain sort the clutter

- Jack calls the first step counterintuitive: you do nothing. You sit and let your brain run wild, even though it will feel uncomfortable.
- His reasoning: years (he guesses 5 to 10) of scrolling have loaded the brain with a huge amount of information, which he compares to "hundreds of newspapers a day stacked over years". The brain has to sort through it.
- You must "get rid of the clutter" and clear your mind before you can find something you are interested in enough to build a life around.
- He avoids the label meditation, which he says has a bad rep, and calls it "quiet time".
- His own routine:
  - Every morning he sits down and is quiet.
  - He focuses on whatever is going on in his head, or on his breath.
  - He counts breaths from one to six (inhale one, exhale two, and so on), then restarts. He often loses count, and says that is fine.
- Alternatives that work the same way: going to the gym without music, or walking outside without music. He says the boring activities are therapeutic because they give real rest, not "I'm tired, I'll scroll".

## Boredom opens the "curiosity intake"

- Jack's term: once you are bored enough, the "curiosity intake" opens. This is the intake for the things you are genuinely interested in and want to learn about.
- He says it is not a magic pill that hands you the answers. It gives you topics to question and dive into that come organically from your own head, not from an algorithm's feed.
- Why it matters: self-generated curiosity is a strong motivator to learn and take action.
- You work out what you want over time by introspecting on what you did and did not like, not by sitting still alone.
- The goal: scroll and use TikTok less, and be more inclined to follow your curiosity. He admits this sounds boring and that sitting with your eyes closed is "simple but not easy" if you are used to constant consumption.
- He notes that the content loop is weighted against human weaknesses, so the best individual defense is letting your brain genuinely rest and not overstimulating it too often.

## Find the primal emotion behind your goal

- Jack believes most people want the same thing, which is to be happy.
- When you decide what you want, also decide why you want it. Ask what primal emotion it will bring: happiness, love, or perhaps fear (for example, fear that you won't get it, or fear of something else).
- Then focus on that primal emotion while doing the things that get you there. His claim is that the back of your head will then focus on what matters to you, not on the TikToks you just watched or the news around you.

## Jack's daily routine summary

1. Allow yourself to be bored and under-stimulated, or at least not over-stimulated.
2. Take breaths. Make genuine rest a daily routine. It does not need to be meditation or sitting alone. A walk outside without music, or the gym without music, works.
3. Rest for about 10 minutes, then let the curiosity intake open.
4. Follow the things you are genuinely interested in and go down those rabbit holes as deeply as you can. He says what you want out of life is on the other side.
5. Keep asking yourself what you want, and leave time to rest so your brain can think about it.
