## Jack's path to Post Bridge: six years of failed online income attempts

- Jack dropped out of his last semester of college about six years before writing. He wanted to make money online and control his own schedule and work.
- He says he "would be a miserable employee" in a traditional job, and that this drove him to try other routes.
- He spent roughly three years trying to earn money online before he learned to code.
- He then spent about another year trying to grow his first app. The Indie Hackers intro says he thought he was a failure until a new idea gained traction.
- Post Bridge is the idea that worked. By the intro's count, the whole stretch took four years of struggling.

## What Jack tried before learning to code

Jack lists these experiments as part of "trying everything":

- Print-on-demand T-shirt designs.
- Affiliate marketing blog posts.
- YouTube videos: more than 2,000 in under two years.
- Crypto: "I made money and lost it all."

After these he decided to learn to code and build his own apps. None of the pre-coding experiments is credited with the eventual result in the available text.

## Post Bridge: product and revenue

- Post Bridge is a social media scheduling tool for small teams, with agent-friendly workflows.
- Jack launched it about one and a half years before the article.
- It generates $50k CAD in MRR, which he gives as $35K USD.
- Most of his focus currently goes into Post Bridge.

## Growth shape: plateau, then doubling

- MRR plateaued for eight months.
- In the last six months, Jack doubled MRR after that plateau.
- Growth was not a smooth curve. The article's framing is years of struggle, then a product that took off, then a flat stretch, then a second jump.
