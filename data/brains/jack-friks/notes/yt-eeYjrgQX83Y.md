## Hitting $6k and $2.8k MRR in four weeks with a new couples app

- Jack built a couples app (widget for sending love letters to a partner) from idea to first revenue in 5 days, including app store review.
- By week 4 it had about $6,000 in proceeds since release. He states this is in USD, after Apple's cut, minus about $100 in refunds.
- MRR was $2,800, roughly 400 active subscriptions at $5 a month. The paywall offered monthly or lifetime only; he removed weekly.
- Pre-viral downloads were around 10,000 (he is unsure whether it was 10,000 or 20,000). The app had a hard paywall throughout this period.
- His original goal was a $5,000/month app by end of year from zero. Total proceeds passed $5,000 within weeks, so he raised the target to $10,000/month, with $5,000 MRR by end of year.
- He openly says a large part of this was luck.

## How one viral tweet drove 14 million views and about 30,000 downloads

- Someone quote-tweeted his launch, swapping the love note image for a grocery list: "Starts with love notes, then life kicks in."
- It blew up. Jack added context and tied it to the app; his own post got 327,000 likes and 14 million views.
- That brought roughly 30,000 additional downloads almost overnight, taking the app to about 40,000 total downloads.
- The 40,000 downloads made only $6,000, which he calls low for the volume. His earlier app made $15,000 from 30,000 downloads in a month, because that app was better set up for monetization.
- He expects not to get another random viral Twitter post, so he plans a repeatable organic content system instead.

## Moving from hard paywall to premium (freemium) to fix ratings and word of mouth

- About a week after the viral post, he built a whole new version of the app and shipped it. He feared it would go badly.
- He made the app premium rather than hard-paywalled, changed the logo and the whole app scheme (not just a basic menu).
- Ratings rose from 2.9 to 4.2 in that week.
- His reasons: people rate five stars more readily when there is no hard paywall, and many Twitter followers rated five stars because they know him.
- Freemium helps word of mouth: someone can share the app with a friend or partner, who can try it without entering a credit card or going through "50 onboarding steps" to hit a hard paywall.
- His goal: "make a good app that people want to use and eventually pay for."
- Revenue per download should trend upward as he fine-tunes, tests and iterates.

## Using Twitter to document the build in public

- Jack argues Twitter can be a great marketing channel. He used it to document everything: features being built, reviews arriving, what he is thinking and doing.
- He finds documenting fun, and the story ("the plot is getting thicker") is part of what draws attention.
- Example of real engagement: a user accidentally spent shared heart points on a pig skin and their girlfriend was annoyed. Jack refunded the points, locked the skin and fixed it. He took it as proof that people care about the in-app points and actually use the app.
- He says he fixed the app "like 17 times" in the first month.

## The plan to reach $10k/month through free organic video

- Core claim: the first $10,000/month "can be attained for free for $0" with organic short-form video.
- Formula for content: it must be good, entertaining and tell a story, and use a proven format that other content has already used, adapted to his own app.
- Example tactic: a fun skit video where the app appears in the video without being explicitly mentioned.
- Step-by-step plan for the next month:
  1. Finish polishing the app and add sound effects and features his fiancee wants.
  2. Post daily videos on Instagram and TikTok, trying different formats and templates.
  3. When one hits 10,000+ views, double down and recreate it in different ways.
  4. Once it is rolling, automate posting with PostBridge (his own product) to free up time, since he is still running his main business.
- Precedent: a year earlier he used this approach to get 30,000 downloads in one month for free on another app, which netted $15,000 that month (pinned on his Twitter).
- No paid ads in the plan.
