## Curiosity Quench: what the app does and the core idea behind it

- Jack's app, "Curiosity Quench", is meant to end doomscrolling. At day 365 he was still building it alone.
- Its goal is not to block or restrict anything. It helps people find things they would enjoy more than scrolling their phone.
- His thesis is that curiosity is the cure for boredom, and "you can't stop" curiosity. He applies this to the social media era this way: find a topic you care about and try it.
- The flow:
  1. Browse more than 1,000 topics and swipe right to save one to a list.
  2. Get a plan to try the topic.
  3. Each step in the plan links to curated content that helps you do it.
  4. Finish the plan and "quench" the curiosity.
- He built it because he believes most people scrolling TikTok have a lot of potential. He wants them to act on their real curiosity instead of feeding dopamine loops. He also built it for himself.

## Building in public: a daily video log for 365 days

- Jack started by documenting the build on TikTok. His first video said he was building an app "to destroy TikTok", meaning the addiction to it and not TikTok itself.
- He then made 365 daily videos. About 360 of them are daily YouTube logs, which are longer and go deeper on what he is doing.
- In the first month he had nothing to show and only talked about the idea. Many people reacted well, especially on TikTok, with comments like "this is a great idea." That feedback is why he went ahead and built it.
- His advice is that 365 days "isn't a long time" and passes quickly if you commit every day. He has enjoyed the logs and will keep making them for now.

## From no-code web app to a mobile app

- He didn't know how to code when he started. His early attempt to code it himself was "a mess" and he gave up quickly.
- He made a no-code prototype by about day 10.
- He launched the no-code web version around December, roughly 60 days in.
- The first version was primitive and made no money. He says it wasn't very good and didn't help people much.
- The web app was the only version for the first four months or so.
- The mobile app was released in February, about 4 to 5 months after he started.
- Early web users gave the mobile app a starting base of users.
- Over the year he learned to code and to design. He says he is "not a professional by any means."
- He got design help from a freelancer he credits as "Cats". She did a redesign that changed the look a lot.
- He removed the ugly banner ads and the "claim credits" ads that he had put everywhere.

## Validation signals that kept him going

- A reel showing the web app got 400K views at around day 60. He said he didn't expect it.
- He later refers to 300K real views of him showing "a really bad web app."
- He took that as proof of interest: "I'm sure there's something here." The traction is what made him start coding the web version for real.
- Installs on the mobile app reached 95,000. About a month before the video it was only about 50,000, so a lot happened at once.
- After launch there was roughly three months of little progress, especially on revenue. The app wasn't making money and wasn't sustainable at that point.
- He could have quit then but didn't. The reminder he pasted everywhere was "KEEP GOING" in all caps, which he says was really all it took at that stage. He adds that it obviously took much more than that.

## Results at 1 year and the 80/20 pattern in revenue

- The app has made more than $30K so far. He calls that "pretty good."
- About half of that, roughly $15K, came in the last two months. He didn't state the dollar figure, so this is derived from "half of $30K".
- In his words, two of the 12 months gave 50% of the results. He cites it as an example of the Pareto principle, the 80/20 rule.
- The lesson is that results can come late and in a lump. Most of the year had low results, and he kept going anyway.
- At the time he was running a sale: 40% off annual plans and 30% off monthly plans.

## Why marketing became the priority over product

- Recently he wasn't working on the app itself. Marketing was the priority.
- His reasoning is that you can only make the app so good. You can always improve it, and he wants to, but if he doesn't tell people about it, nobody downloads it and nobody sees the better parts.
- At this stage, distribution mattered more than more product polish.
- He still keeps the product sharp. The site now has a clear mission, a clear goal and many testimonials, which it lacked at the start.
