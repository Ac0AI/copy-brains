## Why Jack aims for about $50/day online instead of a job

- In October 2024 Jack had made about $50 a day for roughly a month and a half from an app he built. He says he is not selling T-shirts or dropshipping from Alibaba. He is "actually helping people."
- He treats $50/day as a milestone because it lets him avoid employment. He is not saying everyone should skip jobs. It is what he wants for his own life.
- His main reasons are time freedom and autonomy.
- He says "I would much rather have my own business, my own company than work for somebody else."
- He had planned to get a job in the six months before this video. About a month earlier his app was not making money, and then he had "a turning point."

## The math of $50/day and living cheaply

- $50/day is about $1,500 a month. He says it is still close to $1,500 after tax, because his government would not tax someone earning under roughly $16,000 a year.
- He lives near Toronto, Canada, and says he spends very little. He lives with his mother and pays about $600 in rent.
- He admits living on under about $16,000 a year is not very sustainable for most people. He says he is lucky to be in this position.
- He sees low expenses as what makes a small income enough to keep him independent. In his words, $50 a day "can keep employment away."

## Scalable leverage: why $50/day and $500/day take similar effort

- Jack says that from his side, going from $50/day to $500/day (or $5,000) takes about the same effort. The difference is more people coming to his website or app.
- A user pressing the buy button costs him almost nothing. He pays only small backend costs and some credits.
- He built the app once. People pay for its premium features or to remove ads, and he does not have to rebuild it for each sale.
- He argues that having no need for employment now can turn into having no need for employment later, because the same product can scale.
- He does not claim the income is easy. He says it is "hard but really fun."

## Paying customers are the validation that matters

- Jack says payment from users is worth far more than kind comments. "People paying me for my product... is immensely more valuable than people just commenting saying like oh that's cool."
- Paying users show the product is liked and is helping them. They also contact him to say so.
- He uses a hypothetical to make the point. If 10,000 people downloaded the app and nobody paid for premium features, that would be "okay" and have room to grow, but it would not be real validation.
- He says payment validation is what supports a "keep going mentality." It tells him the business could actually work.

## Value creation and ownership as the basis for a business

- He says helping people is "the only way to have a successful business." He calls anything else "a fugazi" or a scam, and says "something scummy."
- His goal is to "provide value and get paid for the value that I provide," and to deliver more value than people pay for. He calls this the main goal: "overdeliver."
- He prefers owning the business because the cause and effect is firsthand. Any upside comes back to him in full. If he made $10,000 on a good day, he would be paid the whole $10,000 and not a bonus.
- He feels he can make more of a difference this way than by working for someone else.

## Building in public and following what he wants

- Jack documents the process on his channel as raw as possible. He says that is the whole point of the channel. He also made this video as a record for his future self.
- His public mission is to end doomscrolling, through his app that helps with doomscrolling. He is also writing a book called "consumption control."
- He studied computer programming for two and a half years and got very good grades. He did not want to work eight hours a day at a desk for someone else.
- He now works more than 8 hours a day, but on his own projects. He says there is an "unlimited ceiling" when he builds something of his own, and a "very limited ceiling" when he works by the hour.
- He says he has less "bottled up anxiety" when he follows what he actually wants from life. He says he has spent a lot of time asking himself what that is, and is not only going with his gut.
