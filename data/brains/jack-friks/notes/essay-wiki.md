## Post Bridge: origin, build and launch model

- Jack built Post Bridge from his own need. Marketing Curiosity Quench took him about an hour a day, half of it posting the same video to each platform by hand. The existing schedulers cost "like 10 times more than I want to pay."
- He first mentioned "a tool that helps me post all of the videos I'm making to Instagram, TikTok, Twitter, and YouTube all at once" in a build log on 28 September 2024.
- He validated the idea with mockups on TikTok before writing code.
- He built it in about a month, starting in September 2024. It launched in October 2024 with a hard paywall and no free tier.
- It is built on Next.js and Supabase. Jack runs it alone and answers support himself each morning before other work.
- It publishes to ten platforms: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, LinkedIn, Facebook, Pinterest, Threads, Bluesky and Google Business. It has per-platform caption overrides, a calendar and a bulk upload tool.
- Pricing is set by the number of connected accounts, not per channel. Existing customers are grandfathered through price changes.

## Post Bridge revenue trajectory and public transparency

- Post Bridge reached US$7,000 MRR after four and a half months.
- In June 2025 he ran a filmed challenge to reach US$30,000 a month without working past dinner. He reported US$12,000 on day 15, US$16,416 on day 21 and US$17,163 on day 24. The day-24 post came alongside a Y Combinator rejection.
- Indie Hackers described an eight-month plateau. Revenue then doubled in the first half of 2026 to about US$35,000 (CA$50,000) a month, roughly eighteen months after launch.
- Post Bridge publishes revenue in real time through TrustMRR, which reads it from Stripe. As of 6 September 2026 it showed about US$40,500 over the trailing 30 days and 1,725 active subscriptions.
- In spring 2025 he declined a public US$1 million offer for the company. He said it was "probably worth about half of that." He says the publicity of refusing added over US$100,000 in annual recurring revenue within a month.
- He keeps a public, spreadsheet-backed ledger of every project since 2019. It marks each as success, "okay" or failure and computes a hit rate. As of September 2026 it lists more than twenty projects, most marked as failures.

## Positioning Post Bridge as a publishing layer for AI agents

- From 2026 Jack positioned Post Bridge as a publishing layer for AI agents. It ships a REST API, an MCP server and the `postbridge-cli` command-line tool, which was published to npm in June 2026. Assistants such as Claude and ChatGPT can list accounts, schedule and delete posts, and read analytics.
- An agent-mode skill repository went up on GitHub in March 2026.
- Post Bridge is listed in Anthropic's Claude connector directory and as a plugin inside ChatGPT.
- The product tagline is "Post to 10 social platforms from one place or your AI agent."
- Alongside the ChatGPT listing, Post Bridge published step-by-step guides to posting from ChatGPT, Claude, Grokbot and Gemini.
- Days after the ChatGPT listing surfaced in early September 2026, Post Bridge set four consecutive all-time daily signup records:
  - 4 September: 341
  - 5 September: 508
  - 6 September: 645
  - 7 September: 713
- The prior average was about 50 signups a day. Jack identified ChatGPT as the source.
- On the 5 September record day he reported 64 trials and 71 new customers.

## Organic short-form video marketing system

- His marketing is entirely organic and takes about one hour a day.
- He builds short videos from formats that have already worked. He re-posts them until they stop working.
- He has a repeatable "2x2" template that he posted more than 300 times. He credits it with 60,000 to 70,000 signups.
- Starter Story reported in 2025 that his short-form videos had passed 300 million views.
- Curiosity Quench grew from six-second Instagram and TikTok videos about hobbies, several of which passed a million views.
- In the 30 days after September 2024, Curiosity Quench drew about 15 million views, 40,000 installs and US$12,000 in revenue.
- For Lovelee, a quote-tweet of the launch post brought 40,000 downloads in a week and a half. A TikTok and Instagram format he found added 11,000 downloads in 48 hours.
- A 31 October 2025 X post showing Lovelee as a shared grocery list reached 14.3 million views.
- On audiences, he says: "to not grow a personal brand at all. It's just to build cool things, do cool things, and then like share them."
- Each app gets its own Instagram and TikTok accounts. He built Lovelee as "my own case study" for Post Bridge.

## Curiosity Quench: validating, building and pricing the first app

- Jack began Curiosity Quench in October 2023. It aims to reduce phone scrolling by giving users action plans for hobbies, with curated content for each step.
- He had tried and abandoned learning to code three or four times. It only stuck once he was building something he wanted to exist.
- He validated the idea with a 60-second TikTok that drew comments asking for the product.
- He built the first version in about four months while learning. He describes the process as feeding error messages into ChatGPT until it worked.
- He posted a numbered daily build log on YouTube for over a year.
- The app ran first on the web and reached the App Store in early 2024. It launched on Product Hunt on 28 April 2024 (day 196 of the log), after which he shut down the web version.
- It was priced at about US$30 a year with a seven-day trial.
- Downloads: 26,000 in a month by September 2024, and 100,000 total on 27 October 2024.
- Revenue: about US$3,000 a month by March 2025, and about US$60,000 in its first year. It later fell as he stopped marketing it.
- After the app appeared to peak at US$3,000 a month, a US$15,000 month came two months later.
- In December 2024, one year after he began coding, he reported over US$50,000 net income and US$7,000 MRR across his apps.

## Lovelee: a five-day launch challenge

- Lovelee lets couples send hand-drawn notes to a partner's home screen. Jack built it for himself and his fiancée.
- He launched it in five days as part of a filmed two-month challenge to reach US$10,000 a month.
- At week six it had 56,000 downloads and US$3,194 MRR. His ledger records more than 100,000 downloads and about US$40,000 in revenue in the first six weeks.
- The launch tweet was "picked up by Russian news."

## Idea validation and solo operating principles

- His core rule: the "easiest way to make a successful business is really just to solve your own problems."
- Before building, he asks whether he would pay to solve the problem.
- He tests demand with a landing page or short video: "if they don't click buy and a thousand people come, clearly the idea is probably not that good."
- He treats failure as normal and publishes it. His ledger includes abandoned apps and two books that sold poorly: the first at under 100 copies ("80% being family"), the second at about 20.
- He works alone in sprints rather than fixed hours. That runs from one or two hours a day in quiet periods to ten around a launch.
- He focuses on one project per day beyond customer support and stops at dinner time.
- Four years of earlier failures funded what came after:
  - Print-on-demand t-shirts and YouTube channels.
  - A Cardano crypto channel with 3.5 million views.
  - An NFT collection of 6,969 pieces. It sold out in a week, but most of the US$400,000 to 500,000 gross went to the developer's 40 percent commission, taxes and the market decline. That left about US$30,000 to 50,000 in profit.
- He keeps a folder of images captioned "keep going" that became a merchandise line.
