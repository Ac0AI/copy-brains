## Starting organic social from zero: new account, warm-up week, and posting cadence

Jack markets his mobile apps with organic short-form video on Instagram and TikTok and spends $0 on ads. He says he never had money for ads and did not like them. He started with zero followers on every page and has repeated the process on multiple pages for the same app.

His account setup rules:
- Create a brand new account for each business or app.
- Use that account only to scroll and follow content in the niche, so the algorithm knows who to push your first posts to.
- Post nothing for the first week. He used to say 2-3 days but now says a full week, so the algorithm does not read the account as a bot.
- After the warm-up, post one video a day. If posting several, space them at least 3-4 hours apart.
- Post manually. He runs a social media scheduling tool and still tells people not to use it on new accounts until they are warmed up.
- A properly warmed account should get hundreds of views per video. If it does not, "something is wrong" and he would stop posting and fix it. Without warm-up you post "videos to the void".

Why a fresh account beats an old one: on a personal account that posts off-topic content, Instagram shows the video to the existing audience first. If they do not engage, it is not pushed further. A new account tests across many audiences and has wide reach potential.

## Finding content formats: scroll your niche, save, and recreate

Jack's first step in organic social is finding ideas, and he says it is not hard.

- Scroll the niche on the new account and click save on videos that are already viral for the target audience.
- Look at the saved videos when it is time to create.
- Look for formats that are simple and low effort. Many videos are full productions, and you do not need one.
- Set a timer for 10 minutes a day. He says you will get the hang of what goes viral in a week or two.
- He has not been able to use AI to find formats and does not think this is outsourceable yet.

His example, a "Curiosity Quench" page for hobbies and anti-doom-scrolling:
- He saw a 2x2 image video with around a million likes.
- He first tried "things to do instead of scrolling" and it did not work.
- He iterated to hobbies. One post hit about 10k views, when he had been stuck below 1,000.
- He remade it and one post reached about 100k views.
- He then moved the same format to a food app, made 30 posts, and one hit about 2 million views.
- He now posts only that one format, a 2x2 image grid with a caption. He found it after making "hundreds of videos".
- He keeps using a format as long as it still gets results, even if he has used it many times.

Formats transfer across niches. A format validated in one niche can be applied to another, but you still scroll your own niche on a new account so the algorithm knows your audience.

## Testing and iteration: volume, angles, and patience

- Jack tests one new piece of content a day when trying new formats, and iterates on what works.
- Usually it takes 30 to 100 posts before something does well. In his example, hits came after roughly 30 to 40 tries.
- The common pitfall is making the same video over and over and expecting a different result. Each post should change an angle.
- If a post hits 10,000 or 100,000 views on the first try, keep doing that.
- Expect a month or two of work before real results, especially the first time.
- Views do not convert directly to signups. Converting attention is a separate skill.
- Start manually. Use CapCut as the simplest editing tool. "If you can't do it manually, there's no point of automating it", because you will not know what works. He suggests worrying about automation after about 100 pieces of content.
- Once he found a working format, he automated the 2x2 grid image generation with an assistant he built and is working on producing it in bulk.

Jack's 80/20 as summarized with the host: find the format, pick the niche, and post consistently.

## Making organic content that drives signups, not just views

Jack says getting views is not the goal. Views must go to the business, so content has to be highly relevant to what is being promoted.

Why people get "100k views but two signups":
- The video has no call to action.
- The pinned comment does not say to go download the app for a specific result.
- The main caption does not tell people to download it, say what it is called, or give the website link.

His tactics:
- Put the call to action in the caption and pinned comment. "You have to kind of spam it in their face."
- It does not need to be in the video.
- Do not make the content look like an ad. That is the "biggest mistake" in organic content. If it looks like an ad, people scroll and the algorithm gives it about 20 views.
- Make content people engage with. The algorithm pushes posts further when people comment or rewatch.

## Comment-to-DM automation with ManyChat

Jack had tried ManyChat before he saw social success and disabled it. He now thinks he overlooked it.

- The pattern is "comment X and I'll DM you a link" to the app, tool or guide.
- He says it is a big deal especially for B2B things, tools and guides.
- It is probably not important until you have a working piece of content that catches. Once you have a working system or content, it "could be huge".
- Comments are also a strong algorithm signal.

## Using AI to generate content ideas once you have a format

Jack's view is that AI helps after you have a working format, but does not replace scrolling to find one.

- He tested it live: describe the app (a food diary app), explain the format that gets views, and ask for more low-effort video format ideas related to the app.
- Output he found usable: split-screen or swipe comparisons such as how you feel after a bagel versus oatmeal, "foods that secretly drain you", underrated snack ideas, and "foods that make you smart or dumb" shown with app logs.
- Output he rejected: ideas that read like ads, such as "What I logged today in the app".
- Copy is the hardest part. Tell AI to write like Gen Z and use lowercase so it does not read as AI.
- AI is not autonomous. You need a sense of what works, which comes from scrolling about 10 minutes a day for a week.
- He had not used ChatGPT for ideation as much as he should, and says it can bridge the gap between seeing a format and adapting it to your app.

## Attention first, then conversion

- Jack frames it as two skills: get attention in a niche, then convert that attention into people using the SaaS or mobile app.
- He says the skill transfers to any business, though consumer-facing products are easier.
- The numbers: he went four or five years making basically nothing before reaching about $10-20k a month, and "it all happens really fast" when it does.
- He says anyone can do this because it is accessible and content takes little to make, but you have to do it longer than you expect and develop a system.
- He keeps a pinned guide on Twitter covering warm-up and common pitfalls, and tells people to reread it when unsure.
