## Why Jack lost most of $500,000 in crypto income

- In early 2021 Jack switched from general YouTube videos to crypto videos. His third video hit 60,000 views and his fourth 120,000, while his top video on the old channel had about 12,000.
- The new channel earned over $3,000 in YouTube ad revenue in its first month.
- He describes the crypto audience as a niche "no one is serving," and he was "the only one really making videos around it."
- He loved the topic at first, and that enthusiasm helped him post daily.

## How the $500,000 was made: an NFT project plus a mining add-on

- In summer 2021 he launched an NFT project of stick figures he drew himself.
- He hired a developer on a 40% commission.
- He added a mining operation on top, which netted about $700 to $1,000 every five days, boosted by his YouTube channel.
- The NFT collection (6,969 items) sold out in a week.
- Combined net revenue came to roughly $500,000, paid entirely in cryptocurrency.
- The plan behind it was to make daily videos and build something to sell to his audience. That way he could control his own time and income on the internet, which is why he had dropped out of college and quit McDonald's.

## The tax mistake: crypto income is taxed at the price received

- Income paid in crypto is taxed at its value when received, not at its value when the tax bill arrives.
- He did not understand this. He held all his crypto and sold none of it, even as prices fell.
- When the tax bill and accountant invoice arrived, the total was $31,000.
- The crypto he had earned was by then worth only about $70,000.
- He sold 70% of his crypto just to pay the accountant and the taxes, which took his entire net profit for roughly a year and a half.
- He was left with about $30,000 of profit, mostly from the mining proceeds, because the NFT sales "evaporated completely."
- His lesson in effect: when income is paid in a volatile asset, set aside cash for the tax bill at the time you receive it.

## Spending mistakes and admin friction

- He paid his developer about CAD $200,000.
- He then hired other people to work on the project. They either did not move the needle or delivered subpar work, so he "was losing money fast" paying for work that made no progress.
- His accountant dropped him because she was scared to file his crypto taxes.
- His replacement was a crypto specialist who put "crypto" in the corporate name. As a result he could not get a business bank account.
- Fixing it meant switching accountants and paying about $700 to change the corporation name.

## Burnout and the pivot away from crypto

- The loss sat in his stomach for a month. He "didn't want to get out of bed" and felt like an idiot for having "the easiest goal wealth" and still fluffing it.
- Months of daily videos on the same topic drained his spark. He realized he did not want to talk about crypto all day for the rest of his life, because the topic "just not something that grabs me."
- He started a blog and newsletter called The Freaking Philosophy, about lessons and failures, aimed at building a philosophy for a better life.
- He moved his YouTube channel to the new topic and cut off the 25,000-subscriber crypto channel.
- He accepts that he lost thousands of subscribers and business learning potential, and says he does not care.
- He writes the newsletter for many hours a day, and is also working on a book.

## Principles he takes away

- "Easy money will elude you just like it did me, so don't go for easy, go for doing something you actually want to do for the rest of your life."
- He says what you are doing, who you are with and where you are matter more than how much money you make or lose. Money matters less in the long run than fulfillment.
- He used to think success was about hustling and making money. Now he thinks it is about finding your "good work," meaning work you can do really well, and making that work.
- He ties his choice of topic to sustainability: a niche can grow fast, but if you do not enjoy it you cannot keep making content about it for years.
