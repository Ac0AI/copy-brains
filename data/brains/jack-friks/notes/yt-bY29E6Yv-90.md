## How Jack found a TikTok format that drove 11,000 downloads in 48 hours (Lovely, week 6)

- Context: Jack is running a public two-month challenge to take a consumer mobile app (Lovely, a couples app) to $10k/month. The target was originally $5,000/month, stretched to $10k.
- By week 6 the app had about 56,000 downloads. His launch tweet went viral and was picked up by Russian news, which brought about 3,000 downloads. A quote tweet a week and a half later brought about 40,000.
- Roughly 11,000 of those downloads came in the last 48 hours, from his organic TikTok and Instagram strategy.
- The first week of the TikTok effort gave minimal results. It took about a week and a half to find a working format, which he calls "exceptionally fast" and partly luck.
- At week 6 the app was at $3,194 MRR in USD (about $5,000 for him as a Canadian), about a month after he built it.

## The daily routine for finding formats on a burner TikTok account

- He uses a burner phone with TikTok and a US VPN.
- Every morning he scrolls TikTok for a while on the new account, finds videos in his niche that already did well, saves them, and makes videos based on them.
- The goal is to find a format that works, not to reinvent the wheel: "You're not trying to recreate the wheel here."
- His written guide covers account creation, warm-up and content testing. The main step is finding videos in the niche by scrolling on a fresh account.
- Once a format is found, he no longer needs to search. He batch-records content (pre-recorded doodles) and posts three a day: morning, after lunch and after dinner.
- His plan is to "milk" the format until it is overused and stops working as well.

## The winning format: show a cool in-app feature, not an ad

- The winning video shows the app's shared whiteboard feature, which looks like a lock screen and is used to send love notes from one phone to another. He copied this from another app in his niche.
- At first these videos got 1,000 to 2,000 views each, and one hit about 10k on Instagram. Later ones reached 500k, 120k, 100k and 90k views, and one on Instagram approached 2 million.
- Views are not the metric, downloads are. A different video got 200k views but only about 100 downloads. A 100k-view video in the winning format brought about 1,000 downloads.
- The app is visible in the video, but it is not an ad: the video shows a feature people want, and that is enough to get past the "this is an ad" reaction.
- Viewers who watch it are likely couples, which is the audience the app wants.
- The account had zero followers a week earlier and 1,500 after one video blew up. "All it really takes is one thing to blow up."
- He notes this applies to B2C mobile apps, not to B2B SaaS like his other product PostBridge, because SaaS is hard to make not look like an ad.

## Engagement tactic: deliberate misspellings

- He put an on-purpose spelling mistake in a video's text because "everybody loves being right."
- The video drew 1,200 likes and many comments correcting the error.
- His reasoning: the algorithm rewards comments, since commenting means people are watching.

## Free app with premium features

- He moved the app from paid to free, with premium features, and says it is better for the long term.
- Downloads now convert less (conversion and proceeds in the last 24 hours looked "terrible"), and he accepts that. "Slow is better" for when the money comes in; if the app is good, the money will come.
- Users who enjoy the app and leave a positive review feel better about paying.
- The model is common in the couples niche, and couples come back frequently. One partner will often buy a subscription and share it.
- He had about 150 monthly trials pending, which could be about $700 MRR in theory but is more likely $200 to $300. Users who do not convert right away may come back and convert later.

## Reinvestment plan to scale the format

- Lovely had made about $7,000 USD over the last 90 days.
- He plans to put about $4,000 to $5,000 back into scaling the working format over the next month.
- The plan is 10 to 20 TikTok and Instagram accounts run by UGC creators (for example, girls' profile pictures), recording many videos with varied hooks, posting about three a day per account.
- He had done everything solo up to this point, including running PostBridge, and had not hired anyone.

## General playbook for a first $10k/month mobile app

- He says growing an app is not easy now but very possible, especially B2C with a broad, helpful product.
- Getting to the first $10k/month on TikTok is "relatively simple": spend a lot of time on TikTok, find formats that already worked, recreate them so they relate to your app but don't read as an ad, and funnel downloads.
