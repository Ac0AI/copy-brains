## Why YC's investors quietly love it while publicly criticizing it
- Dalton frames the video around the word "secretly". Investors often say on blogs and social media that YC "sucks" and that founders should go straight to their funds.
- Founders ask YC about this often: "an investor told me doing YC doesn't make sense for me."
- His point is the gap between what investors say to founders they are passing on and how they behave as investors.
- Investors who criticize YC publicly also invest in YC companies all the time.
- The stats he cites from the video, counting investments into YC companies:
  - a16z: 234
  - Sequoia: 139
  - Founders Fund: 104
- Anyone can check the YC top companies list and see who invested. Dalton says it is all the biggest firms, and they are voting with their pocketbooks.

## YC as a filter: the restaurant analogy
- Dalton compares top VC firms to high-end restaurants. They like to give the impression that they go out to farms and hand-select the live chickens themselves.
- Most of what is on the menu comes from purveyors who find the best ingredients, prepare them and deliver them "in a nice box in a nice package".
- Dalton says that with so much demand, it makes sense for these firms to prefer things pre-selected and packaged. That is the service YC provides to VCs.
- The packaging is better for two reasons. YC companies tend to be further along than the average company walking in, and more technical.

## Why the love stays secret: the "first check" conflict
- Dalton points out that VCs also say "we want to be your first check". That clashes with their love of YC companies.
- Why it stays secret, as he frames it:
  - VCs would rather be the first check.
  - They know that if a company does YC, they won't be.
- Dalton calls it "a really tricky game".
- His position is that, whatever they say, the top investors cannot pass on a really great company. "If they need YC because they're smart", they invest.

## Seed funds have a different squeeze
- Dalton asked how seed funds fit into this dynamic.
- He says seed funds are more limited than traditional VC funds. A VC that misses your A can still do your B or C.
- A seed fund knows its main chance to invest in you is the first round, and that it will be diluted after that. That is why it tries to get as big a chunk as possible at as low a price as possible.
- Seed funds have still written thousands of checks into YC companies, but at higher prices.
- You can't really blame seed funds for discouraging YC, given those dynamics.

## How founders should weigh investor advice
- Dalton's framing is that the people marketing to you are running businesses with their own incentives, and so is YC.
- Founders should understand those dynamics and make the best decision for their own company.
- The key question when someone gives you advice about YC is whether they are offering to invest.
- Dalton's closing line is: "spend a little bit more time looking at what people do rather than what they say or they meme or they tweet." The best YC companies were funded by the best VC firms and seed funds.
