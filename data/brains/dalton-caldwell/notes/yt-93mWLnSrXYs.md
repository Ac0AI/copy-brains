## Why founders should know where their startup creates wealth

- Dalton says the reason for the video is to give founders one piece of advice: understand how your startup creates wealth.
- The more wealth you create for your users and customers, the better.
- He says that if a founder has no good answer to "where is the wealth created?", "I think that's a problem."

## Positive-sum vs. zero-sum thinking

- He argues that people who distrust startups are not so different from investors. Both sort companies into a "positive-sum, creates wealth" bucket and a "scam" bucket.
- He says the anti-tech view puts everything in the bad bucket, so Google, Gmail and everything Standard Capital funds count as ripping people off. He clearly does not hold that view.
- He treats this as a difference in criteria for who creates wealth, not a difference in whether wealth creation matters.

## Marketplaces as examples of positive-sum value

- Dalton points to eBay as an original dot-com-era startup. As he tells it, the Beanie Babies maker was so badly run that it shipped certain Beanie Babies to only some parts of the US. eBay let people with a glut of one type sell to buyers elsewhere, so "everyone was better off."
- He cites Whatnot, "one of my best investments at YC," as a modern eBay competitor. Many people make a living selling on its live streams, and "it certainly seems positive sum to me."

## Value delivered vs. value captured

- Dalton's test for whether a startup makes wealth is to compare how much value its customers get with how much the startup itself captures.
- His example is Google. Users get far more value from it than Google captures, such as businesses getting customers and people researching a company. Standard Capital gets value because people searching for various terms end up on its website.
- He says "the actual value that's being captured by the corporation is a tiny fraction of how much their users are being benefited."
- His one-line conclusion for founders is to create more value than you capture, and to have a clear idea of where your startup creates wealth.

## Being much better, not slightly cheaper

- Dalton says you create value not by being the lowest-cost option, but by being "so much better than other options."
- He says "trying to be slightly cheaper than someone else is not creating value."
