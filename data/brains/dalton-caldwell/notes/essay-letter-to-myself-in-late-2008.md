## Treat the cash you have as the real runway, not the next raise

- Dalton wrote this letter to his 2008 self, when he was founder/CEO of imeem (about 90 employees, good metrics, high burn). He says the 2020 crisis is not the same as 2008, but he hopes the advice he failed to follow then helps founders today.
- He says he felt he had little cash only because he was counting on a fundraise. In reality "You have plenty of cash. Get this new reality into your head."
- He tells his past self to stop thinking or talking about future fundraising, because that line of thinking "will lead to lots of wasted cycles." Those cycles should go into making the most of the cash already in the bank.
- A layoff that leaves the company unprofitable and "counting on fundraising to save you is a stupid plan."

## Renegotiate every contract immediately

- Dalton's advice to his 2008 self was to renegotiate all contracts "today", because there was far more to renegotiate than he realized. He figured this out "eventually, but much too late."
- At imeem, that meant contacting the music industry right away. He expected them to threaten him, but "this is the business you chose to be in."
- His 2020 example: startups can renegotiate their AWS costs by about 50% if they push.

## Do one deep layoff, not several small ones

- He calls multiple small layoffs "a form of cascading failure."
- The advice is to do one layoff, "much much deeper than seems correct," and do it decisively, so the company becomes profitable. For imeem he says that meant a cut of about 70%, not 5-10%.
- Cutting once and cutting hard lets him reassure the people who remain that the company is truly profitable and won't need to cut again.
- The goal is profitability. He treats a layoff that leaves the company burning cash as a half-measure.

## Make your own decisions instead of averaging advice

- Some people around him gave good counsel and many did not. He warns against the trap of averaging the advice you hear.
- The founder should be decisive and decide for themselves, even if the decisions are unpopular. Some people will be angry and call him too "paranoid".

## Ignore advice that is optimizing for a sale that won't happen

- By that point, no one was going to buy imeem. Smart-seeming people still said it might happen, and told him "don't take too drastic of measures to get profitable because then you won't be able to sell the company."
- His verdict: ignore those people and this "garbage advice."

## Overreact to survive

- His rule: "It's a better tradeoff to overreact to market conditions that lead to company survival than to have people say that you overreacted after the fact."
- He frames it as a leadership duty: "People are counting on you to do the right thing."
- He closes by telling his 2008 self "you will be OK."
