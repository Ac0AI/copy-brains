## Dalton's definition of a tarpit idea

- Dalton explains that YC kept seeing founders apply with the same idea over and over. YC often funded several of them, and the idea usually didn't work.
- The metaphor is the dinosaur tarpit: it looks like a pool of water from afar, the dinosaur goes to drink, and it gets stuck and dies. That is why tarpits are full of dead dinosaurs, and why these idea spaces are full of dead founders.
- He says the most common misreading is treating "tarpit" as a synonym for "an idea I don't like". Tarpit ideas are a small subset of ideas. Plenty of ideas are neither good nor bad, just normal.
- The tell is that other founders keep coming up with the same idea constantly. If they don't, it isn't a tarpit.
- It's not a static list. When technology changes, an old tarpit can become viable, and ideas he mocks now may stop being tarpits.

## Warning signs that an idea is a tarpit

- Most startup ideas get honest skepticism from friends. Tarpit ideas get lots of praise, because nobody wants to discourage you.
- Example: a restaurant discovery app. The problem is "I can never decide where to eat", and friends all say "I have that problem all the time".
- It sounds too good to be true. If you're getting too much positive feedback, or it feels like "why does this not already exist, it seems so easy", that is not the shape of things that work. Dalton says to be a little skeptical.
- It has been done many times, many people have failed, and nothing has changed. Dalton says founders often avoid doing research so they don't learn things they don't want to learn.
- It is one of the first ideas to pop into people's heads, so there is an endless supply of people who haven't started a company yet and land on the same one.

## How LLMs change the analysis

- Dalton says he is now open to classic tarpit ideas no longer being tarpits.
- What he wants to see: a founder who can explain why the new technology makes something possible that wasn't possible before.
- What he dislikes: a founder who doesn't acknowledge that many people have tried and failed. He jokes that for some founders history starts when they turn 18.
- Example: he funded a company doing fully AI-generated podcasts this batch. He says he would not have funded it before LLMs and voice generation, so he is in an "okay, let's see" phase.
- YC has funded generative video and audio companies since roughly 2016-2017, and he says quality keeps improving.
- His test: if you can't draw a connection between the technology and why your problem can be solved today, and the idea has been tried many times, that is tricky.

## "Co-pilot for X" is common, not a tarpit

- Dalton separates common from tarpit. "Co-pilot for X" (real estate agents, lawyers, investment bankers, dentists, dog walkers) is a very common AI idea.
- It isn't a tarpit, because not enough people have tried it yet. It doesn't mean you shouldn't try it.
- It is the kind of idea people produce when they try to derive AI startup ideas from first principles.
- He says the point of the video isn't to crush anyone's dreams. Many people start companies to fix a problem they truly care about. The "depressing" lesson is that the current technology only addresses a certain set of problems, and each new chunk of technology means re-exploring which problems are addressable.

## Flavor 1: "The world should work differently"

- The founder believes the way something works is bad, and everyone would have to adopt a new scheme at the same time.
- Example: coordinating with friends to see who is free to hang out, or event planning. It would be better if all your friends wanted to hang out all the time.
- It rests on everyone installing a new app to see what their friends are doing, and people don't seem to want that.
- Dalton says the idea comes from a good, utopian place. It runs into the fact that human behavior is very hard to change.
- He calls it a perfect tarpit because no one will tell you no.

## Flavor 2: Arbitrage and getting rich fast

- The founder has secret knowledge or a limited window, and believes the right idea at exactly the right time lets them skip the usual 15 years it takes to build a startup.
- Dalton says this tends to lead to building whatever is currently raising money, or an arbitrage play. He cites a lot of adtech as an example.
- Example: during the pandemic, many startup ideas came straight out of r/WallStreetBets. One founder, doing well there, planned to quickly create an investment advisory business so friends could hand over their money.
- His reaction is that people forget markets don't only go up. He worries about the friends who put their life savings in.

## Takeaways and the question Dalton asks

- Dalton's summary: the tarpit list isn't static, and ideas from the first video may no longer be tarpits. If an idea looks too good to be true, get skeptical.
- When founders ask him "is my idea a tarpit?", he asks whether they have talked to any users. They often haven't.
- His advice, as he describes it from YC's videos: talk to users and get a customer, rather than picking an idea based on a video.
