## Mission-first: build the product around a purpose
- Garry says an auteur "clearly has a mission". Hideo Kojima's mission for Death Stranding is to be "nothing less than an actual cure for loneliness".
- Garry's reading: video gamers are "notoriously lonely people". Kojima aims at shut-ins such as Japan's Hikikomori, who sometimes interact with no one but delivery people.
- The game's design carries the mission. The player is Sam Porter Bridges, a porter who brings disconnected post-apocalyptic cities online by delivering food and medicine. Garry compares this to a DoorDash delivery person.
- Kojima's stated aim: players should feel there are others like them, and "use in the real world" what they learn in the game. Garry notes a player may spend 50 hours on it and come out "changed".
- Garry's takeaway: think about what effect you want to have in the world, then "orient your whole project around it".
- Norman Reedus, quoted by Garry: "It's not a kill 'em all game. It's a bring people together game."

## Make your own decisions instead of outsourcing them to surveys
- Garry argues that once you have a direction, it is tempting to survey and pick the most popular, safe choice. An auteur does not do that.
- Example: Death Stranding art director Yoji Shinkawi says the focus is not on getting as much attention as possible. The team develops characters that are interesting and cool to them, rather than basing them on market research.
- Garry's claim: "When things get big, they get safe." Outsourcing decisions to a survey or quantitative thinking feels safer.
- He says an independent approach, whether an indie software maker or an indie game developer, often yields much better creative output.
- For something truly creative, he says you "just can't do it that way".

## Take action: know what to do yourself, what to delegate, what to outsource
- Garry says you must delegate, but not everything. You have to hold on to some of the important stuff.
- Example: Kojima edited the launch trailer himself. He first builds the structure of what the trailer should convey (story, world, gameplay and "most importantly, what the message is"), then chooses a song.
- Standard practice is to outsource launch trailers to a film company. The source quotes a figure of 200 million, which Garry's editor note reads as yen (about $1.8 million USD), probably a translation slip.
- Kojima says he is likely the only game creator who edits his own trailer.
- Garry's takeaway: great founders and great auteurs know what they can outsource, what they can give to their team, and have an intuitive sense of the few things they must do themselves. "It's not everything, it's just a few things."

## Great auteurs collaborate: say "yes, and"
- Garry warns against misreading auteurship as control-freak behavior: "Great auteurs are incredible at collaboration."
- Reedus contrasts directors who "short circuit" when you add to their scene with Kojima, who says "yes, and then let's add this" and keeps building on others' contributions. "His mind is always going forward."
- Garry's rule for every creative project: you need to listen, say yes, and incorporate other people's ideas. Only then can you collaboratively create something greater than you could individually.

## Showmanship sets the tone and expectations
- Garry calls the right level of showmanship "really underrated".
- Example: the entrance to Kojima's studio is theatrical. Conan O'Brien's visit reaction was "Oh my God." Everyone enters the studio this way, not just celebrities.
- Why it matters, per Garry:
  - It sets the tone for newcomers, prospective hires and partners.
  - It sets expectations so the team and everyone around you treat the work as something that could be great and special.
  - It shows up in the office, the brand and the design.
- His line: "It takes a little bit of showmanship to do something no one else has ever done."

## Garry's four habits of auteurs (summary)
- **Mission:** have one, and orient the whole project around the effect you want to have.
- **Make your own decisions:** don't rely on surveys or on what will be popular.
- **Take action:** know what you should do, what your team should do, and what to outsource.
- **Showmanship:** don't be afraid to be a showman when doing something nobody has done before.
