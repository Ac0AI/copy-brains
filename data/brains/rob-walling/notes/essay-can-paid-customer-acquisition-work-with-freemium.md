## Why Rob avoids freemium for bootstrapped businesses

- Rob is "not a fan of using freemium in a bootstrapped business." He endorses the essay "Why Free Plans Don't Work", and says he wishes he had written it.
- Freemium is possible if you have funding. Growing organically on your own revenue doesn't work when customer lifetime value (LTV) is extremely low.
- His analogy: freemium is like building a campfire in the middle of a dry forest. A skilled builder can make a warm fire, but you're more likely to burn the whole place down.
- He doesn't know of any bootstrapped business that has used freemium successfully. He knows of many funded businesses that have not.
- Low-LTV businesses typically only work with a viral loop, where every customer you sign up brings in X more customers. That lowers acquisition cost enough to turn a profit eventually.
- Viral loops are very hard to pull off. You have to be an expert (he cites Peldi, Sean Ellis and Matt Inman), or get lucky, or both.
- He hates "basing a business on luck and outlier-class talent."

## Can paid acquisition work with a low-LTV freemium product?

- The question came from a reader whose blended LTV was about $1, once high initial churn and all the free users were counted. That reader concluded that AdWords-style paid marketing was doomed.
- Rob agrees that paid acquisition won't work against a ~$1 LTV.
- He says the framing is the problem. Averaging LTV across every signup, free users included, hides the real economics.

## Reframe the funnel around paid users

- Look only at users who sign up for the paid plan. They have a higher LTV, though a lower conversion rate.
- Don't ask "I can only spend $1 to acquire a free user." Ask "I can spend $50 to acquire a *paid* user," and optimize that funnel.
- The $50 figure is Rob's illustrative example, not a fixed benchmark.

## The cash cost of waiting for free users to convert

- Some free users convert over time. Rob recalls Dropbox saying a decent share convert within 12 months, which he thinks was about 3%. He is unsure of the exact figure.
- Waiting for conversions needs "piles of cash" to sustain the business in the meantime.
- You also need funds to support the other ~97% who never convert.
- Both costs typically require outside funding, which is why freemium fits funded companies better than bootstrapped ones.

## What to do instead when competitors are all freemium

- Think about charging for your product.
- Go upmarket or find a way to differentiate.
- Ask what you can build that gives customers $19, $39 or $79 per month of value.
- Unless you have a large bank balance, Rob doesn't see how you can compete with free. The exception is a product that is very, very different from the competition.
