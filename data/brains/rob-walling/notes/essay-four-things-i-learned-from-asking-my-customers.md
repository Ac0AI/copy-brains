## Using Survey.io to run customer development on an existing product

- Rob used Survey.io, a free tool from Sean Ellis and Hiten Shah, to survey customers of DotNetInvoice with pre-written questions. You enter the product name and it generates 8 questions. Sean Ellis has used them at companies like DropBox, Xobni, LogMeIn and Lookout.
- The questions cover how people found the product, how they would feel if they could no longer use it, and what alternative they would use instead.
- The survey has two aims: find out whether a core group of users really needs the product, then learn which features matter most to that group.
- Rob's problem with the usual "What should we build next?" survey is that you can get 40 feature suggestions with no way to tell which ones serve the core customers who really need the product.
- He had put the survey off until the QuickBooks integration launched. MicroConf then pushed him to act, because a key takeaway was that he needed to talk to his customers more.
- Cost: about 15 minutes of setup and 90 minutes parsing results. He called it "one of the most fruitful 105 minutes I've spent in some time." The findings will shape DotNetInvoice's direction for a year or more.

## Last-click attribution hides how much SEO drives sales

- 85% of survey respondents said they originally found DotNetInvoice through Google. Because only existing customers were surveyed, this "blew my mind."
- Search engines account for less than 40% of overall traffic. Google Analytics goal tracking credited only 37% of sales to search.
- Rob attributes the gap to the "last click problem." Analytics assigns the conversion to the last thing a user clicked, not to how they first found the product.
- He tried KISSmetrics the year before to avoid this problem but hit tracking problems. His first step was to get it running again to see which number is right.
- If 85% holds up, he knows where to invest his time for the next 6-12 months: SEO.
- The lesson is that asking customers how they first found you can contradict analytics, and the answer should drive where you spend effort.

## Testing for product-market fit with the "very disappointed" question

- Rob cites Sean Ellis's rule: you probably have product-market fit if more than 40% of customers would be "very disappointed" if they could no longer use the product.
- DotNetInvoice scored 48% "very disappointed" and 33% "somewhat disappointed."
- Rob would like the "very disappointed" number to reach at least 60%. He was still encouraged that a core group of users really needs the app.
- Most "very disappointed" respondents worked in one or two related verticals.
- Action: focus marketing and product development on features that should increase sales in those two niches, even at the expense of sales in other niches.

## Finding competitors you did not know you had

- Rob had assumed Freshbooks was the main competitor, and 37% of respondents said they would go there if DotNetInvoice disappeared.
- Two other answers surprised him:
  - 26% said they would write their own invoicing tool.
  - 22% said they would use no alternative at all, which he took to mean MS Excel or MS Word.
- Action: update marketing messages to target these hidden competitors.
- Planned home page copy: "Stop using Word and Excel, save time and get paid faster with DotNetInvoice."
- Planned developer landing page headline: "Don't start from scratch, start with DotNetInvoice."

## Customers' stated benefits can differ from the benefits you market

- Rob's marketing had pushed advantages over Freshbooks: you keep control of your data, and source code is included so you can customize.
- Customers named different primary benefits:
  1. Automated invoices and reminders.
  2. Easy and simple to use.
- He speculated that data control and source code are expected features, so customers did not mention them.
- Competitors have both of the top benefits too, so they are not DotNetInvoice's lead marketing message.
- He did not plan to change messaging because of this. He valued having a list of the top 4 reasons people are most likely to buy.
