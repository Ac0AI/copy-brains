## Why $1/month pricing fails: the unit economics

Rob argues that "you can't make money charging $1 per month" unless the market is gigantic and you have the expertise or funds to reach it, and even then "support will kill you."

- A meager $2k/month goal needs 2,000 customers at $1/month.
- That means supporting a lot of non-technical customers for little money, on nights and weekends while still holding a full-time job.
- At a 1% conversion rate, 2,000 customers needs 200,000 total unique visitors (total, not monthly).
- Promotion and word of mouth may ramp to thousands of uniques per month. Unless the market is massive and you can rank high in a major search engine, reaching 200k uniques takes years without an ad budget.

## Why low prices rule out paid acquisition

- At $1/month (or $7/month) there is no budget for ads. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is too high relative to lifetime value (LTV).
- Founders are forced into "free" channels: SEO, word of mouth and virality. These are super competitive, and without the skill to optimize them, growth is hard.
- Rob's counterintuitive observation: lower-priced products tend to have higher churn, higher support burdens and more price-sensitive customers.

## Move upmarket and sell to businesses

- After running into this problem himself with several B2C plays, Rob moved each successive product upmarket and shifted to selling to businesses.
- Even at $10/month, he says it is hard to grow a SaaS past tens of thousands of dollars in MRR without a lot of incoming traffic.
- Charging $50 or $100/month (or more) is "such a better way to go," based on his own companies and those he advises.

## Aspirational Pricing

- Rob's term for the approach: when customers ask you to lower your price, ask "What would I need to build to make it worth the price I'm charging today?"
- Then build those features, as long as they fit your product vision.
- He advised a reader (building a product for teachers, who had a free work-around) to add more value rather than lower the price. The reader had considered $1/month as a "no-brainer"; Rob recommended $5-7 at a minimum.

## If you are stuck at $5-7/month: charge annually

- Rob's fallback for low-priced products is annual billing.
- It brings the cash in up front, so you can reinvest in the product and grow the business faster.
