## Product-market fit is a spectrum with five stages, not a binary switch

- Rob argues most founders treat product-market fit (PMF) like a light switch: you either have it or you don't. He says that is wrong.
- PMF is a spectrum. Each stage needs a different focus to keep progressing.
- The better question is "what stage am I in, and what should I focus on to strengthen it?"
- He built the framework from watching hundreds of SaaS companies, through TinySeed and conversations with thousands of founders.
- He extends Paul Graham's definition ("something people want") beyond the early stage, because "the work of strengthening PMF doesn't stop at $20K MRR."
- The point is focus, not labeling: "Every stage has different work. Know which stage you're in, and do that work."

## Stage 1 (pre-PMF) and Stage 2 (weak PMF): metrics, focus, pitfalls

**Stage 1: Pre-PMF**
- Metrics: month-over-month growth under $500; monthly revenue churn high (5-10% or more); MRR usually $0-$5,000.
- Looks like: early adopters are enthusiastic but don't convert to long-term users. The founder does all sales, product work and customer interaction.
- Focus:
  - Customer interviews to identify the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) and pain points.
  - Experiment with different value propositions.
  - Test marketing and sales channels to find initial traction.
- Pitfalls:
  - Not driving enough leads: "You can't build a business with a small trickle of new customers."
  - Ignoring negative feedback or only seeking positive reinforcement.
  - Mistaking audience interest for actual PMF.
- Advice: if pre-PMF, don't worry about scaling marketing. Worry about whether you're building something people actually want.

**Stage 2: Weak PMF**
- Metrics: month-over-month growth $250-$1,000; monthly revenue churn moderate (3-7%); MRR usually $2,500-$20,000.
- Looks like: regular usage but inconsistent satisfaction and retention. Early signs that certain customer segments resonate. The founder still does most of the work.
- Focus:
  - Strengthen retention through better features and onboarding.
  - Invest in marketing and sales.
  - Identify and target the most promising customer segments.
- Pitfalls:
  - Weak value proposition and unclear messaging.
  - Spreading marketing across too many channels without mastering any.
  - Not tracking funnel conversion rates.

## Stage 3 (emerging PMF): repeatable channels, retention and pricing

- Metrics: month-over-month growth $500-$2,500; monthly revenue churn low (1-5%); MRR usually $15,000-$40,000.
- Looks like:
  - Inbound interest and word of mouth become noticeable.
  - Funnel conversion rates improve.
  - Retention improves as customers regularly find value.
  - The sales process becomes more defined and repeatable.
- Focus:
  - Invest in customer success so users achieve their desired outcomes.
  - Evaluate the pricing structure.
  - Strengthen brand and messaging to differentiate from competitors.
- Pitfalls:
  - Failing to find repeatable marketing channels.
  - Letting customer support quality slip as the company grows.
  - Unwillingness to hire and delegate.
- Advice: at emerging PMF, don't get distracted by enterprise sales. Focus on finding repeatable channels and strengthening retention.

## Stage 4 (strong PMF): hiring, pricing power and sustainable growth

- Metrics: month-over-month growth $2,500+; monthly revenue churn very low (0-3%); MRR usually $30,000-$83,333.
- Looks like: strong brand recognition, a loyal and engaged customer base, a predictable flow of inbound leads, and established sales and marketing processes.
- Focus:
  - Hire to scale operations across functional areas.
  - Keep evaluating pricing power as the company becomes more established.
  - Develop a long-term vision that enables sustainable, profitable growth.
- Pitfalls:
  - Failing to anticipate plateaus before they hit.
  - Ignoring employee satisfaction and company culture.
  - Not balancing growth with profitability.
- Advice: at strong PMF, don't keep doing everything yourself. Hire and build the team that can take you to the next stage.

## Stage 5 (mature PMF): lifetime value, defensibility and endgame

- Metrics: month-over-month growth $5,000+; monthly revenue churn very low (0-3%); MRR above $83,333.
- Looks like:
  - Widely recognized in the market; the product is often the default choice for the ICP.
  - Word of mouth keeps growing, along with expansion and enterprise opportunities.
  - Customers face significant switching costs and rarely leave.
  - Expansion revenue can exceed churn.
- Focus:
  - Maximize customer lifetime value through pricing tiers, add-ons and upsells.
  - Maintain speed and feature velocity as the team grows.
  - Build internal leverage: professionalize operations and strengthen leadership.
  - Decide the endgame: 10x growth, or build to sell, and at what price.
  - Defend the position through competitive intelligence and a deeper moat.
- Pitfalls:
  - Key person dependencies in critical areas.
  - Getting disrupted by nimbler competitors.
  - Failing to recognize diminishing returns on existing channels.
  - Letting culture or pace erode as the team grows.

## Caveats: when metrics mislead about PMF

- **Audience or marketing skill can fake it.** With a large audience or exceptional marketing, you can accumulate customers and MRR without real PMF. The key indicator is whether customers get value and stick around.
- **Churn is the tiebreaker.** If monthly churn is 10% or higher, "you're effectively pre-PMF regardless of your other metrics."
- **Big contracts can mask problems.** A few $100K contracts can make a company look further along than it is. Watch retention and expansion, not just revenue.

## Quick reference: stage benchmarks

| Stage | MoM growth | Monthly revenue churn | Typical MRR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pre-PMF | < $500 | 5-10%+ | $0-$5,000 |
| 2. Weak | $250-$1,000 | 3-7% | $2,500-$20,000 |
| 3. Emerging | $500-$2,500 | 1-5% | $15,000-$40,000 |
| 4. Strong | $2,500+ | 0-3% | $30,000-$83,333 |
| 5. Mature | $5,000+ | 0-3% | > $83,333 |

- Ranges overlap by design, because the stages are a spectrum.
- Use the table to decide where to focus: lead generation and customer interviews early, retention and channels in the middle, hiring and pricing power later, and endgame and moat at the top.
