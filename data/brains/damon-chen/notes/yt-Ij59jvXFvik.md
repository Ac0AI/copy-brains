## Early path: side projects built for fun while employed at Cisco

- Damon worked at Cisco for eight years before going full-time as an indie hacker.
- He built Indie Log (originally "Lonely Dev") at the start of the pandemic. Working remotely made him lonely, and Indie Hackers was text-only, so he wanted a warmer, video-based community where people post short clips of what they did that day.
- He then built Howdy Influencer, Backlogs and other projects. He treats these as side projects to reset his mind and avoid burnout: "just want to get distractions so that I won't get burned out from a single project."
- None made revenue. He kept his bi-weekly Cisco paycheck, so there was no pressure, and he didn't want to charge his Indie Log community members, who were friends.
- Earlier still he tried becoming an eBay or Shopify merchant and a photographer, just to make money. Entrepreneurship was always on his mind.
- He credits Peter Levels' "12 startups in 12 months" as the inspiration for building many projects before launching Testimonial.
- His advice: keep the fun in building, not only when a product earns revenue.

## Pivoting Indie Log into Testimonial and making $3k in 10 days

- Testimonial was a direct pivot from Indie Log. The video upload, hosting and streaming core already existed.
- The moment he decided to quit his job, he felt pressure to make money, at least be "ramen profitable". He also didn't want to push so hard that he burned out.
- His rule: reuse as much existing code as possible so a failure costs little opportunity time.
- He has no marketing or sales team, so he looked for a product that sells itself. Social proof came to mind: help makers and business owners collect video testimonials and embed them on their websites.
- He built the MVP in about a week and launched on Product Hunt in December 2020.
- It made about $3k in the first 10 days, mostly from a lifetime deal. It reached roughly $13k MRR by the time of this interview (April 2022).

## Skipping validation: build it and let payment validate

- On Testimonial, Damon did not spend time on idea validation.
- His reasoning: the time spent validating is about the time it takes to build, and paying customers are the only real validation.
- On people replying "cool idea, I'd buy": "in the end they will never come back and they will never be becoming a customer."
- This works best when the MVP can be built in about a week and much of the code is reused.

## From lifetime deal to subscription SaaS

- The first 10 days were mostly lifetime-deal sales. Seeing real demand, Damon decided he was "a huge fan of the current business model" (recurring revenue) and turned Testimonial into a SaaS.
- He announced that in three days the lifetime deal would end and the product would be subscription-only. The announcement itself brought in a couple thousand dollars more.
- After that he focused only on subscriptions.

## Pricing: starting at $10 and doubling up to $60/month

- He tested pricing a lot. Testimonial started at $10/month, then he doubled it, and it reached $60/month.
- He says raising the price quite a lot "really helps speed up revenue growth."

## Product-led growth built into the product

- As a solo founder he can't spend time on manual work like cold outreach, so he builds growth into the product and automates as much as possible to focus on building.
- Free tier limits are the upgrade trigger: free users can collect two videos; when they collect a third they must upgrade to see it.
- Embedded widgets carry a "powered by Testimonial" logo on the free plan, which acts as distribution.
- Beyond that he iterates quickly on real user feedback and does marketing by building in public on Twitter.

## Side products and acquiring Support Man

- Embed.so brings in about $150 MRR; Channel.so has made no revenue yet. Both reuse code from Indie Log and Testimonial.
- He doesn't spend much time on them. His rule: he only works on a side project if Testimonial also benefits, since they are about 2% of his total revenue.
- He acquired Support Man, an Intercom app by Noah Bragg that syncs low-star Intercom reviews to a Slack channel so support can follow up with angry customers.
- The logic is complementary: Testimonial captures happy customers (a 4-5 star review is a good moment to ask for a testimonial), while Support Man catches unhappy ones. Intercom integration is a big ecosystem opportunity.
- The deal: Damon tweeted that he wanted to buy it, the founder was already in his Slack, asked for something over $30k, and they settled at $20k. It closed in under two hours because he already knew and trusted the seller.

## Twitter growth: be genuine, humble and an underdog

- He grew from about 1,000 followers in the Indie Log days to roughly 27,000, almost 30,000, in about a year.
- His strategy: "be genuine, be humble and be an underdog." He calls himself "just a mediocre software engineer" who spent eight years at Cisco without being promoted to tech lead.
- He doesn't try to teach. He shares his own story so it resonates with others.
- Recommendations: the book *Getaway* by Andrew Gastaki (as transcribed), and Peter Levels as his all-time favorite indie hacker.
