## Why Damon diversifies across multiple businesses

Damon Chen, a bootstrapper since early 2020, runs a portfolio of businesses rather than one. Two flagship projects are testimonial.to and pdf.ai, plus others he built and others he acquired.

- His stated view: diversifying is "an important strategy" that reduces risk and maximizes opportunities.
- He acknowledges the "go all in, avoid distractions" camp has a point, but says diversification is the approach that has worked for him.
- Portfolio overview lives at damon.vc.

## Spreading risk and stabilizing income

- Damon compares it to investing: you don't put all your money in one stock, so a loss in one is covered by others. He applies the same logic to business.
- Relying on one business is "much riskier," especially for bootstrappers, who mainly build for themselves and their families and therefore need stable income.
- He argues the "go all in and reach unicorn/IPO in 5 to 10 years" outcome has a pretty low chance of happening, so he sets it aside.
- With several businesses, he is not solely dependent on any one. If one underperforms, others make up for it, giving a more stable income stream.
- His conclusion: "the safest and the easiest way is just to build multiple businesses."

## Playing in different markets: B2B and B2C

- Owning several businesses lets him "dip my toes into various ponds."
- Testimonial.to is B2B: it helps business owners collect, manage and distribute testimonials efficiently.
- PDF.ai is B2C: individual consumers use AI to chat with PDF files.
- He says it is simply fun to play in both B2B and B2C.

## Continuous learning from each new business

- Each business is "a new adventure" with its own lessons, as long as you stay open to new opportunities.
- Example: he started out building Testimonial.to with little exposure to AI. After the ChatGPT buzz, he got into PDF.ai, which he acquired rather than built from scratch.
- Working on it exposed him to OpenAI's tools and the very fast pace of AI development, keeping him up to date.
- He compares it to "playing a game that's constantly leveling up."

## Freedom as a solo bootstrapped founder

- As a solo founder with no VC, he has no commitment forcing him to do only one thing, so he can explore freely.
- He describes his DNA as "the creator": when he sees something interesting he gets hyped and wants to try it.
- Focusing on a single project would mean missing opportunities.
- His framing: every new opportunity is a new dart to throw. Keep collecting darts and the chance of eventual success rises.
- He grabs new opportunities as they come up.

## Avoiding burnout and keeping the work engaging

- Diversifying makes the journey less boring and more exciting, and lowers the chance of burnout.
- He has seen many founders desperate to grow one business, hit a plateau or decline, and get discouraged after putting in huge effort without matching reward. That can lead to giving up or burnout.
- With multiple businesses the workload balances out: if one feels overwhelming, he can work on another, which takes load off and keeps the whole system balanced.

## Easier exits: avoiding the "now what?" void

- Damon notes many successful founders feel lost after selling their one and only business ("that is their baby"), asking "what's next, what am I going to do?"
- Because he runs several, selling one is not a problem: he can move his extra bandwidth to the businesses he still runs, so he doesn't expect to fall into that void.
- He says this benefit hasn't applied yet but will help in the future if he sells a business.
