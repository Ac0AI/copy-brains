## Product and pricing: Testimonial.to at roughly $4k MRR

- Damon builds Testimonial.to, a tool for collecting video (and text) testimonials. It was his pivot after several earlier products, mostly video-related; he reused front-end and UI work from those, so he spent $0 on Upwork freelancers for Testimonial.
- Pricing he states: $60/month or $80/year on the standard paid plan, and an "ultimate" plan at $180/month or $1,440/year. His biggest customers, the ones on the ultimate plan, are SaaS companies.
- Customer mix: e-commerce merchants, product makers, podcasters and SaaS founders. Anyone with customers who might vouch for them is a target.
- Metrics he shared in June 2021 (first time publicly): a bit over 100 active paying subscribers, and MRR just crossed $4,000 (a bit under $4k the month before). Roughly $50k ARR.
- New subscribers: 30 net-new active subscribers the prior month, 11 so far in the current month (early June).

## Quitting a $200k job by taking unpaid leave

- Damon worked at Cisco with a total package close to $200,000 a year (about $15-16k/month).
- Rather than quit outright, he took unpaid personal leave in 2020. He keeps medical coverage, which matters because he has a two-year-old daughter.
- His reasoning: a lower-risk way to test whether Testimonial takes off before resigning. Losing the paycheck created the urgency to make money that he lacked while collecting biweekly pay.
- He launched Testimonial.to right before Christmas 2020.
- Looking back, he wishes at 20 he had known about side projects and passive income instead of only chasing a full-time job at his "dream company" (Cisco).

## Growth goal and distribution channels

- His stated goal is $100k by end of year, which he calls realistic given the current trajectory. He says he doesn't want to set expectations so high that missing them is demoralizing.
- Distribution: nearly all customers come from Twitter. He runs no cold email, no outbound and no paid ads.
- He sees Twitter as both a strength and a single point of failure, and says LinkedIn is the next big channel, especially for SaaS buyers on the ultimate plan.
- Being a solo bootstrapper with limited time is why he hasn't diversified yet. He plans to reinvest recurring revenue into other channels over time.

## Product-led SEO: ranking for "testimonial" without writing content

- Damon tweeted in June 2021 that Testimonial.to was ranking high on Google for the keyword "testimonial", ahead of HubSpot.
- He wrote no dedicated testimonial blog content. His explanation is that the product generates traffic by itself:
  - Customers send a link to their own customers to record a video or text testimonial, which drives visitors to the Testimonial.to site.
  - Customers embed collected testimonials on their own websites, which creates backlinks.
- On why the site ranks, Damon says he is "not a SEO guy" and isn't sure what exactly qualifies.

## Earnest Capital: small investment for resources, not cash

- Damon took a small investment from Earnest Capital (under $100k, exact number not disclosed) in exchange for a little equity. He still owns the vast majority of the company.
- Why: he didn't need money. He's a technical solo founder, "totally newbie" at marketing and sales, and wanted Earnest's pool of successful founders, warm intros and resources. He says Earnest has emailed its list for him, which drove new customers.
- His explanation of how Earnest's deal works: they invest an amount for a percentage of equity, and as the company generates revenue it returns a multiple (he cites 3x) of the investment to buy back equity, leaving Earnest a small residual stake (he says around 2%). His illustrative numbers ($100k, 6%, 3x) were loose, so treat them as an example of the shape, not terms.
- On extra money: no plans to raise more. He'd use Upwork freelancers for any big feature work, and will re-evaluate after a marketing strategy and feature roadmap are in place.

## Using an equity-compensated advisor instead of a co-founder

- He considered a co-founder but hasn't found one. Instead he recently brought on an advisor he met on Twitter, Darren Westall, and gave him equity.
- Darren founded Pager.co, a platform for marketing agencies and recruiters, described by Damon as a multi-million dollar business in England.
- The arrangement: a weekly or biweekly meeting where Darren lays out a marketing strategy.
- Why he picked him: Damon wants someone only a few years ahead of him, who can give hands-on, executable advice, rather than a founder of a Nasdaq-listed company giving high-level advice. He believes he can take Testimonial to multi-million dollars in 3-5 years.
