## Origin of IndieLog (formerly Lonely Dev) and the problem it solves

Damon Chen is a software engineer at a Silicon Valley tech company and an indie hacker. He joined the Indie Hackers community several months before this interview and built two products: IndieLog and Backlogs.

- IndieLog is a video community where indie hackers vlog their startup journey together.
- Backlogs is a tool for collecting user feedback in one place. He built it while building IndieLog, after receiving feedback scattered across many channels.
- IndieLog came from his own problem. From March, lockdown meant working from home with less contact with others, and he wanted a channel to express himself.
- He first considered a YouTube channel but rejected it: "why do I have to expose myself to such a big audience? I don't want to be an influencer."
- He saw Indie Hackers posts about loneliness, remote happy hours and virtual meetups. That suggested a small "sub-YouTube" community made up only of indie hackers, gathering to talk about their work and day.

## Positioning: a human, video-only alternative to text forums

- Damon notes that text forums like dev.to, indiehackers.com and some subreddits already exist and are "great", and he did not want to create another one.
- IndieLog is focused only on video. The goal is "something a little more human where people can have more personalized interactions."
- With video, people see each other and feel emotions, and members can build real relationships.
- A 2-minute limit on IndieLog videos keeps updates short. Longer content belongs on YouTube.

## Launch and early traction

- He launched IndieLog on the Indiehackers community. One day later he had several new video uploads, which is where it began.
- Several weeks later he posted on Product Hunt, which also brought a lot of traffic.
- After that he did little promotion. He tried to be consistent: updating his own work on IndieLog almost every day and staying very active interacting with other members.
- At the time of the interview the community had fewer than 200 members. He viewed small as a plus: "It's like a friend circle I created all around the world", where he sees members' updates every day.

## Features built to drive member activity

Damon's stated common goal for all features was to motivate members to post more video updates.

- **Topics:** a new topic every few weeks so all members talk about the same thing together. It gives new members a way to start when they want to say something but don't know where to begin.
- **Products:** a place to list the products members are working on. It helps members keep updating progress on a product, and videos link to each product's page, so the page shows all relevant videos.
- **IndieTuber:** a place for members to list their YouTube channels. Many indies also post longer videos on YouTube, so this promotes their channels given IndieLog's 2-minute limit.

## Future plans: retention through gamification and weekly check-ins

- Focus more on promoting the community.
- Add weekly check-ins so people constantly update their progress.
- Add more gamification, such as challenging members to post an update every week. Members who manage 50 consecutive weeks in a year could earn stickers or other perks. He said he would see how much capacity he had for it.
- He was candid about marketing: "I'm not a marketing expert, lots of things I need to learn from the beginning."
