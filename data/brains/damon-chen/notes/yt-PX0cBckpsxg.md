## Why Damon started building: from an 8-year corporate job to side hustles

- Damon spent 8 years at his original software engineering job (Cisco, going by the mugs and t-shirts he mentions) and never switched, unlike most Silicon Valley engineers who change jobs every 2 to 3 years for a raise.
- He didn't stay by choice: he applied elsewhere and "got all rejected." He expected to stay until retirement.
- The turning point was childcare. His first kid was born in 2018, and in 2020 (as she was about to start daycare) Covid hit. He and his wife either had to risk daycare or have one of them quit to care for her at home. He quit.
- While at home he learned web development on Udemy during her naps and late evenings. His original goal was only to pick up a modern stack to land a better job at a new company.
- Instead he "accidentally fell in love" with side hustling and building hobby projects.

## Outcome: hobby projects became a full-time business

- Four years after starting (as of early 2024), his hobbies became his full-time work, and he can make a living from it.
- Income is now "even better" than his old job.
- He is the founder of:
  - a SaaS product that helps business owners collect, manage and distribute testimonials
  - PDF AI, an AI product that lets users chat with PDF documents
  - other small businesses
- He runs a small but "very efficient" team.

## Why side hustling worked: no money pressure

- He recalls that when he started hustling he still had his full-time job, which is "probably the main reason" he could fully enjoy building without worrying about making money.
- He applies the same principle to his YouTube channel: no pressure to grow subscribers or monetize, because those are unnecessary at this stage.
- His rule: "If there's no fun there's no point in doing it."

## Learning from and giving back to the indie community

- When he started four years earlier, he was helped by founders he admires: Arvid Kahl and Rosie (Indie Hackers), Ling Tha (key values), and Peter Levels (Nomad List).
- Having been "in the arena" for four years, he feels he has his own stories to share and wants to give back the way they helped him.
- He positions the channel as a personal record of his ups and downs, in the spirit of Matt Damon planting potatoes on Mars in *The Martian*, which he hopes might also help or inspire others.

## Why a YouTube channel (not a newsletter), and why now

- **Memories:** He wants to leave something for his kids to look back on, to see how their dad looked and talked 20 or 30 years later. He tweets nearly every day, but argues nobody will read a Twitter feed "word by word," whereas video is "more vivid."
- **Fun and new experience:** Making videos is a different experience from quickly typing tweets, and a way to step out of his comfort zone.
- **Giving back:** Sharing the story and lessons from his own journey.
- He had procrastinated on starting a channel for a long time, always telling himself he would start.
- What finally pushed him was a quote from Ali Abdaal on a Chinese saying: the best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago, and the second best time is now. His takeaway is to start now and let hindsight explain why it was the right time.
