## Early path: unpaid leave, failed projects, and Testimonial's launch

- Damon spent 8 years as a self-described "mediocre" engineer at Cisco. When COVID hit he was working from home with his wife and a 1-year-old, and he asked for 6 months of unpaid leave to spend time with his child.
- During that leave he built 5 side projects. The first was Indielog (indielog.com), a community where indie makers could vlog and share their journey. Three more projects failed and made no money, so four projects in a row earned $0 over about 6 months.
- The 5th project was Testimonial, a platform for business owners to collect video testimonials. It reused about 80% of the Indielog code. He added a landing page and Stripe payments and launched on Product Hunt at the end of his leave.
- Lesson he lived: reuse existing code and ship a thin, monetized version fast, rather than starting from scratch on each idea.

## Launch pricing and first revenue

- At the Product Hunt launch he sold lifetime access to Testimonial for $199.
- He sold 30 lifetime deals and made $6K in the first two weeks. After four projects with $0 revenue, this was the first proof of demand.
- He moved to subscription pricing shortly afterward.
- Early customers came from the Indie Hackers community (he had met makers through indiehackers.com and indielog.com) and from social media. He tweeted that he was close to $1,000 MRR, and Andrew Gazdecki (founder of acquire.com) saw it and signed up for the highest plan.

## Quitting the job: validation threshold and deal with his spouse

- He went back to Cisco after the leave, got staffed on a new project, and kept building Testimonial on nights and weekends.
- Four months later it made $2K MRR. He acknowledges that is not much in the Bay Area, but it was enough traction to try going full time.
- Quitting would cut household income by 50% and mean dipping into savings. He and his wife set explicit conditions:
  - He would go back to a tech job if he could not reach $100K ARR within a year.
  - She would cover the mortgage and bills in the meantime.
- Takeaway: he turned a risky decision into a time-boxed experiment with a clear revenue target and a fallback.

## Working routine as a solo founder with young kids

- Kids were at home, so he split the day around their schedule:
  - Daytime: short breaks for bug fixes and answering support tickets.
  - Night: building new features after the kids went to bed (they napped and slept early).
- He even answered support tickets from his phone at the playground.
- Support and bug fixing stayed in the daytime gaps; deep feature work was reserved for uninterrupted night hours.

## Growing Testimonial to $800K+ ARR: product-led growth

- Damon describes himself as an engineer who doesn't know much about marketing or sales, so he leaned on product-led growth.
- The core growth loop:
  1. A customer asks their own customers for testimonials.
  2. Testimonials appear on a "wall of love" landing page that carries the Testimonial logo.
  3. Visitors to the wall of love sign up for their own Testimonial account.
- The logo also pushes upgrades, because people pay for the paid plan to remove it.
- He hired his first employee at $400K ARR, to help with marketing, and says he's proud of scaling to mid-six figures by himself.

## Growth levers with outsized impact: affiliates and the .io domain

- **Affiliate program:** anyone who signs up a paid customer earns 30% commission every month, and the referred customer gets 15% off for the first year. It contributes about 10-15% of Testimonial's total revenue.
- **Buying testimonials.io:** he noticed many people mistakenly visited testimonial.io instead of testimonial.to. He spent $35,000 to buy the .io domain. When he checked recently, that domain had brought in $80K in lifetime revenue, so he judged it worth the investment.

## PDF.ai: spun out of Testimonial

- Damon says he had no interest in building another AI tool. The idea came from Testimonial customers, many of them e-commerce merchants who wanted a better testimonial collection rate.
- Their problem: merchants' customers often didn't know how to use the product, which led to negative reviews.
- His idea was a chatbot that lets a merchant's customers interact with product user manuals, which usually come as PDFs.
- PDF.ai grew to $500K+ ARR in a few months and, with Testimonial, brought his total to $1.3M+ ARR, all bootstrapped by himself. The channel breakdown for PDF.ai is behind the paywall in this source.
