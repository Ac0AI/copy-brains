## Commercialization and keeping OpenClaw free and open source

The speaker in this source is Peter Steinberger (OpenClaw's creator); Theo's contributions are the founder and open-source commentary below. Theo says that running your own company is a common misconception of freedom:

- "People seem to think like when you run your own company, you get more freedom somehow. But it's actually the exact opposite."
- A company means a payroll to meet, employees who need enough (but not too much) work, benefits to handle, and income to guarantee pay.
- It also means investors who need to see growth that gives them a chance of making money back.
- "I have never felt more like my day was like locked in and controlled by external forces as I have as a founder running a company."

## Why open source is freer than a company

Theo argues open source is "way more freeing even if you have users that are upset with you."

- It is easier to say no when you have users than when you have investors.
- Open source is "the safest way to figure this out."
- Companies have to take certain paths because of external pressures, and the opportunity to experiment shrinks when that happens.
- He frames staying open as protecting the ability to experiment.

## Fund the open source you actually use

Theo calls out the gap between what people spend and what they give the projects they rely on:

- He asked the room to raise a hand if they bought a Mac Mini for OpenClaw; many did (about $700 each).
- Then he asked who donated to the project; "a much smaller number."
- His line: "You can spend $700 on a Mac you didn't need. You can't spend $5 a month on the project."
- People spend about $200 a month across three AI subscriptions but pay nothing for the project that brought them there.
- He asks people to donate to the open source projects they depend on.

## Launch more, and launch every surface

Theo advises Peter to be louder about shipping:

- "You got to launch more." The market is at a point where companies announce a dark mode for one page.
- Each surface (iOS app, iPadOS app, Android app) has different users and deserves its own launch.
- Each launch should be watched daily, with a short video posted on Twitter.

## Self-adapting software and the AI labs

Theo's views on where agentic software is heading:

- He likes the idea of agents making changes on your machine from plain language; setting up a new Linux machine was more fun with tools that modify it for you.
- He notes the big labs want to let agents run with more trust (he cites Sam Altman moving from checking every command to running Codex in yolo mode and leaving it on), but they can't be the ones to say so.
- That makes it valuable to see someone push the opposite extreme and show how far these things can go.
- He thinks one of Peter's advantages is being slightly outside the AI hype: coming in later, he wasn't distracted by MCPs and fine-tuning, and could mix some AI with "a little bit of the crazy" that the big labs wouldn't dare ship.
