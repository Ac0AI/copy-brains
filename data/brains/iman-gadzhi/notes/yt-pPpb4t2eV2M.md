## Why Iman says growth marketing is the most beginner-friendly online business

- Iman argues most beginners fail (he says "over 90%") because they pick models that are viable but hard, like "playing a new video game on the hardest difficulty."
- He scores business models on six metrics: unit gain per transaction, startup capital, speed to first dollar, skill requirements, fulfillment complexity, and scalability.
- His ideal beginner model:
  - At least $2,000 per project, so a handful of sales reaches $10k/month.
  - Startup capital close to $0.
  - First dollar in 14 to 30 days.
  - Basics learnable in a week, low fulfillment complexity, and growth without more hours.
- His verdict: partnering with creators to build and launch digital products for them fits every criterion, and AI now does the specialized work.

## How he rates the common beginner models

- **High-ticket sales:** Good unit gain, near-zero startup cost. Needs 2 to 6 months to first dollar and "one to two years" of practice to get consistent. Scalability is poor because every sale needs a call, so it trades time for money.
- **Drop shipping:** $20 to $50 per sale, so $10k/month needs 200 to 500 sales. Needs $1,000 to $5,000 to test products and run ads. At his agency, they tested "20 plus products before finding one that works." Skills needed: ads, product research, support, logistics. Margins thin as ad costs rise.
- **Affiliate marketing:** $50 to $250 per sale. Needs $1,000 to $5,000 for ads or audience building. Two to four months to first dollar. Thin margins: 60 to 90% of revenue goes back into ads, so $10k revenue might leave $1k to $4k.
- **Trading:** Unit gain is undefined (win $200, lose $300). Needs $5,000 to $10,000 capital. He can "count on one hand" the people he knows who are consistently profitable long term. He calls it gambling with money you need.

## The creator gold rush: why the market gap exists

- Iman cites the creator economy at over $250 billion, projected at $500 billion by 2027, with over 50 million creators.
- Most creators have an audience and trust but no digital product (course, template, guide, community).
- His claim: everyone with an audience, even those who say trading or drop shipping, ultimately earns through digital products sold to that audience.
- Small creators spend 6 to 8 hours a day on content and lack time or tools to build products. That is the gap.
- Whop (he is a co-owner) data: users took 4 years to earn the first billion, 6 months for the second, and are on pace for the third in 4.5 months.
- He says the next 12 to 24 months are the best window to start.

## The model: partner with micro creators for a revenue share

- The "growth marketer" partners with a creator, builds the digital product with AI, and keeps a percentage of profits.
- Target micro creators: 10,000 to 100,000 followers on a very specific niche (fitness coaches, language teachers, photographers, gardening). He cites Linktree data of 41 million such creators.
- He says only about 1% already monetize with a digital product.
- Examples he cites:
  - A creator with 17,000 followers led to six figures.
  - A sober living homes creator with 39,000 followers produced $57,000+.
  - A student got $6,000 setup fee plus a 70/30 split in week one.
- Math he gives: a creator making $30,000/month from 5,000 followers at a 30% split pays $9,000/month with no extra work, recurring while the creator keeps promoting.
- Targets: 1 to 2 new partnerships a month at about $5,000/month each, adding $5k to $10k in monthly recurring revenue each month.
- Suggested split when starting: 70% to the creator, 30% to you, to make it a "no-brainer." Later it can move to 60/40, 50/50, or favor you.

## Step 1, Find: win creators with a monetization game plan

- Mistake: generic outreach like "Hey, want to work together?" He notes you would ignore it yourself.
- Instead, give value first: a custom monetization game plan so the creator realizes they are "sitting on a gold mine" and you "have the map."
- The plan contains:
  - An audit of their profile showing which content performs and what the audience wants to buy.
  - Money left on the table and projected revenue.
  - Tailored product ideas with pricing based on audience size and niche.
  - A launch strategy and the exact 14-day process to sell.
  - Validation carousels the creator can post to test demand.
- Workflow he demonstrates: paste the creator's Instagram handle into Ghostwriter (his AI tool); it transcribes and analyzes their content in minutes, then generates the plan.
- Outreach message is short and offers value up front. Then a call to settle which product idea and the revenue split.
- He cites one student who signed a second creator 2 days after the first at a 30% share, and another who got $3,000 upfront plus 20% from a one-hour conversation.

## Step 2, Build: create the product with specialized AI

- Tool shown: Synthesis AI. Flow:
  - Feed in the Ghostwriter audit export of the creator.
  - Choose the expert path (product built around the creator's personal brand, face and voice).
  - AI generates hundreds of ideas and surfaces the top 20, each with a score (the demo ranged from 27 to 34).
  - Pick the highest-scoring unique value zone, then a format (coaching offer, ebook, or video course).
  - AI generates the transformation map, offer USP and name, a five-week action plan, 15 worksheets, and a program outline with module content.
- He says this used to take him "4 to 6 months day and night" when building programs 7 to 8 years ago.
- He says he built a product with it in about 90 minutes that more than 13,000 people bought, earning over $370,000.

## Step 3, Launch: the 14-day Instagram story sequence

- Creators already post stories daily, so audiences are trained to watch them. The creator does the selling; the growth marketer supplies the scripts and support.
- Three phases:
  - Days 1 to 3, warm-up: curiosity, gathering intelligence, investment, identification, community.
  - Days 4 to 9, value delivery: authority, trust, reciprocity, social proof, story.
  - Days 10 to 14, open cart: the product is released.
- Workflow: export the product charter from Synthesis, paste into a Google Doc (shared "anyone with the link"), create the product in Ghostwriter, then generate each day's story copy (with hook variations, CTA variations, visual suggestions) and send to the creator.
- Typical result he cites for a creator with 50,000 to 100,000 followers: 100 to 250 sales at $100 to $200, so $10k to $50k revenue and $3k to $15k for a 30% cut.
- Creators can reopen the offer monthly. He says doing 1 to 2 launches a month with different creators takes people from $5k to $30k/month.
- His own claim: 10 story sequences written with Ghostwriter produced $1.4 million, about $140,000 per sequence.
