## Why Iman thinks an agency is the best launchpad

Iman (in conversation with Eddie Maalouf, who runs a 20 million plus a year agency) agrees that an agency is the best way to get to roughly 50-100K a month with no cash or capital.

- He says that if he started an agency today with no money, he would be at 100K a month "by the end of April guaranteed", because he knows how to get clients and service them.
- Past a few million a year in profit, he argues it gets harder: you have to hire hundreds of people, the team stops being "10 of us in a room", and it becomes an HR and sales-team headache.
- He frames an agency as a skill-building Launchpad. You learn sales, copy, ads and cash flow, then use that to go start or buy other businesses.
- He calls the 50K-a-month floor a safety net. Because no one can take the skill from you, he can take bigger swings.
- He says agency income tends to compound with age. Repeat clients and referrals build, and people prefer an agency that has been around seven years over one that has been around one. He contrasts this with e-commerce, where a brand can go stale.

## Agency exit multiples and the "agency as engine" model

Iman describes the exit math and how he uses his agency to fund acquisitions.

- **Multiples on profit:** a small agency sells for roughly 1-2x yearly profit, so 100K profit gets about 200K cash. Bigger agencies reach about 7-10x.
- **Offer received:** they were already offered 6x on profit but declined to grow further. His example: 7 million profit at 6x is 42 million cash.
- **Exit target:** about 150 million, probably in the next 3-5 years. He would stay a couple of years because the agency is the engine for his other companies.
- **Acquisition play:** he buys companies with good products but weak marketing and uses the agency to scale them. He bought a 7-location restaurant franchise and says he tripled sales across all seven in 90 days.
- **Investments:** he put 1.2 million cash into a 10% stake in a company, aiming for a 100 million exit, which would make his stake worth at least about 10 million.
- **Cash flow:** the agency brings solid cash about 10 months a year, enough to reinvest and buy three or four companies a year.
- **Stress:** at 200 employees, "there's problems every day". He says that at 50K a month he would live the easiest life, so selling means getting "five to 10 years worth of my money all in one day".

## Team structure and getting out of client communication

Iman describes how communication and structure evolved as the agency grew.

- **Progression:** WhatsApp group chats at the start, then Slack (easier to bookmark, no personal numbers shared). He ran early tasks from a handwritten notebook until about 60-70K a month, then moved to a CRM and project management tools.
- **Advice on infrastructure:** when small, keep it quick and intimate. Adding rules and software too early puts everyone "in a box".
- **Hire the opposite of yourself:** if you are good at sales, hire fulfillment; if you are good at fulfillment, hire sales. Hiring another salesperson just takes your best player off the court.
- **Early on, do both:** his first employee was a VA at 1,000 a month, which was hard to afford.
- **Current setup:** 8-10 direct reports (heads of email marketing, Facebook ads, Google, HR, marketing). He admits he is over-involved and talks to 30-40 people regularly.
- **The first hire to be free:** someone whose job is to talk to clients. Otherwise "you're just an employee for like 30 other businesses". At 50 clients he was talking to only one of them.

## Choosing a niche: follow who responds, then check size and access

Iman says to pick the niche from where clients actually come in, not from research alone.

- **Start broad:** they cold emailed small businesses and saw who replied. Early niches were kids' birthday parties, chiropractors and car shops. If 100 of 10,000 emails respond and most are chiropractors, learn chiropractors.
- **Why:** he says people go deep on a niche they then cannot close, because there are too few of them or they are unreachable.
- **Birthday party venues:** closed Monday to Friday, so owners answered cold emails within minutes and got on calls. He says he rarely shares this niche because it was so good.
- **Three tests:** how big is the market, how accessible are the buyers, and do you like the work. The first two matter more.
- **Too small example:** custom car wrap shops, about 50 per state, only around 2,000 reachable, and it got hard once competitors arrived.
- **Good examples:** chiropractors and nail salons (answer the phone every time, roughly one on every corner). He says nail salons can easily reach 100K a month.
- **Expansion:** the agency now has three divisions under one roof, local, coaching and info products, and e-commerce. He recommends starting with local.

## Getting clients: run ads for yourself and never rely on one source

- Iman says his biggest early obstacle was getting clients, and about 80% of his time went to it.
- He used cold email, Instagram DMs, cold calls and even driving a Google Maps route to walk into 14 dentists a day. One client in a day was a win, and 20 days a month meant roughly 20 clients.
- **Hedge channels:** emails may take six months to work, so do not sit idle; use several sources at once.
- **Ads turned it around:** he says it is like a basketball coach who does not play. Once he ran ads for his own agency in 2020, it took off. At the start of 2020 he had about 15 clients; in July 2020 they signed 15 clients in one day from ad-booked demos.
- **Personal brand:** "your content is really your resume". He says that in the last 18 months a personal brand became necessary for agencies.
- **Quality over reach:** he needs about "10,000 of the right people", not a million views. Build content aimed at your niche (for example, dentist marketing) and avoid surface-level content that gets likes from the wrong people.
- **Example:** a "day in the life" video does nothing for clients who want to know what works for them.

## Content operations behind the personal brand

- He has six video and photo people plus 12 editors, serving about four YouTube channels, six Instagrams and client work.
- **Weekly rhythm:** a Monday afternoon meeting covers a 10-day content schedule across all pages. His team posts everything; he only responds to comments and DMs.
- **Approval:** the team sends a sheet with each post, thumbnail and caption, and he has final say.
- **Planning ahead:** big days need about a month. Their April Fools stunt, renaming the company from "Bad Marketing" to "good marketing" with t-shirts for staff, took that long.
- **Doubling down:** film ahead and copy what is already working for them or others.
- He suggests learning the camera basics yourself (ISO, aperture), which "you can just learn in like one day".

## Which agency models to avoid as a beginner, and why

- **Info-product growth agencies and "growth operator" deals** (split profits with a creator): he does run an info agency, but only after 10 years, and he hires people with multiple six-figure salaries.
- **Reasons against:** you depend on the creator, organic reach runs out, then you must spend money against top marketers. The creator can also decide you are useless.
- **Rule of thumb:** wait until you have run an agency for "at least two years" before those models.
- **Service-educated vs uneducated clients:** compare knowledge out of 10. Local businesses are about 1-3, e-commerce owners about 5-7, info creators about 8-10. Target clients who know less than you about your service.
- **E-commerce warning:** a client who can afford you and knows little about their main growth driver is an alarm bell.
- **Hiring evidence:** a media buyer posting drew 2,600 applications, trimmed to 700 and then about 150, with very high quality.
- **Progression:** their agency went local first, then e-commerce, then info. He says some things can go from 5 years to 2, but not to 2 months.
- **Beyond ads:** other agency types are automations (Zapier or Make, learnable in 60-90 days), Excel, and Discord setup and security. He says his automation employees earn hundreds of thousands a year.

## Skills, imposter syndrome and staying organized

- **Learn the full process:** he tells a student to learn copy, sales and ads together, then expand. He says a single call at 10-15K from an agency owner's experience pays far more than being only a copywriter.
- **Imposter syndrome:** you may be a 6 in Facebook ads and Zapier while the client is a 0, so you already add value.
- **Niche terminology trick:** learn key terms (for dentists: "patient", root canal, teeth whitening, Invisalign) and clients feel you know their world. He would watch a 10-minute video on the car ride in. He calls imposter syndrome "just in your head".
- **Organization:** avoid analysis paralysis. Write down the top five things each day, which adds up to 25 important tasks a week. At scale, his calendar is color-coded with every minute planned, including family dinners.
- **Tools:** he recommends GoHighLevel for running client accounts, including Google review work.
