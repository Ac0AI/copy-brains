## Why Iman advertises on Facebook and what it does for a business

- Iman cites Facebook at 1.93 billion daily active users and $28 billion in yearly ad revenue, with roughly $1 cost per click and an 11.54 cent CPM.
- He runs two businesses that use ads. His agency, IIG Media (started at 16, run for five and a half years), makes about $1.2-1.5 million a year in profit. Its CMO runs it and Iman works only 4-5 hours a week on it.
- He says he hasn't touched a client ad account in about two years. The agency gets new clients through referrals and by advertising for itself, then advertises for its clients.
- His education company helps agency owners, and about 95% of its business is organic. It still runs some ads for new customers.
- Four benefits he lists:
  - **Testing:** results in as little as a day, to validate whether the market wants an offer.
  - **Targeting:** Facebook has the most user data of any ad network.
  - **CPC:** you pay only per click. Clicks can cost as little as one cent, though typical averages are 30 cents to $2 depending on market and country.
  - **Predictability:** once the funnel and offer are dialed in, you can put in $1 and consistently get $2, $3, $5 or even $10 out.
- Goals he lists: sell more, generate leads, distribute content, drive app installs.

## Account setup: page, Business Manager, ad account, payment

Iman's rule is to give Facebook as much real business information as possible, because accounts get disabled easily.

- **Page:**
  - Use real brand info.
  - Post at least 20 posts before running ads. Post 3 times a day for the first 7 days, then once a day, and keep the page active.
- **Business Manager:**
  - Use a work email, not Gmail.
  - Complete business verification as soon as it's offered. It proves the business is registered and helps avoid bans.
- **Ad account:**
  - Business Manager holds the ad accounts, pages, pixels and payment methods.
  - The time zone must match the currency (US time zones with USD), or ads won't deliver and you'll need a new account.
  - You start with one ad account. After Facebook first charges your card, the limit rises to three per Business Manager. He recommends creating all three immediately as backups.
- **Payment:**
  - He advises against PayPal, saying it seems to raise the odds of shutdowns.
  - Repeatedly declined cards also cause shutdowns. For those clients, use direct debit from the bank account so there are never declines.
  - Never put your own card on a client's account. The client adds their own card, so billing goes straight from Facebook and the business name matches the card.
- **Shutdowns:** he says that across his team and clients, hundreds of ad accounts (possibly 1,000) have been shut down. In e-commerce and education, it is "almost more rare to have your ad account not shut down than it is to have it shut down", so he says not to stress about it.

## Domain verification and the pixel

- Domain verification has been required to run ads since the iOS 14 update. Methods: DNS, HTML file, or meta tag. His agency most often uses DNS: copy the code from Business Manager, add a TXT record at the domain registrar (host "@"), then click verify. If stuck, search "how to add txt dns record on [registrar]".
- The pixel is code placed on every page that tracks clicks, conversions (purchases, leads, add to cart, initiate checkout) and revenue. Create it in Events Manager.
- Two install options:
  - Paste the code into the header of all pages.
  - Use the Conversions API (server-side tracking).
- Wordpress, ClickFunnels, Leadpages, Shopify, WooCommerce and Webflow have native integrations where you paste the pixel ID. He tells people to search "how to install facebook pixel on [builder]".

## Campaign structure and budget split

- There are three layers:
  - **Campaign:** the objective (conversions, engagement, lead gen, video views, traffic). He mostly optimizes for conversions.
  - **Ad set:** targeting, bids, placements, schedule and budget.
  - **Ad:** the copy, headline, image or video, and destination URL.
- He usually builds one campaign per offer, with separate testing and prospecting campaigns:
  - Testing campaigns: one for audiences, one for creatives, one for landing pages.
  - Winners are promoted into a prospecting campaign that holds the best creatives, audiences and landing pages.
- Budget split: 70-80% prospecting and 20-30% testing.
- Testing campaigns use ad set level budgets, so each ad set gets enough spend for a statistically significant call. Prospecting campaigns use campaign budget optimization, because they are more consistent and need less upkeep.

## Targeting: go broad

- Think about the majority of your customers (age, gender, interests) and use demographic targeting.
- For interest targeting, test one interest at a time so there are fewer variables.
- One of the agency's historically best audiences across all industries is broad targeting, meaning no segmentation. Facebook's machine learning has so much data that it tends to beat micromanaged targeting, and more pixel data makes broad work better.
- Always pick large audiences. Small ones cause delivery problems, high frequency and CPMs that skyrocket. Profitable ads need low CPMs.

## Creatives are the biggest lever

- If you take one thing from the video: "80% of your focus should be on developing better creatives and testing more variations of them."
- A great creative can work with bad targeting, structure and bidding. Perfect targeting, structure and bidding can't save bad creatives.
- Formats:
  - **Single image:** simple, but it has made his clients "multi multi multi eight figures".
  - **Video:** his best performing format, with no length limit. It needs a strong hook, a dynamic video to keep people watching, and a strong call to action at the end.
  - **Carousel:** good for showing multiple products or services. It usually gets a higher CPM, so test whether it's worth it.
  - **Collections:** an on-platform instant experience that takes more screen space and tends to get higher click-through rates.
- Three keys to a high performing ad:
  1. It stops the scroll, with a visual that triggers a reaction.
  2. It earns the click, mainly through curiosity, without being clickbaity (which gets ads rejected).
  3. It doesn't look like an ad. It should look like a post from a friend, because people have "banner blindness".

## Ad copy that avoids disabled accounts

- Don't focus on benefits or promises about the end result, as typical copywriting courses teach. Claims put your account at risk of being disabled.
- What works for him: talk about product features, and tell a story or use a metaphor or analogy.
- Avoid:
  - results-oriented claims
  - health claims
  - personal attributes
  - sensational words such as "fantastic", "wonderful", "secret" and "unbelievable"

## Budget, ROAS and optimization

- ROAS is driven by three metrics:
  - **Cost per click:** a function of CPM and click-through rate. Facebook penalizes ads it considers bad with high CPMs and rewards high engagement with low CPMs.
  - **Conversion rate:** his rule of thumb is above 2% for e-commerce and above 1% for education. Invest in conversion rate optimization.
  - **Average order value:** raise it with post-purchase upsells and by experimenting with pricing.
- Testing budget: never go below 0.5x the product price per ad set, which would equal a 2x ROAS if you get a purchase. Ideally test everything at 1x the product price or more. Example: testing a $2,000 product with $20 a day for 3 days is only $60, which would be a 33x ROAS if it sold, which is unrealistic.
- Prospecting budget has no rule: set it as high as possible as long as it brings in sales profitably.
- Optimizing:
  - Account for the customer journey length (7 days, instant, or 30+ days). If customers take about 3 days to convert, don't judge yesterday's results.
  - After an ad set spends its full testing budget, keep it if it hits your target KPIs and kill it if not. Kill clear losers fast, because they drag down overall account performance.
  - Never judge ROAS alone. Look at CPC, conversion rate and AOV side by side, find the one hurting most, and fix that bottleneck.
