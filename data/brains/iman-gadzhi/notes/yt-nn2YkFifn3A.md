## Becoming Wealthy Starts With Feeling Deserving of It, and Poverty Plus Proximity to Wealth Gave Iman That

- Iman grew up with a strange mix: his mom was on government benefits and worked temporary jobs (Harrods, Chelsea and Westminster hospital), his stepdad paid for private school but little else, and he was surrounded by wealthy people.
- For the last four years in that house there was no hot water and no heating. When his stepdad found out he was starting a business, he cut the wifi.
- He built his early business on a 25 pounds a month limited hotspot plan on Three, in 2015.
- He says the wealth around him did not motivate him; it normalized wealth. He argues that most people repel money because they don't feel deserving of it.
- His view: wealthy people are not necessarily smarter, "a lot of times it's just because they feel deserving of it." As a kid he would see a sports car and think "that'll be my reality very soon."
- He sees comfortable middle-class households as a weak spot, with enough comfort to lose the fire but no poverty-level hunger. He says he got "the best of both worlds."
- He started reading Rich Dad Poor Dad and Think and Grow Rich at 14 and calls those books his "phantom mentors."
- Book needs change by stage: Think and Grow Rich and Rich Dad Poor Dad for the start, then denser books like Reality Transurfing from roughly 100k a year toward a million. At the beginning he says it is simply discipline: "just do it like that's it." Mindset and shadow work matter later.

## Fulfillment vs Peace vs Joy: Pick What You Optimize For

- Iman rejects "happiness" as a goal, calling it "a whole load of [ __ ]". He says he is "not very happy" but doesn't care, because he optimizes for fulfillment.
- His three categories:
  - Fulfillment: doing something really hard (boxing, building a company, running a marathon). Example: after getting beaten up in a fight you don't feel happy, you feel fulfilled.
  - Peace: moving to a farm, walking in the woods.
  - Joy: waking up in Bali with friends, riding mopeds to a waterfall.
- The three conflict: fulfillment usually requires hard things that give neither peace nor joy in the moment, and joy (adventure) conflicts with peace (calm).
- His "terms and conditions" for copying his life: "it comes with not too much joy and pretty much next to zero peace" in this season, but more fulfillment and the things others want.
- Wealth normalizes pleasure. Using a taste analogy: at his level it's like losing your sense of taste, you still go to restaurants but get no pleasure from them. A grinder earning their first 100k gets more satisfaction than he does at eight figures.
- He flies private in Europe and first class long haul because it adds comfort and makes it easier to do bigger, harder things, not because it adds joy. He says he considered flying economy to reset but thinks it isn't sensible.
- He says the first time he buys his own jet with company branding would give him real pleasure, then it normalizes too.
- He tells people to go in "with your eyes open" about what the lifestyle does and doesn't give.

## Why an Agency Is the Best Stepping Stone (and Why Boring Beats Sexy)

- Iman says an agency should never be the end goal (he finds it a depressing business to run for 30 years) but it is "the best stepping stone possible."
- An agency is leverage: you hire people (contractors first, then full-time) to deliver the service. He compares it to law firms that pay a lawyer around 75 pounds an hour and bill the client 300. Clients prefer the agency because it has the team plus time for systems, operations and client delivery.
- Agencies throw off free cash flow. His example: outgoings of 1,000 a month versus 10,000 turned over. Agency owners talk profit only: "nothing else matters."
- Friends' agencies reach 10k, 20k, up to 100k a month profit, then the owners want e-commerce for the cool factor, while e-commerce owners want the agency's free cash flow.
- E-commerce reality: very low margins, cash fronted to suppliers, constant reinvestment. He knows owners of eight- and nine-figure-valued brands, including one doing about 30 million a year, with little profit and not much cash. His agency's most successful e-commerce clients sell things like glow-in-the-dark teddy bears and radio devices, not the next jewelry or clothing line. Coloured sand is fine: "if coloured sand makes money then who cares."
- His principle: make the business practical, then the life can be whatever you want. Don't chase the lifestyle before the business exists.
- Spotting fakes: look at how opulent someone's lifestyle is. He hired a full-time crypto researcher in September who "maybe has a drink once a month"; a real trader can't be partying because they're the only point of reference for their success. He can party hard because he built infrastructure and a team, whereas he couldn't in his first two or three years when he was the strongest link.
- Working from the beach is "horrendous," he says; sand and sun don't work for laptops.
- His own businesses are boring in practice. In his software company he and his co-founder spend their catch-ups on LTV, churn and bug fixes. "My life is good because of it."

## Starting Small: Low-Downside Businesses and Asymmetric Bets

- Iman's analogy: Formula One drivers start in go-karts; do not start with a super difficult business.
- He argues against beginners starting real estate, trading, or e-commerce. You can burn 1,500 dollars on ads just to find a product doesn't work, or lose 2,000 trading crypto. His rule: start where you can "fall, scrape your knees, get no's, get losses" with no real downside, "bowling with the bumpers on."
- Core framework: look for asymmetric returns like a chess player or fighter. Always ask what the downside is, aim for lots of upside with as close to zero downside as possible, and keep making those bets.
- To go from 0-10k or under 50k toward 100k and then a million, his first instruction is to increase income:
  - Sell the value you bring at your current job (while recognizing most companies are dead ends), or
  - Start a cash flow business or offer services, or
  - Get a good sales job making 10-30k a month and save 50-60%.
- He says don't be romantic about it: "do whatever you want I don't really care," just increase income first.
- Wealth for stepping into software: he and his co-founder bootstrapped AgencyFlow, an all-in-one platform for online boutique agencies (replacing Slack, Asana, invoicing tools, Pipedrive). It launched publicly in February (2022), received an eight-figure offer he turned down, and they plan to sell for hundreds of millions in three to five years. They were practical from day one and it came from lived agency pain.
- Target positioning: not for 3-person agencies or large traditional agencies. Teams up to roughly 30 can use it, but large sales teams should move to tools like Close or HubSpot. "There's a tool for everyone at their current stage."
- He says the agency experience let him see what the market needs before building software, whereas many people start software without knowing the need.

## Monk Mode and Environment Design

- Iman's monk mode began in cape Town in spring 2020 during lockdown, when South Africa banned alcohol and cigarettes and travel beyond the supermarket. He then repeated it deliberately for six weeks: no alcohol, 45 minutes of phone screen time a day, strict carnivore diet.
- His general protocol for others: at least 15 minutes of meditation a day, a screen-time cap, and exercise (he omits exercise from his own because it's already ingrained).
- Also no porn or masturbation unless in a relationship. He ranks from worst to best: porn and masturbation, then masturbation with intention/imagination, then sex with a person, then sex with someone you care about. He says "sexual energy is the most powerful energy that man has and most guys just waste it into a tissue."
- Duration: 21 to 90 days. Three weeks is already intense; he doesn't recommend more than 90 days because "you'll go crazy." Afterward blow off steam (sometimes 3-4 days, sometimes up to four months), then do another period.
- Environment is everything: he can't do monk mode in London or Dubai in peak season (December-February) because friends, girls and visitors flood in. That's why he keeps a house in Cape Town to get away from distractions. He picks locations by season, since Cape Town's summer is November-February.
- Suggested locations for starting an agency: Cape Town, Romania, Georgia, Colombia, Brazil, Eastern Europe generally. Half the cost and better quality of life means "you're already earning twice as much money." He prefers time-zone-friendly places and says Bali caused trouble with sales calls for him.
- Slow-mad: stay in a place six to twelve weeks (not four or five days) so you can keep protocols. For people who can't travel: work with what you have, and sell excess stuff (cars, watches) to fund a three-week trip, which he says can cost about 1,500 pounds.
- What holds people back is mostly what they consume, not what they fail to do: news, the YouTube subscription box, junk content, food, drink, and people they associate with. He gives a mild nod that Twitter and TikTok can have good pockets of information.
- Consuming 87 hours of videos without action is pointless. "You got to know what game you're playing."

## Moving to Dubai, Taxes and Wealth Structuring

- Iman left the UK in January 2021 because the government telling him he couldn't leave his house was his limit. He looked for countries "going in the right direction" and chose Dubai (also cites Miami in the US) because they don't antagonize wealthy people.
- His argument: wealthy people create the jobs and have to provide value in a free market. He dislikes bankers and government but not the wealthy. He says he never villainized the wealthy when his family was on benefits.
- Tax approach (he says "minimize, not avoid"): once you control your income and aren't on earned career income, you may never need to pay personal tax again. One method he mentions: take loans against assets. Companies still pay corporate tax, VAT and employer tax.
- Staying clean with UK tax authorities: he spends under 45 days a year in the UK (allowed 90), owns no UK property, and has no ties, to avoid giving HMRC a reason. He is skeptical of UK landlord rules, such as being unable to deduct loan interest and tenant rules on sales.
- Time-wise he says most of his income reached its current level in 2021-2022, and now earns over ten million a year.
- Dubai property as an investment: his apartment was worth 7.2 million dirhams when he moved in (four bedrooms plus maid's room, beach, sea views) and rent was 600,000 dirhams a year (about 7.5% of value). A year later it is worth 9.2 million and similar apartments rent for around 800,000. He plans to buy mainly Dubai apartments and some houses, and rent them out rather than use Airbnb.
- Seasons: he would never raise a family in Dubai or Cape Town, but they suit this season of life.
- Cost of living: in Cape Town or Bali long-term, 20-30k a month gives a king's life and he struggles to spend over 30k a month there; in London 30k a weekend is easy and 30k a month is not king-level. He says location freedom is a key advantage of agency and software businesses.

## Money Philosophy, Crypto and Investing

- Iman says he has "no respect for money" in the sense of treating it like scarce air. Money is energy and has to flow: pay suppliers and partners on time, don't hoard, provide value in excess of what you take. People who worship and hoard money usually have little of it.
- He saves and invests 80% of what he makes and spends 20%, which he forces himself to do. He notes you need higher income before that split is possible.
- Crypto story: he put about 1 million dollars into crypto, which grew to over 10 million at the peak. In a video he screen-shared a 6.8 million dollar portfolio, then 3-5 days later it was 3.5 million. The big lesson was taking profits. He says he is not a genius and you can't time tops or bottoms, so dollar cost average out on the way up and in on the way down, using key price ranges and standing orders.
- Current posture (October 2022): about 70% cash, fully out of crypto by April (last sale was Luna), then bought roughly 1 million dollars of Bitcoin with orders at about 20k, 19k and 18k. He expects 16-17k "almost a guarantee" and possibly 14-13k, and says "if Bitcoin goes down to 14 15,000 I'm deploying five million dollars."
- He sees the stock market falling another 20-30% around November, and real estate collapsing about 18 months after stocks, so around 2023. He's stacking cash for that, especially Dubai property. Cash gives "time to think."
- He sees most NFTs as scams but frames NFT as a pricing model: charge more upfront and take royalties (8.5% on resales for his project).
- Watches: his portfolio is around 3.5 million dollars, nearly all bought at retail, and he won't sell because resellers get blacklisted by brands. He picks pieces no longer in mass production and not hype pieces, and he buys Patek, Rolex and others through relationships. Example: a Patek Nautilus 5712 a friend bought for 140,000 pounds in February, which Iman bought pre-owned with box and papers for 77,000. He also holds 300-400k of Hermès-type bags (Birkins and Kellys) as investments.
- Ego-driven mistake: he bought three Bored Apes for a total of 1.2 million dollars as a brand and press play, sold two for about 100-150k loss each, and kept one with a Soviet hat. His lesson: "don't ever make a decision based off of ego."

## Gentlemen's Croquet Club: Memberships as Assets and Vetting for Community Quality

- Iman co-founded Gentlemen's Croquet Club (GCC), an NFT-based members club of about 700 members out of 975 NFTs. The floor price was once about 16 ETH-equivalent per his figures, about 6.5 more recently. At one point one NFT was worth roughly 50,000 dollars per his words.
- His idea: your seat at the table should be an asset. In clubs like Annabel's or a 10,000 pound a year gym club like Kicks, when you leave you just stop paying; with an NFT you can sell your spot. He wants masterminds structured the same way.
- Why people pay: the price reflects the people in the room, not the facility. He kept a Kicks membership at 10k a year without using the gym, for the meetings and relationships.
- Vetting: members must do a video call application explaining why they deserve to pay 5,000 dollars. He wants no anonymous or ego-driven members, says money doesn't make someone interesting, and has a "no lame people" rule. He lets go of staff who are great at work but lame, with a 60-day or six-year hiring philosophy, and takes 10 team members to visit the schools he builds in Nepal each November.
- Product stack: a custom vault that authenticates via MetaMask; courses on buying watches at retail, investing in crypto and watches, social circles and Instagram; three research reports a week from his crypto researcher (some say "don't do anything"); an upcoming member Rolodex directory so members can see who lives in a city and contact them via Instagram, Twitter or email.
- Gentleman's training: he wants it to teach people to be gentlemen (wine, cigars, conduct) because he is "new money" who had to learn these things, and went skiing for the first time six months earlier.
- A concierge partnership (under NDA) with an extremely selective private membership concierge service, with an app launching in 3-4 months. Members skip an application with a sub-1% acceptance rate. Examples: a private jet leg quoted 16k via the concierge versus 22k via his broker; a Chanel/Hermès-type bag sourced in two days; restaurant reservations at the busiest London spots.
- He deliberately avoids marketing until key features are live ("once we do marketing it's game over").

## Psychedelics and Meditation

- Iman did LSD at 15, bought with Bitcoin (he says that is not recommended for most people), and later tried mushrooms and ayahuasca.
- His view: psychedelics make sense "when you're lost in life." He hasn't used them in a while because he's in an execution phase where he knows what he needs to do. If he were at a real crossroads he would consider them.
- Meditation is a staple in his routine, and he notes that without it for two or three weeks he feels the difference.
