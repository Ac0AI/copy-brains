## How Iman structures a performance-based e-commerce deal (live two-call close)

**Context:** Iman closes an e-commerce brand (Swedish store, run by two founders aged 23) for his Facebook ads agency. They spent €60k on ads and generated €200k revenue in their first month. The founder was running ads himself 4-5 hours a day. The first month billed 7,144 pounds (about $12,000 including the base retainer). Iman expected the second month's performance fee to land around 15,000 pounds.

## The two-call close and stalling

- Iman had just traveled about 36 hours and hadn't watched the demo call recording. He told the client directly that he couldn't make an offer that day.
- He used the first call as a stall: gather preliminary information, say "no pressure", and pencil in a second call. He called it "a prime example of me stalling" since he "basically knew nothing about the client going into it".
- He now uses Zoom's waiting room feature to avoid two-call closes: put the prospect in the waiting room, confer with his media buyer Danny for a few minutes, then reconvene.
- On the second call, the two founders asked to talk privately. Iman had them mute and call each other, rather than kicking the decision to a later date. They came back and said yes.

## Questions to qualify and set expectations

- Asks what success looks like in 6 months. The client answered 500k to 1M a month at a 2.5-3 ROAS.
- Asks whether they mean total business revenue or revenue the agency brings. He pushes for a specific number "so we can just gather expectations", since "500k a month at a 7x ROAS" wouldn't be realistic.
- Pulls real numbers: spend, revenue, best-selling product share (70% from one LED spotlight), and margins (bought for about $6, sold for about 30 euros).
- Asks why they want an agency. Answer: to stop managing ads all day and focus on warehouse, stock, and expansion into other countries.
- Asks about past agency experience. The client's prior agency got hacked and lost their business manager and ad money.
- Pushes expansion: a UK client of his, told to advertise in the US, saw US ads reach 3x the business's entire prior volume within 2.5 months. He frames it as "maybe you're thinking a little too small".

## Pre-framing results and culture fit

- Early in the call he states two things must be a green light: results, and culture fit. He calls this a way to "get them to qualify themselves in a way that's not cringey".
- Results get a green light because the client fits his mold: a store doing 100-150k a month wanting 500k.
- He asks the culture question directly: "Why do you think you would be a good fit for our agency culture?" and gives an example of declining Hodinkee (about 15k a month retainer, a dream client) because "one person had to approve it with another person who had to approve it", which isn't how his agency works.
- Culture is "more important to us than anything": long-term partnership, "a relationship", not a car-salesman transaction.

## The pitch positioning

- Results-based: "not based on impressions, not based on clicks". The offer only works if the agency improves on what the client does.
- Lean team (himself, Danny, Kieran, Louise), no office since 2018, so the retainer pays for value rather than "an espresso addiction" or a "swanky office".
- Differentiator vs one-dimensional agency owners: he has spent 5-6K a day of his own money on ads, has run a 100,000-person email list (6-7 emails a week), and has negotiated Stripe fees.
- Fully end-to-end after onboarding: the agency does creatives and copy; the client only supplies assets. The client translates the copy to Swedish (done in two minutes, and they add the final touch).
- Over-communicate (weekly Looms, updates) while never chasing the client for creatives.
- Admits upfront "we're expensive", framed as "zero risk, infinite rewards".

## The offer structure (performance above a benchmark)

- Base retainer of 1,600 pounds a month (about $2,200), described as "tiny".
- Performance fee of 5% of "return on ad spend" (revenue minus ad spend), but only above 100k per month.
- Example: spend 100k, get 300k back = 200k net, 100k above the threshold, so fee is 5% of 100k = 5k. Another: spend 100k, bring back 350k = 250k net, fee on 150k.
- Rationale for the 100k threshold: the client already did about 140k net (200k minus 60k), so he won't "take credit for what you've already done", but 140k "could have been a fluke", so he leaves a margin of safety.
- Rule of thumb: for a client starting from scratch he charges a flat 5% with no stipulation. For a client already doing a decent amount, he sets a baseline and only charges above it. Charging 5% of an existing 200k/month "for doing literally nothing" would be a bad deal.
- A fixed baseline also helps retention: a client already paying 20k+ often starts thinking "we could have a team and an office for this", which he has seen cause churn. Setting a baseline means clients can't say they could have done it themselves.
- Terms: 6-month minimum, then rolling month by month, leave anytime. He introduced the 5-6 month minimum only around 9 months before this recording.
- He doesn't take on clients under about 4,000 pounds a month in billables. The base retainer is "inconsequential" to him; the aim is to push clients toward 400-500k a month, where the money gets "interesting".
- Incentive alignment: no percentage of ad spend or revenue, only of profit after ads. Additional channels (Google, YouTube, Pinterest) cost no extra since the fee is on net outcome. SEO is out of scope (paid traffic only).
- Clients can keep running parallel campaigns and testing their own products.

## Closing and onboarding

- After the "yes", next steps are immediate: send the invoice, collect company details (registered name, address, VAT ID) on the call, create a Slack channel, send the onboarding link (45 minutes to complete, so the team doesn't chase logins for 3 weeks), then book the onboarding call.
- The cofounder Kieran (background in law) adds contract amendments, delivered within 30-60 minutes.
- Result he shares: first-month bill 7,144 pounds, expected second month around 20-25k dollars total billable, and he sees potential for 40k. The client reported a day of about 350,000 Swedish crowns (about $35,000) in sales while the agency managed 10 ad accounts for them.

## Scaling Facebook ads at $12k/day (e-commerce account walkthrough)

A second segment (a media buyer walking through a client account; Iman shares it as part of his program) covers a client spending about $12,000 a day across 6 ad accounts, over $1M in 30 days. Before the agency, they spent no more than $2,000 a day.

- Campaign structure: CBOs treated as assets, never edited once live; underperformers are killed and relaunched. Over 400 campaigns across six accounts historically, budgets $50 to $600 a day. ($12,000 at $40 per ad set would be 300 ad sets, which is unmanageable.)
- Mostly CBO, one-day attribution window (tested vs seven-day click), kill on a one-day window.
- Scale aggressively the same day something works; kill less aggressively than you launch, because turning off too fast seems to hurt the pixel.
- Test about four creatives a week per product, pay attention to video ads.
- Funnel benchmarks cited: click to page view over 85% (account at 88%), page view to add to cart about 9% (5% still good), add to cart to checkout over 90%, checkout to purchase about 65%, store conversion about 5%.
- Facebook performance is "not linear"; launching more assets reduces the role of luck, then you kill the losers.
- Inspiration: the Facebook Ads Library. Scroll for ads running for months (they're working) and ads with multiple versions; check it isn't retargeting.
- Reporting: build ads reporting pivot tables with a standardized attribution setting (needs at least two ad accounts selected) to see totals across accounts.
