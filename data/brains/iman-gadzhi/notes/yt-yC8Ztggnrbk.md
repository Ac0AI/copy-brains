## Prospecting is building a contact list of businesses in your niche

- Iman defines prospecting as "simply building a list of businesses within your Niche businesses that will want to buy from you."
- Example: for kitchen and bathroom remodelers, build a list of every remodeler he can find.
- The list holds whatever contact info the outreach method needs. Cold calling needs a phone number, not an email.
- He teaches four methods: networking, manual lead sourcing, software, and B2B lead generation specialists.
- Each has pros and cons, so he recommends combining them to cover all bases.
- He says these are the methods he used in his agency over 6 years and taught to thousands of students in Agency Accelerator.
- Prospecting is ongoing. He says to build the habit of keeping the prospect list as big as possible.

## Method 1: Networking (best for the first client)

- If you have a competitive advantage, "use it unapologetically." Friends and family who work in your niche are "absolute gold."
- They can buy from you and also spread the word about your services.
- Tactic: ask for a chat in person, or by Zoom or phone, then break down your service and value proposition.
- Be open that you are a beginner getting your first clients and case studies. He says people appreciate the honesty, and most business owners have been in the same position.
- Two possible outcomes, both wins:
  - They are interested and buy.
  - You ask if they know anyone who would be interested and leave with referrals.
- Disadvantage: volume. Your network is only so big.
- Advantage: when you are starting out, it gives warm, qualified prospects. In his words, "the easiest way to sign your first client."

## Method 2: Manual lead sourcing (free, high quality, slow)

- Manual lead sourcing means looking for leads online. Search your industry plus location on Google, jump from site to site, and save details into a CRM.
- Google Maps: search an industry and location, then copy phone numbers and emails from the left-hand panel into your list. It is the easiest route for local businesses.
- LinkedIn: search niche keywords. For remodelers, that means terms like contractor, remodelers, builders. LinkedIn Sales Navigator adds advanced search and lead recommendations.
- Instagram: business owners follow each other there. Find one good prospect, go through their followers or following, and keep going down the rabbit hole to build the list fast.
- Online directories: sites where businesses register their contact details. He calls them a "game changer" and "truly underrated."
  - Common for service businesses such as plumbers, electricians, roofers and remodelers.
  - UK example: Checkatrade. Most countries have similar directories.
- Advantage: quality. You find everything yourself, so you know the contact details are correct and active. It costs $0.
- Disadvantage: very time-consuming and tedious.
- He calls it a great place for beginners to start.

## Method 3: Prospecting software (most efficient, lower list quality)

- Lead generation software lets you enter niche, location, size and revenue, and it pulls thousands or tens of thousands of leads quickly.
- You then import the list into your sales pipeline. His own agency software, Floy, has an import function for this.
- What to look for: it should be user-friendly and give you lots of leads quickly. Compare a few alternatives found by searching "lead generation software."
- Trade-off: quantity over quality. The list will not be perfect, and most tools can't tell whether a prospect is still active.
  - Skim the list and delete irrelevant prospects.
  - Use lead verification software, which flags dead leads when you run a list through it.
- Advantage: time saved and sheer volume. Asked whether the quality hit is worth it, he says "100%."

## Method 4: B2B lead generation specialists (outsourced prospecting)

- This is the advanced method. It applies his "contractor arbitrage" idea (outsourcing service delivery) to prospecting.
- Hire freelancers on Upwork or Fiverr by searching "B2B lead generation." Hundreds will build lead lists for a small fee.
- Risk: some charge for a subpar service and send random, unqualified businesses, sometimes outside your niche.
- Mitigation:
  - Check client reviews and proof of work, which both platforms show.
  - Buy the lowest package as a low-risk test.
- Package tiers: freelancers usually offer three tiers.
  - The cheapest tier has the highest cost per lead.
  - The top tier is the most expensive overall but has the lowest cost per lead and the most prospects.
- His advice: always buy the first tier, even though each prospect costs a bit more. Judge the list quality. If it's good, buy the top tier. If it's bad, find someone else.
- Advantage: a huge number of qualified leads. Downsides: cost, and risk if you aren't careful.
- His recommendation: if you have extra money to invest in your agency, give it a shot.

## Choosing the right mix: three questions

- **Have you used your network as much as possible?** If you have an unfair advantage, use it.
- **How much time do you have?**
  - With a demanding job that leaves about 2 hours a day, automate lead sourcing as much as possible.
  - Manual sourcing gives high-quality leads but is too time-consuming in that case. Lean on B2B lead generators and prospecting software.
  - Someone with a day job likely has enough cash to bankroll this for a few weeks without signing clients to pay the bills.
- **How much money can you invest?**
  - Early on there are two ways to grow: time investment (you do the tasks) or capital investment (you pay software or others to do them).
  - With money, invest in software or B2B lead generation experts.
  - Without money, do manual lead sourcing. It takes more effort but gets the same result.
- Whichever you pick, he says to use a combination of methods, since each has advantages and disadvantages.
