## Reading as a non-negotiable early habit

- Iman credits reading as the single biggest contributor to his success. From 2014 to 2017 he read a book every week.
- He says "there is no medium that's as powerful on earth as books" and that medium doesn't matter (print, audio, borrowed, library), though audiobooks "go in through one ear and straight out the other" for him.
- Cheap sourcing: in 2016 he bought used books at Brixton Bookmongers in London for 2-3 pounds instead of about 15 pounds new at Waterstones. If you can't afford that, borrow from a friend or use a library.
- Zen beginner's mind: early on he had no ego about executing. A book said meditate, so he meditated. A book said journal, so he journaled. He executed "blindfully" and says that strategy is powerful if you pick the right books.
- Today his ego makes him more selective, picking only what resonates. He says that is partly good but also a drag.
- Reading is non-negotiable when starting a business or career. Later, in a phase of pure doing, he reads less because he already knows what to execute.

## Acquire high income skills, even without a plan for them

- His first business, flipping Instagram accounts (about nine months, started with a page he ran at 14 in 2014 that someone offered to buy), taught him copywriting and social media growth.
- He learned videography/photo in 2016, which landed his first social media marketing client.
- Those three skills (copywriting, social growth, photo/video) combined into a creative agency. For 18 months the agency was exclusively creative; he pivoted to an advertising agency in early 2018. At 17 he was pulling in 25,000 a month.
- Advice: learn a high income skill even if you can't see how it pays off. You can't predict how it will help seven years later.
- Reading fed copywriting too: he kept a Evernote "lexicon library" of new words.
- One skill he wishes he had learned: coding.

## Abundance mindset: don't brake while accelerating

- He says many people have "your foot on the gas but also your foot on the brake," usually unconsciously.
- Media and school teach that wealthy people are evil or sacrificed ethics. His experience with self-made entrepreneurs is different: they see money as "a transfer of value," and a business must provide value to the market.
- People who work hard without results often haven't done the internal work; they don't believe they're worthy of wealth or that abundance exists.
- He recommends going beyond shallow books like The Secret and reading heavier ones such as Reality Transurfing, which he read at 15 and found life-changing.

## Meditate daily for emotional control

- He believes half of building wealth is "emotional homeostasis": managing emotions through thick and thin.
- Example: crypto and stock markets are a transfer of emotion; the winner has the best long-term view and the best emotional control.
- Meditation is observing your thoughts and realizing "your thoughts are not you." It exposes triggers behind habits such as eating and spending.
- His example question: when you have a good month in your business and find a way to spend all of it, why do you do that?
- He has meditated for seven years and says it helped surface unconscious behavior.

## Stack high value skills after high income skills

- High income skills (copywriting, sales, content creation, programming) are easy to quantify and get paid for directly. High value skills matter just as much but aren't paid directly.
- Sequence: build high income skills first, because you need revenue; then collect high value skills for long-term benefit.
- Example 1, talent: he credits about 70% of his business success to his team. He is very good at finding, convincing, attracting and retaining talent, using mission, compensation and performance bonuses. Danny is CMO of the agency, Kieran runs the education company, and the software company is the only one with a business partner. He says no one can manage multiple businesses without senior people running day-to-day operations.
- Example 2, accounting and cash flow: even with an expensive bookkeeper and accountant, he reads his own P&L and understands run rate.
- His agency is the cash flow business funding his lifestyle and investments. His other businesses carry millions in R&D costs that won't turn to revenue for years, so he tracks how long he can fund R&D before recouping.
- For his e-commerce eyewear brand Gadgey, restocking the G1 blue light blockers takes three months, and developing and stocking a new model takes five months, so cash flow planning is critical.

## Focus on who you must become, not on tactics

- Over five years he has asked "who do I need to become" to make the money he wants, rather than which tactic to implement. Tactics "come naturally" when character is right; he says character is about 80% of the equation.
- He admits ego made decisions worse from roughly 2017 to early 2020, with a chip on his shoulder from a bad high school experience and dropping out. He says he only dialed his ego in over the last year to year and a half.
- His traits to work on for going from 10 million to 25 million after tax by age 25: punctuality (he's "notoriously late" and slow to reply outside Slack; lateness "is breaking a promise to yourself"), humility, and gratitude (things feel stale or numb).

## Invest early, but build the business first

- He says to start investing as soon as possible, even 50 a month, because it compounds. He thinks starting at 18 instead of 20 would have added roughly 5 to 20 million dollars by 21.
- The jump from a few million at 20 to about 10 million liquid at 21 (investments plus cash reserves, not net worth) came from investing.
- Passive income view: investing is "the only true passive income." Affiliate income decays; he saw one software affiliate payout of 15,000 a month fall to about 8,000 in a year.
- Investing keeps lifestyle in check: his lifestyle barely changed when the agency went from 30,000 to 100,000 a month.
- He now puts 50% to 70% of monthly income into investments. His budget is what the portfolio earns monthly. His next goal is a 25 million portfolio by 25, which he says would let him spend four to six times as much as now.
- Target setting: at 16, after reading Money: Master the Game, his number was 2 million for a comfortable life; he has reached about five times that. Pick your own number, for example 1 million.
- Order of operations: until you reach 10,000 a month, don't expect investing to get you there; just dollar cost average small amounts into any asset class. "Your business is going to make you rich but your investments will make you wealthy long-term." Growing a business from 10k to 30k a month is far easier than growing a portfolio the same way, which could take a decade.
