## Attention is the real asset: protect it from feeds

- Iman treats YouTube as entertainment, not education: "in life you get ahead by applying not learning." He says the same of his own videos and most books ("15 pages of gold and then 300 pages of fluff").
- Real education is intentional: you know the specific problem in your business or career, then immerse yourself in that exact topic. Watching a podcast "because it might be useful" is a trap.
- Tactic: install a tool like Unhook. Turn on hide home feed, hide the video sidebar recommendations, and hide comments. Leave only the subscription box.
- He admits this costs him views ("shooting myself in the foot") but wants viewers to get ahead.
- He says content value shifts by stage: useful when beginning, less applicable at intermediate level, and relevant again at an exceptional level when you view it from the perspective of someone who has made it.

## Delete TikTok and mute people on Instagram

- Unless short-form is directly tied to how you make money (e.g. an affiliate marketer or short-form editor, or one of the roughly 1 in 50 who gets leads from TikTok), delete TikTok. Otherwise it only robs attention.
- His own team grew his TikTok to about 1.5-2 million followers before he had ever downloaded the app. He downloaded it only three or four months before the video, when short-form (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) was getting about 200 million views a month, so he could understand the platform for work.
- The upside of one good clip is outweighed by the "mental spiral" of the other junk.
- On Instagram, he accepts it matters as a "social CV" (dating, career), but he has about 90% of accounts muted. He only follows close friends and c-suite or top managers of his companies (CEOs, CMOs).
- Rule: you need the same focus and protection around your attention that he applies.

## Plan the next day the night before

- Iman calls this the most important productivity hack. Setting intention the night before makes your brain work on it overnight, so you start the day with real intention.
- Keep it simple: write down three to five tasks (three main things works too) the evening before.
- Today he checks his calendar for the next day's calls, meetings and duties, with an assistant helping, then writes the 3-5 things to accomplish.
- From 2016 to roughly 2019-2020, when he drove the business himself without today's leadership team (business partners and CEOs across his companies), he planned his day hour by hour.
- He suggests trying it that same night.

## Remove daily decisions: uniform, laid-out clothes, meal prep

- **Dress the part.** Dress for the context. Especially when young, overdress, because people already try to discredit young founders and a hoodie and graphic tee does not help. For meetings he wears smart casual or a suit; on a casual Zoom or YouTube recording a suit would look odd.
- **Home uniform.** On days of calls at home he wears the same thing: the same few jeans or trousers, the same shoes, and the same brand of t-shirt in different colors. This removes thinking about it.
- **Shoes indoors.** He wears shoes in the house because it signals "serious work" mode; flip-flops or sweatpants belong to off time. Avoid sloppy clothing and take yourself seriously.
- **Lay out clothes the night before.** The worst morning problem is decision fatigue about what to wear. It takes two minutes at night and makes mornings seamless, especially if you work out in the morning or struggle to wake up.
- **Meal prep weekly.** Prep the same breakfast, lunch and dinner for the whole week. To avoid boredom, change the meals week to week but keep them identical day to day. Early on he did this (and intermittent fasting, so no breakfast, for a few years).
- **Food is fuel.** Unless on holiday, retired or rich, food is not leisure. Restaurants and holidays are for enjoyment. His daily food: lots of meat, fish, healthy fats and lots of avocado. He now has a full-time chef at nearly every home, which he looked forward to once he made serious money.

## A simple three-step morning routine

- Most morning routines are "absolute BS." Iman used to run a 10-step routine that took four hours, so he is not judging, only reporting what works.
- Wake up at whatever time suits you; 5am doesn't matter. Exception: if you have school or a job, get early working or exercise hours in.
- Why his timing shifts: he dropped out of school at 17 because he already had an employee and "crazy money," and had to support his single mom. He woke early while in school, disliking it. He has woken at 5, 6, 7:30 and now 8:30, because he spends most of the year in Dubai and his team is in America and Europe, with leadership calls often 5:30 to 9 pm.
- Rule: three steps, no 10 or 12 steps, nothing that takes four hours. From waking to being at your desk working should take no more than an hour to an hour and a half.
- Past routine in London (about three years before): wake 6:30, journal over coffee, personal trainer arrives, walk two minutes to the gym.
- Current routine: cold plunge in the home gym, shower and get ready, then coffee at the desk and 30 minutes of "me time" writing ideas, vision for his companies, and reviewing corporate structure, investments and which holding companies and trusts they sit in.
- He trains at 4 pm now; earlier on, first thing in the morning was the only practical time.
