## Why a personal brand matters and what it is

- Iman says everyone already has a personal brand: "the world's perception of you." The only question is whether it helps or hurts you (jobs, college, private clubs, Rolex waitlists, dating).
- He says the one thing he'd credit most for 10+ years of success and $100M+ in earnings is an intentional personal brand, not the biggest one. He calls it the most valuable asset anyone can build.
- He also says it is not a business model. It is an asset that makes whatever you do easier: it is the "petrol and fuel" for his software companies, info business and eyewear brand (his smallest business, still a couple of million dollars a year in profit).
- Your brand should serve you, not the other way around. The goal is to get rich, not to get famous.
- His examples of brand-driven value:
  - George Heaton (Represent): the brand was struggling 5-6 years ago, then grew to hundreds of millions in value after he started posting founder-led content. He has only a few hundred thousand Instagram followers.
  - Guillaume (Lemlist): bootstrapped to a multi-hundred-million valuation with zero outside funding, mostly through LinkedIn content.

## Step 1: Clarify your intention and work backwards

- Decide what you sell first, then define your perfect follower, then plan how to attract more of them.
- Examples of intentional brands:
  - Nico Leonard: luxury watch retailer, built his brand around luxury watches.
  - Ryan Serhant: luxury real estate, built around luxury real estate.
  - A pastry shop owner who showed off his pastries to bring people into the shop.
- His own case: at his agency IG Media he built a marketing/SMMA brand to attract clients.
- Questions to answer: who is my perfect follower, and how do I attract more of them?

## Micro personal brands: audience quality over size

- "You do not need millions of followers to get rich with a personal brand. You just need a thousand of the right ones."
- Track profit per follower, not vanity metrics. A thousand followers at $150 each beats a thousand at $1 each.
- Example: a friend with about 4,000 YouTube subscribers and 5,000 Instagram followers makes multiple five figures a month from coaching.
- A real estate agent should attract a few thousand people looking to buy property. A female personal trainer should attract women who want to lose weight.
- He practices this himself. His main channel has about 5.5M subscribers and attracts beginners (titles mention "100,000", a common beginner goal). His separate Iman Gadzhi Business channel (about 10-15,000 subscribers, weekly uploads) targets owners making over $250K a year.
  - Titles are ultra specific, and thumbnails use terms like "cart abandonment" to deter beginners.
  - Views are lower on purpose. He licenses a business-to-business offer and only works with established businesses.
- He tells wrong-fit audiences, in effect, "I love you, I respect you, I just don't serve you."

## Step 2: Pick your platform with three questions

1. Which platform matches your intention? Go where your ideal audience already spends time (corporate law services: LinkedIn over TikTok; photographer targeting influencers: Instagram over X).
2. Which suits your skills and hides your weaknesses? Writers should use X/LinkedIn; people who like video should use YouTube, TikTok or Instagram.
3. Faceless or front-facing?
   - Front-facing always wins because "people connect with people."
   - If faceless, his options are X (alias) or YouTube (walkthroughs without a face cam).
- It is fine to pick more than one, but do not try to master all of them from day one. "A jack of all trades is a master of none."
- His own path: he started on YouTube because he liked videography, not from strategy. He later added X and LinkedIn once he'd built copywriting skill, and now has about 12 million followers across platforms.
- "Confidence comes from competence." His 2015-2016 videos are still online and were bad. Reps fix it.

## Step 3: Content as negative cost of acquisition

- Ads cost money per client. Content that sells on the back end means platforms pay you for views while you earn from sales. He calls this "negative cost of acquisition."
- Proof from one VSL on his YouTube channel:
  - About 10 million views.
  - YouTube AdSense paid him $266,000.
  - About 12-13,000 people bought a $1,500 info product at the end, roughly $20M in sales, with no acquisition cost.
- Consistency: 439 videos in 9 years, about one a week. But "consistency does not guarantee results"; the right strategy can grow an audience in months.
- Example: a channel called Callaway grew to 75,000 subscribers and 1.6M views in 4 months with 21 videos. Iman guesses it made at least $250K, possibly up to $1M, in that time.

## The three content pillars

- **Authenticity:** be unapologetically yourself and don't emulate others.
  - He has dressed the same way for years. He made tens of millions before buying a car (a Range Rover, purely a utility vehicle). His first watch was a £20 Timex from Argos, and he spent millions on watches before buying a car.
  - He shared private voice notes from a time of crippling anxiety. "An audience does not resonate with a hero, they resonate with the journey."
- **Impact (value):** you don't need to be an expert.
  - Joe Wicks isn't a pro bodybuilder or chef but built about 5M Instagram followers and tens of millions in revenue by documenting his journey.
  - "Level one wants to learn from level five on how to get to level three." Being close to your audience's stage is your advantage.
  - Three angles: teach something, present new opportunities, share personal experience.
- **Intention:** make content that attracts the right audience, not a big one. A big audience may come, but it shouldn't be the goal.

## Content types, categories and ideation

- Two types, and you need both:
  - Superficial content makes you known (tells your story, builds trust).
  - In-depth content makes you money (builds authority by educating).
- Four categories:
  - Actionable: show how, e.g. "the eight-step SMMA outreach process."
  - Narrative: tell a story, e.g. "I never followed my passion and neither should you."
  - Theoretical: explain why, e.g. "my five-step system to building a hundred-million-dollar team."
  - Attractor: share results.
  - Actionable and theoretical lean in-depth. Narrative and attractor lean superficial.
- Ideation:
  - Study what performs in every niche, not just yours.
  - Most good content is packaged as niche, problem, solution. Copy the packaging and swap in your own niche, problem and solution.
  - On YouTube, use VidIQ to find outlier videos. His example: a "make $10,000 as a student" video with a 13x outlier multiple, repackaged for another niche.
- Structure ("power of three"): big idea, three main points, three subpoints, a clear takeaway.
- Hooks: the first line of a video or copy. Include one of: bold claim, spark curiosity, promise value.
- Challenge: create 5 pieces of content in 10 days without posting, just to get reps in.

## Step 4: Monetize with information and one-on-one coaching

- A brand can turbocharge a current business. Example: Dr. Alex, a US chiropractor who built a TikTok brand, is booked months or years ahead, serves celebrities and can price as high as he wants.
- If starting from a clean slate, he says to sell information. Everyone already pays for info (books, school, a tennis instructor, a boxing coach, a corporate lawyer). The only difference is the packaging: one-on-one coaching, group coaching, courses or a book (he doesn't recommend writing a book).
- Use AI to synthesize research into an actionable plan. His software company Synthesis AI does this. "You're not going to beat AI" at synthesizing information.
- Find a unique value zone (UVZ) using the "third layer theory":
  - Go down three layers from the market level to reach untapped but big enough markets.
  - Don't sell "health" or even "weight loss." Sell weight loss for new mothers (postpartum weight loss).
  - Selling to a UVZ makes sales much easier.
- Start with one-on-one coaching:
  - Charge roughly $3,000 to $20,000 per client.
  - You only need 3-5 clients a month, and about 1,000 right-fit followers can support a full-time income.
  - Product effort is minimal. He still does coaching himself.
- He rejects the "don't sell your time for money" idea. Taylor Swift on tour and the CEO of Google both sell their time. The point is to make your time as valuable as possible.
