## The core model: rapid-testing digital products

- Iman argues drop shipping and Amazon FBA are not extinct but no longer offer first-mover advantage. Digital products (ebooks, PDFs, planners, templates, courses) still do, because AI has removed the technical and cost barriers.
- A digital product is created, delivered and consumed online, so after creation it can be sold infinitely with no inventory, shipping or storage.
- He frames it as a slot machine with unlimited spins: launch a minimum viable product (MVP) fast. If it sells, double down. If not, "spin again with a new idea." "The only way to lose is to stop spinning."
- He warns against the "perfectionism trap" of spending months building a course before knowing demand exists. His line: "stop focusing on perfection and start focusing on progress."
- His expectation: the first sale might take weeks or months, but "if you can get one sale, you can get 10."

## Step 1: Pick and validate a topic

- **Option 1, your own skills:** ask about your interests, skills and spare-time activities. You only need to be a few steps ahead of the buyer, whether that is a level 10 helping an 8 or a level 3 helping a 1. Start with the audience, find their problem, and reverse engineer.
- **Option 2, let AI choose:** tools that scrape markets (he names Synthesize's "generate random ideas" feature) recommend topics based on what already sells.
- Ideas he says are working: AI prompt packs, website templates, lead magnet templates, social media automation tools (mass unfollow, lead scraping, DM automation setups), and white-labeled software (he names AutoDS and System IO as allowing rebranding).
- **Go a level deeper ("unique value zone"):** market, then niche, then specific angle. Example: digital planners, then wedding planner, then a wedding budget tracker for destination weddings. Buyers think "this was made just for me."
- **Manual validation methods:**
  - ClickBank: check the gravity score, which shows how many affiliates successfully sell a product.
  - Facebook Ads Library: count advertisers for similar products, how long ads have run (months suggests profitable), and creative variety (many variations suggests testing and profit). He mentions the Ad Sparrow Chrome extension.
  - Marketplaces like Etsy: sort by category and filter by most money made.
- Copy-and-improve tactic: find a seller like "Mr. Notion," read his reviews, see what buyers dislike, and make yours better.
- Synthesize AI cross-references ClickBank, the Ads Library and other sources to save hours of research.

## Step 2: Build the store page before the product

- He recommends creating the store page first because writing the description, problem and audience forces you to see if the topic suits you. If not, pick another topic, since AI-made products are cheap to redo.
- Treat the page as digital real estate that must look professional and trustworthy.
- Platforms: Etsy, Gumroad and others. He discloses he co-owns his preferred platform (name garbled in the transcript), which handles hosting, payments, delivery, support and analytics. His stat: 571,000 customers but only about 10,000 sellers, a supply and demand imbalance.
- The page needs a clear headline speaking to the customer's pain or desire, plus a VSL (video sales letter) explaining what the product does, who it helps, what is inside and why to act now.
- He names Ghost Writer OS as a tool with an agent that writes landing page copy.

## Step 3: Create the product with an MVP

- Start with an ebook as a "gateway product": cheap and easy to make, quick to proof of concept, and good for building trust. Once it sells, turn it into a course or something more intricate.
- Workflow: have Synthesize AI generate the ebook copy by synthesizing existing information on the topic, then brand it in Canva by searching "A4 ebook templates" and pasting in the content.
- Ship the imperfect V1. His own first version was not the best either.

## Step 4a: Traffic method one, influencer arbitrage

- Partner with micro influencers (20-50K followers, sweet spot 30-40K) who have loyal audiences but little monetization. This is how he expects the first 1-3 sales.
- **Finding them:** search a niche keyword on Instagram (e.g. "productivity" for a Notion product), then use the suggested accounts feature, which groups similar small accounts. Also use niche hashtags and check who they follow.
- **Volume:** find 200-300 influencers a day and message at least 150 daily. Reach out mornings and evenings, since influencers work in the afternoon. Follow up 2-3 times a day (morning, afternoon, evening) for a maximum of 3 days, then move on.
- **Qualify with SocialBlade:**
  - At least 3% engagement rate.
  - 10K+ views on recent reels, with real views near or above follower count.
  - Story views matching follower size, plus consistent posting.
  - Audience 17+, preferably older.
- **Red flags:** purchased followers, declining followers over 30 days, low engagement despite high follower count, constant gambling or get-rich-quick promotion.
- **Questions to ask once they reply:** active story views, average audience age, top location, gender breakdown, story views for the last 7 days, link clicks from stories in the last 30 days. Cross-check against SocialBlade.
- **Viability calculator:** story views x engagement rate x conservative conversion rate x price. His example: 35,000 views x 0.23 = about 8,050 people; x 4% = about 322 sales; x $85 = about $27,370. (The transcript's figures are garbled, so treat the arithmetic as illustrative.)
- **Outreach scripts:** "Hey, Sophia, I have a partnership proposal for you. What's the best way to discuss it with you?" or "Hey, John, I'm looking to collaborate with you on a commission-based promotion. Are you open to hear more about it?" Send by DM or reply to their story, which triggers a notification.
- **Deal structure:** commission-only, typically 30-60% of each sale. With no cost of goods, you pay nothing upfront and take little risk. Create an affiliate link for each influencer on the selling platform. He says he avoids encouraging big risks for beginners.
- **Three-story sequence:**
  1. Survey/warm-up: curiosity without naming the product, e.g. "I found a way to make $1,000 a month with your solution" plus a poll asking if they want it shown.
  2. Method and results: reference the winning poll, show proof or screenshots, briefly explain the method.
  3. Call to action with urgency: "first come, first serve" plus the link.
- He cites the Brazilian creator Natan as having made millions with this sequence.

## Step 4b: Traffic method two, paid ads (after the first sales)

- Reinvest profits from influencer sales. Pursue ads only once there is proof of concept.
- **Google search ads:** target people already searching for a solution (e.g. "notion templates for productivity"). Find keywords with Google Trends or Ubersuggest. Set the daily budget equal to the product price (a $30 product starts at $30/day). Use AI for headlines and remove irrelevant search terms that do not convert (e.g. "notion sound effects").
- **Facebook ads:** people are scrolling, not searching, so the ad must grab attention. Create five image ads and five video ads (the partnered micro influencer can make video content). Optimize for sales with daily budget equal to the product price, run 7 days, then make variations of winners and pause the worst performers.
- He says tools like Ghost Writer can generate the ad copy and headlines.

## Step 4c: Traffic method three, organic content

- He calls organic content the best way to sell at scale ("the unlimited money glitch") and his own competitive advantage. He says he made well over seven figures from a single YouTube video, though the results came years after posting.
- It compounds slowly and has a steep learning curve, so he places it third. Get quick wins first.
- **Start faceless.** This keeps flexibility to test topics or sell multiple products without being tied to one niche, and to pivot freely. His example is the faceless channel Business Basics.
- **Rule:** make content intentional and tied to the problem the product solves. For a Notion product, show how organized you are, demo templates and show before and after productivity.
- **Funnel:** attract, provide value, build interest, convert with a CTA. Start with short-form (YouTube Shorts, Instagram, TikTok) by modeling viral content from other creators in your niche, then add a CTA such as "click the link in my bio."
- He has seen success with Pinterest too. Master one platform first, chosen by where your ideal customers spend time, then expand.
