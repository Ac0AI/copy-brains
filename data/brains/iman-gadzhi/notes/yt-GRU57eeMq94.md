## Early foundation and reading habit

- Iman started building himself first: from 14 he read roughly a book a week (2014 to late 2016), buying used books at Brixton Bookmongers in London instead of 12-13 pound new copies at Waterstones, so three to five books cost what one new book did.
- Early reading: Rich Dad Poor Dad, Think and Grow Rich, The Way of the Superior Man, The Kybalion, The Five Love Languages. He read The Fountainhead (about 1,100 pages) at 15 and decided "if I can read this I can read anything."
- At 14-15 his plan was to become a real estate agent (inspired by Million Dollar Listing) and move into property. He had decided he would never go to university.
- He dropped out of private school making roughly $8-10k. He was already doing client work in class: reaching out to prospects and sending proposals, "all the things that actually don't work," as a beginner does.
- He grew up on government benefits with his mother, in private school, with the most financially challenged background among his peers. He says that drove him.

## Money, children and inheritance

- He plans to leave his children nothing and to give wealth to causes such as schools in Nepal. His reason: he never wants them to get pride from something they have not earned.
- He would hand down something meaningful (for example a watch) only once they have "made it on their own," around 30.
- He argues money complicates life: "it's way worse to cry in a Lambo," because with no next checkmark left, the unhappiness is yours alone to face.
- He says he has felt unhappy for stretches of a couple of months even with every checkmark ticked.
- He expects to treat sons and daughters differently (he frames it as masculine vs feminine energy). A son gets no income support. A daughter pursuing a lower-paying craft with deep respect and full effort might get her income supplemented.
- He credits his Russian mother's "non-bullshit" outlook as one of his biggest superpowers.

## Disagreement with the "grind in your 20s" advice

- He disagrees with Gary Vaynerchuk's "eat [expletive] in your 20s" line unless you are compensated for it.
- His view: the best time to build is roughly 18 to 26. But collecting experiences matters as much as collecting money, and he would not want a life with no 20s or 30s.
- Once you reach a certain stage, "hard work is about as low down as it gets on the importance factor" for moving the needle. He says mainstream business content gives good sound bites but not stage-specific, practical nuance.
- Numbers he cites: at 17 he made $30-40k a month; at 18 he was flown business class to New York for a client training and paid $20,000 for two days; that month was his first with $200,000 profit; he later made $10M in 2021.
- He says his liquid net worth could, via low-risk yield farming, return about $1.9M a year net after tax.

## Stacking skills in the right order

- His core principle: stack skills so each remains valuable even if a specific business disappears. He asks of any skill: "if that business goes away, will it still give me dividends for decades?"
- His order of stacking: self-education and meditation first, then body (gym, nutrition), then social skills (he was going out with 21-22 year olds at 16), then wealth.
- He thinks order matters. People who stack wealth first often "go crazy" chasing women and then find their health and social skills are not in order.
- His pattern: throw himself into something in an unbalanced immersion, then back out and integrate it sustainably. Example: meditating an hour a day at 14-15, now 15 minutes in the morning and 30 before bed.
- He also names "private victories" (from The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People) as the work that sits behind visible "public victories" like expensive watches. He has it tattooed as a reminder that public wins require many times more private ones.

## Monk mode and seasonal work cycle

- He claims 70% of success is: stop drinking, stop smoking weed (he rejects "it makes me creative"), and get nine hours of sleep. Tactics matter less than whether someone digests and implements the information.
- His year: about six months of monk mode (no alcohol, no caffeine, no sugar, 30 minutes of meditation a day), roughly three to four months of looser living, and a transition period. He says he is the hardest-working on his team for the first six months and the least hard-working for about three months.
- He notes this only works because he now has a strong team. He could not do it in 2017.
- His self-awareness point: know your own rhythm rather than fighting it. In his case he cares only about work early in the year and only about wine, travel and experiences by June to August.
- He also says place matters: he makes his most progress in Cape Town (stripped back, "love of the craft") rather than London or Dubai, where visible wealth such as 20-30 million dollar yachts keeps the "not enough" feeling alive. He would not raise children in a big city.

## What he builds into his education programs

- His education company teaches agencies, because a service business is the best route from zero to $10k a month: you can fail for four months with no costs. By contrast, his e-commerce blue light blocker brand has had $150,000 production batches that took about nine months to turn into revenue.
- He says he could make more money teaching e-commerce but sticks to agencies because that is how he reached $20-30k a month at 17.
- Two tiers: a program for getting to $10k a month, and one for scaling agencies from about $10k to $100k a month.
- He deliberately "sneaks in" life content: the $10k program has four to five hours on mindset, meditation, detox, removing distraction and EMFs, and his monk mode protocol. The $100k program includes detox coaching, psychedelics, Epsom salt baths, steam sauna, liver cleanses and sleep biohacking.
- He has spent hundreds of thousands on biohacking gear (a Bemer, hyperbaric chambers, red light therapy) and concluded "less is more." He worked with a detox coach, Josh Mason, found via a YouTube presentation.

## Hiring, delegation and the semi-retirement plan

- Once past a certain stage, he says the most important thing is hiring and maintaining company culture, so that for every task someone on the team can do it better than him and he is "just there to steer the ship."
- His team gets profit share once they have been in the company long enough, so they are as incentivized to grow it as he is.
- Stated goal (announced as a first public statement): reach $20M net after tax by the end of 2022, roughly double what he had then. After that, semi-retire for 10-15 years, working a maximum of 20 hours a week.
- His reasoning: $20M can yield about $2-3M a year in cash flow if invested well. He is not shutting businesses down. He plans to keep scaling them while using the other hours for skiing, tango in Buenos Aires, cooking and climbing Everest.
- He expects to marry and have kids around his late 30s to 40, and to stay in business until his deathbed because he loves building.

## Mental health and closing advice

- In summer 2020, after a very peaceful six months in Cape Town, he had six to eight weeks of daily panic attacks, crying multiple times a day, and suicidal thoughts. The symptoms often lifted around 5 to 6 pm. He calls it a "dark night of the soul." He leaned on friends he describes as spiritual rather than wealthy.
- His advice: do the inner work as early as possible. He says most billionaires will not examine why they still feel "not enough," and he sees the same insecurity in himself about his business. For his physique he feels nothing to prove, for his business he still does.
- Practical tip: meditate, and take walks without your phone to be alone with your thoughts.
- He says he plans to eventually help with men's social and dating life and then trauma and spiritual work, before going offline.
