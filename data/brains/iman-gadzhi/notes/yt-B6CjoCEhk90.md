## Monk Mode as a protocol: five variables, 21-day first cycle, and why Iman rejects rigid programs

Iman has run intense focus periods since age 14 (about seven years at recording) and named them "monk mode" in 2020. His first formal version came at the start of 2020, during six weeks in Cape Town: no caffeine, no alcohol, keto/carnivore, no more than 60 minutes of screen time a day, and 45 minutes of daily exercise (even on Saturdays, his usual day off; a brisk walk counted). Version 2 added an hour a day of meditation, which he calls "extremely extremely hard."

His definition: a period of immersion you commit to, with intense focus and even isolation, and he sees "immense power" in simply stating that you are in such a phase.

- Constants across all his cycles: no alcohol, no caffeine, limited screen time, sticking to the diet (strict keto).
- Variables he adds or removes per cycle: once-a-day prayer (he is not religious but spiritual and believes in God), meditation, exercise.
- Lengths vary: some cycles 60 days, others a strict 21-day burst, after which he eases off slightly without abandoning his routines.

## The five-variable monk mode template

Monk mode is exactly five variables: three non-negotiables plus two of your own. Iman warns that ten daily rules becomes too much.

**Non-negotiables**
- Meditation: at least 10 minutes every day, any kind (he has done up to an hour).
- Exercise: 30 minutes every day (gym, yoga, run, basketball, brisk walk). The goal is an elevated heart rate. On days you fear overtraining, take a long walk.
- No alcohol or weed: "not even a beer." He rejects the "I can still smoke a joint at night and be fine" argument.

**Example variables to pick from**
- No caffeine (his personal favorite)
- Stick to a diet (he does keto)
- Pray once a day
- X hours of a specific outreach per day (e.g. 2 hours of outreach "no matter what the day is", or 10 Looms a day)
- Screen time under a set limit
- Delete Instagram from phone and computer

## Tracking, timeframe and why not 75 Hard

- Write the protocol on a whiteboard or set it as desktop wallpaper so you see it daily.
- Log your streak at the end of each day.
- First cycle: 21 days, since it will be "a shock to the system." Take about a week off, then try 30 days and work up. Jumping straight to 60 days may be too much.
- He criticizes programs like 75 Hard as too rigid and too long for entrepreneurs. Example: training twice a day is not beneficial for entrepreneurs, whereas he trains 45 minutes about five times a week anyway. A protocol should let you add what matters to your business.
- Action step: write out your protocol (three set plus two variables), post it in the community, and reply to your own post after the 21 days.

## Computer setup: starve distraction

Iman's thesis after four years of testing (he says he spent over $200,000 on health optimization and about $150,000 on desks, computers, keyboards, mics and office setups): "less is more." Focus is largely starving distraction; the analogy is horses wearing blinders. He adds that adding new productivity apps often just adds clutter. His desktop stack, which he says everyone should use, even someone aiming for a first five-figure year:

- **Facebook News Feed Eradicator** (Chrome extension): use Facebook on desktop with no feed or ads. "Zero value" in checking what others are up to.
- **Unhook** (remove YouTube recommendations): blocks home and sidebar recommendations. He keeps comments on, because he sometimes reads them.
- **Focus for Mac** with hard lock mode: choose sites to block and a timeframe, then it locks. It can allow specific sites (e.g. Business Manager for running client ads) while blocking others. Turn it off for prospecting or outreach; turn it on for studying, building your website, onboarding, hiring and systems work.
- **Brain.fm** for work music, which he has used for three-plus years and says is the only audio he works to. Spotify is blocked via Focus because notifications, new releases and playlist hunting hook you, and he calls working to rap "the worst idea ever." Brain.fm has research backing it.
- **Clean desktop:** one "triage" folder with subfolders, Mac Stacks turned on, and never more than six items on the desktop. His view: messy desktops usually come with messy minds.

## Phone and YouTube rules

- Minimalism: if you do not use an app daily, remove it, and remove anything that could distract.
- Add the Screen Time widget to the home screen. Many people are at about 8 hours a day while "building an agency." Use app limits to taper (e.g. from 1.5 hours on Instagram to 1 hour, then 30 minutes).
- For agency owners, the phone and social media are likely "the single biggest detractor" to success. Delete Instagram and Facebook from the phone; both work well for prospecting on desktop (Facebook even has a dedicated app).
- Iman runs two phones: one with four apps that nobody has the number for, used from waking (about 6:30) until about 1 pm, and a full one turned on at 1 pm. He gets a cortisol rush just from notifications arriving. A cheap used phone (about $150-200) or an iPod would do. His claim: turning your phone on in the morning costs about 50% of your focus. Cheaper alternative: keep the phone off or on airplane mode for the first hour, then push it later. He suggests gamifying how low you can get your screen time.
- In his office the phone, iPad and other devices stay downstairs. He distrusts people with six monitors, an iPad, an Apple Watch and constant pings: one good 5K screen is all he needs.
- YouTube: delete it from phone and tablet, and unsubscribe from all but about 10 channels (he has about 15). Use it at a desk, intentionally, with Notion or Google Drive open for notes and a watch-later list.
- His argument: "99% of the stuff you're watching is dumbed down" and made for someone else's benefit. About 70% of his own content exists to drive lead flow for GradiAgency.com (the transcript says "gradiency.com"). The real gems tend to be low-view interviews with software founders or high-level entrepreneurs.
- Students already have the paid material to rewatch (main course, bonus courses, Q&A recordings, student interviews).

## Spending on your environment: justified expenses

- Rule: when money first comes in, spend it on making work easier and more pleasant before status items. Most of it is a business expense (check with your accountant), unlike cars, drinks or luxury clothes.
- Gate: roughly after your first $5,000 month for the big desk and chair items; for headphones, after your first or second client, to create a positive feedback loop (sign a client, buy something that makes you excited to work, sign more).
- Why he skips a supercar despite being able to afford many: insurance, depreciation per mile, and he does not go anywhere to drive it, so it would be an unused liability that distracts from the business. Watches have no mile counter and hold value. He was holding over $1 million in a safety deposit box while in London for three months.
- Clothes: he buys expensive but unbranded pieces (a Bottega or Lauren black t-shirt around $400), so there is no need to wear them out to justify the purchase. Test every purchase by how it changes your daily routine, not just its price.

## Desk, computer and gear list

- **Desk:** Fully stand-up desk, hardwood. His first laminate desk showed fingerprints, so he switched. The highest-end model was about $1,000 in the US, and his current one about £1,100 (the transcript says "1100 pounds"). Treat it as a 10-year purchase. Footrest mat that mimics a forest floor.
- **Chair:** Herman Miller Aeron, about $1,500, with a 10-year warranty; he has had it three years. It is "the Rolls Royce of chairs."
- **Computer:** a fully specced MacBook Pro, replaced yearly because he sees "no higher ROI." He gives the old one to a team member or friend. This is not a recommendation for beginners.
- **Monitor:** an LG 5K with integrated 1080p webcam, single-cable setup, built-in speakers (no external ones). He says he spent upwards of $7-8K trying other monitors.
- **Audio:** Shure SM7B through a Focusrite Scarlett interface (admits it is overkill; he previously used a Blue Yeti), Bose QuietComfort 2 headphones (second-best he has found; AirPods Max are about $600, similar sound quality but overkill). He says over-ear headphones noticeably deepen focus 30 minutes into a Brain.fm session.
- **Other:** yellow paper (a habit from hearing a famous author used it), a Montblanc pen, incense burning on every floor (about $10-15 for 50 sticks plus a $20 holder on Amazon; he accepts the health trade-off), and tidy cable management. He says it does not need to be perfect and a couple of visible cables is fine.
- Mindset: he treats his desk as "my temple," so small expenses that improve it are insignificant relative to a four-year-old agency.
