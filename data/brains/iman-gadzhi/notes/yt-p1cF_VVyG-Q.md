## How Iman prices and structures SMMA retainers (October 2020)

- **Standard pricing:** minimum set retainer of £4,000/month, rising to about £12,000/month, with a percentage of return on ad spend (ROAS) added in the past. Case study or webinar funnel builds are charged £15,000-£25,000 upfront.
- **New approach:** he blends the funnel build into the retainer. He charges less per month but locks in a longer minimum, so the guaranteed contract value stays similar. Everything rolls month by month after the minimum.
- **Deals in one weekend:**
  - £2,500/month, 6-month minimum (£15,000 guaranteed)
  - £4,000/month, 5-month minimum (£20,000)
  - £3,500/month, 5-month minimum (£17,500)
  - £2,500/month, 6-month minimum (£15,000)
  - He sums these to roughly $80,000 of guaranteed contractual income (his total in the video does not match the line items, so only the "~$80,000" is reliable), with no new team members needed.
- **Why longer minimums:** "churn is not a fun thing at any agency." Longer client lifetime value beats chasing whales. His goal is an average retention of 12-18 months.
- **Guaranteed value over headline price:** he compares an £8K/month client on a 3-month minimum (£24K) with a £4K/month client on a 6-month minimum (£24K). He now thinks in terms of the "guaranteed value of the client."
- **Extending an existing client:** he dropped a 15% ROAS fee for a client paying £3,000/month plus 15%, in exchange for a 6-month extension. That put the client on at least 10 months total.
- **When to take percentage of ROAS:** only for info product businesses without a phone call funnel (a direct purchase on a sales page) or for e-commerce. Phone call funnels are "a nightmare" to track for a percentage.
- **Cost of lower-margin deals:** he accepts smaller deals when the team can deliver cheaply. A case study funnel takes his team (Kieran and Danny) about a day, so it costs little of his time. He would not take a £4,000/month, 3-month deal on that basis.

## Anchoring price with a guess and the "machine" framing

- **Anchoring:** on one call he asked the prospect to guess the agency's price. The prospect guessed "at least 4K per month," which was right. Iman then quoted £2,500/month with a 6-month minimum, so it "seems like a total steal." He says this is not part of his normal script.
- **Cost-replacement framing:** the prospect was paying about $4K/month in commissions to closers. Iman framed the agency as replacing that: "a machine never wants a raise," works 24/7, and is never sick.
- **Value framing:** the client was making multiple five figures a month and shouldn't be running his own ads at that scale. Iman told him, "you're fired" from ads so he could focus on CEO work.
- **Guarantee:** at that time he guaranteed 1.5x return on ad spend, with a refund of service fees if missed. He had refunded only once in the agency's history. He says the guarantee puts all the risk on him, which is why he needs to trust his team on every deal.

## Logistics handling versus objection handling

- **The distinction:** objection handling is for prospects who are not sold. Logistics handling is for prospects who are sold but need to understand how the service works.
- **Example:** a prospect with a working system feared Iman's team would replace it with a new funnel and cut his income. Iman explained the sequence: take over and improve the current system, build the new funnel in parallel, run ads to it, and only retire the old system once the new one outperforms it. After that the deal closed.
- **Tonality:** the prospect sounded unenthusiastic, but Iman read it as a logistics concern, not a lack of interest.
- **Taking payment:** he does not leave a call until payment is taken. "Unless I take payment, they ain't a client." Even £1 with a card on file counts as a commitment.
- **Billing friction:** invoicing took 22 minutes of a 47-minute call because of a European client's credit limit. He found German, Swiss and Dutch clients commonly have this problem, and US and UK clients less so. He prefers automatic billing over manual invoicing.
- **Easing payment:** he invoices on the call, asks for company details in the Zoom chat, and sets up Slack and the strategy session right away. With one client he considered offering PayPal to close faster but decided against it.

## The demo call plus close structure

- **Setup:** his CMO Danny runs a 15-minute demo call to qualify prospects. At the time they were doing 2-3 demo calls a day. About 50% of demos were qualified, though the latest ones were nearly all qualified. Iman then takes the 45-minute discovery and close.
- **Delegating the decision:** Iman does not take deals he is unsure about. Danny can push for a lower-priced deal if he is confident he can run the account without it becoming a burden. Iman needs trust in him first.
- **Friction and call count:** a third call nearly lost one deal. One prospect was 8 minutes late because of a time zone mix-up. "Any sort of added friction really ruins the likeliness of the sale." He prefers a one-call close, but will do two calls when needed and says a second call is fine if it is guaranteed to happen.
- **Not a tactic:** a prospect said he noticed Iman's "I need to talk to Danny" tactic. Iman said it was not a tactic: the guarantee means he really must check that the account is viable before agreeing.
- **Goal:** the last thing he wants to remove himself from is sales. Once he does, he will have a "totally automated agency."

## Which clients to take (and which niches to avoid)

- **Info product businesses are hard:** about 80% of his clients are info product businesses, and about 50% of deals require extra work such as building a case study funnel or cleaning up automations and emails. He calls it "the hardest niche in the world."
- **Advice for beginners:** don't run ads for info product businesses at first. Start with local businesses or e-commerce. E-commerce is "a hell of a lot easier," but still harder than local businesses.
- **Deals he turns down:** he nearly turned down a £4,000/month deal because of the complexity of the funnel build, and he declines B2B all-in-one platforms because the agency does not do B2B. In one case he considered referring a prospect to a strategic referral partner.
- **B2B check:** for a B2B prospect he asked for the client's lifetime value and how much they would pay to acquire a customer. He reasoned from his own B2B agency numbers: he is comfortable spending about £6,000 to acquire a client against an average retainer of about £6,000 per month. The prospect's number fit his estimated cost per scheduled call. The deal was £2,500/month, 6-month minimum, with no guarantee, using deliberately pessimistic assumptions (a 15% close rate versus the prospect's actual 80%).
- **Cost to serve:** he keeps complexity low internally and with clients, which he says is why the agency gets such good results.

## Margins, business buckets and team

- **Agency:** 75-90% margins, which he calls his "lifestyle business." He never lets it dip below 75% (he later says 65%, so treat the floor as roughly 65-75%). His stated goal is $150K/month profit, after hitting $100K/month the previous year.
- **Education company:** 25-35% margins. Costs include around £30,000/month on advertising, £4,000/month on software, £35,000/month on staff, schools in Nepal, and events. He won't let it fall below about 20%.
- **Clothing line (Gadzhi):** about 60% margins. He is willing to scale it to seven figures in revenue at zero profit, because it is a passion project and he reasons about each business in a separate bucket. Plan: an influencer manager and 10-20K/month on influencer promotions, rather than scaling to 300K/month through ads.
- **Hiring:** he cut 150 performance marketer applicants to seven, then three final interviews. Candidates had spent more than $20 million on ads. Rather than paying a recruiter £4-8K, he passed the strong runners-up to his students.
- **Rhythm:** after months focused on other companies, he spends the next several months on agency growth. He expects the agency to stay in maintenance mode while he works on others.
