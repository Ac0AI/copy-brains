## Action-based goals beat result-based goals

Iman argues that you should stop setting result-oriented goals such as "get to $10,000 a month" or "get in shape". His reasons:

- Result goals put the outcome on a pedestal, which tells your brain you don't deserve it.
- They give you no objective way to judge whether today's work is moving you forward, so you fall back on feelings. That is why people feel busy but never arrive.
- Set a destination without a map and you end up consuming hours of content and trying countless methods.

His fix is to set goals as actions repeated consistently. Fitness example: instead of "put on 10 lb of muscle", set "complete 220 weightlifting sessions this year and have 250 days eating over 3,000 calories". The result becomes a byproduct. If you miss, you know exactly where and by how much you failed. He says most people fail because they don't do what they already know they should, not because they lack information.

## How to pick a money-making skill: four parameters

Iman says there is no one-size-fits-all answer, so he scores each skill on four parameters:

- **Ease of learning:** pick something you can learn the basics of fast. Highest-paying means nothing if it takes years (his example: learning Mandarin).
- **Perceived value:** how much clients will pay. Formatting computers is easy but pays low; a tax adviser who saves companies eight figures can justify a seven-figure fee.
- **Barrier of entry:** tools or upfront investment. Beginners should avoid high barriers "like the plague".
- **Personality fit:** the most overlooked. A skill that fights your personality will fail even if it scores well elsewhere (coding for someone who hates screens, sales for someone who hates socializing). Play to strengths, not weaknesses.

He adds that if you already have a skill, such as blockchain development or coding, you can ignore ease of learning and barrier of entry. A high barrier then works in your favor by thinning the competition.

## His skill scorecard: why copywriting, sales and e-commerce win

Iman rates seven "top income skills" for 2024:

- **UX design:** medium difficulty to learn. Perceived value has dropped because Wix, Squarespace and Webflow let businesses do it themselves. Low barrier. Suits creative introverts.
- **Copywriting:** basics are easy via proven structures that remove about 90% of the guesswork. Very high perceived value because it directly generates sales and clients can track the return. Low barrier (computer and internet). He sees the best copywriters as analytical, logical thinkers. Write at "a fifth grader level" and use Grammarly.
- **Coding:** hard to learn, high value, medium barrier. Suits analytical introverts.
- **Sales:** easy in its simplest form, cart recovery (see below). High value, since you can attribute revenue directly. Barrier is a smartphone and internet. Works for extroverts and introverts, but poorly for people who need creative work and dislike repetition.
- **SEO:** easy, but value has fallen from tool-driven supply. Much of it can be done with AI. Boring for analytical or creative people. He recommends copywriting instead if you are above-average capable.
- **E-commerce:** building a Shopify store is easy; getting traffic was the hard part, but viral TikTok product videos changed that about 18 months before this video. High value, low barrier (phone and TikTok account).
- **Blockchain:** high value but extremely hard to learn, with few resources. Not for beginners.

His conclusion: copywriting, sales and e-commerce have the best balance. Pick one by gut feel, "no need to overthink it", because any of them can work.

## Action-based goals for each skill (copywriting, cart recovery, viral TikTok)

Iman defines success per skill as a volume of actions:

**Copywriting ("learn as you earn"):**
- Pick 10 to 20 brands you'd love to work with and write emails, ads or landing pages for them as if they were clients ("mock clients"), using one copywriting framework.
- Send the best pieces with a note like: "I love the work that you guys do, I've recently started taking on a few copywriting projects and to practice I decided to write you a few emails for your brand... feel free to send them to your email list or use them on your ads."
- Some will respond, and you then offer to write for them on a retainer.
- Goal: write 1,000 pieces of copy in 6 months, about 5 a day. He says sending that volume to 500 to 1,000 businesses and getting no client is "virtually impossible".

**Sales (cart abandonment recovery):**
- Find online sellers (stores, coaches with a course or high-ticket program). Offer to message everyone who started checkout but didn't buy, answer questions and nudge them to finish.
- Work for free upfront and take a commission on recovered sales. Zero risk for the seller, since you only get paid on sales they'd otherwise have lost.
- He says this can lift an online business's revenue by 30 to 50%.
- Goal: send 10,000 cart abandonment messages by year end, about 55 a day (he notes the average 20-something sends about 128 messages a day).
- Math: a 5% recovery rate (which he calls "terrible") at a 20% commission is about $10,000.

**E-commerce (reposting viral product videos):**
- Download product videos that already have millions of views from other TikTok accounts and repost them without editing. Find the right viral videos and products.
- Goal: 20 videos a day for 6 months, about 3,600 videos.
- Conservative math: 1% pop off (36 videos) at 100,000 views each is 3.6 million views. At 0.1% buying, that is 3,600 orders. At $50 per order, about $180,000 revenue, and at a 30% margin about $54,000 profit.

## Why you need an execution plan, and what he recommends

Iman says 90% of people fail at step five, the plan, because they improvise it from a few YouTube videos. His fitness analogy: 220 gym sessions needs a trainer's protocol (exercises, order, reps, sets, weights, deload periods) and 3,000 calories a day needs a nutritionist's meal plan. In business, the equivalent is a plan for how you will write 1,000 pieces of copy, send 10,000 messages or post 3,600 videos.

Two options: build it yourself (valid if you can't afford anything else, but a higher failure risk) or have a professional build it. Hiring a top copywriter or store owner for a personalized plan is too expensive for most, so Iman says he paid for three experts to record step-by-step courses:

- Copywriting: Penta Profit program from his CMO Louis Berger (emails, ads and sales copy using AI and fill-in-the-blank structures).
- Sales: Six Figure Sales Rep program from his former sales manager Paul Daly (appointment setter or online sales rep).
- E-commerce: a program from Jordan Welch showing how to build a Shopify store from scratch and get the first $1,000 in sales organically.

He says each cost him six or multiple six figures in upfront speaking fees to produce.

## How he packages and sells the entry offer

Iman's funnel structure in this video:

- He builds urgency through "lost opportunities": a free event (Time's Up), seven free master classes cut from his $1,195 program, and a limited enrollment for his Agency Accelerator. He claims 16-year-olds reached $22,000 a week from the free event.
- He targets people who wanted Agency Accelerator but lacked the money, with a goal of making their first $1,000.
- Each course sells separately for $1,500. Digital Launchpad bundles all of them plus other courses (trading, men's fashion, digital nomad) for $270 a year, or $37 a month (monthly gets fewer perks and no 40% discount).
- Annual subscribers lock in Agency Accelerator at $995 versus the $1,995 full price, and what they spend on Digital Launchpad counts as credit toward it. Enrollment for Agency Accelerator was closed at the time.
- He positions the low-priced tier as a way to earn the first $1,000 and then upgrade, and says it is not meant to make people millionaires or $10K a month.
