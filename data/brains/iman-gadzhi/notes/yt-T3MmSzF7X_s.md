## Prevent objections before pricing

Iman says a good closer handles objections but a great closer prevents them, and the goal is the prospect pulling out their credit card, not objection handling. He wants closing to be as easy as possible. Before discussing price on any SMMA sales call he does two things:

- **Answer every question first.** "A confused buyer never buys," so if the prospect still has questions, don't go into pricing.
- **Run a 1 to 10 tie down.** He asks: on a 1 to 10 scale, with 1 being "get me off this call right now" and 10 being "this is exactly what I've been looking for," where do you fall?
- This gauges interest and surfaces doubts. If they say 8 or lower, follow up with: "gotcha, appreciate you sharing that with me. Out of curiosity, what's stopping you from being a nine or a 10?"
- The answer shows their main concerns, lets you address them, and moves them toward a 10 so they're sold on the offer by the time price comes up.

He says that if discovery calls are run correctly there should be few or no objections to handle.

## Every objection is a trust objection hidden behind a smoke screen

Iman argues every objection is essentially trust-based: the prospect doesn't trust you, your agency, or themselves. Nobody says "I don't trust you and I think you're lying," so they use "smoke screens" that feel socially acceptable and let them exit without feeling forced to buy.

- Typical smoke screens: "I need to speak to my spouse," "I need to run the numbers and think about it for a day or two," "it's too expensive and I might not afford it."
- His test: if your agency were guaranteed to work and hit their goals, would they need to think about it, would money be a problem? No, they'd get creative to find a solution.
- His analogy: if you were diagnosed with a terminal illness and a guaranteed cure cost $100,000, you'd borrow, sell assets or take loans to get it.
- The aim is for prospects to trust your agency can deliver so completely that money, time and other objections "melt away."
- So the real issue is never that they lack money or need to consult someone; they don't yet trust you to get them to their goals.

## The five categories of objections

Iman says every objection, however unique it seems, falls into one of five categories:

1. Think about it
2. Business partner or spouse
3. Finances
4. Doubt
5. Logistics (his favorite)

Knowing the categories lets you build a framework for each and handle any objection thrown your way.

## The five-step framework, steps 1 and 2: diffuse and reframe

**Step 1: Diffuse.** Beginners treat objections as rejections and get defensive, which is the last thing to do. Instead, avoid confronting the prospect and make their concerns feel valid for the moment.

- Lines: "I can respect that," "I totally understand that," "yeah, that's not a problem at all."
- Why: the prospect has effectively said no and expects you to push back. Pushing triggers fight or flight and the sale is lost for good.
- His analogy: a 10-year-old told by mom to brush their teeth who says no, knowing a fight is coming. Prospects feel the same, so show you're on their side.

**Step 2: Reframe to the goal.** Using Covey's "begin with the end in mind," shift focus from the objection to the end goal they want.

- Line: "Just so I know, before you think about this, do you actually feel like we can get you to your goals? Because for me personally that's the only part that really matters."
- This reminds them why they considered the service, makes the call outcome-focused, and reveals how convicted they are.
- Listen to tonality. Some say yes just to be polite. If your gut says something is off: "From your tonality just there I can sense maybe something is missing. If you're not being honest with me, maybe you're just being polite, I'd rather know what's on your mind. If it's not the right fit, it's not the right fit. So to go back, do you really feel like we can get you to your desired goal?"
- Doing this effectively jumps you to isolating the objection (step 4).

## Steps 3 to 5: tie down, isolate, overcome

**Step 3: Tie down.** If they sound confident you can get them to their goals, use a tie down, a micro commitment that gets a yes or agreement and shows they're following along.

- Benefits: feedback on where the prospect is, control of the call, "yes momentum," and a sense of how close the deal is.
- Get them to say why your service can help: "Okay cool, why do you feel like that's the case?" Keep it natural, like a friend supporting a friend in a decision.
- Having them verbalize the reasons strengthens their commitment and reduces doubt.

**Step 4: Isolate the real objection.** Once they've said you'll get them results and why, ask: "Yeah, got you. Now when you think about it, what specifically do you feel like you need to think about, just so I have an understanding and maybe I can help?" This surfaces the actual objection, whether financial, time or something else.

**Step 5: Overcome it** with analogies, logic and reframes, chosen to fit the type of prospect.

- He quotes Sun Tzu: know your enemy and know yourself. The better you understand the prospect, the better you choose analogies and reframes.
- Be confident and firm, which is not the same as rude. You have the client's best interest at heart and are helping them see this is the right decision.

## Service over sales mentality

Iman's mindset for the final step is "service over sales." His rule of thumb: of 10 prospects, one will never buy, one will buy regardless, and eight need your help to decide. Helping them decide from a place of authority is what wins them.

- Treat firmness as service, not pressure.
- Most closes are decided by how well you guide the undecided majority.
