## Iman's framework for choosing an AI side hustle

- Iman says the real problem is too many options, not a lack of them. People save 20 "perfect" ideas, start none, and fall behind those who "just picked one."
- He covers five AI side hustles he calls proven, all using specialized AI tools so a beginner needs no experience.
- To avoid analysis paralysis he ranks them with a scoring method (see the CLEAR section below).
- Order of the final ranking, best first: AI digital products, AI voice agents, AI websites, AI short-form clipping, AI UGC.
- His closing advice: "I don't care what AI side hustle you start, just as long as you start a side hustle."

## AI UGC (user-generated content) ads

- **Why it works:** Brands run UGC-style videos as ads. Content that looks shot on an iPhone now often beats high-production ads. Unpaid UGC depends on virality, and the only way to offset luck is volume across platforms.
- **The gap:** Brands need a constant stream of new creatives each month because the videos have short life cycles. Making them manually is time-consuming.
- **Workflow:**
  1. Write the script.
  2. Upload or create an AI face.
  3. Upload product pictures.
  4. The software outputs a video of a person talking to camera and holding the product.
- Iman names Pictory as an example tool. He says you can spin up 20 variations in an hour, in any language.
- **Pricing:** A single AI UGC creative is $50 to a couple hundred dollars. The real money is monthly retainers for a batch of videos. Example: 5 retainers at $2,000/month is $10,000/month.
- **Pros:**
  - Near-zero startup cost.
  - Low skill barrier.
  - Demand repeats because brands need new creatives forever.
  - High margins.
- **Cons:**
  - Everyone is starting, so sales skills matter.
  - AI videos can look off and audiences are better at spotting them.
  - Some brands avoid AI UGC entirely.
  - It is a service, so income scales with your work and has a natural ceiling.
- **CLEAR score: 12/25.** Competition 1, Longevity 1, Effort 5, Autonomy 2, Recurring 3.

## AI websites for local businesses

- **The gap:** Plumbers, dentists, gyms and renovators often run on a Facebook page and a phone number. They have been pitched $5,000, 2-month builds in technical language they don't understand.
- **How it works:** Tools like Lovable and Bolt build a site from a plain description and a few uploaded images, so there is no code. Iman notes "you're not the person solving this problem... AI is."
- **Pricing:** It is a one-off project. Example: $999 for the build plus a $99/month maintenance and hosting fee. A beginner can charge $1,000+.
- **Upsell path:** Once the site impresses the client, sell SEO or paid traffic. His example is a cheap $999 site followed by $1,999/month for SEO/ads. This takes more skill and often a team, so he suggests starting with websites only and adding services later.
- **Pros:**
  - Higher ticket than UGC.
  - Minimal upfront investment.
  - Obvious demand that needs no education.
  - You can show prospects a demo of the finished site.
- **Cons:**
  - Competition, since the tools are free for everyone and DIY builders exist.
  - One-and-done pricing means "you never stop selling."
  - Hard to reach full autonomy.
- **CLEAR score: 15/25.** Competition 2, Longevity 5, Effort 4, Autonomy 2, Recurring 2.

## AI short-form clipping

- **The gap:** Podcasters, YouTubers and coaches sit on hours of long-form content. Cutting an episode into 20 vertical clips takes hours, so they don't do it.
- **How it works:** AI tools such as Opus Clip scan long content, find the best moments, cut the clips and burn in captions. Your job is quality checking and delivery. Hours of editing becomes "the same time it takes to grab a coffee."
- **Pricing:** Monthly retainers of $1,000 to $2,000 per client. Example: 5 clients at $1,500 is $7,500/month.
- **Pros:**
  - Recurring by design.
  - Lowest skill barrier of the five (you only need to tell good from bad).
  - Almost no costs beyond the software.
  - Growing demand.
- **Cons:**
  - Crowded, which leads to price competition ("a race to the bottom").
  - Small ticket size, so you need volume.
  - You are "essentially a factory": more clients means more clips and more hours, and income stops when production stops.
  - Creators may eventually do the clipping themselves.
- **CLEAR score: 14/25.** Competition 2, Longevity 2, Effort 4, Autonomy 2, Recurring 4.

## AI voice agents

- **The problem:** Local businesses (dentists, clinics, law firms, home services) live on phone calls. A missed call goes to the next business, and a customer is worth hundreds or thousands. Owners rarely notice because they never see the missed calls.
- **The pitch:** Walk in and tell them exactly how much they are losing, then offer to stop it. The agent answers on the first ring 24/7, sounds human, holds the conversation and books into the calendar.
- **Setup (Vapi is his suggested starting tool):**
  1. Train the agent on its job: what it says, what it asks, what it does with the answers.
  2. Give it calendars, knowledge bases and extra instructions.
  3. Set up call forwarding from the business's number.
- **Pricing:** Setup fee of $1,000 to $3,000, then $800 to $1,500/month management. Example: 5 clients at $1,000/month is $5,000/month plus a setup fee per new client.
- **Leverage:** Once the agent answers the phones, canceling means missing calls again, so clients feel the loss immediately and don't cancel.
- **Pros:**
  - Highest ticket of the service models.
  - ROI is obvious in the pitch.
  - Sticky clients.
  - Runs without you day to day.
- **Cons:**
  - Steepest learning curve and the most effort of the service models.
  - Glitches, updates and changes still come back to you.
  - Growth still means another client and another agent.
- **Why it still ranks high:** The difficulty filters people out. "Fewer people push through the barrier," which is why it pays the most.
- **CLEAR score: 16/25.** Competition 4, Longevity 3, Effort 2, Autonomy 4, Recurring 3.

## AI digital products

- **Core idea:** Every service above ties money to your showing up. A digital product (ebook, program, template, paid community or tool) is built once and sells the same way to each buyer. Iman calls it leveraged or front-loaded income rather than "passive income," which he says is BS.
- **What AI changed:** AI can research a topic, structure it and produce a full draft faster than a human. You no longer need to be an expert or have a production team or custom platform. You need to point the AI at a problem.
- **Distribution options:**
  - Sell to your own audience.
  - List on a digital product marketplace.
  - One of his favorites: build it for someone else's audience and take a cut of every purchase.
- **The math:** Price at $500 and 20 sales/month gives $10,000. Price at $1,000 and 10 sales gives $10,000, which is less than one sale per day. The 10th sale costs the same as the 200th.
- **Pros:**
  - No marginal cost per sale.
  - No ceiling tied to your hours.
  - Almost no costs beyond tools.
  - No client to manage or deliver to.
  - You step into demand that already exists.
- **Cons:**
  - A winner isn't guaranteed first time. He compares it to "a slot machine with unlimited plays."
  - It doesn't sell itself, so you need a distribution channel working.
- **CLEAR score: 20/25.** Competition 4, Longevity 4, Effort 4, Autonomy 5, Recurring 3. It is the only option where stopping work doesn't automatically stop the money.
- Iman's team built two tools for this, Synthesize AI and Ghostwriter OS, and he says he has seen it work firsthand.

## The CLEAR method for scoring a side hustle

- Iman scores each side hustle out of 5 on five criteria, for a perfect 25.
  - **Competition:** how crowded is it? A high score means the lane is open; a low score means "knife fighting a thousand other people on day one."
  - **Longevity:** will it still pay in 5 years, or is it a trend gone in months?
  - **Effort:** how much effort to acquire the skills needed to succeed?
  - **Autonomy:** does the business run without you, or does income stop when you stop showing up?
  - **Recurring:** does it keep paying, or do you wake up at zero every month hunting for the next payment?
- **Final scores:**

| Side hustle | Score |
|---|---|
| AI digital products | 20 |
| AI voice agents | 16 |
| AI websites | 15 |
| AI clipping | 14 |
| AI UGC | 12 |

- His takeaway: every service model ties income to effort, and digital products is the only one where that link can be broken.
- He notes the biggest online entrepreneurs mostly run digital product businesses, and "everything starts as a side hustle." Which hustle works best also depends on the market.
- His advice for beginners on the service models: start with one simple offer (for example websites) and add services like SEO or ads later.
